**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterisation in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

  This is the Court's own framing, not merely a party submission. It is thin, but it is the only factual basis offered.

- **Peaceful nature:** No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. The peaceful nature is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed under Article 11, not by any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited by the Court in support of its necessity finding. The Court states only: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints." The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely in the reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others), not in any specific facts drawn from the present case.

---

**(c), (d), (f):** As noted previously, these elements are not discussed, and accordingly no factual grounding can be identified for them.
