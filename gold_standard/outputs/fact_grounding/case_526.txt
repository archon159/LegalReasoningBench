Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is thin — the Court does not independently establish the existence of assemblies as a factual matter; it appears to take it as given from the description of the complaints.

**ii) Participation:**
The same passage provides the only grounding: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." Again, this is a characterisation of the applicants' role rather than an independently established factual finding.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the nature or conduct of the assemblies that would support a finding that they were peaceful. This element is implied purely by the Court's proceeding to apply Article 11, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit in the opening paragraph: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the only concrete facts stated — arrest, dispersal, and conviction — and they serve as the factual basis for the interference finding.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity assessment is not grounded in any case-specific facts stated in this reasoning section. The conclusion rests entirely on reference to prior case-law: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" — but no specific facts from that material are quoted or described. The Court references the leading cases of *Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, and *Kasparov and Others* as the basis for its conclusion, but does not articulate which facts of the present case map onto those precedents.

In short: the necessity finding has no independently stated factual grounding in this text beyond the bare description of arrest, dispersal, and conviction already noted under interference.
