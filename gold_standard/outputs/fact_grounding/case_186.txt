**Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element**

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase: "public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
The Court does not elaborate on the nature, size, location, or purpose of the assemblies. The existence of assemblies is taken as a given, grounded solely in this brief characterization.

**(ii) Participation:**
The only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."
Again, no further factual detail is provided. The Court does not examine the specific roles of individual applicants.

**(iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the conduct of the assemblies or the behavior of participants. This element is implied structurally but has no explicit factual basis in the text.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
These are the only concrete facts identified as constituting the interference. No further detail about the circumstances of arrest, the legal basis used, or the nature of the convictions is provided in this passage.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding in the text is notably thin. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."

This is the only factual statement supporting the necessity assessment, and it is essentially negative in character — the Court identifies no new facts, rather than grounding its conclusion in specific established facts. The substantive factual basis is outsourced entirely to the prior leading cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov), which are referenced but not quoted or summarized here. No specific facts from the present case are cited to support the necessity finding.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**Overall observation:** The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. The Court relies almost entirely on a reference to prior case-law and a generic statement about having examined "all the material submitted," without identifying any case-specific facts that drive its conclusions. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case procedure where the Court treats the matter as governed by established precedent.
