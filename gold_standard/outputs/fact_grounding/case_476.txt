Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterisation: *"public assemblies."* This is the Court's description, not a party submission adopted by the Court. No further factual detail about the nature, location, size, or organisation of the assemblies is provided in this reasoning section.

**ii) Participation:**
The factual grounding is: *"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies"* — which implies the applicants were present at the assemblies — and the Court's characterisation of them as *"organisers or participants of public assemblies."* Again, this is the Court's own framing.

**iii) Peaceful:**
There is no factual grounding whatsoever in this reasoning section. The Court does not refer to any facts establishing the peaceful nature of the assemblies. The implication of peacefulness rests entirely on the legal logic of applying Article 11, not on any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit and specific:
*"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."*

These are the two concrete state acts identified by the Court as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most significant finding, yet it has the weakest factual grounding in this text. The Court states:

*"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."*

This is a negative formulation — the Court does not identify specific facts grounding its necessity assessment. Instead, it relies entirely on the comparison with prior leading cases (*Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, *Kasparov*) and general principles. No specific facts about proportionality, the conduct of authorities, the reasons given for dispersal, or the severity of sanctions are cited in this section.

---

**Summary observation:**
The factual grounding across all elements is notably thin. The reasoning section functions essentially as a repetition-case formula, where the Court's conclusions rest on analogy with prior judgments rather than on an independent factual analysis set out in this section. Any richer factual record would need to be sought in earlier parts of the judgment (e.g., the "Facts" section), which are not reproduced here.
