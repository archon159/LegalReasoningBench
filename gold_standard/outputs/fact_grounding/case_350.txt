## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly state a finding on this as a threshold matter, but the following facts ground the implicit acceptance that an assembly existed:

> "the appearance of a large group of protestors walking in a cluster could reasonably be perceived as one [a march]"

> "the procession in the present case was undeniably peaceful"

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds its implicit acceptance of participation in the following factual observation, which is framed so as to accept participation under either version of events:

> "irrespective of whether the applicants were heading to a lawful public gathering, as they alleged, or were already in the process of conducting an unauthorised rally, as the Government claimed, their dispersal and arrest constituted an interference with their right of peaceful assembly"

**(iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds its explicit finding of peacefulness in the following:

> "the procession in the present case was undeniably peaceful, and so was the applicants' conduct"

No further factual elaboration is provided by the Court to support this finding. It is stated as an unqualified conclusion.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference in the following facts:

> "their dispersal and arrest constituted an interference with their right of peaceful assembly, as did the ensuing administrative charges brought against them"

> "the measures taken against the applicants, in particular the interception of their march, or perceived march, their arrest, pre-trial detention and the administrative charges, were based on the finding that they had held an unauthorised demonstration and had not complied with the police's orders to end it"

> "the applicants were arrested and sentenced to five days' administrative detention"

The Court also notes that the Government did not contest this:

> "the Government did not dispute that there had been an interference with the right to peaceful assembly"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court makes no factual findings on this element. It proceeds on a pure assumption without grounding it in any established facts:

> "Even assuming that the applicants' arrest and administrative sentence complied with domestic law..."

No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

As with prescribed by law, the Court makes no factual findings here. The legitimate aim of "prevention of disorder" is mentioned only within the same assumptive clause:

> "Even assuming that the applicants' arrest and administrative sentence complied with domestic law and pursued one of the legitimate aims enumerated in Article 11 § 2 of the Convention – presumably, prevention of disorder..."

No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element receives the most substantial factual grounding.

On the absence of any assessment of disturbance caused:

> "the applicants' march was dispersed and the applicants were arrested and sentenced to five days' administrative detention without any assessment of the disturbance they had caused, merely because they had marched without authorisation and had allegedly ignored the police's orders to stop"

On the peaceful nature of the conduct (feeding into proportionality):

> "the procession in the present case was undeniably peaceful, and so was the applicants' conduct"

On the sole justification being formal unlawfulness:

> "the present case is identical to several other Russian cases in which the Court found violations of Article 11 of the Convention because of the police stopping and arresting protestors for the sole reason that their demonstration as such had not been authorised, the formal unlawfulness of the demonstration being the main justification for the administrative charges"

On the chilling effect and the identity of the first applicant:

> "the measures also had serious potential to deter other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate. The chilling effect of the sanctions was further amplified by the fact that they targeted the first applicant, a well-known public figure, whose deprivation of liberty was bound to attract wide media coverage"

On the severity of the sanction:

> "sentence them to a term of imprisonment, albeit a short one"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
