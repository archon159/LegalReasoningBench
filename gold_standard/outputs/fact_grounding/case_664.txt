Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element:

---

**(a) Scope**

- *Assembly exists / participation / peaceful*: The only factual grounding available is the following phrase: "The applicants complained principally of the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the **location, time or manner of conduct of public events**."

This is the sole factual anchor for all three scope sub-elements. It is thin — the Court does not describe the assemblies in any detail, does not confirm they were peaceful, and does not describe the applicants' individual participation. No richer factual grounding can be identified in this passage.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same phrase as above: "the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the **location, time or manner of conduct of public events**."

The Court also states: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were based on **legal provisions**" — this confirms the governmental/legal origin of the interference, but provides no further factual detail.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court states: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were based on **legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements**."

This is the entirety of the factual and legal grounding offered. The Court provides no further specification of which legal provisions, what their content was, or in what precise respect they failed the quality of law standard. The reasoning is delegated entirely to the Lashmankin precedent: "In the leading case of **Lashmankin and Others v. Russia**, no. 57818/09 and 14 others, §§ 402-78, 7 February 2017, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

No independent factual grounding beyond this can be identified in the passage.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court states the interferences "were moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'." As with the prescribed-by-law element, the only grounding offered is the reference to Lashmankin: "issues similar to those in the present case." No independent facts are cited in this passage to support the necessity finding.

No further factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**Overall observation:** The reasoning section is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. The Court relies almost entirely on the Lashmankin precedent and the general statement about "restrictions on location, time or manner of conduct of public events." It explicitly states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" — which confirms that the Court considered the facts but chose not to restate them, treating the case as governed by the leading case without independent factual elaboration.
