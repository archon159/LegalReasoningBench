## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
No explicit factual grounding is provided. The Court treats the picket as an assembly without stating any facts that establish this characterisation. No quote is available.

**ii) Participation**
No explicit factual grounding is provided. The Court treats the applicant's role as organiser/participant as given without stating the underlying facts. No quote is available.

**iii) Peaceful**
The factual grounding is the absence of any assertion to the contrary by the authorities:
> "The national authorities and the Government did not assert that the picket organisers had had any of the above intentions."

The Court grounds its finding of peacefulness entirely on this absence of assertion, rather than on any positive factual description of the picket's character.

---

### (b) Interference

The factual grounding is the act of refusal and its stated basis:
> "the refusals to authorise the applicant's picket by reference to its aim constituted an interference with her right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

And the legal mechanism used:
> "The refusals were based on sub-paragraph 2 of section 12(1) of the Public Events Act, which empower the relevant regional or municipal authorities to make proposals aimed at modifying the purpose of an event if it is incompatible with the provisions of the Act."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The factual grounding for the Court's concern about the quality of the law is:
> "The refusals were based on sub-paragraph 2 of section 12(1) of the Public Events Act, which empower the relevant regional or municipal authorities to make proposals aimed at modifying the purpose of an event if it is incompatible with the provisions of the Act."

And the Court's characterisation of how those provisions operate in practice:
> "those provisions of the Public Events Act, that confer an unduly wide degree of discretion on the executive authorities to propose changes to the location, time or manner of conduct of a public event, those powers often being used in an arbitrary and discriminatory way."

However, as noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not resolve this element separately. No further specific facts from the present case are cited to ground the lawfulness assessment — the Court relies on its prior findings in *Lashmankin* and then folds the issue into proportionality.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The factual grounding is the stated reason given by the authorities for the refusal, and the failure of courts and government to connect that reason to any listed aim:

> "The sole reason for opposing the picket at issue was its subject matter and its organisers' alleged failure to demonstrate that it necessitated any public discussion."

> "Neither the national courts nor the Government explained how that hypothetical failure could have been linked to any of the legitimate aims listed in the second paragraph of Article 11."

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

Several facts ground this assessment:

The purpose of the applicant's picket:
> "the applicant aimed to attract the attention of the public and of State officials to what could be perceived as a public health issue which, in her view, merited public discussion."

The sole basis on which the authorities opposed the picket:
> "The sole reason for opposing the picket at issue was its subject matter and its organisers' alleged failure to demonstrate that it necessitated any public discussion."

The nature of the authorities' approach:
> "The domestic authorities, therefore, took a formalistic approach in opposing the applicant's event on the basis of the contents of its message."

The absence of any justification offered by the authorities:
> "the national authorities did not put forward any convincing argument that would justify such a restriction."

The absence of any assertion of violent intent (which feeds into the proportionality assessment as well as the peacefulness finding):
> "The national authorities and the Government did not assert that the picket organisers had had any of the above intentions."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding is available, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all in this reasoning.
