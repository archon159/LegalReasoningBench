## (a) Scope

### i) Existence of an assembly

The Court does not explicitly analyze this, but the following facts are stated by the Court as part of its own assessment:

> "the applicant was a member of the political opposition and a known public figure who took active part in the post-election demonstrations and whose arrest happened when the rallies were in full swing"

> "this was a period of increased political sensitivity in Armenia involving opposition rallies held in protest against an allegedly unfair presidential election result. The response of the authorities that followed, including the arrests and detention of scores of opposition supporters, was condemned by the PACE and described as a 'de facto crackdown on the opposition'."

### ii) Applicant's participation

> "the applicant was a member of the political opposition and a known public figure who took active part in the post-election demonstrations and whose arrest happened when the rallies were in full swing and was indirectly linked to his participation in the ongoing protests, as the applicant was alleged to have been illegally armed while on his way from a demonstration."

### iii) Peaceful character of the assembly

No facts are explicitly stated by the Court to ground this sub-element. The Court does not address it directly. The PACE characterization of the crackdown as targeting "opposition supporters" and the framing of the protests as political rallies is present in the text, but the Court does not explicitly use these facts to establish peacefulness as a doctrinal finding. **No factual grounding can be identified for this sub-element.**

## (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following facts, stated in its own assessment:

**On the general context:**
> "this was a period of increased political sensitivity in Armenia involving opposition rallies held in protest against an allegedly unfair presidential election result. The response of the authorities that followed, including the arrests and detention of scores of opposition supporters, was condemned by the PACE and described as a 'de facto crackdown on the opposition'. The charges brought against many of them, especially those based solely on police evidence, were suspected to have been 'artificial and politically motivated'."

> "there was evidence suggesting some opposition activists may have fallen victim to arrests and prosecutions on 'seemingly artificial charges' already in the period from 20 to 29 February, that is before the events of 1 March 2008"

**First factor — identity and participation of applicant:**
> "it is undisputed that the applicant was a member of the political opposition and a known public figure who took active part in the post-election demonstrations and whose arrest happened when the rallies were in full swing and was indirectly linked to his participation in the ongoing protests, as the applicant was alleged to have been illegally armed while on his way from a demonstration."

**Second factor — controversial initiation of the case:**
> "the trigger for the applicant's arrest was an alleged anonymous telephone call received at the PDFOC alleging that the applicant and the persons accompanying him were armed. There is, however, no objective evidence to support the fact that such a telephone call was indeed received at the PDFOC, such as for example a recording of that conversation or its detailed transcript. The precise nature of that anonymous telephone call was never revealed or examined at any stage of the proceedings either"

> "The police officers immediately proceeded to take the applicant into custody on such precarious grounds without first making any attempts to verify the information provided or carrying out any searches or checks on the spot."

> "This initial suspicion against the applicant was almost immediately forgotten once the circumstances of his arrest gave rise to a different charge, namely that of an assault on a police officer which, moreover, was based exclusively on the statements of the police officers concerned."

> "the striking vagueness of all the official documents concerning the initial reasons for the applicant's arrest"

> "there were no genuine reasons for taking the applicant into custody and the fact that he was arrested on such precarious grounds actually gives an impression that the intention was to deprive the applicant of his liberty at any cost and that his arrest may have been effected in bad faith"

**Third factor — drug test:**
> "Both the record of his 'bringing-in' and the initial police report indicated the alleged illegal possession of arms as the sole reason for the applicant's arrest and mentioned nothing about any drug-related suspicions."

> "Even the investigator's decision justified the need for a drug test with nothing more than the fact that a baton had been found in the applicant's possession, although the Court has difficulty seeing the connection between the two."

> "the decision to institute a criminal case stated that the applicant had been brought in on suspicion of, inter alia, drug use but there is no evidence whatsoever to support that statement."

**Fourth factor — joinder with main political case:**
> "the applicant's criminal case was joined with the main criminal case no. 62202608 instituted against the leaders and supporters of the opposition in connection with that protest movement. Moreover, this was done with reference to his participation in organising and conducting the rallies in question."

> "The materials of that criminal case and the applicant's alleged involvement in a conspiracy to 'usurp State power' were relied on to extend the applicant's detention."

> "strict restrictions were placed on his contact with the outside world, again with reference to that criminal case, even though the only formal charge against the applicant was that of minor assault."

**Fifth factor — delayed weapons charge and conviction based solely on police testimony:**
> "the weapon in question, namely a spring baton, was allegedly found in the applicant's possession on the very first day of his arrest. However, for unexplained reasons no charge in that respect was brought against the applicant for the following four months, which casts doubt on the credibility and genuineness of that charge."

> "the bringing of that charge happened around the same period when the authorities gave up on their attempts to charge the applicant with the offence of usurpation of State power, which gives an impression that the authorities wanted to secure the applicant's conviction at any cost."

> "as for the applicant's conviction for assault, that conviction was based exclusively on the testimony of the police officers concerned and the findings of fact made in that respect by the domestic courts appear to have been a mere and unquestioned recapitulation of the circumstances as presented in that testimony."

> "The manner in which the proceedings relating to that charge were conducted is strikingly similar to other cases where opposition activists had been prosecuted and convicted for similar acts, in similar circumstances and on the basis of similar evidence, which points to the existence of a repetitive pattern and casts doubt on the credibility of the criminal proceedings against the applicant."

**Sixth factor — OSCE/ODIHR monitoring:**
> "the applicant's criminal case, while on the whole being seemingly unrelated to the protest movement, was nevertheless included among the cases monitored by the OSCE/ODIHR as part of a trial monitoring project of more than a hundred cases instituted against the leaders and supporters of the opposition in connection with the events of 1-2 March 2008."

**Conclusion on interference:**
> "The entirety of the materials before it allows the Court to draw sufficiently strong, clear and concordant inferences as to the applicant's prosecution, and consequently the resulting conviction, being linked to his involvement and active participation in the protest movement led by the opposition."

## (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its finding of unlawfulness on the following facts stated in its own assessment:

> "the applicant was prosecuted and convicted of certain acts, namely illegally carrying a cold weapon and an assault on a police officer, under Articles 235 § 4 and 316 § 1 of the CC criminalising such acts, whereas the true reason for his criminal punishment was his active participation in the protest movement."

The Court does not separately state additional new facts here; it explicitly relies on its prior findings regarding interference:

> "Having regard to its findings regarding the existence of an interference, the Court considers that the situation in the present case is comparable to that examined in the cases of Hakobyan and Others and Huseynli and Others."

> "the impugned interference with the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly could only be characterised as manifestly arbitrary and, consequently, unlawful for the purposes of Article 11 of the Convention."

The factual grounding for the lawfulness finding is thus entirely derivative of the facts identified under interference above. No additional independent facts are stated by the Court for this element.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly states it does not examine this element:

> "That being so, it is not required to determine whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim and, if so, whether it was proportionate to the aim pursued."

**No factual grounding is identified or needed, as the Court declines to examine this element.**
