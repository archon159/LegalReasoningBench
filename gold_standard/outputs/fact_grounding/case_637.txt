I will go through each element where doctrine was found to be explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:**
The Court does not explicitly analyze this as a separate step, but the factual grounding is implicit in repeated references to 'the demonstration.' The Court itself uses the phrase 'the dispersal of the demonstration' and 'the demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner.' These are the Court's own words, not merely party submissions.

**(ii) Participation:**
The Court grounds this in the following: 'a lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention.' The factual basis is the applicant's status as 'a participant in an unauthorised demonstration,' which the Court itself states: 'doubts about the credibility of the formal grounds, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the applicant, who was a participant in an unauthorised demonstration.'

**(iii) Peaceful:**
The Court grounds this explicitly in: 'a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner.'

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the existence of interference in the following facts, which it treats as established: 'the dispersal of the demonstration by the police and his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence.' These are stated as the subject matter of the complaint and are treated throughout as given facts. The Court also refers to 'his arrest and conviction' as established: 'doubts about the credibility of the formal grounds, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the applicant.'

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

**(i) Foreseeable:**
The Court grounds its negative finding in: 'the Court noted the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being arbitrarily banned or dispersed.'

No specific legislative text or concrete factual detail is quoted by the Court in this passage — the Court relies on its prior analysis in Gafgaz Mammadov by reference, without restating the underlying facts here.

**(iii) Safeguards against abuse:**
The Court grounds this in: 'doubts about the credibility of the formal grounds, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the applicant, who was a participant in an unauthorised demonstration' and 'the possibility of public assemblies being arbitrarily banned or dispersed.' Again, the Court relies on the Gafgaz Mammadov analysis by reference rather than restating the underlying facts independently.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not make its own factual findings on this element. The aims mentioned (protecting rights and freedoms of others, preventing disorder, national security) appear only in the Government's submissions, which the Court neither explicitly accepts nor rejects. **No factual grounding can be identified for this element in the Court's own reasoning.**

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court grounds its negative finding on three distinct factual points, all stated as the Court's own findings:

1. 'a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner.'

2. 'a failure by the authorities to take into consideration the fact that notice of the demonstration had been given.'

3. 'a lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention.'

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
