## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a)(i) Assembly exists

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

- "the demonstration of 2 April 2011"
- "the first applicant's administrative arrest and conviction for failing to stop participating in an unauthorised demonstration"

---

### (a)(ii) Participation

The Court does not make a clean factual finding that the applicant participated. Instead, it grounds its reasoning on the legal/normative point that the conviction was directed at assembly-related activities, regardless of the applicant's denial:

- "the first applicant's administrative arrest and conviction for failing to stop participating in an unauthorised demonstration, under Article 310.1 (failure to comply with a lawful order of a police officer) of the CAO, was directed at activities falling within the scope of freedom of assembly, and he was sanctioned for engaging in such activities, despite denying that he had intended to take part in the assembly in his appeal"

The factual anchor is thus the conviction itself and what it was directed at, not an independent finding that the applicant actually participated.

---

### (a)(iii) Peaceful assembly

The Court grounds its acceptance of the peaceful character of the assembly on:

- "The Court notes that the case file, including the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties, does not suggest that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was violent."

This is the Court's own assessment of the case file. The Court then accepts the applicant's assertion as a consequence of this finding: "The Court therefore accepts the first applicant's assertion that the demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up until his arrest." The factual grounding is the absence of evidence of violence in the case file, not merely the applicant's assertion.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on:

- "the first applicant's administrative arrest and conviction for failing to stop participating in an unauthorised demonstration, under Article 310.1 (failure to comply with a lawful order of a police officer) of the CAO, was directed at activities falling within the scope of freedom of assembly, and he was sanctioned for engaging in such activities"
- "The first applicant's arrest and the administrative proceedings against him could not but have had the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate"

The factual grounding is thus the arrest, the administrative proceedings, and the conviction — all established from the case file.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding can be identified in the reasoning text. The Court does not discuss this element explicitly and provides no factual basis for it in the excerpt provided.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified in the reasoning text. The Court does not discuss this element explicitly in the excerpt provided.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court does not conduct an explicit necessity analysis but grounds its implied finding of a violation (by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov) partly on:

- "The first applicant's arrest and the administrative proceedings against him could not but have had the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate"

Beyond this, the necessity analysis is not factually grounded in the excerpt itself — the Court explicitly defers to Gafgaz Mammadov "§§ 50-68" without reproducing that reasoning here.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified. This element is not discussed.
