# Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

## (a) Scope

### i) Assembly exists
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, and there is no specific factual finding establishing that the gathering qualifies as an assembly. The Court simply proceeds on this basis. **No factual grounding can be identified beyond the bare reference to "the demonstration of 19 June 2011."**

### ii) Participation
The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "the applicant who participated in the demonstration was arrested and convicted"

> "in describing the circumstances of the administrative offence, the police who arrested the applicant and the domestic courts both stated that the applicant had failed to stop participating in the unauthorised demonstration (see paragraphs 11 and 23 above)"

> "the impugned 'behaviour' of the applicant actually consisted of his participation in the demonstration"

### iii) Peaceful assembly
The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "the authorities dispersed the demonstration shortly after it began, despite the fact that it had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up to that point"

> "the applicant in fact was arrested and convicted for his participation in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration"

> "Nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the applicant committed any reprehensible offence during the demonstration"

---

## (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following facts:

> "it has not been disputed between the parties that the demonstration in issue was dispersed by the police, and that the applicant who participated in the demonstration was arrested and convicted"

> "in describing the circumstances of the administrative offence, the police who arrested the applicant and the domestic courts both stated that the applicant had failed to stop participating in the unauthorised demonstration (see paragraphs 11 and 23 above)"

> "the impugned 'behaviour' of the applicant actually consisted of his participation in the demonstration"

The Court also grounds the rejection of the Government's counter-argument on the following fact:

> "the key circumstance constituting the basis for the administrative proceedings against the applicant was the fact that the demonstration in which he participated was unauthorised"

---

## (c) Prescribed by law

### i) Foreseeable
The Court grounds its concerns about foreseeability on the following facts:

> "Article 49 of the Constitution required only prior notification about a planned public assembly. On the other hand, the Law on Freedom of Assembly provided the relevant local executive authority (in the present case, the BCEA) with broad powers to prohibit or stop a public assembly. Also, the Law vested it with the rights to restrict or change the place, route and/or time of a gathering, and to designate specific areas for public assemblies"

> "A number of international reports have stressed that a system of notification set forth by the Constitution has been replaced in practice by a system of authorisation"

> "The Commissioner for Human Rights of the Council of Europe in his Report of 6 August 2013 stated, in particular, that 'peaceful protesters have ... been effectively banned from demonstrating in central Baku since 2006, despite advanced notification of the assemblies'"

> "the demonstration of 19 June 2011 was dispersed because it had not been authorised by the BCEA"

### ii) Accessible
**No factual grounding can be identified. This element is not discussed.**

### iii) Safeguards against abuse
The Court grounds its implied concern on the following facts:

> "the Law on Freedom of Assembly provided the relevant local executive authority (in the present case, the BCEA) with broad powers to prohibit or stop a public assembly"

> "the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed"

Note: the Court does not explicitly frame this as a safeguards analysis, and no definitive conclusion is reached.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim
The Court does not make explicit factual findings grounding a confirmed legitimate aim. It folds the question into the necessity analysis without formally endorsing any aim. **No factual grounding for a confirmed legitimate aim can be identified.** The Government's assertion of public safety and prevention of disorder is noted in the parties' submissions but is not explicitly adopted by the Court.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding that the interference was NOT necessary on the following facts:

**Regarding the dispersal:**

> "the organisers of the demonstration of 19 June 2011 gave prior notice to the BCEA in accordance with Article 49 of the Constitution and Article 5 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly"

> "the authorities have not explained why, instead of taking measures to minimise the disruption to traffic and implementing other safety measures, they decided to refuse 'authorisation' of the demonstration and subsequently to disperse it"

> "The domestic courts in their turn did not attempt to examine whether the absence of authorisation justified the dispersal"

> "Taking into account that the Constitution required only notification about a public assembly, not its authorisation, the Court considers that the authorities ignored the circumstances that were particularly relevant for assessment of the necessity of the interference"

> "the authorities dispersed the demonstration shortly after it began, despite the fact that it had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up to that point"

> "It has not been argued or demonstrated that it would have been difficult for the police to contain or redirect protestors, or control the situation otherwise, protect public safety and prevent any possible disorder or crime. Nor has it been shown, either at the domestic level or before the Court, that the demonstration posed a high level of disruption of public order"

**Regarding the arrest and conviction:**

> "none of the documents drawn up by the police indicated whether any order had been specifically addressed to the applicant (apart from a demand addressed to the protesters at large to stop the demonstration). Nor did they specify who had given such an order and when, or indicate its exact wording"

> "the applicant in fact was arrested and convicted for his participation in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration"

> "at the material time, no penalty in the form of deprivation of liberty was provided for under the CAO for participating in an unauthorised public assembly or generally for violation of rules on holding public assemblies. Nevertheless, the applicant was sentenced to five days' 'administrative' detention on charges that he 'had failed to stop participating in the unauthorised demonstration'"

> "the arbitrary reference to Article 310.1 of the CAO as a ground for the applicant's arrest and conviction also made it possible to apply a penalty which was otherwise not applicable to the action held against him"

> "The authorities made no effort to balance the applicant's right to participate in the demonstration against any damage this could cause to other public or private interests"

> "the domestic courts' decisions do not contain any findings that the applicant's specific actions during the demonstration necessitated his arrest and conviction"

> "Nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the applicant committed any reprehensible offence during the demonstration"

---

## (f) Positive obligation
**No factual grounding can be identified. This element is not discussed.**
