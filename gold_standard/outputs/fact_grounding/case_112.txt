I will go through each element where I found explicit discussion or implication, and identify only the facts the Court itself accepted or established.

---

**(a)(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly establish this as a factual finding, but proceeds on the basis of it throughout. The closest factual grounding is the Court's own uncontested references to "the demonstration of 2 April 2011" and "the demonstrations" as events that occurred. No specific passage establishes this as a formal factual finding. The fact is implied by the entire structure of the analysis.

---

**(a)(ii) Participation**

The Court does not make an explicit factual finding on participation as a separate matter. It is implied by the reference to "his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence" and to "the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration." However, these references appear in the context of summarising the Gafgaz Mammadov findings rather than as independent factual findings about the present applicant. No verbatim factual finding can be isolated.

---

**(a)(iii) Peaceful**

The Court makes an explicit factual finding:

> "The Court notes from the outset that none of the official records or the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties suggests that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 had been violent. The Court therefore accepts the applicant's assertion that that demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up until his arrest."

Note: the Government had asserted that "the demonstration of 2 April 2011 had led to damage to property and police officers being injured," but the Court explicitly rejects this by finding no support for violence in the official records or domestic court decisions. The Court does not adopt the Government's characterisation.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not make a discrete factual finding establishing interference. It is implied by the accepted facts of "the dispersal of the demonstrations by the police and his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence," which are stated in the opening of the complaints section:

> "The applicant complained that the dispersal of the demonstrations by the police and his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence had been in breach of his freedom of assembly and freedom of expression."

The Court does not separately verify these facts as established — it proceeds on the basis of them without explicit endorsement as factual findings. No stronger factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(c)(i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds this on the following, drawn from its application of Gafgaz Mammadov:

> "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed"

> "doubts about the credibility of the formal ground, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the participant of an unauthorised demonstration"

The Court does not, however, set out independent factual findings about the content of the legislation in the present judgment. It relies entirely on the analysis in Gafgaz Mammadov, which it finds applicable due to the similarity of the cases:

> "Having regard to the material in both case files and the parties' submissions, the Court notes that the issues raised by the present complaints are essentially the same as those examined in the Gafgaz Mammadov case... The facts of that case and the present cases are similar to a significant degree."

No additional independent facts about the legislation are stated in this judgment.

---

**(c)(iii) Safeguards against abuse**

Same factual grounding as (c)(i) above:

> "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed"

Again, no independent factual findings beyond the reference to Gafgaz Mammadov.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not make any factual finding grounding or rejecting a legitimate aim. The Government's assertion that the dispersal pursued "the aim of protecting the rights and freedoms of others and preventing disorder" is recorded in the parties' submissions section but is not adopted by the Court. The Court proceeds without explicitly resolving this element. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court grounds this on the following facts, again drawn from Gafgaz Mammadov as applied to the present cases:

> "a failure by the authorities to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified"

> "a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner"

> "a lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

The factual basis for the first of these is partially grounded in the applicant's submission that "the authorities had not taken into consideration the fact that the organisers had given prior notice of the demonstrations to the relevant authority" — but the Court does not explicitly state it accepts this as an established fact in the present judgment. It applies the Gafgaz Mammadov finding to the present case on the basis of similarity. The factual grounding is therefore indirect, mediated through the reference to that judgment rather than through independent fact-finding here.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
