## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts stated as established:

> "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration..."

> "the demonstration during which he delivered a speech"

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:

> "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration..."

> "the applicant was convicted on account of his involvement in the demonstration during which he delivered a speech"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court grounds this on:

> "There is no indication in the case file that the demonstration was not peaceful or that the demonstrators, including the applicant, engaged in acts of violence or presented a danger to public order beyond the level of minor disturbance."

> "nor could the applicant's speech be construed as encouraging any violent behaviour"

Regarding the Government's submission on slogans, the Court does not simply accept the Government's characterisation. Instead it explicitly rejects it as a basis for finding the assembly non-peaceful:

> "In so far as the Government referred to the slogans chanted by the demonstrators, the Court notes that it has already examined similar slogans and concluded that they did not constitute an incitement to violence."

> "there is nothing to suggest that these slogans had originated from the applicant himself"

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on:

> "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration amounted to an interference with the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly, even though the pronouncement of the judgment was suspended."

> "he was nevertheless faced with the threat of a sanction of one year and three months' imprisonment on account of his involvement in a peaceful demonstration"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this solely on:

> "That interference was prescribed by section 28(1) of Law no. 2911"

No further factual elaboration is provided on foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The sub-elements are not factually grounded in the reasoning text — the Court simply names the statutory provision and moves on.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds this on:

> "That interference was prescribed by section 28(1) of Law no. 2911 and pursued at least one of the legitimate aims set out in Article 11 § 2 of the Convention, namely the prevention of disorder."

No specific facts are cited to explain *why* the prevention of disorder was the relevant aim. The Court asserts it without grounding it in particular factual circumstances of the case.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of disproportionality on several distinct factual elements:

**On the reason for unlawfulness:**
> "The demonstration was considered unlawful under the domestic law mainly due to the absence of the requisite prior notice."

**On the peaceful nature and absence of danger:**
> "There is no indication in the case file that the demonstration was not peaceful or that the demonstrators, including the applicant, engaged in acts of violence or presented a danger to public order beyond the level of minor disturbance."

**On the slogans:**
> "the Court notes that it has already examined similar slogans and concluded that they did not constitute an incitement to violence"

> "there is nothing to suggest that these slogans had originated from the applicant himself"

**On the applicant's own speech:**
> "nor could the applicant's speech be construed as encouraging any violent behaviour"

**On the severity of the sanction:**
> "he was nevertheless faced with the threat of a sanction of one year and three months' imprisonment on account of his involvement in a peaceful demonstration"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all.
