Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterization in the opening paragraph: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**." This is the Court's description of the complaints, not a formal finding of fact. It is thin factual grounding — the Court does not independently verify or establish the existence of the assemblies or the applicants' roles in them.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit in the opening paragraph: "their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**." These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding. Not discussed.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding. Not discussed.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is strikingly thin. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a negative formulation — the Court does not cite any specific facts to ground its necessity assessment. Instead, it relies entirely on analogy to prior case law: "In the leading cases of *Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, and *Kasparov and Others v. Russia*, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**." The factual grounding is thus delegated by reference to those earlier judgments rather than established independently in this reasoning section.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding.

---

**Overall observation**: The reasoning section is notably sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. The Court proceeds almost entirely by reference to prior case law rather than by independent factual analysis, and the only concrete facts mentioned are the arrest, dispersal of assemblies, and conviction for administrative offences.
