I will go through each canonical element and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court grounds this entirely by reference to prior case law, without restating facts:
> "The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Frumkin, cited above, §§ 99 and 137, and Yaroslav Belousov, cited above, §§ 168-71)."

No independent factual grounding is offered in this text beyond the cross-reference.

**ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this in the description of the applicant's offences:
> "As regards the applicant personally, it does not transpire from the description of his offences that he was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character."

**iii) Peaceful character**
The Court grounds this in the same finding about the applicant's role, and later elaborates:
> "the criminal file did not disclose any pre-existing intention by the applicant to participate in violent acts, and no such intention was established by the trial court."
> "the applicant was not among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character"
> "the applicant's overall conduct degenerated from peaceful to violent, it was sporadic in nature and appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police against the protestors, the majority of whom were peaceful."

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding in the fact of prosecution and conviction:
> "the applicant's prosecution and criminal conviction for acts he committed in the course of the dispersal of the demonstration constituted an interference with the exercise of the freedom of assembly."

The underlying factual basis for what those acts were is stated earlier:
> "He was sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment under those provisions for attending an authorised public event, using metal barriers to block the police's movement, throwing pieces of tarmac at the police, punching an unidentified police officer and snatching a helmet from a police officer's hand."

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court adopts the conclusion from prior case law without restating any facts:
> "The parties made essentially the same submissions on the lawfulness of the interference as in Yaroslav Belousov (cited above, § 173). The Court sees no reason to reach a different conclusion in this case (ibid., §§ 174-75). It finds that the applicant's pre-trial detention and prosecution were lawful..."

No independent factual grounding is offered in this text for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court states the legitimate aims without grounding them in specific facts beyond the nature of the charges:
> "It finds that the applicant's pre-trial detention and prosecution were lawful, and pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and crime and protecting the rights and freedoms of others."

No further factual elaboration is provided for this element specifically.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following established facts:

On the nature of the charges and evidence:
> "he was found guilty of two criminal offences, namely participation in acts of mass disorder (Article 212 of the Criminal Code) and committing violent acts against a police officer (Article 318 of the Criminal Code). He was sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment under those provisions for attending an authorised public event, using metal barriers to block the police's movement, throwing pieces of tarmac at the police, punching an unidentified police officer and snatching a helmet from a police officer's hand."

On the quality of evidence for the assault charge:
> "the police officer in respect of whom the applicant was found guilty of assault only testified that the applicant had been pulling his helmet from his hands, and the same testimony was given by another police officer. The judgment did not specify what evidence corroborated its finding that the applicant had punched another police officer, who remained unidentified."

On the tarmac-throwing:
> "there was nothing to suggest that the applicant had hit anyone with those pieces of tarmac."

On the barrier-handling:
> "there were no witnesses to the applicant handling the barriers, and that he denied his involvement in that."

On the domestic courts' failure to examine the police's role:
> "in assessing the mass disorder case, the domestic courts did not examine the applicant's allegation that the police had contributed to the onset of violence and had used excessive force."

On the applicant's injuries:
> "According to the photographs submitted by the applicant, he had multiple injuries resembling traces of truncheon blows on his back. The allegation of beating was also supported by the results of the applicant's examination at the hospital (see paragraph 10 above)."

Note: The Court here treats the photographs and hospital examination results as established facts supporting the allegation, not merely as a party submission, by stating they "supported" the allegation and finding it "indispensable" that the trial court assess them.

On the domestic courts' dismissal of those allegations:
> "the domestic courts dismissed his arguments about use of force by the police, merely referring to the previous refusal to open a criminal case in relation to the conduct of the police."

On the applicant's intent and role:
> "the criminal file did not disclose any pre-existing intention by the applicant to participate in violent acts, and no such intention was established by the trial court."
> "the applicant was not among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character"

On the nature of the applicant's conduct:
> "At the height of the clashes, the applicant threw pieces of tarmac, and his confrontation with the police took place when they were already arresting protesters. While the applicant's overall conduct degenerated from peaceful to violent, it was sporadic in nature and appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police against the protestors, the majority of whom were peaceful."

On the chilling effect:
> "the applicant's criminal conviction, and especially the severity of his sentence, must have had the effect of discouraging him and other opposition supporters, and the public at large, from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate. The chilling effect of the sanction imposed on him was further amplified by the large-scale proceedings in the case, which attracted widespread media coverage."

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court declines to assess this as a separate issue. The only factual grounding offered is in the context of the necessity analysis (see above under (e)), where the Court notes the domestic courts failed to examine the police's role. No distinct factual grounding is provided for a standalone positive obligation finding, consistent with the Court's explicit decision not to rule on it separately.
