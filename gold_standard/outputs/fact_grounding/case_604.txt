**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the phrase: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The Court also refers to the applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies." These are the only factual anchors, and they are stated as background description rather than as findings following contested factual analysis.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court states no facts from which peacefulness could be inferred. It is a structural implication of the Court proceeding with the analysis, not a finding grounded in identified facts.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This is the only concrete factual description of what the government did. The Court does not elaborate further on the nature, duration, or severity of the arrest or conviction.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet it is notably **not grounded in any specific facts from the instant case**. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a negative formulation — the Court affirms the absence of distinguishing facts rather than identifying positive facts that ground the necessity finding. The necessity conclusion rests entirely on the prior case-law cited (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others), not on facts quoted or adopted from the present case.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**Overall observation:**

The factual basis of this decision is strikingly thin. The only facts explicitly stated are that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, that these assemblies were dispersed, that the applicants were arrested, and that they were convicted of administrative offences. Beyond this, the Court relies entirely on the similarity of the case to prior leading cases, without identifying which specific facts of the instant case map onto those precedents or why they are analogous.
