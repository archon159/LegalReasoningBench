**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following description from the opening paragraph:

> "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the situation, not merely a party submission. It establishes that public assemblies took place and that the applicants were organisers or participants. However, it must be noted that the Court does not explicitly verify or make findings on the peaceful nature of the assemblies — this is simply assumed without factual grounding in the text provided.

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the concrete governmental acts the Court treats as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is the central finding, but — and this is significant — **the Court provides no independent factual grounding** for its necessity assessment in this text. It reasons entirely by reference to prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court thus acknowledges that material was submitted but does not quote, summarise, or adopt any specific facts from it. The necessity conclusion rests on the similarity to prior cases, not on any independently stated factual findings in this text.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
