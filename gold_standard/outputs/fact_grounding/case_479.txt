## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, but the following facts ground the implied finding:

> "the demonstration and the reading of a press statement, scheduled for the end of the event, had been prohibited by the police"

> "the rally began at about 12 noon and ended with the group's arrest within half an hour"

> "the group of demonstrators was informed a number of times that their march was unlawful"

> "the demonstration, which was organised under the authority of the Human Rights Association"

**(ii) Participation**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element. The only factual grounding that can be identified is:

> "The applicant and other demonstrators did not comply with the security forces' orders and attempted to force their way through."

This places the applicant within the group of demonstrators, grounding the implied finding of participation.

**(iii) Peaceful**
The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "There is no evidence to suggest that the group in question represented a danger to public order, apart from possibly disrupting traffic."

> "There were at most fifty people, who wished to draw attention to a topical issue."

> "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings"

Note: the last quote is a normative statement, but it is grounded in the factual finding that no violence was found.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court states this is undisputed and grounds it in:

> "the demonstration and the reading of a press statement, scheduled for the end of the event, had been prohibited by the police"

> "There is no dispute as to the existence of an interference in the applicant's right of assembly."

No further factual elaboration is provided by the Court on this element, as it is treated as conceded.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this solely in:

> "This interference had a legal basis, namely section 22 of Law no. 2911 on assemblies and marches"

No further facts are identified by the Court regarding foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The sub-elements are not factually grounded because they are not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds the finding of legitimate aim in:

> "the disputed measure may be regarded as having pursued at least two of the legitimate aims set out in paragraph 2 of Article 11, namely the prevention of disorder and the protection of the rights of others, specifically the right to move freely in public without restriction"

The factual basis underlying this is:

> "notification was required seventy-two hours prior to the event"

> "the group of demonstrators was informed a number of times that their march was unlawful and would disrupt public order at a busy time of day"

> "the rally began at about 12 noon" [i.e., during rush hour / a busy time of day]

Note: the Court does not explicitly link these facts to the legitimate aim finding, but they are the only facts in the reasoning that could ground the aims of disorder prevention and protection of others' freedom of movement.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

> "There is no evidence to suggest that the group in question represented a danger to public order, apart from possibly disrupting traffic."

> "There were at most fifty people, who wished to draw attention to a topical issue."

> "The rally began at about 12 noon and ended with the group's arrest within half an hour."

> "It is particularly struck by the authorities' impatience in seeking to end the demonstration, which was organised under the authority of the Human Rights Association."

> "the group of demonstrators was informed a number of times that their march was unlawful and would disrupt public order at a busy time of day, and had been ordered to disperse. The applicant and other demonstrators did not comply with the security forces' orders and attempted to force their way through."

Note: the last quote is the primary factual basis the Government relied upon to justify the intervention. The Court acknowledges these facts but weighs them against the others listed above, ultimately finding them insufficient to justify the intervention.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions positive obligations in general terms but does not ground them in specific facts of the case. The relevant passage is purely normative:

> "although the essential object of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities with the exercise of the rights protected, there may in addition be positive obligations to secure the effective enjoyment of these rights"

> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to lawful demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens"

No specific facts of the case are identified by the Court as grounding a positive obligation finding. As noted previously, the Court does not reach a separate conclusion on positive obligations, and therefore no factual grounding for a violation of a positive obligation can be identified.
