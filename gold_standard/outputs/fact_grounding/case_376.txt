## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**Assembly exists / Applicant sought to participate:**
The Court does not explicitly state facts about the nature of the assembly or the applicant's role. The factual grounding is only implicit in the Court's acceptance that the applicant 'could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11.' No specific facts are quoted or adopted by the Court to ground this conclusion.

**Peaceful character:**
The Court refers to the assembly as a 'planned peaceful assembly' and states: 'where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings.' However, the Court does not explicitly identify any specific facts from the record that establish the peaceful character of the planned demonstration. The characterisation appears to be accepted without factual elaboration.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds this on the fact of the ban itself, but does not quote or adopt specific facts beyond accepting the parties' common ground: 'The Court notes that the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11; nor did they deny that the ban on the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of his rights under that provision.'

No additional facts are quoted or adopted by the Court to ground the interference finding.

---

### (c) Prescribed by Law

The Court grounds this conclusion on a single fact: 'The Court observes that the ban was based on section 8(1) of the Assembly Act and considers that it was thus prescribed by law.'

The only factual grounding is the identification of the legal basis — section 8(1) of the Assembly Act. No further facts are cited.

---

### (d) Legitimate Aim

The Court states: 'the Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others.'

The Court does not explicitly adopt or quote specific facts to ground this conclusion. It does not, for instance, make findings about actual traffic conditions or actual risks to other road users. The conclusion appears to be accepted at a general level without factual elaboration. The Government's submission about 'securing the rights of others, that is, those of other road users' is noted but the Court does not explicitly adopt it as an established fact — it simply accepts the aims as legitimate without grounding this in specific findings of fact.

---

### (e) Necessity in a Democratic Society

This is the element most explicitly grounded in facts. The Court identifies the following:

**The basis for the domestic ban:**
'The Court observes that in the domestic court decision dealing with the case, the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues.'

**The Government's position as characterised by the Court:**
'The Government's submissions were, in essence, confined to the affirmation that the demonstration would have seriously hampered the free flow of traffic in the area.'

Note: the Court characterises this as the Government's submission, not as an established fact about actual traffic impact.

**The general principle applied to the facts:**
'a demonstration in a public place may cause a certain level of disruption to ordinary life including disruption of traffic.'

**The cross-reference to Körtvélyessy:**
'Concerning the assessment of the relevant facts of the present case, the Court bases its findings on those in the Körtvélyessy case (see paragraphs 29 and 30 of that judgment, cited above), which concerned a similar problem relating to the very same location.'

This is significant: the Court explicitly states it is grounding its factual assessment on findings made in a prior case concerning the same location, rather than making independent factual findings from the record before it. The specific facts from paragraphs 29 and 30 of Körtvélyessy are not quoted in the reasoning text provided, so they cannot be reproduced here.

**The conclusion drawn from these facts:**
'the Court finds that the reason for banning the planned peaceful assembly was, if at all relevant, not sufficient to meet any pressing social need.'

---

### (f) Positive Obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
