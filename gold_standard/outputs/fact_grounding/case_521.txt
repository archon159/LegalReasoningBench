## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following fact:
> "the applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia."

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:
> "The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

**iii) Peaceful character**

The Court grounds its finding that the demonstration was NOT peaceful on the following facts, which it explicitly adopts from the Commission's findings and the UN Secretary General's report:

> "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus"

> "The Court sees no reason to depart from these findings, which were based on the UN Secretary General's report, on a video film and on photographs submitted by the respondent Government before the Commission."

> "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'"

> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on:

> "the applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds the 'prescribed by law' finding on:

> "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 25-26 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law (see paragraph 29 above)"

> "when, as in the Foka case, an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law for the purposes of the Convention"

No facts are cited to ground the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. As noted previously, these sub-elements are not explicitly discussed, and no factual grounding for them can be identified in the reasoning text.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not independently establish facts to ground the legitimate aim finding. It notes the Government's submission of aims (national security, public safety, prevention of disorder and crime) but does not explicitly adopt these as factually established in a separate step. Rather, the factual grounding for the legitimate aim is effectively merged into the necessity analysis, where the Court establishes the violent and border-violating character of the demonstration. Those facts are quoted under (e) below.

No standalone factual grounding for legitimate aim can be identified that is separate from the necessity analysis.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its proportionality finding on the following facts:

The violent character of the demonstration:
> "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus"

The specific acts of the demonstrators, drawn from the UN Secretary General's report:
> "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'"

The entry into 'TRNC' territory:
> "the demonstrators had forced their way into the UN buffer zone. According to the 'TRNC' authorities, they also entered into 'TRNC' territory, thus committing offences punished by the 'TRNC' laws"

> "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory"

The nature of the intervention:
> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions the positive obligation in general terms by reference to case-law, but does not ground it in any specific facts of the case. The reference serves as a legal principle to contextualize the margin of appreciation, not as a factual finding. No factual grounding can be identified for this element beyond the general statement:

> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens. However, they cannot guarantee this absolutely and they have a wide discretion in the choice of the means to be used."

This is a statement of legal principle, not a factual finding, and no case-specific facts are attached to it.
