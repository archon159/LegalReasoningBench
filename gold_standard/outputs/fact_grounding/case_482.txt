Here is the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Participation**

The only factual grounding for both the existence of a trade union action and the applicant's participation in it is the following phrase:

> "the disciplinary sanction of reprimand imposed on her for her participation in trade union activities"

This single clause simultaneously grounds both the existence of a collective trade union activity and the applicant's participation in it. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text about the nature, date, location, or form of the activity.

**iii) Peaceful**

No facts are cited to ground the peaceful character of the assembly. The Court does not mention any facts — from either party — that bear on this question. The peacefulness of the activity appears to be assumed without factual grounding in the reasoning text.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for interference is the same clause as above:

> "the disciplinary sanction of reprimand imposed on her for her participation in trade union activities"

The Court also draws on the Karaçay reasoning to characterise the nature of the interference:

> "the sanction complained of, although very light, had been such as to dissuade trade union members from legitimate participation in strikes or other trade union actions to defend the interests of their members"

This is the Court's own characterisation (adopted from its prior case law), not a party submission, and it constitutes the factual-cum-legal grounding for treating the sanction as an interference with Article 11 rights.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's assessment of necessity rests on two factual elements:

First, the nature and severity of the sanction:

> "the sanction complained of, although very light, had been such as to dissuade trade union members from legitimate participation in strikes or other trade union actions to defend the interests of their members"

Second, the absence of any countervailing facts or arguments from the Government:

> "the Government have not put forward any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion in the present case"

This second statement is notable: the Court grounds its necessity finding partly on the factual vacuum left by the Government's failure to adduce any justification. No Government submissions are quoted or adopted as established facts, precisely because the Court treats their absence as significant.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.
