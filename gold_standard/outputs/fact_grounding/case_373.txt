---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court's factual grounding is minimal and largely borrowed from prior case law. The only factual language in the text is the description of "a peaceful public assembly" and the reference to "the gathering in question" in Navalnyy and Yashin. The Court does not independently state facts establishing that an assembly took place. No verbatim factual finding can be quoted beyond the characterisation itself.

**ii) Participation**
The Court states: *"the measures applied to the applicants as participants in a peaceful public assembly."* This is the only factual grounding — the applicants are described as participants, but no underlying facts are stated to establish this.

**iii) Peaceful**
The Court states: *"disproportionate measures taken against them as participants in a peaceful public assembly"* and again *"the measures applied to the applicants as participants in a peaceful public assembly."* The peaceful character is asserted as a characterisation, but no underlying facts (e.g., absence of violence, conduct of the applicants) are stated to ground it. The factual basis is not independently established in this text.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court states: *"disproportionate measures taken against them as participants in a peaceful public assembly, namely their arrest followed by their conviction for an administrative offence."*

This is the only factual grounding for interference. The specific measures — arrest and conviction for an administrative offence — are identified. No further facts about the circumstances of the arrest or the nature of the conviction are stated in this section of the reasoning.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court states: *"the measures applied to the applicants as participants in a peaceful public assembly did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society."*

This is the Court's conclusion on necessity, but it is not grounded in any independently stated facts within this reasoning section. The Court does not identify specific facts — such as the conduct of the authorities, the proportionality of the response, or the circumstances of the dispersal — to support this conclusion. Instead, it relies entirely on the prior case law: *"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of the present complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..."*

The factual grounding is thus delegated to the cited leading cases (Frumkin, Kasparov, Navalnyy and Yashin) rather than stated in this text. No specific facts from the present case are quoted or adopted to support the necessity finding.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning section is strikingly thin on independently stated facts. The Court relies almost entirely on prior case law and a general reference to "all the material submitted to it," without articulating the specific facts of the present case that ground each doctrinal finding. The only concrete facts stated are the arrest and conviction for an administrative offence. All other factual grounding is either absent or delegated by reference to earlier judgments.
