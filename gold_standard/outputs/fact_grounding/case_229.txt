Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Existence of an assembly:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." The Court does not elaborate further on the nature, size, location, or form of these assemblies.

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." This is the sole factual basis for the implied finding that the applicants participated or attempted to participate.

**iii) Peaceful character:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court says nothing about the conduct of the assemblies, the behaviour of participants, or the presence or absence of violence. The peaceful character is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds its finding of interference in the following facts, quoted verbatim:

"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the only factual basis provided. No further detail about the arrests, the dispersals, or the administrative proceedings is given in this reasoning section.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is where the absence of factual grounding is most notable. The Court does not ground its necessity finding in any specific facts from the present case. Instead, it reasons as follows:

"In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The necessity finding is thus grounded not in specific facts from the present case but in the absence of any distinguishing facts or arguments relative to the prior leading cases. The Court's reasoning is essentially negative: nothing in the submitted material displaces the conclusions already reached in analogous cases.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (f) Positive obligation**

As noted in the prior analysis, these elements are neither explicitly discussed nor implied by the reasoning. Accordingly, no factual grounding can be identified for any of them.
