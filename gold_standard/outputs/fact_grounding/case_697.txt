## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly state facts grounding this element. It refers throughout to "the applicant organisation's public rally of 19 April 2000" but does not articulate facts establishing why this constitutes an assembly. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the label itself.

**Participation**
No explicit factual grounding. The Court does not articulate specific facts about the applicant's role in planning or attempting to hold the rally. The interference finding implies participation, but no facts are quoted by the Court to ground this.

**Peaceful character**
No factual grounding. The Court never articulates any facts establishing the peaceful nature of the planned rally. It is entirely implied.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court accepts the parties' agreement as its factual basis, stating: "The parties agreed that there had been an interference with the exercise of the applicant organisation's right to freedom of peaceful assembly within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention." The Court adds only "That view is shared by the Court" without articulating independent factual grounding beyond this agreed position.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this finding explicitly in the domestic court's determination:

> "The Court observes that the Plovdiv Regional Court established in its judgment of 28 March 2001 that the municipality's prohibition of the applicant organisation's public rally of 19 April 2000 was issued in violation of the Meetings and Marches Act."

Additionally, in the admissibility section, the Court provides a factual detail about *why* the domestic court found the prohibition unlawful:

> "the Plovdiv Regional Court declared null and void the municipality's prohibition on a technicality stemming from the fact that it had been decided not by its mayor but by its secretary."

These are the only facts the Court explicitly relies upon. It does not independently examine the text of the Meetings and Marches Act or assess foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards — it simply adopts the domestic court's finding of unlawfulness.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
