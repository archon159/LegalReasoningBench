## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not articulate any specific fact to ground this. It simply refers to "the protest in question" and "the demonstration" as background context. No factual grounding is explicitly provided beyond these bare references.

**ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this on one stated fact:
> "Given that the third applicant participated in the protest in question..."

No further factual detail is provided about the nature or extent of his participation.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**
The Court grounds the peaceful character of the assembly on the absence of contrary evidence rather than on a positive factual finding:
> "in the absence of any evidence to suggest that the third applicant or other protesters presented a danger to public order"

> "Nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the third applicant committed any reprehensible offence during the demonstration."

---

### (b) Interference

The factual grounding is the same as for participation, since the Court links the two directly:
> "Given that the third applicant participated in the protest in question, the Court accepts that there was an interference with his right to freedom of assembly."

No additional facts are cited to characterise the nature of the interference (e.g., the manner of dispersal, arrest, or conviction). The Court notes it has regard to "the submissions of the parties and the information available in the case file" but does not quote or adopt specific factual submissions from the parties.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding is provided. The Court explicitly declines to make a finding, assuming the element arguendo:
> "even assuming that the interference was 'prescribed by law'..."

No facts are identified or relied upon in relation to this element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding is provided. As with "prescribed by law," the Court assumes this element without making findings:
> "even assuming that the interference was 'prescribed by law' and pursued 'legitimate aims'..."

The aims of "prevention of disorder" and "protection of the rights of others" appear in the necessity section but are not grounded in any specific facts accepted by the Court as established.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court relies on the following facts:

On the absence of danger posed by the applicant and other protesters:
> "in the absence of any evidence to suggest that the third applicant or other protesters presented a danger to public order"

On the subject matter of the protest:
> "in view of the vital issues to which the protesters sought to draw attention"

On the domestic courts' failure to identify specific wrongdoing by the applicant:
> "the domestic courts' decisions do not contain any findings that the third applicant's specific actions during the demonstration necessitated his conviction."

On the absence of reprehensible conduct:
> "Nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the third applicant committed any reprehensible offence during the demonstration."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
