# Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

## (a) Scope

### (i) Assembly exists
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, and there is no specific factual finding grounding it. The existence of an assembly is simply assumed throughout. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the general references to "the demonstration," "the march," and "the gathering" used descriptively throughout the judgment.

### (ii) Participation by the applicants
The Court grounds this in the following facts:

> "it appears from the police reports that the applicants joined the front line of demonstrators at about 4.40 p.m."

> "the video footage shows that the police intervention began less than two minutes after the applicants joined the front lines"

### (iii) Peaceful character of the assembly
The Court grounds this in the following facts:

> "it is not disputed between the parties that the applicants did not engage in acts of violence."

> "one police officer reported that he had been injured by a stone thrown by the demonstrators before the police intervention"

> "the video footage shows that at least three adolescents among the demonstrators threw stones at the security forces when the group was heading towards the main road before the beginning of the march"

> "some of the demonstrators reacted against this behaviour and that, more importantly, the vast majority of the demonstrators were not involved in any violent acts during the period before the intervention of the police"

> "As to the damaged vehicles, there is nothing to indicate that the damage in question occurred before the intervention of the police"

> "despite certain sporadic violent acts involving the throwing of stones – which were not committed by the applicants – the demonstration was mainly of a peaceful character before the intervention of the police"

---

## (b) Interference
The Court grounds this in the following fact, which it explicitly accepts:

> "It is not in dispute between the parties, and the Court accepts, that the intervention of the police to disperse the demonstration at issue constituted an interference with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly."

No further specific factual elaboration is provided for this finding; the Court accepts it as undisputed.

---

## (c) Prescribed by law
The Court grounds this in the following fact:

> "The Court further accepts that the interference in question had a legal basis, namely sections 22 and 24 of Law no. 2911"

The Court also notes, in the necessity section, the following facts that are relevant to the legal basis:

> "in the absence of notification in accordance with Law no. 2911, the demonstration was unlawful in terms of domestic law"

> "the march was held on a main road in contravention of section 22 of the same Law as in force at the material time"

No factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse, as the Court does not address these individually.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim
The Court does not ground this in specific established facts. It simply accepts the characterisation as a matter of legal assessment:

> "The disputed measure may also be regarded as having pursued at least two of the legitimate aims set out in paragraph 2 of Article 11, namely the prevention of disorder and the protection of the rights of others."

No specific facts are cited as the basis for this acceptance. The Court does not adopt the Government's broader factual claims (national security, public safety, prevention of crime) as grounding additional legitimate aims.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

### Conduct of demonstrators
As quoted above under (a)(iii), the Court grounds its assessment on:

> "the vast majority of the demonstrators were not involved in any violent acts during the period before the intervention of the police"

> "despite certain sporadic violent acts involving the throwing of stones – which were not committed by the applicants – the demonstration was mainly of a peaceful character before the intervention of the police"

### Security considerations
The Court grounds its rejection of the security justification in the following facts:

> "the reports of 29 and 31 May 2010 referred to by the Government did not specifically concern the demonstration at issue"

> "As to the report of 2 June 2010, it mainly mentioned that the protestors might, if provoked, attack the security forces and their vehicles with stones. In the Court's view, such a report could not demonstrate in itself that the participants of the gathering had violent intentions."

> "the demonstration was organised by a political party"

> "these [banners] were displayed during a peaceful gathering, which limited their potential impact on 'national security' and 'public order'"

> "the mere existence of some buildings belonging to the security forces along the road taken by the demonstrators cannot be considered, in itself, sufficient to justify the dispersal of the demonstration"

### Disruption to ordinary life and authorities' conduct
The Court grounds its assessment on the following facts:

> "although the demonstrators began gathering in front of the BDP party office at about 3 p.m. that day, traffic on the road in question was not stopped until about 4.30 p.m., when the demonstrators were heading towards the road"

> "the applicants joined the front line of demonstrators at about 4.40 p.m."

> "the video footage shows that the police intervention began less than two minutes after the applicants joined the front lines"

> "it appears that the flow of traffic was affected only for a period of less than twenty minutes before the intervention of the police"

> "the police warned the demonstrators a number of times"

> "the authorities dispersed the march swiftly only a few minutes after it had started and less than twenty minutes after the traffic had been blocked"

> "the extent of the disruption to ordinary life caused by the demonstration at issue prior to its dispersal was not such as to justify such swift intervention by the police"

The Court also grounds its conclusion on the manner of dispersal, cross-referencing its Article 3 findings:

> "the Court further refers to its findings in relation to the use of water cannon in the circumstances of the case, which led to a violation of Article 3 of the Convention under its substantive limb in respect of the second applicant, and notes that the manner in which the demonstration was dispersed resulted in a serious injury"

### Overall conclusion on necessity
> "taking into account the authorities' impatience in seeking to disperse the march and the manner in which the force was used, the Court considers that the intervention of the police was disproportionate and not necessary in a democratic society"

---

## (f) Positive obligation
As noted previously, the Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding can be identified for this element, as it is entirely absent from the reasoning.
