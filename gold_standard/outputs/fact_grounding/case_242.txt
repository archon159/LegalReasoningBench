**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope**

As noted in the prior analysis, scope is implied rather than explicitly discussed. The only factual grounding available in the text is the following description from the opening paragraph:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

and

> "participants and organisers of public assemblies"

These phrases imply the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation in them. However, the Court does not explicitly ground its scope assessment in any developed factual analysis. There is no factual statement in the text establishing that the assemblies were peaceful.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for interference is found in the opening paragraph:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants and organisers of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

This is the Court's own characterisation of the facts, not merely a party submission, and it identifies two concrete governmental acts: arrest and criminal conviction.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, but notably the Court provides **no independent factual grounding** in this reasoning section. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This sentence acknowledges that material was examined but does not identify or quote any specific facts. The necessity conclusion rests entirely on the reference to prior case-law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

The factual similarity to those leading cases is asserted but not demonstrated through any specific facts drawn from the present case.

---

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
