Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterization in the opening paragraph: "participants of public assemblies." No further facts about the nature, location, or organization of the assemblies are provided in the reasoning text. The Court does not elaborate.

**ii) Participation:**
Again, the only factual grounding is: "participants of public assemblies." No further facts about how or to what degree the applicants participated are provided.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The reasoning text contains no facts — not even implied ones — establishing that the assemblies were peaceful. The Court simply does not address this. I cannot map any quoted text to this sub-element.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the existence of interference in the following facts, stated in the opening paragraph:

"the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."

These are the only concrete facts provided, and the Court treats them as the interferences complained of.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss this element at all, and no facts in the reasoning text speak to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss this element, and no facts in the reasoning text speak to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity finding is not grounded in any newly stated or independently assessed facts in this reasoning text. Instead, it rests entirely on the reference to prior case law:

"In the leading cases of Gafgaz Mammadov v. Azerbaijan [...] the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court thus grounds its necessity assessment not in specific facts about what happened, but in the similarity of the present case to the leading cases. No specific facts about proportionality, the weight of the aim pursued, or the conduct of authorities are cited.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed; no factual grounding to identify.
