## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court's own language establishes the existence of an assembly:
- "In the present case the authorities displayed zero tolerance towards the assembly, even though it was peaceful and involved only four participants."

**ii) Participation**
The Court does not explicitly state facts grounding the applicant's participation beyond the general framing. The closest factual grounding is:
- "It follows that the applicant was arrested, transferred to the police station and charged with an administrative offence for the sole reason that the public event had not been approved."

This implies her participation/organisation, but the Court does not explicitly ground this in stated facts.

**iii) Peaceful**
- "In the present case the authorities displayed zero tolerance towards the assembly, even though it was peaceful and involved only four participants. There is no evidence that it created any disruption of ordinary life."
- "The domestic authorities made no attempt to verify the extent of the risks posed by the protestors, or to verify whether it had been necessary to terminate the public event."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court does not explicitly label the government's actions as "interference" and does not ground this element in explicitly stated facts using that framing. However, the following facts are stated by the Court and collectively imply interference:
- "It follows that the applicant was arrested, transferred to the police station and charged with an administrative offence for the sole reason that the public event had not been approved."

No further explicit factual grounding is offered by the Court itself for this element as a distinct doctrinal step.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its assessment on the following facts:
- "the domestic authorities found that she had not respected the notification time-limit and that the applicant disputed that finding."
- "The Court cannot discern any such flagrant non-observance of domestic law or arbitrariness in the interpretation or application of the domestic provisions concerning the notification time-limit in the present case."
- "Nor have the applicant questioned the 'quality' of those domestic provisions."
- "The Court therefore accepts that the applicant had not respected the notification time-limit and that the refusal to approve the public event had been therefore lawful."

Note: the last sentence is the Court's own conclusion, grounded in the absence of arbitrariness and the absence of any challenge to the law's quality.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not itself identify or accept a legitimate aim. No facts are stated by the Court in support of any legitimate aim finding. **No factual grounding can be identified for this element.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding, all stated by the Court itself:

- "In the present case the authorities displayed zero tolerance towards the assembly, even though it was peaceful and involved only four participants."
- "There is no evidence that it created any disruption of ordinary life."
- "The domestic authorities made no attempt to verify the extent of the risks posed by the protestors, or to verify whether it had been necessary to terminate the public event."
- "Nor was there any noticeable assessment of whether the applicant's escort to the police station and administrative arrest had been necessary in the circumstances."
- "It follows that the applicant was arrested, transferred to the police station and charged with an administrative offence for the sole reason that the public event had not been approved."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. **No factual grounding exists or can be identified for this element.**
