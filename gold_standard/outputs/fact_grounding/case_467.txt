**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- *Assembly exists / Participation:* The only factual grounding is the following passage: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

  This sentence establishes that there were public assemblies, that the applicants were organisers or participants, and that the assemblies were dispersed. However, it should be noted that this is a characterisation in the framing of the complaint, not a distinct factual finding by the Court itself.

- *Peaceful character:* No factual grounding. The Court does not mention any facts relating to whether the assemblies were peaceful or violent. This sub-element is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference — arrest and criminal/administrative conviction.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding. The Court does not discuss this element at all, and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding. The Court does not identify or discuss any aim pursued by the authorities, and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most explicitly decided, yet it has the thinnest factual grounding in the text. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a negative formulation — the Court does not identify specific facts that ground its necessity finding. Instead, it relies on the structural comparison with the leading cases: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia...; Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia...; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia..., the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case." The factual grounding for the necessity assessment is thus delegated entirely to those prior judgments rather than stated in this text.

The only concrete facts mentioned in connection with the necessity finding remain those already quoted: arrest, dispersal of assemblies, and conviction for administrative offences. No further facts — such as the conduct of authorities, the behaviour of participants, or the proportionality of the specific measures — are stated in this reasoning section.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding. This element is entirely absent.
