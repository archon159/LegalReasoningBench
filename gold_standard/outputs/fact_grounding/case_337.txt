## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this as a legal question. The factual grounding is implicit in the Court's repeated references to the events as "demonstrations":
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 17 November 2012"
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 26 January 2013"

No further factual elaboration is provided in the reasoning section itself.

**ii) Participation**
Again implicit. The factual grounding is the same references:
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 17 November 2012"
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 26 January 2013"

**iii) Peaceful assembly**
The Court explicitly grounds this on the following facts:
- In the first case, referencing Gafgaz Mammadov: "the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, **which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner**"
- In the second case: "The authorities also failed to acknowledge that **the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration** was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

No further factual detail about the conduct of the demonstrations is provided in the reasoning section itself to substantiate the peaceful character — the Court treats it as established without elaborating on the underlying facts in this section.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds interference in the following facts:
- "the dispersal of the demonstrations by the police and his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence"
- "the dispersal of the demonstrations and the applicant's arrests and convictions could not but have the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies"
- "in the second case the sanction applied to the applicant under Article 298 of the CAO was very harsh. **He was ordered to pay AZN 500, whereas at the material time the minimum wage in the country was AZN 93.5**"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**
The Court grounds its concerns on the following, referencing Gafgaz Mammadov:
- "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed"

For the second case, the Court grounds its acceptance of lawfulness on the following fact:
- "the applicant has failed to sufficiently substantiate his allegation that the demonstration in which he participated was a spontaneous one"

This is a factual absence — the applicant did not establish the spontaneous character of the assembly — rather than a positive factual finding.

**ii) Accessible**
No factual grounding identified. Not discussed.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse**
Grounded in the same passage as foreseeability:
- "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about **the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed**"

Additionally, the Court notes:
- "doubts about the credibility of the formal ground, namely, Article 310.1 of the CAO, invoked by the authorities for arresting and convicting the participant in an unauthorised demonstration"

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding identified. The Court does not itself make any factual findings in support of or against a legitimate aim. The Government's claimed aims are noted in the parties' submissions section but the Court does not adopt them.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of a violation on the following facts:

- "the authorities' failure to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified" (first case, referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, § 60)
- "the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, § 61)
- "the lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, §§ 63-64)
- "the dispersal of the demonstrations and the applicant's arrests and convictions could not but have the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies"
- "in the second case the sanction applied to the applicant under Article 298 of the CAO was very harsh. **He was ordered to pay AZN 500, whereas at the material time the minimum wage in the country was AZN 93.5**"
- "The measures applied in the present cases and the fear of sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies undoubtedly have a chilling effect on the exercise of freedom of assembly. This deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
