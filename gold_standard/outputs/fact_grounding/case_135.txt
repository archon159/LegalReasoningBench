## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

#### (i) Has there been an assembly?

The Court grounds this on its prior findings in related cases and on the nature of the events:

> "the demonstrations held at Freedom Square were found by the Court to constitute a peaceful assembly and in fact a platform for expression on a matter of major political importance directly related to the functioning of a democracy and of serious concern to large segments of the Armenian society (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, §§ 230-33 and 246, and Myasnik Malkhasyan, cited above, § 72)."

> "It is undisputed that, after nine days of peaceful protests, violence erupted in Yerevan on 1 and 2 March 2008 after the demonstrators had been ejected from Freedom Square and large crowds had gathered in the area of the Myasnikyan monument and a number of adjacent streets where it appears that clashes between some protesters and law-enforcement officers took place and public and private property was damaged."

---

#### (ii) Did the applicant participate in the assembly?

> "the applicant was an opposition politician and a high profile member of Mr Ter-Petrosyan's pre-election campaign who took part in the rallies in the Armenian capital following the allegedly unfair presidential election of 19 February 2008."

> "the applicant's conviction for 'organising mass disorder' concerned his involvement in the post-election protest movement, including his participation and speeches made at the assembly at Freedom Square."

---

#### (iii) Was the assembly peaceful?

The Court grounds this explicitly on its prior findings:

> "the Court is mindful that it has already scrutinised the circumstances of the assembly at Freedom Square and found that it had been peaceful, without any incitement to violence or acts of violence (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, § 245, and Myasnik Malkhasyan, cited above, § 72)."

> "the demonstrations held at Freedom Square were found by the Court to constitute a peaceful assembly and in fact a platform for expression on a matter of major political importance directly related to the functioning of a democracy and of serious concern to large segments of the Armenian society."

The Court also grounds this negatively — by rejecting the factual basis for the Government's contrary position:

> "the Court has already established that there was no convincing evidence to suggest that there had been a build-up of arms at Freedom Square and has rejected the allegations that the police were deployed at the square in order to carry out an inspection for weapons, finding that the main, if not only, purpose of the police operation in the early morning of 1 March 2008 was to disperse the assembly (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, §§ 230 and 245, and Myasnik Malkhasyan, cited above, § 80)."

---

### (b) Interference

> "the applicant's conviction for 'organising mass disorder' concerned his involvement in the post-election protest movement, including his participation and speeches made at the assembly at Freedom Square, and therefore amounted to an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

The Court also grounds the interference finding in the general legal principle it applies to the facts:

> "The term 'restrictions' in Article 11 § 2 must be interpreted as including both measures taken before or during an assembly, such as a prior ban, dispersal of the rally or the arrest of participants, and those, such as punitive measures, taken afterwards, including penalties imposed for having taken part in a rally."

The factual anchor for this is the criminal conviction itself, which the Court treats as an established fact throughout, e.g.:

> "the applicant was found guilty under Article 225 § 1 of the CC, as one of the leaders of the opposition, of organising mass disorder in connection with the post-election protests which had taken place in Yerevan from 20 February 2008 onwards, including the assembly at Freedom Square."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding identified. The Court explicitly declined to examine this element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding identified. The Court explicitly declined to examine this element.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively grounded element. The Court's assessment rests on several distinct factual findings:

**On the general political context and the pattern of prosecutions:**

> "The response of the authorities that followed, including the arrests and detention of scores of opposition leaders and supporters, was condemned by the PACE as a 'de facto crackdown on the opposition', while the charges brought against many of them, especially those under Articles 225 and 300 of the CC, were suspected to have been 'artificial and politically motivated'."

> "The applicant's case appears to follow a pattern of criminal prosecutions of opposition supporters, which the Court finds alarming."

**On the absence of incitement to violence in the applicant's conduct:**

> "While stating that the applicant had prepared the crowd gathered at Freedom Square for use of violence and disobedience, the domestic courts failed to provide concrete examples of such behaviour which, in the Court's opinion, could be characterised as incitement to violence."

> "the only acts which the applicant was found to have committed in pursuit of that plan are not sufficient for the Court to conclude that they were anything but examples of legitimate exercise by the applicant of his right to freedom of peaceful assembly and expression of opinions in the context of the public debate surrounding the conduct of the presidential election, including the criticism voiced in that respect."

**On the absence of evidence linking the applicant to the arms build-up:**

> "As regards the alleged build-up of arms at Freedom Square for the purpose of instigating mass disorder, there was no concrete indication in the domestic judgments of the applicant's involvement in these alleged acts."

> "the Court has already established that there was no convincing evidence to suggest that there had been a build-up of arms at Freedom Square and has rejected the allegations that the police were deployed at the square in order to carry out an inspection for weapons, finding that the main, if not only, purpose of the police operation in the early morning of 1 March 2008 was to disperse the assembly."

**On the vagueness and abstraction of the domestic courts' reasoning regarding the violence of 1-2 March:**

> "the findings of the domestic courts regarding the applicant's alleged responsibility for the violence in question were drafted in very general and abstract terms, without any specific factual details which would convincingly point to any wrongdoing by the applicant. While stating that the applicant had 'arranged and managed the activities of ... organisers and participants of the mass disorder', no specifics of such acts were provided in the domestic judgments. Nor were any details provided regarding the 'orders' allegedly given by the applicant, including whether such alleged orders contained any incitement to violence."

**On the spontaneous and unorganised nature of the violence:**

> "a number of credible reports suggested that the gathering of people in the area of the Myasnikyan monument, including their later being armed, had been spontaneous and unorganised developments and that the escalation of violence later that day might have been a response to the earlier dispersal of demonstrators from Freedom Square, including its heavy-handed nature, as well as a number of other similar or uncontrollable events which had happened later that day."

> "The Court notes that the Government have not produced any evidence in the present case which would prompt it to doubt the above reports or the findings reached in that case."

**Overall conclusion on the factual basis:**

> "the domestic courts failed to provide specific examples of any reprehensible acts attributable to the applicant, including incitement or instigation of violence, in the course of his involvement in the protest movement and the rallies which gripped Armenia in the aftermath of the presidential election."

> "the applicant appears to have been sanctioned for being an active member of the opposition and for having availed himself of his right to freedom of peaceful assembly rather than committing any reprehensible acts."
