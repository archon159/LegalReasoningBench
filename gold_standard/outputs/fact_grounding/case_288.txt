## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Has there been an assembly?**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

- 'the majority of the acts which formed the basis of the applicant's conviction for being a member of an armed terrorist organisation under Article 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code ("the CC") fell within the ambit of Article 11 of the Convention, namely his participation in certain press conferences as well as a demonstration and two marches'
- 'the Malatya Assize Court relied on his participation in several demonstrations, press conferences and panels'
- 'events concerning the labour rights of workers at the State-run company Tekel, the centenary of International Women's Day, Nawruz celebrations, or 1st May International Workers' Day'

**ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?**

- 'it is not in dispute that the applicant's participation in the above-mentioned events (see paragraphs 5-7 above) had a decisive impact on the national court's conclusion regarding his membership of the PKK'
- 'the Malatya Assize Court relied on his participation in several demonstrations, press conferences and panels in accordance with instructions which the PKK had published on certain websites'

**iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**

- 'there is no finding in the reasoning of the national courts indicating that the events in question were of a violent nature or that the organisers or participants, including the applicant, had violent intentions'
- 'it seems that the Malatya Assize Court merely listed the events without providing any information as to their nature'
- 'the Government did not argue before the Court that those events had not been peaceful'
- 'demonstrations and events which had not been found to be violent'

Note on the third quote: the Court does not merely record the Government's silence as a party submission — it treats that silence as a relevant fact in its own assessment, explicitly drawing on it to support its conclusion about the peaceful nature of the events. It is therefore adopted as part of the Court's own reasoning.

---

### (b) Interference

- 'It is common ground between the parties that an interference with the applicant's rights under Article 11 of the Convention did take place, specifically on account of his criminal prosecution and resultant conviction on the basis of, inter alia, acts protected under Article 11 of the Convention'

The Court here explicitly adopts the common ground between the parties as an established fact.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court does not ground its assumption on any specific facts. It merely identifies the legal provisions by name: 'Article 314 § 2 of the CC and section 5 of the Law on the Prevention of Terrorism.' No factual analysis underlies the assumption. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Similarly, the Court does not ground its assumption on specific facts. It states only that 'the national authorities may be considered to have pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and crime and the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.' This is an assumption, not a factually grounded finding. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element is the most extensively grounded in facts. The Court relies on the following:

**On the absence of justification for treating participation as PKK membership:**
- 'there is no justification in the national courts' judgments for the conclusion that when the applicant participated in those demonstrations he had been acting for the PKK or on its behalf upon its specific instructions to him'

**On the absence of findings of violence:**
- 'there is no finding in the reasoning of the national courts indicating that the events in question were of a violent nature or that the organisers or participants, including the applicant, had violent intentions'
- 'it seems that the Malatya Assize Court merely listed the events without providing any information as to their nature'

**On the failure to assess the Article 11 dimension:**
- 'the trial court's conclusion appears to have been based to a very significant extent on its finding that the applicant's participation in the impugned events had been upon the instructions of the PKK, without carrying out an assessment as to whether his participation in the events could have been regarded as falling within the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly under Article 11 of the Convention'

**On the failure to address the applicant's submissions:**
- 'the Assize Court did not address the applicant's repeated submissions that the events in question had been legal and that the YDGM was a structure of a political party – the DTP'

**On the failure to explain the organic link:**
- 'the Malatya Assize Court failed to provide any reason as to why the applicant's participation in certain events held in a national and/or international context, such as events concerning the labour rights of workers at the State-run company Tekel, the centenary of International Women's Day, Nawruz celebrations, or 1st May International Workers' Day amounted to an organic link with the terrorist organisation'

**On the mismatch between PKK instructions and the acts imputed:**
- 'while it is true that the trial court relied on the calls made by the websites and media outlets controlled by or belonging to the PKK, it appears that they either did not fully correspond to the acts imputed to the applicant (with the exception of the one concerning the Nawruz celebrations) or post-dated them'

**On the severity of the penalty and chilling effect:**
- 'the applicant was sentenced to a very severe penalty, namely nine years' imprisonment, which must have had a chilling effect on his right to freedom of assembly'
- 'neither the domestic courts nor the Government were able to demonstrate that the criminal sanction in question following demonstrations and events which had not been found to be violent had corresponded to a pressing social need'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or is needed.
