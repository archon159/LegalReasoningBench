**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope — Assembly exists, Participation, Peaceful character**

The only factual grounding offered is the following description from the opening paragraph:

"the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

and:

"organisers or participants of public assemblies"

These are the only facts the Court states. It does not provide any further factual detail about the nature, size, location, or conduct of the assemblies. There is no factual grounding at all for the peaceful character of the assemblies — the Court simply proceeds as if this is uncontested.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

"the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

This is the entirety of the factual basis for the interference finding. No further facts are provided.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Here the Court provides no independent factual grounding whatsoever. The necessity assessment is grounded entirely in prior case-law, not in facts specific to this case:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..."

The Court references the leading cases of *Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, and *Kasparov and Others* as the basis for its conclusion, but does not identify which specific facts of the present case map onto those precedents. The necessity conclusion is thus effectively fact-free in this reasoning section — it rests on the absence of any distinguishing facts rather than on positive factual findings.

**(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, these elements are not discussed. Consequently, there is no factual grounding to identify for them.
