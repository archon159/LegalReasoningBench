## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not state a fact explicitly establishing this, but the following passage provides the implicit factual grounding:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration between 1 and 31 August 2006"

**(ii) Participation (attempted)**
Same passage provides the implicit factual grounding:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration between 1 and 31 August 2006"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**
> "there was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities."

---

### (b) Interference

> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration between 1 and 31 August 2006 amounted to 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

> "under section 14 of the Assemblies Act the Chişinău Municipality was obliged to give reasons in writing for rejecting Hyde Park's application to hold an assembly, which it did in its decision of 18 July 2006"

> "According to section 12(6) of the Assemblies Act, an application could be rejected only if the Municipality was in possession of evidence that the provisions of sections 6 and 7 would be breached with serious consequences for society."

> "The Municipality's decision appears not to have been based on any of the reasons provided for in sections 6 and 7 of the Assemblies Act."

Note: The Court does not reach a definitive conclusion on this element, and the factual grounding is therefore used only to flag a potential problem, not to ground a finding.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court explicitly declines to decide this point and offers no factual assessment in support of or against any legitimate aim.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's assessment is grounded on the following facts:

On the Municipality's stated reason for refusal:
> "the Municipality rejected the application to hold the protest demonstration planned for 1-31 August 2006 on the ground that, in its opinion, one day of protest was sufficient."

On the inconsistency of that reason with domestic law:
> "The Court noted above that such a reason appears to be inconsistent with the requirements of the Assemblies Act which, in its sections 6 and 7, sets out the grounds on which an application to hold an assembly can be rejected by a Municipality."

On the absence of any public order concern:
> "there was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.
