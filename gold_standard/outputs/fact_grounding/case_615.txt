I will go through each element where the Court made a finding, and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a)(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not independently establish the facts of the assembly in this judgment. It relies entirely on its prior finding in *Mushegh Saghatelyan v. Armenia*, no. 23086/08: "it has already examined and dismissed a similar claim by the Government, finding that there was not sufficient and convincing evidence to conclude that the organisers and the participants of the assembly at Freedom Square had had violent intentions and that the assembly in question had not been peaceful."

No independent factual grounding is quoted or established in this judgment beyond the reference to the prior case. The fact of the assembly at Freedom Square is treated as given.

---

**(a)(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on two facts, stated as follows: "the applicant, who was the main leader of the demonstrations held at Freedom Square and was, moreover, on site during the dispersal."

These are the only facts explicitly stated by the Court itself to ground participation.

---

**(a)(iii) Peaceful**

The Court's factual grounding is again drawn from the prior judgment: "it has already examined and dismissed a similar claim by the Government, finding that there was not sufficient and convincing evidence to conclude that the organisers and the participants of the assembly at Freedom Square had had violent intentions and that the assembly in question had not been peaceful (see Mushegh Saghatelyan v. Armenia, no. 23086/08, §§ 229-233, 20 September 2018)."

No independent facts are established in this judgment. The Court does not adopt the Government's factual assertions about violence — it explicitly rejects them by reference to the prior finding.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding on two facts:

First, the nature of the act: "the dispersal of the assembly at Freedom Square had interfered with the right to freedom of assembly of its participants (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, § 234)."

Second, the applicant's presence: "the applicant, who was the main leader of the demonstrations held at Freedom Square and was, moreover, on site during the dispersal."

Again, the substantive factual basis is drawn from the prior judgment rather than independently established here.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court explicitly declined to decide this element and made no factual findings in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court explicitly declined to decide this element and made no factual findings in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's factual grounding is drawn entirely from the prior judgment and is quoted as follows: "its dispersal was without sufficient justification and took place under somewhat dubious circumstances, apparently without warnings to disperse and with unjustified and excessive use of force, and that it was a disproportionate measure which went beyond what it was reasonable to expect from the authorities when curtailing freedom of assembly (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, §§ 240-248)."

The Court adds: "The Court sees no reasons to depart from that conclusion in the present case."

The specific facts underlying this conclusion — the absence of warnings, the use of force, the circumstances of the dispersal — are not independently recited in this judgment. They are incorporated by reference to *Mushegh Saghatelyan*. No additional independent facts are stated by the Court in this judgment to ground the necessity finding.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed; no factual grounding to identify.

---

**Overall observation:** A striking feature of this judgment is that virtually all factual grounding is imported by reference to *Mushegh Saghatelyan v. Armenia*, rather than independently established. The Court in this judgment functions largely as an applying court, transposing prior findings to the present applicant on the basis of his role as "main leader" and his presence "on site during the dispersal."
