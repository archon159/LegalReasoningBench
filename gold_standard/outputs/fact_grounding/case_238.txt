I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**"
> "their arrest in relation to the **dispersal of these assemblies**"

These are the Court's own characterisations of the situation, not merely party submissions. However, it must be noted that the Court does not independently verify or elaborate on the nature of these assemblies — it simply proceeds on this basis.

**ii) Participation**
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**"

Again, this is the Court's own framing of the applicants' role.

**iii) Peaceful character**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not mention any facts relating to whether the assemblies were peaceful or not. This element is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit:
> "their **arrest** in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, but the factual grounding is notably thin. The Court does not cite specific facts about the circumstances of the dispersals, the conduct of the applicants, or the proportionality of the sentences. The only grounding offered is:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a negative statement — the Court found no new facts distinguishing this case from prior cases — rather than a positive enumeration of facts. The necessity finding is therefore grounded primarily in the reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) rather than in independently stated facts from the present case.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.
