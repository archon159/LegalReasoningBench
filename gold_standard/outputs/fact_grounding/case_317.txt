## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
No explicit factual grounding stated by the Court itself. The Court simply treats the planned demonstration as falling within Article 11 without identifying specific facts. The closest is the Court's own framing: *"the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 5 January 2005"* — but this is a procedural description rather than a factual finding about the nature of the assembly.

**ii) Participation**
No explicit factual grounding. The Court refers to "the applicants" and "Hyde Park's application" throughout, but does not make a distinct factual finding about participation.

**iii) Peaceful character**
The Court grounds this in the absence of contrary evidence, stated as its own finding: *"There was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities. Rather their intention was to draw attention to the plight of Moldovan students in Romania."*

---

### (b) Interference

Grounded in the following fact, stated as common ground adopted by the Court: *"It is common ground between the parties, and the Court agrees, that the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 5 January 2005 amounted to 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11."*

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**
Grounded in two facts identified by the Court:

First, the legal standard under the Assemblies Act: *"According to section 12 (6) of the Assemblies Act, an application could be rejected only if the Municipality was in possession of evidence that the provisions of sections 6 and 7 would be breached with serious consequences for society."*

Second, the Municipality's actual reasoning, which the Court found inconsistent with that standard: *"Contrary to this provision, the Municipality's decision appears to have questioned the reasonableness of Hyde Park's decision to stage a protest. Indeed, the wording of the Municipality's decision suggests that it considered the applicants' planned protest devoid of foundation."*

**ii) Accessibility**
No factual grounding identified.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse**
The same facts as under foreseeability apply here implicitly — the Court identifies section 12(6) as the relevant safeguard and finds the Municipality acted contrary to it — but the Court does not separately articulate this as a finding about safeguards. No additional distinct factual grounding.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declines to decide this element. No factual grounding is established by the Court for or against any legitimate aim.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's necessity finding is grounded in several distinct facts:

**The Municipality's stated reason for rejection:**
*"the Municipality rejected Hyde Park's application to hold a protest demonstration planned for 5 January 2005 on the grounds that, in its opinion, 'the protest was unwelcome and unfounded'."*

**Inconsistency of that reason with domestic law:**
*"The Court noted above that such a reason appears to be inconsistent with the requirements of the Assemblies Act which, in its sections 6 and 7, sets out the grounds on which an application for holding an assembly can be rejected by a Municipality."*

**Absence of any public order concern:**
*"There was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities. Rather their intention was to draw attention to the plight of Moldovan students in Romania."*

**The timing and authorship of the courts' reasons:**
*"those reasons were contained in decisions given by the courts long after the date planned for the demonstrations."*

**The legal framework on authority to authorise assemblies:**
*"sections 11 and 12 of the Assemblies Act give exclusive authority to the local authorities to authorise or not assemblies. The law does not provide, and the Government did not argue the contrary, that other State authorities such as the courts were entitled under the Assemblies Act to exercise this duty in their own name or on behalf of the local authorities."*

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
