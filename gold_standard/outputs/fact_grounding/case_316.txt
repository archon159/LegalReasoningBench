I will work through each doctrinal element in turn, identifying only facts that the Court itself relied upon (not merely party assertions).

---

## (a) Scope

### (i) Has there been an assembly?
The Court treats the existence of assemblies as self-evident and does not ground this in any explicit factual reasoning. No factual grounding identifiable beyond the bare references to "the demonstration of 30 August 2007," "the demonstration of 4 September 2007," and "the demonstration of 10 September 2007."

### (ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?
The Court grounds its finding on Mr Juraveli's non-participation in the 4 September demonstration explicitly:

> "he has provided no evidence to indicate when he arrived at the scene of the demonstration, nor has he shown that he attempted to avail himself of his right to peaceful assembly that day and was prevented from doing so by the authorities; the fact that he was arrested at later demonstrations is relevant only to his victim status in respect of those demonstrations."

For the other applicants' participation in the respective demonstrations, the Court does not separately articulate factual grounding — participation is treated as established by the finding of interference (see below).

### (iii) Was the assembly peaceful?
**30 August 2007:**
The Court grounds its assessment on the nature of the protest and the identity of its target, accepting that even provocative expression directed at a public figure does not deprive the assembly of its peaceful character:

> "The applicants sought to protest against alleged harassment by the Ministry of Internal Affairs. The street outside the Ministry was clearly the most appropriate place to carry out such a protest. Even if their signs and chants were calculated to insult the Minister, he was clearly a public figure of some prominence in Moldova. In a democratic society, greater tolerance should be shown to those expressing opinions which are critical of such figures, even if those opinions are expressed inarticulately or intemperately."

**4 September 2007:**
The Court does not make an explicit factual finding on peacefulness for this demonstration beyond noting the absence of excessive force by police and the absence of special circumstances justifying an immediate response:

> "In contrast to the Turkish cases cited above, there is no suggestion that the police used excessive force in disbanding the present applicants."

**10 September 2007:**
The Court grounds its finding on the video evidence it accepted:

> "From the video of the demonstration submitted by the applicants it is clear that, when asked, the applicants provided the police with the authorisation of the Municipality."

> "For the most part, the video shows that the demonstration took place in the most peaceful manner possible and with minimal disruption to the public. It was well conducted and restrained. Although slogans were shouted in the course of the demonstration, this is a well established feature of peaceful protest."

Regarding the passer-by incident, the Court accepted the following facts from the video:

> "The video does show a passer-by remonstrating with one demonstrator but that took place in the few first minutes of the demonstration and the passer-by had left the demonstration long before the police decided to arrest the applicants."

---

## (b) Interference

**30 August 2007:**
> "the Court considers that the arrest of Mr Ghenadie Brega, Mr Oleg Brega, Mr Hristea-Stan and Mr Juraveli prevented the demonstration from taking place."

**4 September 2007:**
> "the Court finds that the arrest, detention and fining of the applicants amounted to an interference with their rights under Article 11."

The Court also notes the specific measures applied:
> "Ghenadie and Oleg Brega were arrested in the course of the demonstration."

And the level of fines:
> "the Court is struck by the level of fines imposed on Ghenadie and Oleg Brega... these fines were not at the lower end of the scale: they were 80% of the maximum fines which could have been imposed and, moreover, were over twenty times the fine imposed and found by the Court to be proportionate in Ziliberberg."

**10 September 2007:**
> "The Court is similarly persuaded that the arrest of Mr Juraveli, Mr Oleg Brega and Mr Hristea-Stan amounted to an interference with the applicants' Article 11 rights."

---

## (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declines to resolve this element separately, merging it into the necessity analysis. It does not make independent factual findings on foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. No factual grounding can be identified for these sub-elements because the Court did not assess them.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declines to decide this element for all three demonstrations:

> "The Court, for the reasons set out below, does not consider it necessary to decide this point." (30 August)

> "it is also unnecessary to decide whether it pursued a legitimate aim." (4 September)

> "while leaving unanswered the issue of whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim." (10 September)

No factual grounding identifiable, as the Court made no finding.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively grounded element.

**30 August 2007:**

The Court grounds its disproportionality finding on the following facts:

On the adequacy of the authorisation check at the scene:
> "The applicants, when asked, had produced the authorisation papers. It has not been suggested that those papers were not in order and it remains unclear as to why a further identity check was necessary."

On the possibility of a less intrusive alternative:
> "even assuming that such a check were necessary, as the applicants have observed, it could have been carried out at the demonstration, or shortly after it had ended."

On the effect of the arrests:
> "by arresting the demonstrators and taking them to the police station, the demonstration was ended prematurely."

On the nature of the protest and the target:
> "The applicants sought to protest against alleged harassment by the Ministry of Internal Affairs. The street outside the Ministry was clearly the most appropriate place to carry out such a protest. Even if their signs and chants were calculated to insult the Minister, he was clearly a public figure of some prominence in Moldova."

**4 September 2007:**

On the absence of a security zone:
> "in the present case, no security zone existed at the site of the demonstration of 4 September 2007 and, consequently, the location of the demonstration was not, in itself, a relevant or sufficient reason for breaking it up."

On the arrests occurring during the demonstration (distinguishing Skiba):
> "in the present case, Ghenadie and Oleg Brega were arrested in the course of the demonstration."

On the level of fines (distinguishing Ziliberberg, Rai and Evans, and Skiba):
> "the Court is struck by the level of fines imposed on Ghenadie and Oleg Brega. In contrast to the fines imposed in Rai and Evans and Skiba, these fines were not at the lower end of the scale: they were 80% of the maximum fines which could have been imposed and, moreover, were over twenty times the fine imposed and found by the Court to be proportionate in Ziliberberg."

On the absence of special circumstances justifying an immediate unauthorised response:
> "They were demonstrating against what they considered to be the abusive suppression of the first demonstration, that of 30 August 2007: there is no evidence that the effect of their second demonstration would have been any less had they waited to obtain the necessary authorisation."

**10 September 2007:**

On the adequacy of the authorisation check at the scene:
> "From the video of the demonstration submitted by the applicants it is clear that, when asked, the applicants provided the police with the authorisation of the Municipality. It is also clear that, in the course of the eighteen-minute video, several police officers checked that authorisation and questioned the applicants, including Mr Juraveli. In the Court's opinion, that should have been enough to satisfy the police that the demonstration could go ahead; it is wholly unclear to the Court what further enquiries could have been made at the police station in order for the police to satisfy themselves that the demonstration was being conducted within the terms of the authorisation."

On the passer-by incident not justifying arrest:
> "The video does show a passer-by remonstrating with one demonstrator but that took place in the few first minutes of the demonstration and the passer-by had left the demonstration long before the police decided to arrest the applicants."

On the peaceful and restrained character of the demonstration:
> "For the most part, the video shows that the demonstration took place in the most peaceful manner possible and with minimal disruption to the public. It was well conducted and restrained. Although slogans were shouted in the course of the demonstration, this is a well established feature of peaceful protest."

On the context of repeated arrests:
> "in the context of the case, where the applicants had already been twice arrested for carrying out demonstrations, the Court readily understands their desire to do so."

---

## (f) Positive obligation

The Court does not explicitly frame any part of its analysis as a positive obligation. The closest passage is:

> "In failing to allow the applicants to conduct their peaceful demonstration, the police failed to show the necessary tolerance which is to be expected of the authorities in a democratic society."

However, this is framed as part of the necessity/proportionality analysis rather than as a distinct positive obligation finding. No separate factual grounding for a positive obligation analysis can be identified.
