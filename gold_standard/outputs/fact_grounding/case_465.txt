I will work through each canonical element where the Court made a finding, identifying the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this in the following facts it treats as established:
- 'an authorised public assembly'
- 'the events at Bolotnaya Square'
- 'an authorised demonstration'

**ii) Participation**

- 'acts he had committed in the course of the dispersal of the demonstration'
- 'his acts... were committed during his participation in an authorised public assembly'

**iii) Peaceful character (of the assembly and the applicant's initial conduct)**

- 'the majority of whom were peaceful'
- The Court accepts as relevant (though the domestic courts dismissed it without explanation) the content of Ms. N.'s testimony: 'confirming that the applicant had been sporadically pulled out of the crowd by the police and that his initial behaviour had been peaceful'
- 'the applicant used violence against the policemen at the height of the clashes when the police were already arresting protesters'
- 'appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police against the protesters'
- 'there is nothing in the case file to suggest that the applicant was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character'

Note: The Court does not formally declare the assembly peaceful as a threshold finding, but treats it as within Article 11's scope by proceeding with the analysis and by characterising the applicant's violence as incidental and reactive rather than as disqualifying conduct.

---

**(b) Interference**

- 'the applicant's prosecution and criminal conviction for acts he had committed in the course of the dispersal of the demonstration constituted an interference with the exercise of his freedom of assembly'
- He 'was found guilty of two criminal offences, namely participation in acts of mass disorder (Article 212 of the Criminal Code) and committing violent acts against a police officer (Article 318 of the Criminal Code)'
- 'He was sentenced to two years and six months' imprisonment'

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court states only: 'the Court is also satisfied that the applicant's prosecution was provided for by law,' citing prior case law. No specific facts from this case are identified by the Court as grounding this finding. The Court simply cross-refers to its findings in Yaroslav Belousov §§ 174-76 and Barabanov v. Russia § 70.

**No factual grounding from this case can be identified for this element.**

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Similarly, the Court states: 'pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and crime and the protection of the rights and freedoms of others,' again by cross-reference to prior cases, without identifying specific facts from this case as grounding the finding.

**No factual grounding specific to this case can be identified for this element.**

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most extensively fact-grounded element. The Court relies on the following established facts:

On the domestic courts' procedural failings:
- 'the domestic courts abstained from examining the circumstances of the applicant's arrest by the police'
- 'undeniably relied on the statements of the police officers given at least two years after the events in question and after watching the video recording'
- 'the courts dismissed the statements of the eyewitness, Ms. N., confirming that the applicant had been sporadically pulled out of the crowd by the police and that his initial behaviour had been peaceful'
- 'The domestic courts' decisions disclose no attempt to establish the relevant facts on the basis of the comparative assessment of the conflicting testimonies of the defence witnesses and the policemen'
- 'The domestic courts did not establish the particular circumstances in which that behaviour had occurred and dismissed the statements of Ms. N with no explanation'

On the nature and severity of the charges and sentence:
- 'he was found guilty of two criminal offences, namely participation in acts of mass disorder (Article 212 of the Criminal Code) and committing violent acts against a police officer (Article 318 of the Criminal Code)'
- 'He was sentenced to two years and six months' imprisonment for hitting one police officer twice in his hands and another one in the head with the umbrella and in the hands'
- 'the applicant's acts did not endanger the life or health of those police officers'
- 'the prison sentence of one year and six months under Article 318 § 1 of the Criminal Code for using violence against a public official was... more lenient than the partly concurrent two-year prison sentence for participation in acts of mass disorder'
- 'the gravity of punishment under Article 212 of the Criminal Code was attributable to the general context of the applicant's assault on the policemen (already imputed under Article 318 of the Code) and not to the harm he thus inflicted' — here the Court draws on the Government's own submissions, but accepts this characterisation as accurate
- 'The domestic courts did not specify which of the applicant's acts were attributable to participation in mass disorder, as opposed to use of violence against a public official'
- 'In describing the applicant's participation in the mass disorder, the courts merely referred to his assaulting the police officers, also classified as a separate criminal offence'

On the applicant's role and conduct:
- 'there is nothing in the case file to suggest that the applicant was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character and the onset of clashes between the protesters and the police'
- 'the applicant used violence against the policemen at the height of the clashes when the police were already arresting protesters'
- 'the applicant's overall conduct, therefore, although involving certain degree of violence, was sporadic in nature and appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police against the protesters, the majority of whom were peaceful'
- 'it did not attain the degree of aggression which the Court found in other cases to amount to violence calling for lengthy prison terms'

On chilling effect:
- 'the applicant's criminal conviction, and especially the severity of his sentence, must have had the effect of discouraging him and other opposition supporters, and the public at large, from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate'
- 'the chilling effect of the sanction imposed on the applicant was further amplified by the large-scale proceedings in the case, which attracted widespread media coverage'

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
