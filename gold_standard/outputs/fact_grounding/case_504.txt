This is an important methodological question. The reasoning text is notably sparse — it is a repetitive-case judgment where the Court dispenses with detailed reasoning by reference to leading cases. With that caveat clearly stated, here is the analysis:

---

**(a) Scope — implied**

**Assembly exists / Participation:**
The only factual grounding in the text is the following description:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the complaints, not merely a party submission. However, it must be noted that the Court does not independently verify or establish these facts — it simply proceeds on the basis of this description without questioning it.

**Peaceful character:**
There is no factual grounding whatsoever in the reasoning text for a finding that the assemblies were peaceful. The Court does not mention this, and no facts are cited to support it, even implicitly.

---

**(b) Interference — explicitly implied**

The factual grounding for the interference finding is the same passage:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the concrete state acts the Court treats as interferences. No further factual detail is provided — the Court does not describe the circumstances of the arrests, the nature of the dispersals, or the content of the convictions.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society — explicitly discussed**

This is the only element the Court substantively addresses, yet the factual grounding is strikingly thin. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is the entirety of the factual basis offered. The Court does not identify, quote, or describe any specific fact from the case record that bears on the proportionality assessment. Instead, it reasons negatively — no new fact displaced the conclusions already reached in the leading cases. The leading cases themselves (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov) are referenced as the substantive analytical foundation, but their facts are not reproduced here.

---

**Overall observation:**

The honest conclusion is that this reasoning text contains almost no independent factual grounding for any of the canonical elements. The Court's method here is one of assimilation to precedent rather than fact-specific reasoning. The only facts mentioned — arrests, dispersal of assemblies, convictions for administrative offences — do minimal work, and even these are drawn from the applicants' own characterisation of their complaints rather than from the Court's independent factual findings.
