## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court's implicit acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in the following facts stated by the Court:

- "his participation in the rally"
- "an unauthorised political gathering thirty minutes after the start of the rally"
- "the demonstrators"

**(ii) Participation**

The Court's implicit acceptance that the applicant participated is grounded in:

- "the applicant was arrested and charged with an administrative offence for having participated in an unauthorised political gathering thirty minutes after the start of the rally"

**(iii) Peaceful character**

The Court's assessment that the assembly was peaceful is grounded in:

- "The Government did not suggest that he had demonstrated a violent behaviour or had in other way disrupted peace or public order"
- "in the absence of the acts of violence on the part of the demonstrators"

Note: The first of these is a Government omission (failure to allege violence) that the Court treats as factually significant — the Court does not merely report the Government's position but draws a substantive conclusion from the absence of any such allegation.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's implicit acceptance that an interference occurred is grounded in:

- "the applicant was arrested and charged with an administrative offence for having participated in an unauthorised political gathering thirty minutes after the start of the rally"
- "the interference with the applicant's freedom of assembly" (the Court's own characterisation, treating the arrest and proceedings as established interference)

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss this element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified from the Court's own reasoning. The Government asserted "the aim of maintaining public order" but the Court neither adopted this characterisation nor made any factual finding in connection with it.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's assessment that the interference was not necessary is grounded in the following facts:

- "the applicant was arrested and charged with an administrative offence for having participated in an unauthorised political gathering thirty minutes after the start of the rally"
- "The Government did not suggest that he had demonstrated a violent behaviour or had in other way disrupted peace or public order"
- "in the absence of the acts of violence on the part of the demonstrators"
- "Nor did they explain as to why it was impossible for the authorities to allow the demonstrators, including the applicant, to complete their assembly and to impose a reasonable fine on the spot or later on"

The last of these is again grounded in a Government omission — the failure to provide justification — which the Court treats as a factually relevant gap in the proportionality assessment.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss this element.
