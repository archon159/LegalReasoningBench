I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / participation / peaceful:** The only factual grounding available is the phrase "public religious events" — *"The applicants complained principally of the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location and/or time of public religious events."* This is the Court's own characterisation of the subject matter of the complaints. No further facts are stated in the reasoning text to ground these elements. The Court does not elaborate on the nature of the events, the applicants' participation, or the peaceful character of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: *"The applicants complained principally of the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location and/or time of public religious events."* The Court adopts this as the basis for its finding of interference, as it then proceeds to characterise these restrictions as "interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly." No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court's finding that the "quality of law" requirements were not met is grounded in the following: *"the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements."* However, this is a legal conclusion, not a factual statement. The reasoning text provides **no specific facts** about the content, wording, accessibility, or operation of the legal provisions in question. The Court instead grounds its assessment by reference to the Lashmankin case: *"In the leading case of Lashmankin and Others v. Russia, no. 57818/09 and 14 others, §§ 402-78, 7 February 2017, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case"* and *"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."* The factual grounding is thus delegated to Lashmankin by reference, and no independent facts are stated in this reasoning text.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The same observation applies as for (c). The Court's conclusion that the interference was "not 'necessary in a democratic society'" is stated as a legal conclusion without any independent factual grounding in this reasoning text. The assessment rests entirely on the reference to Lashmankin: *"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion on the admissibility and merits of these complaints."* No specific facts about proportionality, the weight of the aim pursued, or the intensity of the interference are stated.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**Summary observation:** The reasoning text is notably sparse in its factual grounding. For most elements, the Court relies on a reference to the Lashmankin precedent rather than setting out independent facts. The only concrete factual statement in the text is that the restrictions concerned "the location and/or time of public religious events," which grounds the scope and interference elements. All other elements are grounded by reference to prior case law rather than facts stated in this judgment.
