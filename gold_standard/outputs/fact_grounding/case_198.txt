Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Existence of an assembly:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "the dispersal of these assemblies." These are the Court's own characterizations, not party submissions adopted by the Court. Verbatim: *"disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."*

**ii) Participation:**
Same factual grounding as above. Verbatim: *"organisers or participants of public assemblies."*

**iii) Peaceful character:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court makes no factual statement — explicit or implicit — about the peaceful or non-peaceful character of the assemblies. The inference that the assemblies were peaceful is drawn only from the structural logic of the analysis and the citation of leading cases, not from any identified fact in this text.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the description of the concrete governmental measures. Verbatim: *"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."*

These are the only facts identified. The Court does not elaborate further on the circumstances of the arrests or convictions in this passage.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is neither discussed nor implied in the reasoning text.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is neither discussed nor implied in the reasoning text.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity finding is notably thin in its factual grounding. The only factual basis offered is the same description of the governmental measures already quoted under interference: *"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."*

Beyond this, the Court grounds its necessity conclusion not in facts but in legal precedent: *"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..."* The Court thus explicitly acknowledges it is relying on prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov) rather than conducting an independent factual analysis. No specific facts from the case are quoted or adopted in support of the necessity finding beyond what is already noted.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.
