This is an important question that requires careful attention to what the Court actually established as fact, rather than what was merely asserted.

---

**Interference (explicit)**

The only factual grounding offered is: "restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of public events."

This is stated in the introductory framing of the complaint, not as a finding of fact by the Court itself. The Court does not independently verify or elaborate on these facts. It then refers to interference in the merits section without grounding it in any additional established facts. The factual basis is therefore thin — it rests on the characterisation of the complaint rather than an explicit judicial finding of fact.

---

**Prescribed by law (explicit)**

The Court states: "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements."

There is no factual grounding for this finding in the reasoning text itself. The Court does not identify which legal provisions were at issue, describe their content, or explain why they failed the quality of law test. The Court instead states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion in the present case," and cross-references Lashmankin and Others. The factual grounding is entirely delegated to that prior judgment and is not reproduced here.

---

**Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

The same applies here. The Court states the interference "was moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'" but provides no factual grounding in this text. Again, the Court relies on: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion in the present case."

No specific facts about proportionality, the weight of the legitimate aim, or the intensity of the restriction are identified in this reasoning text.

---

**Scope — assembly, participation, peaceful character (implied)**

There is no factual grounding for any of these elements in the reasoning text. The Court does not describe the nature of the assemblies, confirm that the applicants participated, or address whether the assemblies were peaceful. These elements are entirely unaddressed factually.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning text is notably sparse in its factual grounding. The Court explicitly acknowledges that it is deciding the case by reference to Lashmankin and Others, treating this as a repetitive case. The phrase "Having examined all the material submitted to it" signals that factual assessment occurred, but the results of that assessment are not reproduced in this text. For virtually every canonical element, the factual grounding must be sought in Lashmankin and Others rather than in this decision itself.
