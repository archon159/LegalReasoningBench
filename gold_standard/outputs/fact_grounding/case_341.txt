## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> 'the applicants held a demonstration in front of the Tbilisi Court of Appeal'

> 'the demonstration, in which just five persons participated, was dispersed a few minutes after it had started'

> 'the applicants were able to voice only a very few slogans'

**ii) Participation**

Grounded on the same facts as above:

> 'the demonstration, in which just five persons participated, was dispersed a few minutes after it had started'

> 'given the absence of any violent behaviour by the applicants during the authorised picket'

**iii) Peaceful**

> 'given the absence of any violent behaviour by the applicants during the authorised picket'

> 'the applicants addressed the Tbilisi Court of Appeal without being violent or rejecting democratic principles in their speech'

---

### (b) Interference

> 'Their prosecution, as conceded by the Government, amounted to an interference with the exercise of the freedom of peaceful assembly.'

> 'Several minutes later they were arrested and subsequently punished by being placed in detention for breach of public order and contempt of court.'

Note: The Court here explicitly adopts the Government's concession as an established fact, so the Government's admission is properly included.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds its serious doubts on the following facts:

> 'both section 76(3) § 1 (f) of the Courts Act and Article 208 § 6(1) of the CCP...expressly and concomitantly stated that actions allegedly constituting a breach of public order and contempt of court could be prevented by court bailiffs and punished by the President of the relevant court if committed inside court-houses'

> 'the Court further attaches importance to a finding of the Constitutional Court of Georgia, which characterised it [the definition of contempt of court] as vague'

> 'the Court...has very serious doubts that the applicants could reasonably have foreseen that their demonstration outside the building of the Tbilisi Court of Appeal, in its yard, could ever have attracted liability under the above-mentioned wording of the domestic provisions'

**ii) Accessibility:** No factual grounding identifiable — not discussed in the Court's own assessment.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse:** No factual grounding identifiable — not explicitly discussed as a distinct element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the legitimate aim but provides limited independent factual grounding. It states:

> 'The Court can accept the Government's argument that the interference in question pursued a legitimate aim: the prevention of disorder and the protection of the authority of the judiciary.'

> 'What is at stake as regards the protection of the authority of the judiciary is the confidence which the courts in a democratic society must inspire in the public at large.'

The Court does not independently establish specific facts grounding the legitimate aim — it accepts the Government's characterisation. No further independent factual grounding is provided beyond this acceptance.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

**On the limited scale and duration of the demonstration:**

> 'the demonstration, in which just five persons participated, was dispersed a few minutes after it had started; during that extremely short period of time the applicants were able to voice only a very few slogans. Those facts already call into question the Government's assertion about the level of disruption of public order'

**On the content of the slogans:**

> 'the majority of those slogans represented critical value judgments, in which the applicants, resorting to a certain degree of political exaggeration and harsh words, referred to a matter of public concern in Georgia – the independence of the judiciary'

> 'The Court considers that the only offensive phrase voiced by one of the applicants during their short picket was the referral to the Minister of the Interior as "Lavrentiy Beria's bastard". However, the Court cannot understand how that expression could constitute contempt of court, as it was not addressed to a member of the judiciary'

**On the absence of violence:**

> 'given the absence of any violent behaviour by the applicants during the authorised picket'

**On the inadequacy of the domestic decision's reasoning:**

> 'in her decision of 29 June 2006 the President of the Tbilisi Court of Appeal, rather than fulfilling her duty to establish convincing grounds justifying the dispersal of the demonstration and the punishment of the applicants by the imposition of detention, mostly paraphrased the general and abstract terms of the qualification of offences of breach of public order and contempt of court under the domestic law'

> 'She did not explain, by reference to the particular circumstances of the incident, how exactly the administration of justice was being obstructed and which specific phrases uttered by the applicants were considered to be severe enough to constitute contempt of court'

> 'the only matter she relied on in her decision – the statement that the applicants had held their demonstration "inside the court building, in its central entrance" – was untrue and thus unacceptable for the purpose of justification under the second paragraph of Article 11'

**On the severity of the penalty:**

> 'they were nevertheless subjected to the most severe penalty applicable to the offences in question, namely thirty days of deprivation of liberty'

> 'The unreasonableness of the drastic penalty in the present case is further magnified when assessed against the above-mentioned absence of sufficient and relevant reasons in the underlying decision of 29 June 2006.'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
