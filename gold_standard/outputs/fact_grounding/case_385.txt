**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists — implied**

The factual grounding is: "public assemblies" and "dispersal of these assemblies." Verbatim: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(ii) Participation — implied**

The factual grounding is the same passage: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(iii) Peaceful — implied**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not refer to any facts establishing the peaceful nature of the assemblies. The implication that the assemblies were peaceful rests purely on the legal logic of proceeding under Article 11, not on any factual statement in the reasoning text.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's finding on necessity is stated as follows: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

However, the necessity finding is not grounded in any specific facts recited in this reasoning section. The Court relies entirely on its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) and a general reference to "all the material submitted to it," without identifying particular facts that support the necessity assessment. No specific facts are quoted or adopted by the Court in this section as the basis for the necessity finding.
