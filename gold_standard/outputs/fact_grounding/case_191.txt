**Factual Grounding for Each Discussed or Implied Element**

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies." The Court does not independently establish the existence of the assemblies through its own factual findings — it characterizes the applicants' complaint in these terms. This is thin grounding; the Court does not make an independent factual finding that assemblies existed, but proceeds on this basis without questioning it.

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage: "measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." Again, this is drawn from the characterization of the complaint rather than an independent factual finding by the Court.

**iii) Peaceful character:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court makes no factual finding — explicit or implied — about the peaceful or non-peaceful nature of the assemblies. The peaceful character is implied only by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any identified fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding in the following facts, quoted verbatim:
- "the dispersal of these assemblies"
- "their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in this reasoning section.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity assessment is notably thin in factual grounding. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it and having taken into account the issue of compliance with the six-month time-limit under Article 35 § 1 of the Convention... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is the entirety of the factual grounding offered for the necessity conclusion. The Court does not quote, identify, or describe any specific facts from the case that bear on the proportionality of the interference. Instead, it grounds its conclusion by reference to its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov) and the absence of any distinguishing facts or arguments. The factual basis for the necessity finding is therefore not independently articulated in this reasoning section — it is delegated entirely to the leading cases cited.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed at all.
