## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**Assembly exists / Participation**

The Court does not explicitly discuss these elements, and there is no discrete factual finding grounding them. The closest the Court comes is the framing of the complaint itself: 'The applicant complained under Article 11 of the Convention about the refusal of his application to organise a demonstration.' This is a characterisation of the complaint, not a factual finding by the Court. No factual grounding can be identified beyond this framing.

**Peaceful character**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court makes no finding on this point.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's acceptance of interference is grounded in the following, which it explicitly adopts: 'it is not in dispute between the parties that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly, and it has no reason to hold otherwise.'

The Court does not go further to identify the specific factual basis for the interference in this passage. However, the nature of the interference is implicit in the framing of the complaint ('the refusal of his application to organise a demonstration') which the Court accepts as the subject matter of the case.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's acceptance is grounded in the following, which it explicitly adopts: 'it is not in dispute between the parties either that the interference was prescribed by law, namely, by section 8(1) of the Assembly Act.'

No further factual grounding is provided. The Court does not examine the qualities of the law itself.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court's acceptance is grounded in the same sentence as the prescribed by law finding: 'it is not in dispute between the parties either that...it pursued the legitimate aims of public safety and the rights and freedoms of others. Again, it has no reason to hold otherwise.'

No independent factual analysis is offered by the Court to ground this conclusion.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively grounded element. The Court's assessment rests on the following factual findings:

**On the right to choose place and modalities:**
'the domestic authorities invited the applicant to exercise his right to demonstrate in cooperation with the authorities, limiting the area in question.'

And: 'the right to freedom of peaceful assembly includes the right to choose the time, place and modalities of the assembly, within the limits established in paragraph 2 of Article 11.'

**On the failure to demonstrate necessity of the proposed restriction:**
'While the limitation proposed by the police may have respected as such the essence of the applicant's rights under Article 11, the Court finds that this was not demonstrated to be the case. It is not the role of the Court to carry out such assessments in the absence of a proper domestic consideration of the matter, in particular if the authorities did not provide "relevant and sufficient" reasons for the proposed restriction.'

**On the comparator demonstration — the Court's key factual finding:**
'The Court notes the applicant's unrefuted assertion that another demonstration planned on exactly the same location for 15 October 2008 had not been forbidden by the authorities.'

And the Court's own elaboration of why this is significant: 'on that date – unlike on 17 October 2008 – five parliamentary commissions were in session (see paragraph 10 above), evidently entailing the presence and movement of numerous MPs.'

Note: The Court explicitly adopts the applicant's assertion here precisely because it was *unrefuted*. This is the Court's own acceptance of the fact, not merely a party submission.

**On the absence of parliamentary activity on the relevant date:**
'especially since on the date of the event planned by the applicant no parliamentary activity was underway (see paragraph 7 above).'

**On the overall conclusion of no pressing social need:**
'the Court cannot but conclude that the prohibition of the demonstration did not respond to a pressing social need, even in the face of the applicant's intransigence in considering the police's conciliatory suggestion (see paragraph 8 above).'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all.
