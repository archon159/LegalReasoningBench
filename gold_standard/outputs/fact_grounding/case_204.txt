I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies"

This is the Court's own characterisation of what occurred. No further factual detail is provided.

**ii) Participation**
The only factual grounding is:
> "measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies"

Again, this is the Court's own characterisation. No further factual detail is provided.

**iii) Peaceful character**
There is no factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the peaceful or non-peaceful character of the assemblies. This element is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

These are the only concrete facts stated: dispersal of assemblies, arrest, and conviction for administrative offence. No further factual detail is provided in this reasoning section.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding for this element beyond what has already been quoted under interference. The Court does not set out any facts relating to, for example, the conduct of authorities, the reasons given for dispersal, the nature of the administrative offence, or the severity of the sanctions. The Court's finding of non-necessity rests entirely on reference to prior case-law:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..."

The Court thus explicitly acknowledges that it examined material but does not reproduce or rely on any specific facts from that material in this reasoning section. The necessity finding is grounded in legal precedent, not in facts stated in this text.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning section is strikingly thin on factual grounding. For virtually every element, the Court either relies on bare characterisations in the opening paragraph or on references to prior case-law. No facts are quoted from party submissions that the Court explicitly adopted as established. The reasoning functions as a repetition/clone judgment, where the substantive factual and legal analysis is delegated entirely to the leading cases cited (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others).
