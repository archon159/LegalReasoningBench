**Factual grounding for each discussed or implied element:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists (implied):** The Court's assessment rests on the following factual statement: *"disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."* The reference to "public assemblies" and their "dispersal" establishes that assemblies took place.

- **Participation (implied):** The same passage provides the factual grounding: *"disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."* The applicants are identified as "organisers or participants."

- **Peaceful (implied):** No factual grounding can be identified. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies. The peacefulness of the assemblies is not addressed in any factual statement in the reasoning text.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: *"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."* These are the two concrete state acts identified as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No independent facts are stated to ground the necessity assessment. The Court does not describe the circumstances of the dispersals, the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, or the severity of the convictions. The finding of non-necessity rests entirely on reference to prior case-law (*Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, *Kasparov and Others v. Russia*) and the conclusion that *"the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."* No specific facts from the present case are quoted or adopted as the basis for the necessity finding.

---

**Summary observation:**

The reasoning is strikingly thin on factual grounding. The only facts explicitly stated and adopted by the Court are: (1) the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies; (2) those assemblies were dispersed; (3) the applicants were arrested; and (4) they were convicted of administrative offences. Beyond these four facts, the Court's conclusions rest on analogy to prior case-law rather than on any factual analysis of the specific circumstances of the present case.
