I'll work through each canonical element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, identifying the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts it treats as established:

> "the March of Dissent in Samara"
> "an opposition rally in Samara that day"

The Court does not separately verify the existence of the rally but treats it as undisputed. The Government's submissions do not contest it, and the Court proceeds on this basis.

**ii) Participation (attempted)**

> "The applicant wanted to fly from Sheremetyevo airport to Samara to participate in the March of Dissent scheduled for the same day. The Government do not dispute that this was the purpose of his flight."

The Court explicitly grounds its finding on the undisputed nature of the applicant's purpose, noting the Government's non-contestation as a basis for treating it as established.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court characterizes the assembly as peaceful but does not ground this in any specific fact it identifies. It simply states:

> "The thrust of the applicant's complaint is that he was prevented from attending a peaceful assembly, the March of Dissent in Samara."

No factual basis is given for the characterization "peaceful" beyond the label itself. This element is asserted rather than grounded in identified facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding on the following facts:

> "The applicant's arrest and detention, and the seizure of his passport and ticket made it impossible for him to fly that day."

> "Having been denied access to the Samara flight, at such short notice, there would have been no other way for him to make it to Samara in time for the rally."

> "The applicant's arrest and detention prevented him from attending the rally."

These three factual steps form a causal chain: arrest and detention → inability to fly → inability to attend the rally → interference with freedom of assembly. All three are stated by the Court as established facts, not merely as party submissions.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds its finding here not in new facts but by cross-reference to its earlier legal finding under Article 5 § 1(c):

> "The Court has already found that the applicant's arrest and detention were not 'lawful' within the meaning of Article 5 § 1 (c)."

The Court does not re-examine the underlying facts at this stage. The factual grounding is entirely carried over by reference to the prior Article 5 analysis, which is not reproduced in this section of the judgment. No additional facts are identified here.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding is provided. The Court explicitly declines to examine this element:

> "The conclusion that the inference was not 'prescribed by law' makes it unnecessary to consider whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim and was 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The Government's submission that the aim was "ensuring the security of air travel" is noted in the parties' submissions section but is never adopted or assessed by the Court. It cannot therefore be treated as factual grounding for the Court's assessment.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

As with legitimate aim, no factual grounding is provided. The Court explicitly declines to examine this element for the same reason stated above. No facts are identified in connection with this element.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
