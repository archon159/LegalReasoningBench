## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**

The Court grounds this on the following accepted fact:

> "it is not disputed between the parties that the applicants participated in the rally of 15 April 2007"

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?**

Grounded on the same sentence:

> "it is not disputed between the parties that the applicants participated in the rally of 15 April 2007"

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**

Grounded on two accepted facts:

> "it is not disputed between the parties that the applicants participated in the rally of 15 April 2007 and that their conduct was peaceful."

> "It has not been alleged that they were among those who had, according to the Government, disturbed the public order during the assembly."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on two factual findings it had itself made or accepted from domestic courts:

> "the Court notes that it has found a violation of Article 3 of the Convention on account of the first and the third applicants' ill-treatment by police during the public assembly"

> "the domestic courts established that the police had exceeded their powers during the operation on 15 April 2007"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court simply accepts this element without grounding it in any specific facts from the case. It states only:

> "The Court is ready to accept that in the present case the use of force by police was 'prescribed by law'"

No factual basis is articulated. **No factual grounding can be identified.**

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Similarly, the Court accepts the legitimate aim without grounding it in specific facts:

> "The Court is ready to accept that in the present case the use of force by police was 'prescribed by law' and pursued the legitimate aim of prevention of disorder and crime."

No specific facts are cited to support this acceptance. **No factual grounding can be identified beyond the bare acceptance.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of disproportionality on the same two factual findings used for interference:

> "given its above findings as regards the ill-treatment in respect of the first and the third applicants, and the findings of the domestic courts as regards the excessive use of power by police during the public assembly on 15 April 2007, the Court considers that the intervention of the police and the force applied were disproportionate and not necessary within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

Additionally, the Court grounds the chilling effect finding on the same facts, without citing new ones:

> "In the Court's view, the use of force by police could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants and others from taking part in similar meetings."

This chilling effect statement is an inference drawn from the established facts rather than a separately grounded factual finding.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
