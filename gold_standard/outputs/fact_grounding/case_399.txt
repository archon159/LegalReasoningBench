## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not separately articulate factual grounding for the existence of an assembly. It refers to "the Maidan protests" as a given, without describing their nature or characteristics in this section. No verbatim factual finding can be quoted for this sub-element specifically. The existence of an assembly is implied rather than grounded in explicit factual reasoning here.

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds participation in its earlier findings, cross-referenced here:

> "The Court reiterates its findings that Mr I. Lutsenko and Mr Y. Verbytskyy were abducted, Mr I. Lutsenko was ill-treated and Mr Y. Verbytskyy was tortured and eventually murdered **in connection with their participation in the Maidan protests** and that the authorities were responsible for those abuses (see, notably, paragraphs 81-93 above)."

And:

> "It is not disputed that Mr I. Lutsenko's and Mr Y. Verbytskyy's participation in the Maidan protests was protected under Article 11 of the Convention."

**(iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds the peaceful character of the applicants' participation in the absence of contrary evidence:

> "In the present case, there is no evidence to suggest that during their participation in the protests Mr I. Lutsenko or Mr Y. Verbytskyy intended to commit or engaged in acts of violence."

The Court also provides contextual factual grounding for why the broader violence at the protests does not negate this conclusion:

> "The Court is mindful of the fact that the specific events at issue took place while there were violent clashes ongoing between some of the protesters and the police in central Kyiv."

And it applies the legal principle that individual peaceful character is not negated by others' violence, but the factual anchor for the applicants themselves remains solely the absence-of-evidence finding quoted above.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding in the following facts:

> "The Court reiterates its findings that Mr I. Lutsenko and Mr Y. Verbytskyy were abducted, Mr I. Lutsenko was ill-treated and Mr Y. Verbytskyy was tortured and eventually murdered in connection with their participation in the Maidan protests and that the authorities were responsible for those abuses."

And more specifically on the purpose of the abuses:

> "Even though the related domestic proceedings have not yet been concluded, the Court finds that there are cogent and substantial elements demonstrating that those abuses were aimed at punishing or intimidating them on account of their involvement in the Maidan protests and/or preventing their further participation therein (see, notably, the relevant domestic and international findings cited or quoted at paragraphs 81-93 above)."

The Court also identifies the nature of the interference by cross-reference:

> "the interference at issue, which consisted of the treatment examined under Articles 2 and 3 above (see paragraphs 90-93 above)."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court makes no factual findings to ground this element. It simply states the negative conclusion:

> "There is nothing in the case file capable of demonstrating that the interference at issue... was 'prescribed by law'."

No specific facts are identified — the Court notes the absence of any material in the case file that could support a finding of lawfulness. No factual grounding can be quoted.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Similarly, the Court makes no factual findings to ground this element. It states only:

> "There is nothing in the case file capable of demonstrating that the interference at issue... pursued a 'legitimate aim'."

Again, the conclusion rests on an absence rather than on identified facts. No factual grounding can be quoted.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court makes no factual findings specific to this element either. It states:

> "Nor is there any ground to suggest that it was 'necessary in a democratic society'."

This follows logically from the prior negative findings on prescribed by law and legitimate aim, rather than from any independent factual analysis. No factual grounding can be quoted.

---

### (f) Positive obligation (implied)

The Court grounds its implicit positive obligation reasoning in the following facts:

> "The Court is mindful of the fact that the specific events at issue took place while there were violent clashes ongoing between some of the protesters and the police in central Kyiv."

> "there is no information that appropriate measures were taken to restore peace or weed out the violent strand of protesters."

> "the enactment on 16 January 2014 of so-called 'anti-protest laws' which provided for harsh penalties in relation to the exercise of freedom of assembly and free speech essentially frustrated the original obstructive, but peaceful goal of the Maidan protests."

> "the authorities' repeated attempts to disperse the protesters by force arguably contributed to the consequent escalation of violence."

It should be noted, however, that the Court uses the word "arguably" in the last finding, which signals some caution about treating this as a firm factual conclusion. The positive obligation reasoning remains contextual and is not developed into a standalone doctrinal finding.
