I will work through each doctrinal element in turn, identifying only the facts the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not make an explicit factual finding that an assembly existed. However, the following facts ground the implied conclusion:

- 'all of them were members of the PFPA and had actively participated in various protests held by the opposition'
- 'preventing them from participating in or organising such protests'
- reference to 'a protest scheduled for 23 February 2014' (this appears in the applicants' submissions section; the Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact, so it is excluded)

**ii) Participation**

- 'all of them were members of the PFPA and had actively participated in various protests held by the opposition'
- 'the proceedings against the applicants in the present cases and the ensuing administrative detention were arbitrary and unlawful measures aimed at punishing the applicants for their political activity, including their previous participation in opposition protests, and preventing them from participating in or organising such protests'

**iii) Peaceful**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court makes no finding — explicit or implied — about the peaceful nature of the assemblies. I cannot map any fact to this element.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following facts:

- 'the applicants' affiliation with the opposition was likewise generally known, as all of them were members of the PFPA and had actively participated in various protests held by the opposition'
- 'all of the applicants were arrested within a short time frame on dubious grounds and in similar circumstances'
- 'the first applicant was accused of "swearing aloud" at no one in particular and for no apparent reason. All the other applicants were suspected of either "suspicious behaviour" or some offence. However, none of those suspicions received any follow-up whatsoever and they were quickly forgotten once the applicants had been charged with disobeying police orders. No specific sources or reasons for those initial suspicions were ever indicated.'
- 'the domestic courts failed to establish facts that were disputed between the parties – the police and the applicants – by carrying out an objective and thorough examination. They ignored the applicants' submissions that the arrests had been politically motivated, and merely recapitulated the circumstances and the charges as presented by the police'
- 'the proceedings against the applicants in the present cases and the ensuing administrative detention were arbitrary and unlawful measures aimed at punishing the applicants for their political activity, including their previous participation in opposition protests, and preventing them from participating in or organising such protests'
- 'The applicants' arrests and administrative detention could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly had a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'

The Court also grounds its assessment in the broader political context:

- 'the period following 2011 (which was the year when the applicants in the Huseynli and Others case were arrested and convicted) were likewise of increased political sensitivity'
- 'There are many international reports alleging that in 2014 (the year in which the applicants in the present cases were arrested and convicted) the authorities continued to resort to various arbitrary measures against those expressing critical opinions'
- 'the PACE in its Resolution of 23 June 2015 stated that "dubiously motivated criminal prosecutions and disproportionate sentences remain[ed] a concern" and called on the authorities in Azerbaijan "to end the systemic harassment of those who [were] critical of the government and to release those wrongfully detained"'

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly work through this element. The implied negative conclusion (that the measures were not lawfully applied) is grounded in:

- 'the proceedings against the applicants in the present cases and the ensuing administrative detention were arbitrary and unlawful measures'
- 'all of the applicants were arrested within a short time frame on dubious grounds and in similar circumstances'
- 'the first applicant was accused of "swearing aloud" at no one in particular and for no apparent reason. All the other applicants were suspected of either "suspicious behaviour" or some offence. However, none of those suspicions received any follow-up whatsoever and they were quickly forgotten once the applicants had been charged with disobeying police orders. No specific sources or reasons for those initial suspicions were ever indicated.'
- 'the domestic courts failed to establish facts that were disputed between the parties – the police and the applicants – by carrying out an objective and thorough examination. They ignored the applicants' submissions that the arrests had been politically motivated, and merely recapitulated the circumstances and the charges as presented by the police'

On the sub-element of safeguards specifically, the last two quoted passages are the closest factual grounding, but the Court does not frame them explicitly in terms of the prescribed-by-law test.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court makes no factual findings directed at this element. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court does not apply this test explicitly. No factual grounding directed at this element can be identified as such. The chilling effect passage could be read as touching on proportionality, but the Court does not frame it that way:

- 'The applicants' arrests and administrative detention could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly had a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'

This passage is better understood as reinforcing the finding of interference rather than as a proportionality assessment.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court makes no findings directed at this element. No factual grounding can be identified.
