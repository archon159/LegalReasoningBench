I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding in the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."

This is thin — the Court does not independently verify or describe the assemblies. The characterisation comes from the applicants' complaints, and the Court does not explicitly adopt it as an established fact. It is simply used as the framing for the complaint. **No clearly adopted factual grounding can be identified beyond this implicit acceptance.**

**ii) Participation**
The same passage provides the only grounding: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." Again, this is drawn from the applicants' framing and is not independently verified in the text. **No clearly adopted factual grounding beyond implicit acceptance.**

**iii) Peaceful**
**No factual grounding whatsoever.** The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. Peacefulness is implied structurally by the application of Article 11, but no fact is cited to support it.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court's assessment is grounded in the following explicitly stated facts:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

These are the two concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's conclusion that the interference was not necessary in a democratic society is not grounded in any specific facts about the individual cases before it. Instead, the Court grounds its assessment entirely in prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The second sentence is notable: it acknowledges that material was submitted but states only that nothing in it displaced the conclusion drawn from precedent. **No specific facts from the present cases are cited as the basis for the necessity assessment.**
