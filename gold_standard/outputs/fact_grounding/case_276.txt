## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly analyze this as a separate element, but the following factual reference grounds the implied finding:
> "the demonstration of 23 February 2003"

and

> "the applicant, whose actions were formally qualified as 'minor hooliganism', were in fact convicted for their participation in peaceful demonstrations"

**(ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this on:
> "the applicant, whose actions were formally qualified as 'minor hooliganism', were in fact convicted for their participation in peaceful demonstrations"

and

> "the Court of Appeal failed to provide details of any acts allegedly committed by the applicant at the demonstration of 23 February 2003"

and

> "the State chose not to prohibit a demonstration but subsequently imposed sanctions on its participants for the mere fact of attending it"

**(iii) Peaceful**
The Court grounds the peaceful character on the absence of any established reprehensible conduct:
> "the Court of Appeal failed to provide details of any acts allegedly committed by the applicant at the demonstration of 23 February 2003 which could be characterised as reprehensible, including any violent or offensive acts, and limited itself to a very abstract finding that the applicant had 'violated public order'"

and

> "No other material before the Court contains any such details either."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on:
> "the applicant, whose actions were formally qualified as 'minor hooliganism', were in fact convicted for their participation in peaceful demonstrations"

and specifically:

> "the decision of the President of the Criminal and Military Court of Appeal of 1 March 2003 constituted an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly"

The factual basis is thus the conviction itself and its true character as a response to demonstration participation rather than to genuinely disorderly conduct.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court does not ground its finding on any specific facts in this judgment. It simply cross-references prior case law:
> "The Court has already found, in similar circumstances, that an interference in the form of conviction under Article 172 of the CAO complied with the requirement of lawfulness (see Galstyan, cited above, § 107, and Ashughyan, cited above, §§ 81 and 82)."

No independent factual grounding is provided in this judgment for this element. The sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not factually grounded here at all.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Again, the Court does not ground this on specific facts but on a cross-reference to prior cases:
> "similarly to those cases, the conviction in the present case pursued the legitimate aim of the 'prevention of disorder' (see Galstyan, cited above, § 110, and Ashughyan, cited above, § 85)."

No independent factual grounding is provided in this judgment for this element.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

On the absence of any identified reprehensible conduct:
> "the Court of Appeal failed to provide details of any acts allegedly committed by the applicant at the demonstration of 23 February 2003 which could be characterised as reprehensible, including any violent or offensive acts, and limited itself to a very abstract finding that the applicant had 'violated public order'"

and

> "No other material before the Court contains any such details either."

On the absence of a legal framework for authorisation of demonstrations:
> "it is not clear on what grounds the Court of Appeal stated that the applicant had participated in an unauthorised demonstration, taking into account that at the material time there was no legal act applicable in Armenia containing rules for organising and holding rallies and street marches, including the rules for authorising such events"

On the State's failure to prohibit the demonstration while later sanctioning participants:
> "the very essence of the right to freedom of peaceful assembly would be impaired, if the State chose not to prohibit a demonstration but subsequently imposed sanctions on its participants for the mere fact of attending it, without committing any reprehensible acts"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
