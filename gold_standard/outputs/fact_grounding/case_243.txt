## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, and there is no specific factual finding establishing the existence of an assembly as a distinct analytical step. The Court simply proceeds on the basis that an assembly existed. The closest factual grounding is implicit in:

> 'members of the DISK, certain members of Parliament and journalists started gathering in front of the DISK's headquarters building in Şişli'

But the Court does not explicitly use this fact to establish the existence of an assembly as a legal matter.

**ii) Participation**

No explicit factual finding. The Court's statement that 'the applicants were not able to exercise their right to peaceful assembly' implies participation was attempted, but no specific facts about the applicants' individual presence or roles are cited to ground this element as a distinct finding.

**iii) Peaceful character**

The Court grounds this on the following explicitly stated facts:

> 'there is no evidence to support this allegation [that members of an illegal organisation threw stones at the police]'

> 'no proceedings were initiated against the applicants or other members of the applicant confederations in connection with this incident'

> 'there is also nothing to suggest that the group waiting in front of the DISK headquarters presented a danger to public order or engaged in violent acts'

> 'There is also no information in the file that the police had encountered any violent or active physical resistance which would explain the use of such an extreme use of force'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on the following fact:

> 'as a result of the police intervention, the applicants were not able to exercise their right to peaceful assembly'

This is a direct factual finding by the Court, cross-referencing paragraph 10 of the judgment (which is not reproduced in the reasoning text provided, but is cited as the factual basis).

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this solely on:

> 'the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely Sections 22 and 24 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act'

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Court does not go beyond identifying the statutory provisions.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the Government's submission on legitimate aim without independently establishing facts to support it:

> 'the Government submitted that the interference pursued, among others, the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder, and the Court finds no reason to differ'

The Court does not cite specific facts of its own to ground the legitimate aim finding. It simply does not contest the Government's characterisation. Accordingly, there is no independent factual grounding adopted by the Court for this element beyond its non-contestation of the Government's submission.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

**On the timing and nature of the intervention:**

> 'the police intervention commenced at 6.30 a.m., before the demonstrators started their march'

> 'on 1 May 2008, upon the order of the Istanbul Governor, operations of ferries and subways were stopped, the roads leading to Taksim Square were blocked and extra police were deployed to the area to block entrance to Taksim'

**On the absence of violence by demonstrators:**

> 'there is no evidence to support this allegation [that members of an illegal organisation threw stones at the police]'

> 'no proceedings were initiated against the applicants or other members of the applicant confederations in connection with this incident'

> 'there is also nothing to suggest that the group waiting in front of the DISK headquarters presented a danger to public order or engaged in violent acts'

> 'There is also no information in the file that the police had encountered any violent or active physical resistance which would explain the use of such an extreme use of force'

**On the methods used by police:**

> 'the security forces tried dispersing the people in front of the DISK Headquarters by using gas bombs, paint sprays and pressurised water. Several people were chased by police officers and beaten'

**On the gas bomb in the hospital:**

> 'the police officers threw a gas bomb in the Şişli Etfal Hospital premises while chasing the demonstrators'

The Court explicitly adopted the following from the hospital chief's testimony (as submitted by the Government, but accepted by the Court as an accurate account of what occurred):

> 'some demonstrators tried hiding in the hospital, opening a banner, and the police threw a gas bomb in the hospital's garden in their pursuit'

The Court then draws on this accepted account to conclude:

> 'the use of a gas bomb in hospital premises cannot be considered as necessary or proportionate in the circumstances of the present case'

**On the outcome:**

> 'as a result of the forceful intervention of the police officers, the demonstrators broke up of their own accord at 10.30 a.m. to forestall any further violence, and consequently they were not able to participate in the Labour Day celebrations'

**On the absence of domestic investigation:**

> 'no official investigation was conducted into the incident at the domestic level'

> 'the criminal complaints lodged by the applicants were to no avail, since no authorisation was granted by the Minister of the Interior for the prosecution of the Istanbul Governor or the Head of the Istanbul Security Directorate'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions positive obligations but does not ground them in specific facts of the case, nor does it make a violation finding on this basis. The references to positive obligations are purely doctrinal, citing *Djavit An* and *Oya Ataman*:

> 'although the essential object of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities in the exercise of the rights protected, there may also be positive obligations to secure their effective enjoyment'

No facts are mapped onto this element by the Court. It cannot be said that the Court grounds a positive obligation finding in any specific facts — because it makes no such finding.
