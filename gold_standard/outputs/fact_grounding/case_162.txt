## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the domestic court's findings, which it accepted:

> "the Administrative Court found it to be established that the applicant had tried to enter Theatre Square, apparently with the intention of holding a rally, and had called on others to do the same. The Court has no reasons to call into doubt the facts as established by that court."

And in the merits section:

> "the applicant was prevented from entering a public space, namely a central square, where he apparently wanted to participate in a rally. He was ordered by police officers to leave and not to call on people, who were apparently present in that area, to join him in his attempt to hold a rally."

**(ii) Participation**

The same factual findings ground this element:

> "the Administrative Court found it to be established that the applicant had tried to enter Theatre Square, apparently with the intention of holding a rally, and had called on others to do the same. The Court has no reasons to call into doubt the facts as established by that court."

The Court also clarified the applicant's position:

> "the applicant does not appear to have claimed before the Administrative Court that he had no intention to participate in a rally as such, but rather that he had not organised or incited others to participate in a rally."

**(iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds the implied peaceful character on the absence of evidence to the contrary:

> "Nor is there any evidence to suggest that the applicant had violent intentions or incited others to commit acts of violence or any similarly reprehensible acts."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on the following established facts:

> "the applicant was prevented by the police from holding a political protest in the centre of Gyumri and, when he disobeyed, was arrested and later sanctioned for those actions."

And more specifically in the necessity section:

> "the applicant was prevented from entering a public space, namely a central square, where he apparently wanted to participate in a rally. He was ordered by police officers to leave and not to call on people, who were apparently present in that area, to join him in his attempt to hold a rally. After he refused to stop his actions, he was arrested and later fined."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declined to make findings on this element. No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court did not assess it.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declined to make findings on this element. No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court did not assess it.

However, in the necessity analysis, the Court does record what the domestic court relied upon, without formally adopting it as a legitimate aim finding:

> "The Administrative Court held that the police actions of preventing the applicant from availing himself of his right to protest and to incite others to do so were justified, finding that a public assembly on Theatre Square could have endangered public order and public health in view of the fact that no notification had been made to hold the assembly as required by law and the state of emergency which had been declared in Yerevan a day earlier."

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively grounded element. The Court's assessment rests on the following established or accepted facts:

**On the prior notification ground:**

The Court grounds its reasoning on the structure of Armenian law as it stood:

> "the Court notes that such notification was required only when a public assembly involved 100 or more participants. Furthermore, if an event involving fewer than a 100 participants spontaneously grew into a bigger event exceeding that number, similarly no notification was required."

The Court then grounds its criticism of the domestic court on what that court failed to establish:

> "the domestic court appears to have simply proceeded on the assumption that the protest, which the applicant had wanted to hold and incited others to join, required a prior notification, without clarifying the number of persons present or the circumstances which gave rise to the gathering in the first place, including the question of whether it could fall into the category of 'spontaneous' gatherings."

The Court also notes the absence of evidentiary support for the Government's factual claims:

> "The Government alleged that there had been more than a 100 persons gathered in that area and that the assembly had not been spontaneous, but failed to support this allegation with any objective evidence. Moreover, the Government's allegation has no basis in the findings of the domestic court which, in fact, failed to carry out any examination of that question."

**On the state of emergency / public order ground:**

The Court grounds its reasoning on what the domestic court actually relied upon:

> "the domestic court based this conclusion exclusively on the fact that a state of emergency had been declared in Yerevan as a result of 'the events which had taken place in the capital of the country on 1 March 2008'."

The Court accepts the gravity of those events as a contextual fact:

> "it can nevertheless be understood from the context that this was an implied reference to the tense situation in the capital of Armenia on the previous day, which had involved clashes between protesters and the law enforcement authorities and resulted in physical damage, loss of life and destruction of property."

But grounds its finding of disproportionality on the absence of equivalent facts in Gyumri:

> "there is no evidence to suggest that anything similar had happened or was about to happen in Gyumri."

And on the blanket nature of the police response:

> "it appears that the presence of the police at Theatre Square and their apparent cordoning off of that area pursued the aim of ensuring that no assembly, whatever its intended purpose or size, took place in that location, thereby cutting any such attempts at the roots."

And on the absence of evidence of violent intent:

> "Nor is there any evidence to suggest that the applicant had violent intentions or incited others to commit acts of violence or any similarly reprehensible acts."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding exists for this element in the reasoning text.
