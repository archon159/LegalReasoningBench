## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court's implied acceptance that a qualifying assembly was planned rests on the following factual reference:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 25 February 2006"

No further factual elaboration is given by the Court on the nature of the assembly itself.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?**
The same passage grounds this implied element:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 25 February 2006"

And further:
> "Hyde Park's application to hold the protest demonstration planned for 25 February 2006"

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court grounds this implied finding on the following factual statement:
> "There was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities. Rather their intention was to stage a protest concerning the Eurovision song contest and the manner in which the Moldovan authorities had organised the voting in Moldova."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its explicit finding of interference on the following fact:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 25 February 2006 amounted to 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11"

The factual act constituting the interference is the rejection decision, described in more detail here:
> "the Municipality rejected the application to hold the protest demonstration on the grounds that the Parliament was not responsible for organising the Eurovision song contest and that the demonstration was groundless"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its explicit (though formally unresolved) discussion of this element on the following facts:

First, the statutory standard that should have been applied:
> "According to section 12(6) of the Assemblies Act, an application could be rejected only if the Municipality was in possession of evidence that the provisions of sections 6 and 7 would be breached with serious consequences for society."

Second, the actual reasons given by the Municipality, which departed from that standard:
> "Contrary to this provision, the Municipality rejected the application to hold the protest demonstration on the grounds that the Parliament was not responsible for organising the Eurovision song contest and that the demonstration was groundless."

Third, new reasons introduced during court proceedings:
> "Later, during the court proceedings the Municipality relied on two new reasons, namely that Hyde Park members had not participated in the Eurovision song contest and that the problem had already been discussed in Parliament."

Fourth, the Court's conclusion on the mismatch:
> "Since the reasons relied upon by the Municipality to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a protest had nothing in common with those provided by sections 6 and 7 of the Assemblies Act, the Court could consider this in itself as a sufficient basis for the conclusion that the impugned measure was not 'prescribed by law'."

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declines to decide this element and provides no factual grounding for any finding. As stated:
> "The Court, for the reasons set out below, does not consider it necessary to decide this point either."

**No factual grounding can be identified for this element.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its explicit finding of a violation on several distinct factual bases:

First, the Municipality's reasons for rejection were outside the statutory grounds:
> "the Municipality rejected Hyde Park's application to hold the protest demonstration planned for 25 February 2006 on grounds which were not provided in sections 6 and 7 of the Assemblies Act. According to the Municipality's decision, 'the Parliament was not responsible for organising the Eurovision song contest, which took place in Ukraine and the protest was groundless because it concerned past events'."

Second, the absence of any public order concern:
> "There was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities. Rather their intention was to stage a protest concerning the Eurovision song contest and the manner in which the Moldovan authorities had organised the voting in Moldova."

Third, the impermissible basis for the refusal — the authorities' own view of the protest's merits:
> "The Court finds it unacceptable from the standpoint of Article 11 of the Convention that an interference with the right to freedom of assembly could be justified simply on the basis of the authorities' own view of the merits of a particular protest."

Fourth, regarding the Court of Appeal's additional reason (aim of protest not clearly stated), the Court explicitly disregards it on the following factual/legal basis:
> "sections 11 and 12 of the Assemblies Act give exclusive authority to the local authorities to authorise or not assemblies. The law does not provide, and the Government did not argue the contrary, that other State authorities such as courts were entitled under the Assemblies Act to exercise this duty in their own name or on behalf of the local authorities."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. **No factual grounding can be identified.**
