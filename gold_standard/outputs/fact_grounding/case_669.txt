## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court's assessment is grounded in the following facts:
- "their plans to hold a peaceful public rally"
- "the IDAHO event of 17 May 2013"
- "the peaceful demonstration had been disrupted"

**ii) Participation**

The Court's assessment is grounded in:
- "they had been unable to proceed with their plans to hold a peaceful public rally owing to the hate-motivated assaults on them and the inaction of the police"
- "the applicants' complaints that the State failed to protect their freedom to participate in the protest rally of 17 May 2013"

**iii) Peaceful character**

The Court's assessment is grounded in:
- "their plans to hold a peaceful public rally"
- "the peaceful demonstration had been disrupted"
- "the applicants to proceed with their peaceful event"

Note: The Court treats the peaceful character of the assembly as an uncontested fact. The disruption came from counter-demonstrators, not from the applicants themselves.

---

### (b) Interference (via positive obligation framing)

The Court's assessment is grounded in:
- "they had been unable to proceed with their plans to hold a peaceful public rally owing to the hate-motivated assaults on them and the inaction of the police"
- "the applicants' complaints that the State failed to protect their freedom to participate in the protest rally of 17 May 2013 and protect them from hate-motivated violence"

No further specific facts are quoted in this section of the reasoning — the Court explicitly cross-references its earlier factual findings: "stem from exactly the same factual circumstances as those it has already examined under Article 3 of the Convention taken in conjunction with Article 14." The factual grounding for the interference is thus located in those earlier findings, which are not reproduced in this excerpt.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding identifiable — this element is not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding identifiable — this element is not discussed.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

Not discussed as a formal doctrinal step. However, to the extent that the adequacy of the state's response is assessed within the positive obligation framework, the following facts are explicitly relied upon by the Court:

- "no proper evaluation of the resources necessary for neutralising the serious threat posed to the LGBT demonstrators was conducted by the authorities during the planning phase"
- "Despite being fully aware of the reality and magnitude of the risk, the police, instead of considering more effective measures which could have allowed the applicants to proceed with their peaceful event, limited their role to designing the dispersal plan as the only alternative"
- "Such an attitude suggests that taking measures aimed at enabling the IDAHO event to take place had never been a real priority for the domestic authority"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court's assessment is grounded in the same facts as identified under (e) above, which are the core factual findings supporting the positive obligation analysis:

- "no proper evaluation of the resources necessary for neutralising the serious threat posed to the LGBT demonstrators was conducted by the authorities during the planning phase"
- "Despite being fully aware of the reality and magnitude of the risk, the police, instead of considering more effective measures which could have allowed the applicants to proceed with their peaceful event, limited their role to designing the dispersal plan as the only alternative"
- "Such an attitude suggests that taking measures aimed at enabling the IDAHO event to take place had never been a real priority for the domestic authority"

Additionally, the Court grounds its positive obligation finding in the cross-reference to its earlier Article 3 analysis: "having regard to... its thorough factual and legal findings above under Articles 3 and 14, which are equally pertinent to the complaints relating to freedom of assembly." The specific facts from that earlier analysis are not reproduced in this excerpt and therefore cannot be quoted here.
