## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not definitively resolve this but proceeds on two hypotheses. For the solo demonstration hypothesis, the factual grounding is:

> 'the applicants' submission that both attempted to stage solo demonstrations, which happened to coincide as to the venue (the object being picketed) and the timing.'

And the domestic courts' classification:

> 'The domestic courts considered the situation as a single group event in the form of a static demonstration.'

For the spontaneous assembly hypothesis, the Court does not separately establish the facts of assembly existence — it proceeds conditionally ('should it be accepted as convincingly established').

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds participation in the following facts it treats as established:

> 'In the proceedings against each applicant the courts limited their assessment to establishing that he had taken part in a "picket" held without prior notification...'

And:

> 'The Court observes that the applicants wanted to protest against a draft law prohibiting the adoption of Russian children by US nationals.'

**iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds this explicitly in its own factual observation:

> 'each applicant's expressive conduct was peaceful and non-disruptive.'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds this in the agreed position of both parties:

> 'The parties agreed that the termination of the applicants' participation in the event, their escorting to the police station, their retention there and their administrative conviction constituted an interference with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly and their right to freedom of expression.'

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's limited engagement with this element is grounded in the following factual-legal observation:

> 'The applicants submitted no convincing argument that other elements of the "interference" as described in paragraph 43 above had had no basis in the domestic law.'

On the reclassification rule (touching on safeguards), the factual grounding is:

> 'the PEA which, irrespective of a statutory distance requirement and compliance with it by simultaneous solo demonstrators, empowered courts to classify post factum a situation as a group event (the "reclassification rule" under section 7(1.1) of the PEA) and, by implication, to punish for non-compliance with the prior notification procedure applicable to such event.'

And:

> 'It is noted that there was no specific statutory distance requirement in Moscow prior to January 2013.'

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court mentions statutory purposes in passing but does not make a formal finding on legitimate aim grounded in specific facts. The mention of purposes is framed as a hypothetical context for the necessity analysis, not as a factual conclusion:

> 'the intended statutory purposes (such as affording the authorities an opportunity to take timely and adequate measures to ensure the requisite order for running a given civic initiative and to secure public safety and protection of the rights of the event participants and others) would, normally, be fully attainable through the reasonable application of a distance requirement...'

This is a statement about what purposes *could* be served, not a finding that they *were* being served in this case. No factual grounding for a formal legitimate aim finding can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's necessity assessment is grounded in several distinct factual findings:

**On the solo demonstration limb:**

> 'the Court has no reason to doubt that each applicant was merely standing with a banner at some – apparently, non-negligible – distance from other protesters in so far as it was practicable in the vicinity of the object being picketed, the State Duma.'

> 'no compelling considerations relating to public safety, prevention of disorder or protection of the rights of others were at stake and relied upon when using this factual assertion for convicting each applicant specifically on account of the non-observance of the notification procedure and also when attributing an active role to at least one of them.'

> 'The only relevant consideration was the need to punish the unlawful conduct solely arising from the non-observance of the notification procedure.'

**On the spontaneous assembly limb:**

> 'The date of the parliamentary examination of the draft law was announced two days before, making it impossible for the protesters to comply even with a three-day notification time-limit for "pickets".'

> 'The failure to inform the public sufficiently in advance of the date of the parliamentary examination of the draft law therefore left the protesters with the option of either foregoing their right to peaceful assembly altogether, or of exercising it in defiance of the administrative requirements.'

> 'In the proceedings against each applicant the courts limited their assessment to establishing that he had taken part in a "picket" held without prior notification and omitted to examine whether there had been special circumstances calling for an immediate response to a current event in the form of a spontaneous assembly and justifying derogation from the strict application of the notification time-limits.'

> 'the automatic and inflexible application of the notification time-limits without any regard to the specific circumstances of the present case amounted to an interference which was not justified under Article 11 § 2 of the Convention.'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed in the judgment.
