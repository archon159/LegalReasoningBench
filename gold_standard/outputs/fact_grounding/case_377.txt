## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not make a separate factual finding on this, but the following fact is stated and relied upon throughout:

> 'the applicant planned to organise a demonstration with an anticipated 200 participants'

**ii) Applicant's participation**

No distinct factual finding. The applicant's role as organiser is implied by the framing of the case but the Court makes no explicit factual statement grounding this sub-element separately.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

> 'there is no evidence in the case file to suggest that the demonstration would have been violent or represented a danger to public order'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds this on the undisputed fact of the ban:

> 'the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11; nor did they deny that the ban on the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of his rights under that provision. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise.'

Note: the Court here adopts the Government's own concession as an established fact, rather than making an independent factual finding.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

> 'There was no dispute between the parties that the restriction imposed on the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly was based on the pertinent provisions of Act no. III of 1989 on the Right of Assembly; and, again, the Court sees no reason to hold otherwise. Therefore, the requirement of lawfulness was satisfied.'

Again, the Court adopts the undisputed position of both parties. No further factual analysis is offered for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court's finding is brief and not grounded in specific facts beyond the nature of the measure itself. The Court states:

> 'The Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others.'

No specific facts are cited to ground this conclusion. The Court appears to accept the characterisation of the measure's purpose from the overall context without identifying particular facts.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court draws on the following facts, which it explicitly identifies and relies upon:

**On the nature of the planned event:**
> 'the applicant planned to organise a demonstration with an anticipated 200 participants'

**On the physical characteristics of the location:**
> 'the Court is not convinced by the Government's explanation to the effect that Venyige Street, a road of five or eight metres in width, with a broad service lane adjacent, could not have helped to accommodate the demonstration without serious traffic disruption'

**On the street being a dead end — critically, a fact the Court identifies as having been overlooked by the authorities:**
> 'their arguments appear not to take into account that the street is a dead end; and the through traffic is thus of limited importance. For the Court, however, this fact cannot be overlooked.'

**On the Government's speculation about the neighbouring thoroughfare:**
> 'the Government's contention that the situation could have developed into blocking the major thoroughfare nearby is largely a matter of speculation'

**On the basis of the domestic court's decision:**
> 'in the domestic court decision dealing with the case, the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues'

**On the absence of violence or public order risk (also relevant to necessity):**
> 'there is no evidence in the case file to suggest that the demonstration would have been violent or represented a danger to public order'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
