I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Existence of an assembly:**
The only factual grounding is the Court's own description of the complaints: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**." This is the sole factual basis in the text. The Court does not elaborate further on the nature or circumstances of these assemblies.

**ii) Participation:**
Again, the only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies." No further facts are provided.

**iii) Peaceful character:**
There is **no factual grounding** in the reasoning text. The Court does not mention any facts relating to whether the assemblies were peaceful or violent. This element is implied purely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for the implied finding of interference is: "their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**." These are the only concrete acts identified in the text. The Court does not explicitly adopt these as established facts with a formal statement of acceptance, but it proceeds on their basis without qualification, and uses the word "interferences" in its legal assessment, which maps directly back to these described acts.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only element explicitly discussed, yet it has **remarkably thin factual grounding** in the reasoning text itself. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a reference to an unspecified body of material, none of which is quoted or summarized in this section.

The only concrete facts that can be identified as grounding the necessity assessment are, again: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" — i.e., the nature of the governmental acts complained of. No further facts (e.g., the manner of dispersal, the proportionality of the sentences, the conduct of authorities) are stated in this section of the reasoning.

The Court's necessity finding is primarily grounded not in stated facts but in a reference to prior case-law: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**." The factual similarity to those cases is asserted but not demonstrated within this text.

---

**Summary observation:**
The reasoning section is extremely sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. The Court relies almost entirely on a reference to prior leading cases and a general statement that it has examined "all the material submitted," without setting out the specific facts that underpin its conclusions on any of the canonical elements. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case procedure (likely a pilot judgment or clone case approach), where the Court treats the factual analysis as already done in the leading cases.
