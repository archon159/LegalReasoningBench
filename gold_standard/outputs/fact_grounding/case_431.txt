**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope**

The only factual grounding for the implied scope analysis comes from the opening paragraph:

> "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**, namely their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

This single passage provides the factual basis for all three implied scope sub-elements:
- That assemblies existed ("public assemblies")
- That the applicants participated ("organisers or participants")
- That the assemblies were peaceful (implied only — there is no explicit factual statement about the peaceful character of the assemblies; the Court simply does not say otherwise)

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for interference is the same passage:

> "their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These are the specific governmental acts identified as constituting the interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss this element at all.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss this element at all.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity assessment is notably thin in factual grounding. The only passage that could be described as factual grounding is again:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

Beyond this, the Court's reasoning on necessity is entirely doctrinal and comparative, not fact-based:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..."

The Court does not identify any specific facts about the circumstances of the dispersals, the conduct of the applicants, or the reasons given by the authorities. The necessity conclusion rests on the analogy with prior cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) rather than on any independently stated facts.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss this element at all.
