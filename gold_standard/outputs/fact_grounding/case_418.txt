**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope**

The only factual grounding comes from the Court's own characterization in the opening paragraph:

> "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This single sentence provides the factual basis for all three implied scope sub-elements:
- **Assembly exists**: "public assemblies" / "these assemblies"
- **Participation**: "organisers or participants of public assemblies"
- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. Peacefulness is implied only structurally (by the Court proceeding to analyze the case under Article 11), not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most striking finding: **there is no independent factual grounding in the reasoning text.** The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

And:

> "Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely by reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov, Oya Ataman, Hyde Park), not by any specific facts about what happened in the instant case. The Court does not identify any particular facts about the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, or the nature of the convictions that drive the necessity finding. The reference to "all the material submitted" confirms the Court reviewed facts, but none are quoted, described, or adopted in the reasoning text itself.

---

**Summary observation:**

The reasoning text is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. Across all elements, the only facts explicitly stated by the Court are: that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, that the assemblies were dispersed, that the applicants were arrested, and that they were convicted of administrative offences. All substantive legal conclusions — particularly on necessity — rest on analogy to prior cases rather than on facts found and stated in this decision.
