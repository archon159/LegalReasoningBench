**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following passage from the opening paragraph:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

This is the Court's own characterisation of the complaints, not merely a party submission. It establishes that there were assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants. However, it should be noted that the Court does not explicitly adopt this as a factual finding — it is framing the complaint. No facts are stated by the Court regarding the peaceful nature of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

The Court identifies two concrete governmental acts as constituting the interference: (1) arrest in connection with the dispersal of the assemblies, and (2) conviction for administrative offences. These are the only facts the Court identifies as grounding the interference finding.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most explicitly discussed, yet it is striking that **no specific facts** from the instant case are cited by the Court to ground its necessity assessment. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

And:

> "Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The necessity finding is grounded entirely by reference to prior case-law (Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, Kasparov and Others v. Russia) and the absence of any distinguishing facts or arguments, rather than by any affirmative factual findings about the circumstances of the instant case. No specific facts from the case at hand are quoted or adopted by the Court in support of this conclusion.

---

**(c), (d), (f)**

As noted previously, these elements are not discussed. Accordingly, no factual grounding can be identified for them in this text.
