## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court does not explicitly ground this in a factual finding. The nature of the event is described incidentally in the context of other elements (see below under necessity), but there is no discrete factual finding that "this is an assembly." No quotable factual grounding can be identified for this element specifically.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?**
No discrete factual finding. The Court's grounding is purely procedural — the applicants are described as having submitted an application — but the Court does not make an explicit factual finding about participation as such. No quotable factual grounding can be identified.

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court grounds this in the following factual finding:
> "Moreover, there was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities or other groups meeting in the park on the day in question. Rather their intention was to hold a peaceful rally in support of freedom of speech."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference in the following fact:
> "the decision to reject Hyde Park's application to hold a demonstration on 14 October 2005 amounted to 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11."

The factual act constituting the interference is thus the **rejection of the application**, which is further grounded in:
> "the Municipality rejected Hyde Park's application to hold a protest demonstration planned for 14 October 2005 on the grounds that other events were scheduled to take place in the Stefan cel Mare Park."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its discussion of this element in the following facts:

On what the law required:
> "under section 14 of the Assemblies Act the Chișinău Municipality was obliged to give reasons in writing for rejecting Hyde Park's application to hold an assembly, which it did in its decision of 7 October 2005."

> "According to section 12(6) of the Assemblies Act, an application could be rejected only if the Municipality was in possession of evidence that the provisions of sections 6 and 7 would be breached with serious consequences for society."

On what the Municipality actually did:
> "The Municipality's decision appears not to have relied on any of the reasons provided for in sections 6 and 7 of the Assemblies Act."

On the subsequent judicial proceedings:
> "It is true that new reasons for rejecting Hyde Park's application to hold an assembly were given by the courts during the subsequent judicial proceedings. However, sections 11 and 12 of the Assemblies Act give exclusive authority to the local authorities to authorise or not assemblies. The law does not provide, and the Government did not argue the contrary, that other State authorities such as the courts were entitled under the Assemblies Act to exercise this duty in their own name or on behalf of the local authorities. Moreover, the Court can but note that those reasons were adopted in decisions given by the courts long after the date planned for the demonstrations."

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declines to make a factual or legal determination on this element: "The Court, for the reasons set out below, does not consider it necessary to decide this point either." No factual grounding is provided, and none can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its assessment on the following facts:

On the reason given by the Municipality for rejection:
> "the Municipality rejected Hyde Park's application to hold a protest demonstration planned for 14 October 2005 on the grounds that other events were scheduled to take place in the Stefan cel Mare Park."

On the absence of capacity concerns:
> "There was no suggestion that the park in which the assembly was to take place was too small to accommodate all the various events planned there."

On the absence of public order concerns:
> "Moreover, there was never any suggestion that the organisers intended to disrupt public order or to seek a confrontation with the authorities or other groups meeting in the park on the day in question. Rather their intention was to hold a peaceful rally in support of freedom of speech."

On the inconsistency of the Municipality's reasons with the Assemblies Act:
> "The Court noted above that such a reason appears to be inconsistent with the requirements of the Assemblies Act which, in its sections 6 and 7, sets out the grounds on which an application to hold an assembly can be rejected by a Municipality."

On the disregarding of the courts' reasons:
> "those reasons were adopted in decisions given by the courts long after the date planned for the demonstrations. For that reason the Court considers that the judicial proceedings following the Municipality's decision rejecting Hyde Park's application for holding an assembly and the reasons given by the courts for upholding that decision must be disregarded."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or can be identified.
