# Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

## (a) Scope

### i) Has there been an assembly?

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

- "the applicants intended to take part in future demonstrations against the G8 summit"
- "a number of mass demonstrations were scheduled to take place over several days, some of which had descended into riots in Rostock city centre prior to the applicants' arrest"
- "the summit was expected to attract a significant number of demonstrators (some 25,000), a large majority of whom were peaceful, but a considerable number of whom were prepared to use violence"

---

### ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?

- "The Court notes that at the time of their arrest, the applicants intended to take part in future demonstrations against the G8 summit."
- "as a result of their detention, ordered by the domestic courts for the entire duration of the G8 summit, the applicants were prevented from taking part in demonstrations against that summit"

---

### iii) Was the assembly peaceful?

The Court grounds its finding of peacefulness on the following facts:

**Regarding the demonstrations generally:**
- "There is nothing to indicate that the organisers of the demonstrations in which the applicants intended to participate had violent intentions."
- "the fact that the police also expected extremists with violent intentions to join the otherwise peaceful demonstrations does not result in those demonstrations losing the protection of Article 11 § 1"

**Regarding the applicants' own intentions:**
- "the domestic courts did not consider that the applicants, by carrying banners bearing the inscriptions 'Freedom for all prisoners' and 'Free all now', intended to liberate prisoners by force themselves"
- "no weapons were found on the applicants"
- "that court conceded that the slogans on the banners at issue in the present case could be understood in different ways"
- "The applicants gave a plausible interpretation of the inscriptions on their banners, which themselves clearly did not openly advocate violence." (Note: the Court here adopts the applicants' explanation as plausible, grounding it in the domestic court's own finding of ambivalence: "Having regard also to the domestic court's finding of the slogans' ambivalent content allowing for different interpretations, the Court considers that it has not been proved that the applicants deliberately intended to incite others to violence.")
- "neither of the applicants was shown to have previous convictions for violent conduct during demonstrations or in comparable situations"
- "neither could such a conclusion, in the Court's view, be drawn from the fact that one of the applicants was considered to have resisted the police's identity check by force and thus to have used force himself – in different circumstances and in a different manner from the display of banners to others at a demonstration"

---

## (b) Interference

- "as a result of their detention, ordered by the domestic courts for the entire duration of the G8 summit, the applicants were prevented from taking part in demonstrations against that summit"
- "The applicants' detention thus interfered with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly under Article 11 § 1. This is uncontested between the parties."

---

## (c) Prescribed by law

The Court leaves this open and makes no factual findings on it. It states only: "It observes that it is contested between the parties whether the applicants' detention was prescribed by a law – section 55(1), paragraph 2, of the PSOA – which was sufficiently precise to be foreseeable in its application in the circumstances of the applicants' case. The Court considers that it can leave that question open and examine the case on the assumption that the interference was 'prescribed by law' for the reasons which follow."

**No factual grounding can be identified**, because the Court explicitly declines to make findings on this element.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds its finding of a legitimate aim on the following fact:

- "The Court is satisfied that the aim of the authorities in ordering the applicants' detention was to prevent them from committing a crime, namely inciting others to liberate prisoners by force. This aim as such is legitimate under Article 11 § 2."

The factual basis for identifying this as the authorities' aim is not independently elaborated by the Court beyond this statement. The Court does not cite specific evidence for what the authorities' aim was; it appears to accept the characterisation of the aim as stated by the Government and reflected in the domestic court decisions, but does not quote specific facts establishing this independently.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its proportionality assessment on a rich set of facts:

**On the nature and weight of the applicants' assembly rights:**
- "the applicants, by taking part in the demonstrations against the G8 summit, intended to participate in a debate on matters of public interest, namely the effects of globalisation on people's lives"
- "through the slogans on their banners, they intended to criticise the police's management in securing the summit, in particular the high number of detentions of demonstrators"
- "a considerable number of demonstrators (more than 1,000 of the 25,000 demonstrators expected) were temporarily detained during the course of the summit"
- "the slogans contributed to a debate on a question of public interest"

**On the severity of the interference:**
- "the applicants were detained for almost six days in order to prevent them from inciting others to liberate prisoners by force during demonstrations against the G8 summit"
- "depriving the applicants of their liberty for several days for having intended to display the impugned banners had a chilling effect on the expression of such an opinion and restricted the public debate on that issue"
- The Court also cross-references its Article 5 finding: "the applicants' detention for preventive purposes did not fall within any of the permissible grounds for deprivation of liberty under Article 5 § 1 and was thus in breach of that provision"

**On the degree of risk posed by the applicants:**
- "the Court cannot consider it established that the applicants had intended, by displaying the banners bearing the impugned inscriptions at the demonstrations, to deliberately stir up other demonstrators prepared to use violence to liberate by force prisoners taken during the G8 summit"
- "it appears, on the contrary, an acceptable assessment of the relevant facts by the authorities, having regard to their margin of appreciation, that the slogans could be considered ambiguous and that the applicants could thus have negligently incited others to violence by displaying the slogans during certain demonstrations"

**On the availability of less intrusive measures:**
- "in the given situation, in which it has not been shown that the applicants were aware that the police considered the slogans on their banners illegal, it would have been sufficient to seize the banners in question"
- "This could reasonably be expected to have had a chilling effect on the applicants, preventing them from drawing up new, comparable banners immediately."
- "Even if their freedom of expression would then have been restricted to a certain extent, their taking part in the demonstrations would not have been made impossible from the very outset."

**On the general security context (accepted as relevant but insufficient to justify the measure):**
- "the summit was expected to attract a significant number of demonstrators (some 25,000), a large majority of whom were peaceful, but a considerable number of whom were prepared to use violence"
- "A number of mass demonstrations were scheduled to take place over several days, some of which had descended into riots in Rostock city centre prior to the applicants' arrest"
- "guaranteeing the security of the participants in the summit and maintaining public order in general in this situation was a considerable challenge for the domestic authorities, where decisions often had to be taken speedily" — the Court accepts this as context but does not treat it as sufficient to justify the measure.
