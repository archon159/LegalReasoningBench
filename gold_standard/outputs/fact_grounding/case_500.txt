## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court's implicit acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in:
> "the applicant and others clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia."

**(ii) Participation**
The Court's implicit acceptance that the applicant participated is grounded in:
> "The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

**(iii) Peaceful character**
The Court's finding that the demonstration had a violent character is grounded in several factual findings it explicitly adopts:

First, the Court adopts the Commission's earlier findings:
> "the Court notes that in the case of Chrysostomos and Papachrysostomou, the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus. The Court sees no reason to depart from these findings, which were based on the UN Secretary General's report, on a video film and on photographs submitted by the respondent Government before the Commission."

Second, the Court explicitly adopts the UN Secretary General's factual account:
> "It emphasises that in his report, the UN Secretary General stated that the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."

Third, the Court accepts witness testimony from trial:
> "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in:
> "the applicant and others clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's conclusion that the interference was prescribed by law is grounded in the identification of specific legal provisions:
> "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 27-28 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law (see paragraph 31 above)."

And on the general principle regarding "TRNC" laws:
> "when, as in the Foka case, an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law for the purposes of the Convention."

The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards have no identifiable factual grounding in the text — the Court does not examine these at all.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not explicitly state which legitimate aim it finds established. It notes the Government's submission of aims but does not separately declare one proven. The factual grounding for the implicit acceptance of legitimate aims (public safety, prevention of disorder and crime) is the same violent conduct relied upon under (a)(iii) and (e):

> "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."

> "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post'..."

> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

Beyond these facts (which the Court adopts), no separate factual grounding is provided specifically for the legitimate aim element.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's proportionality conclusion is grounded in the following adopted facts:

The violent character of the demonstration and border violation:
> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

The demonstrators' entry into the UN buffer zone:
> "the demonstrators had forced their way into the UN buffer zone."

The entry into "TRNC" territory:
> "According to the 'TRNC' authorities, they also entered 'TRNC' territory, thus committing offences punished by the 'TRNC' laws."

And the Court's acceptance of witness testimony on this point:
> "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory."

The Court also grounds its conclusion in the legal principle of wide margin of appreciation:
> "having regard to the wide margin of appreciation left to the States in this sphere."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions the positive obligation in purely abstract, doctrinal terms:
> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens. However, they cannot guarantee this absolutely and they have a wide discretion in the choice of the means to be used."

There is no specific factual grounding provided for this element beyond its use as a general principle framing the necessity analysis. The Court does not apply it to specific facts as a standalone finding.
