**Factual Grounding for Each Discussed or Implied Element**

**(a) Scope**

- *Assembly exists / Participation*: The only factual grounding in the text is the Court's own characterisation in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."

  This is thin factual grounding — the Court names the events (dispersal, arrest, conviction) and the applicants' roles (organisers or participants), but provides no further factual detail about the assemblies themselves.

- *Peaceful character*: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court states no facts from which peacefulness could be inferred. It is implied solely by the legal trajectory of the reasoning (the Court proceeds to a necessity analysis without any suggestion that Article 11's protection was unavailable).

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence." These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet it has **no independent factual grounding in the reasoning text**. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a reference to an unspecified body of material, but none of that material is quoted, summarised, or described. The necessity finding rests entirely on the reference to prior case law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) involving "issues similar to those in the present case" — but what those similar issues are is not spelled out. The factual basis for the necessity assessment is therefore not visible in this excerpt of the reasoning.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**Overall observation:** The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual content. The Court relies almost entirely on a reference to leading cases and a general assertion that it has examined "all the material submitted to it," without setting out the specific facts that ground its conclusions on any of the canonical elements. The only concrete facts mentioned are the dispersal of assemblies, arrest, and conviction for administrative offence — and these appear only in the introductory characterisation of the complaints.
