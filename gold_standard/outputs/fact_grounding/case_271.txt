**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope — implied**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the introductory paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the subject matter of the complaints. It is thin as factual grounding — the Court does not independently verify or describe the assemblies in any detail — but it is the only basis in the text from which the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation can be inferred.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies. The peacefulness of the assemblies is implied structurally (by the Court proceeding to full Article 11 analysis) but is not grounded in any fact stated in this reasoning section.

**(b) Interference — explicitly discussed**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in this reasoning section.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society — explicitly discussed**

No specific facts are identified in this reasoning section to ground the necessity assessment. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a reference to an unspecified body of material, but no particular facts from that material are quoted, described, or adopted in the text. The necessity finding rests entirely on the reference to prior leading cases (*Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, *Kasparov and Others*) rather than on any facts stated in this section.

**Overall observation:**

The reasoning section is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. The Court relies almost entirely on the precedent established in the three named leading cases and on a general reference to "all the material submitted to it," without articulating which specific facts drive its conclusions on any of the canonical elements. The only concrete facts stated are the arrest, dispersal, and administrative conviction of the applicants.
