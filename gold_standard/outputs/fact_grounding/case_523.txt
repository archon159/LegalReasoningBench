**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following description of the applicants and the events:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

and

> "organisers or participants of public assemblies"

These phrases establish, at a minimum, that assemblies took place and that the applicants were organisers or participants. No further facts are provided in the reasoning text to ground the peacefulness of the assemblies. The Court does not quote or adopt any specific factual submissions on this point.

---

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding for the finding of interference is:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

These are the concrete governmental acts identified in the text as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is the central finding of the Court, but notably the reasoning text provides **no specific facts** to ground the necessity assessment. The Court's conclusion rests entirely on a reference to prior case-law:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The Court references "all the material submitted to it" but does not identify or quote any specific facts from that material. The necessity finding is grounded in the comparison to leading cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov) rather than in any independently stated facts about the proportionality of the measures in the instant case.
