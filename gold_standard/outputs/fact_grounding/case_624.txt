# Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

## (a) Scope

### i) Assembly exists
The Court does not separately establish this as a fact. The existence of planned and attempted meetings is treated as given throughout. No specific factual passage is dedicated to establishing that these were "assemblies" in the legal sense. **No explicit factual grounding identifiable.**

### ii) Participation (attempted)
The Court grounds this in the following finding:

> "These measures doubtless had an inhibiting effect on at least some of the members and followers of Ilinden who intended to participate in the events, and may thus be deemed interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly."

And:

> "the police did not fully prevent the members and followers of Ilinden to take part in the planned meetings"

And:

> "the police prevented the members and followers of Ilinden from holding the meeting at their chosen site. They were diverted to a different location"

### iii) Peaceful character
The Court grounds this in two factual findings:

First, the Government's concession:
> "the Government have not disputed the peaceful character of the meetings organised by Ilinden"

Second, the Court's own finding carried over from prior proceedings and applied to the present case:
> "in that previous case it found that those involved in the organisation of the prohibited meetings – of which the meetings at issue in the present case are a continuation – did not have violent intentions"

And its own assessment of the present case:
> "the Court finds no indication that the organisers of and the participants in the meetings under consideration in instant case were contemplating resorting to violence"

---

## (b) Interference

The Court grounds its interference findings in the following specific facts:

On the formal bans and their inhibiting effect:
> "before that the mayors had imposed formal bans on these meetings. These measures doubtless had an inhibiting effect on at least some of the members and followers of Ilinden who intended to participate in the events"

On the seizure of materials:
> "during the first meeting the police seized the decoration for the stage and certain written materials which members of the association had brought to the meeting"

On the diversion to another location:
> "there is no doubt that the police prevented the members and followers of Ilinden from holding the meeting at their chosen site. They were diverted to a different location. Bearing in mind that the time and place of the events were apparently crucial to them... the Court considers that this amounted to an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly"

On the hostile group incident:
> "the members and followers of Ilinden were not prevented by the police from approaching the chosen site, but their meeting was hampered by a hostile group. It seems that the police took certain steps to allow the members and followers of Ilinden to hold the meeting, but it is doubtful whether the authorities took all reasonable and appropriate measures to enable the demonstration to proceed peacefully"

On the April 2001 events, the Court explicitly declines to make a factual finding:
> "On the basis of the available evidence, the Court cannot come to the categorical conclusion that in this case there has been an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly"

On August 2003:
> "it seems that in August 2003 Ilinden was able to hold its meeting without hindrances from the authorities"

---

## (c) Prescribed by law

### i) Foreseeability
The Court grounds its concern — but ultimate acceptance — on the following factual-legal observation:

> "the provision authorising such measures, former Article 185 § 1 of the CCP, was formulated extremely vaguely and did not circumscribe the situations where the prosecution authorities could act"

And the qualified acceptance:
> "the Court is prepared to accept, for the purposes of the present case, that the interferences in these instances were also prescribed by law"

### ii) Accessibility
**No explicit factual grounding identifiable.** The Court accepts the law as prescribed by law without separately addressing accessibility.

### iii) Safeguards
The Court grounds its concern in:
> "former Article 185 § 1 of the CCP, was formulated extremely vaguely and did not circumscribe the situations where the prosecution authorities could act"

And adds a normative statement about how such powers should be used:
> "Such a power must however be used sparingly and only when indeed warranted"

But the Court does not make a finding of violation on this basis. **The factual grounding identifies a problem but the Court does not resolve it as a standalone finding.**

For the mayoral bans, the Court grounds acceptance on:
> "in most cases the mayors or the courts also relied on other reasons for banning the meetings or for upholding the bans"

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the Government's stated aims without independently verifying them against specific facts. The Court states:

> "The Court accepts that the bans were intended to safeguard one or more of the interests cited by the Government, as it did in Stankov and the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden"

**No independent factual grounding is provided by the Court beyond this acceptance.** The aims themselves were stated by the Government (national security, territorial integrity, public order, rights of others, prevention of disorder and crime), and the Court accepts them without grounding them in specific established facts.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of disproportionality in the following facts:

On the persistence of the practice:
> "the authorities persisted in their efforts to impede the holding of the commemorative events which Ilinden sought to organise, much as they had during the period 1994-97, when they had 'adopted the practice of imposing sweeping bans on Ilinden's meetings'"

On the unchanged justification:
> "the authorities' justification for so doing was substantially the same as in Stankov and the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden and thus insufficient to make the impugned measures necessary in a democratic society"

On the ban imposed after the Strasbourg judgment:
> "one of the bans was imposed, with almost identical reasoning, even after similar measures had been declared contrary to Article 11 in the Court's judgment in Stankov and the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden"

---

## (f) Positive obligation

The Court grounds its finding of a violation of the positive obligation in the following facts:

On what actually happened:
> "on one of the occasions when they did not interfere with the applicants' freedom of assembly, the authorities appeared somewhat reluctant to protect the members and followers of Ilinden from a group of counter-demonstrators. As a result, some of the participants in Ilinden's rally were subjected to physical violence from their opponents"

On the inadequacy of the steps taken:
> "it seems that they, while embarking on certain steps to enable the organisation's commemorative event to proceed peacefully, did not take all the appropriate measures which could have reasonably be expected from them under the circumstances, and thus failed to discharge their positive obligations under Article 11"

The Court also grounds the positive obligation itself in the prior finding about the hostile group:
> "the members and followers of Ilinden were not prevented by the police from approaching the chosen site, but their meeting was hampered by a hostile group. It seems that the police took certain steps to allow the members and followers of Ilinden to hold the meeting, but it is doubtful whether the authorities took all reasonable and appropriate measures to enable the demonstration to proceed peacefully"
