Here is the analysis of factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
Note: This is drawn from the applicants' complaints. The Court does not independently state as an established fact that assemblies took place — it merely recites the complaints. There is no passage where the Court explicitly adopts this as a finding of fact.

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." The same caveat applies — this is framed as the applicants' complaint, not an independent factual finding by the Court.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. Peacefulness is entirely implied by the legal conclusion, not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
Again, this is presented as the substance of the applicants' complaints. The Court does not separately state these as independently verified facts, though by proceeding to find a violation it implicitly accepts them.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited by the Court in support of its necessity finding. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a purely formal statement that does not identify any concrete facts. The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely in reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others, Oya Ataman, Hyde Park), not in any facts quoted or adopted from the record.

---

**Summary observation:**
The reasoning is strikingly thin on factual grounding throughout. The Court relies almost entirely on precedent and does not explicitly adopt any facts as independently established. The only factual content in the text comes from the recitation of the applicants' complaints in the opening paragraph, and even there the Court does not formally confirm these as its own findings of fact.
