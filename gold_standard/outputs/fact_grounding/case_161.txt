Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element the Court addressed:

---

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following description from the opening paragraph:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**, namely their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

This is the Court's own framing of the situation, not merely a party submission. It establishes the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation as organisers or participants. There is no factual grounding in the text for the peacefulness of the assemblies — this sub-element is implied but entirely without explicit factual support in the reasoning section.

---

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "namely their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

These are the concrete governmental acts the Court treats as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning section.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law (not discussed)**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is absent from the reasoning.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim (not discussed)**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is absent from the reasoning.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is strikingly thin. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

And:

> "Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The Court does not quote or identify any specific facts from the case record to ground its necessity assessment. It relies instead on the absence of distinguishing facts or arguments, and on the analogy with prior leading cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others). No concrete case-specific facts — such as the conduct of authorities, the nature of the dispersal, or the proportionality of the sanctions — are cited in the reasoning text itself.

---

**(f) Positive obligation (not discussed)**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is absent from the reasoning.
