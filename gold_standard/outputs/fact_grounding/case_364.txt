I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the following statement from the opening paragraph: "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**." This is the Court's own characterisation of the applicants' situation, not merely a party submission. No further factual detail is provided.

- **Peaceful:** No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the nature or conduct of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is found in the opening paragraph: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the specific governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most striking finding: **there is no factual grounding in the reasoning text.** The Court does not cite any specific facts about the circumstances of the dispersals, the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, or the nature of the convictions. Instead, the Court grounds its necessity finding entirely on its prior case-law:

- "In the leading cases of *Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, and *Kasparov and Others v. Russia*, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."
- "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."

The necessity conclusion is thus grounded in the similarity to prior cases, not in any facts from the present case that are quoted or described in the reasoning text.
