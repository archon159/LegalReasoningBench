This is an important question that requires careful attention to what the Court actually established as fact, versus what it merely asserted as a legal conclusion. Let me work through each element.

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists / Applicant participated:**
The only factual grounding is the introductory sentence: "The applicant complained about the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of a public event."

This establishes that there was a public event and that the applicant was connected to it. However, this is framed as the applicant's complaint, not as a Court finding of fact. The Court does not separately and explicitly establish as fact that an assembly took place or that the applicant participated. The factual grounding is therefore thin — it is more of a procedural framing than a substantive factual finding.

**Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the nature or conduct of the assembly.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is again the introductory sentence: "The applicant complained about the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of a public event."

The Court then adopts this as its own finding when it states "the interferences with the applicant's freedom of assembly," treating the existence of restrictions as established. However, the Court provides no further factual detail about what the restrictions actually were, who imposed them, when, or how. The factual grounding is present but extremely sparse.
