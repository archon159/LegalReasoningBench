**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the description of the complaints: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This is the Court's own characterisation of the subject matter, not a party submission. It establishes that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants.

- **Peaceful:** No factual grounding. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. Peacefulness is implied by the Court's proceeding to the interference and necessity analysis, but no facts are cited to support this inference.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the existence of interference in the following facts, stated in its own description of the complaints:

"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the only concrete facts cited. The Court does not elaborate further on the circumstances of the arrests or convictions.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, but it is notably thin on specific factual grounding. The Court states:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is the closest the Court comes to grounding its necessity finding in the facts of the case, but it is entirely generic — it references "all the material" without identifying any specific facts. The necessity conclusion is instead grounded primarily in precedent:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And by reference to the leading cases: "Frumkin v. Russia... Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia... and Kasparov and Others v. Russia... the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

So the necessity finding is grounded in legal precedent rather than in any specific facts of the instant case that the Court identifies and discusses.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed.
