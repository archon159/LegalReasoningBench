**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the Court's own description in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

  This sentence simultaneously grounds the existence of assemblies, the applicants' roles as organisers or participants, and the fact that dispersal and arrest occurred. No further factual detail is provided by the Court.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies. The peacefulness of the assemblies is implied structurally by the Court's proceeding to assess necessity, but no facts are cited to support this inference.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same sentence as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual elaboration is provided.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is strikingly thin. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a negative formulation — the Court does not cite any specific facts from the case to ground its necessity assessment. Instead, it grounds the conclusion entirely in its prior case-law: "Having regard to its case-law on the subject" and by reference to the leading cases of *Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, and *Kasparov and Others v. Russia*, described as cases where "the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

No specific facts about the proportionality of the arrests, the conduct of authorities, the nature of the dispersal, or the severity of the convictions are cited by the Court in support of the necessity finding.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**Overall observation:** The factual grounding across all elements is remarkably sparse. The Court relies almost entirely on a single descriptive sentence in the opening paragraph and on cross-reference to prior judgments, without engaging in any case-specific factual analysis. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case procedure where the Court treats the matter as effectively resolved by precedent.
