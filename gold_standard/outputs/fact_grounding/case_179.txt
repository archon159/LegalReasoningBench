Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element identified as either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterisation in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This is the sole factual basis the Court provides. No further detail about the nature, location, size, or purpose of the assemblies is given.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the conduct of the assemblies or the behaviour of participants. The peaceful character is implied structurally by the Court's willingness to proceed with the analysis, but it is not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is notably thin. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints." This is the only reference to the factual record, and it is entirely negative in form — the Court does not identify specific facts that ground the necessity finding, but rather states that nothing in the material departs from what was already established in the leading cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others). The factual work is thus delegated entirely to those prior judgments, which are not quoted or summarised here.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**Overall observation**: The reasoning section is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. The Court relies almost entirely on a reference to prior leading cases and a negative statement that no new material alters the conclusion. The only facts explicitly stated are the arrest, dispersal, and administrative conviction of the applicants. All other factual matters — the nature of the assemblies, their peacefulness, the legal basis for the interference, the aim pursued — are either absent or subsumed silently into the reference to prior case-law.
