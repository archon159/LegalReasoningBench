## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court's finding that an assembly existed is grounded in the following facts:

> "irrespective of whether the meeting in the present case was to be characterised as private or public it constituted an 'assembly'"

The Court does not elaborate further on the specific facts of the meeting's nature beyond this characterisation. The description of the gathering as "small-scale informal" appears later in the interference analysis:

> "the applicants could not proceed with their small-scale informal gathering"

**(ii) Participation**

No explicit factual grounding is provided by the Court for this element. It is simply assumed from the framing of the case. No verbatim passage can be quoted in which the Court establishes the applicants' participation as a distinct factual finding.

**(iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds the peaceful character on two facts:

First, in the scope analysis:
> "being undeniably peaceful it fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention"

Second, reinforced in the prescribed-by-law analysis:
> "Clearly, the applicants' meeting was not causing any disturbance, and even if the police were obliged to follow-up on an alert about an unreported event, they had no reason to remain in the meeting room after having satisfied themselves of the peaceful character of the gathering"

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on the following facts:

> "the applicants could not proceed with their small-scale informal gathering in the presence of the large number of police officers who were accusing them of a failure to notify the authorities of their meeting and who remained in the room until the participants of the meeting left it."

> "It is clear that the meeting was brought to an end prematurely by the authorities' intervention"

The Court also draws on an analogy with an earlier case it had decided, but that is a legal rather than factual grounding.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**(i) Foreseeable**

The Court grounds the lack of foreseeability on the following facts:

First, that the legal grounds originally invoked were subsequently invalidated:
> "The legal grounds put forward on the spot and in the ensuing administrative proceedings – a failure to give notice under the procedure applicable to public events – have been subsequently refuted by the court conducting the supervisory review which found that no such requirement was applicable to the gathering at hand"

Second, regarding the alternative legal basis (the municipal instruction):
> "In the subsequent civil proceedings reference was made to an instruction issued by the Novorossiysk municipality, in accordance with which school directors had to notify the police of any events taking place on school premises, as a measure to combat terrorism."

> "The Government did not rely on that instruction in their observations, and its text was not submitted to the Court."

Third, the Court's own doubt about the scope of that instruction:
> "The Court doubts that a municipal instruction to school directors could give the police the power to disrupt a gathering on school premises on the sole grounds that the authorities had not been notified of it as an 'event'. Moreover, no such argument was put forward in the present case."

**(ii) Accessible**

The factual grounding overlaps substantially with foreseeability. The key fact is:

> "The Government did not rely on that instruction in their observations, and its text was not submitted to the Court."

And the Court's overall conclusion:
> "The Government did not submit that the interference with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly was based on a legal provision that was accessible and foreseeable in its application."

**(iii) Safeguards against abuse**

The Court does not identify specific facts grounding this sub-element separately. The factual grounding is the same as for foreseeability and accessibility — namely, the absence of any identifiable legal basis with clear scope. No distinct verbatim passage can be quoted that grounds this sub-element in specific facts beyond those already quoted above.
