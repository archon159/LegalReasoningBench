**Preliminary note on methodology**

The reasoning text is notably sparse on facts. The Court does not narrate the events, does not describe the circumstances of the assemblies, and does not quote or adopt party submissions as established facts. It proceeds almost entirely by reference to legal conclusions and prior case-law. This significantly limits the factual grounding that can be identified for each element.

---

**(a) Scope — implied**

The only factual grounding available is the following description of what happened to the applicants:

> "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."

This single sentence provides the factual basis for all three implied scope elements:
- **Assembly exists**: "public assemblies" / "dispersal of these assemblies"
- **Participation**: "participants of public assemblies" / "measures taken against them"
- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding can be identified. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies. Peacefulness is implied structurally (by the Court proceeding with the analysis) but is not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference — explicitly discussed**

The factual grounding is the same sentence:

> "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."

The Court identifies three concrete acts as constituting interference:
1. Dispersal of the assemblies
2. Arrest of the applicants
3. Conviction for administrative offence

No further factual detail is provided about any of these acts — no dates, locations, legal provisions invoked by authorities, or descriptions of how the dispersal or arrest was carried out.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society — explicitly discussed**

This is the central legal conclusion, but it is strikingly without independent factual grounding in the reasoning text. The Court does not identify any specific facts about the circumstances of the assemblies, the conduct of the authorities, or the proportionality of the measures that would ground its necessity assessment. Instead, it grounds the conclusion entirely in:

1. Reference to prior case-law:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts); Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

2. A general statement about the material examined:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The necessity finding is therefore not grounded in any facts explicitly stated in this reasoning text. The Court treats the similarity to prior cases as sufficient, without restating the underlying facts.

---

**(f) Positive obligation — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify.
