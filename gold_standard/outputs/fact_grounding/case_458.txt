## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
No explicit factual discussion. The Court treats the existence of an assembly as given, referring to 'the march' and 'the protest organised by Mr Navalnyy' without grounding this in stated facts. No quotable factual finding.

**ii) Participation**
> 'Mr Navalnyy was arrested while walking peacefully with other participants in the march.'

This is the sole factual statement grounding his participation.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**
> 'Mr Navalnyy was arrested while walking peacefully with other participants in the march. Nothing suggests that he had shown any violent intentions or had called on the participants to engage in the acts of aggression.'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court notes that interference was 'not disputed by the parties,' and accepts this as established:
> 'It has not been disputed by the parties that Mr Navalnyy's arrest during the march and ensuing conviction for administrative offences constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly.'

The factual grounding is therefore:
- the arrest during the march
- the ensuing conviction for administrative offences

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court states the interference 'was based on the relevant provisions of the domestic law' with a cross-reference to Lashmankin. No independent factual findings are made in this passage to ground the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Court does not quote or describe the specific provisions. **No quotable factual grounding beyond the bare assertion and cross-reference.**

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court states the aims pursued without grounding them in specific facts of this case:
> 'That interference...pursued the legitimate aims of prevention of disorder and protection of the rights and freedoms of others.'

The only fact that comes close to grounding the aim of preventing disorder and protecting others' rights is the authorities' stated reason for refusing to approve the march location, which the Court records:
> 'The authorities refused to approve the location of the march in Moscow referring to the risks of disrupting traffic and breaching rights of others.'

However, the Court does not explicitly adopt this as the factual basis for the legitimate aim finding — it records it as the authorities' submission and then immediately proceeds to qualify its weight. **The legitimate aim finding is therefore largely conclusory, with only this partial factual anchor.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The following facts are explicitly stated by the Court:

On the subject matter of the protest:
> 'The protest organised by Mr Navalnyy concerned a matter of public interest, notably fight against corruption.'

On the authorities' reason for refusal:
> 'The authorities refused to approve the location of the march in Moscow referring to the risks of disrupting traffic and breaching rights of others.'

On the conduct of Mr Navalnyy during the march:
> 'Mr Navalnyy was arrested while walking peacefully with other participants in the march. Nothing suggests that he had shown any violent intentions or had called on the participants to engage in the acts of aggression.'

On the domestic courts' reasoning:
> 'The domestic courts, in their turn, did not attempt to examine what the reasons were for arresting Mr Navalnyy in Tverskaya street. The courts justified his arrest and conviction by the mere reference to the "unlawfulness" of the march, without assessing whether it had been possible to ensure that the march proceed peacefully while minimising the disruption of movement of passers-by, road users and public service workers.'

On the actual disruption caused:
> 'It was not demonstrated that the disruption caused by the march in Tverskaya street went beyond of what was inevitable in the circumstances or that the march represented a danger to public order.'

On the authorities' response:
> 'Such tolerance has not been shown in the present case.'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
