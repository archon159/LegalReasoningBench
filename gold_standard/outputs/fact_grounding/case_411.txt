I will go through each canonical element where I found explicit discussion or implication, and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not resolve this definitively. It proceeds on two alternative hypotheses. The factual grounding for the first hypothesis (solo demonstrations) is:

> 'the Court has taken note of the applicants' submission that they attempted to stage solo demonstrations, the venue (the object being picketed) and timing of which happened to coincide.'

The Court does not explicitly adopt this as established fact — it merely takes note of the submission. For the second hypothesis, the Court relies on the domestic courts' classification:

> 'The domestic courts considered the situation as a single group event in the form of a "picket".'

This is the only factual finding the Court explicitly treats as established regarding the nature of the gathering.

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds participation in the following established facts:

> 'the Court has taken note of the applicants' submission that they attempted to stage solo demonstrations'

and, in the spontaneous assembly section:

> 'The Court observes that the applicants wanted to protest against a draft law prohibiting the adoption of Russian children by US nationals.'

Additionally, the domestic courts' findings are implicitly relied upon, as the Court notes:

> 'In the proceedings against each applicant the courts limited their assessment to establishing that she had taken part in a "picket" held without prior notification.'

**iii) Peaceful**

The Court explicitly grounds this in its own assessment of the conduct:

> 'each applicant's expressive conduct was peaceful and non-disruptive.'

No specific underlying facts are spelled out beyond this characterisation. The Court does not explicitly adopt the applicants' own assertion about peacefulness — it makes its own finding.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds interference in the following explicitly agreed facts:

> 'The parties agreed that the termination of the applicants' demonstrations and/or their participation in a group public event, their escorting to the police station and their administrative conviction had constituted an interference with their rights to freedom of peaceful assembly and freedom of expression.'

The factual acts constituting interference are thus: termination of demonstrations, escorting to police station, and administrative conviction. These are not disputed facts but agreed ones, and the Court explicitly adopts them.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

As noted, the Court does not resolve this element separately. However, to the extent it engages with the legal framework, the factual/legal grounding it relies upon is:

> 'Irrespective of the statutory distance requirement and compliance with it by simultaneous solo demonstrators, the Act empowered the courts to classify post facto a demonstration as a group event (the "reclassification rule" under section 7(1.1) of the Act) and, by implication, to punish individuals for non-compliance with the prior notification procedure applicable to such events.'

And:

> 'It is noted that there was no specific statutory distance requirement in Moscow prior to January 2013.'

These are the only facts the Court explicitly establishes regarding the legal framework. No conclusion on prescribed by law is drawn from them.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not formally resolve this element. The only factual grounding for the implicit reference to candidate legitimate aims appears in section (a):

> 'no compelling considerations relating to public safety, prevention of disorder or protection of the rights of others were at stake and relied upon when using this factual assertion for convicting each applicant specifically on account of non-observance of the notification procedure'

This is a negative factual finding — the Court establishes the *absence* of facts that would support a legitimate aim. No positive factual grounding for a legitimate aim is identified by the Court.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most extensively grounded element. The Court relies on the following established facts:

On the solo demonstration hypothesis:

> 'the Court has no reason to doubt that each applicant was merely standing with a banner at some – apparently, non-negligible – distance from other protesters in so far as it was practicable in the vicinity of the object being picketed, the State Duma'

> 'each applicant's expressive conduct was peaceful and non-disruptive'

> 'no compelling considerations relating to public safety, prevention of disorder or protection of the rights of others were at stake and relied upon when using this factual assertion for convicting each applicant specifically on account of non-observance of the notification procedure and also when attributing an active role to at least one of them'

> 'The only relevant consideration was the need to punish them for unlawful conduct arising solely from the fact that they had not complied with the notification procedure.'

On the spontaneous assembly hypothesis:

> 'The Court observes that the applicants wanted to protest against a draft law prohibiting the adoption of Russian children by US nationals. The date of the parliamentary examination of the draft law had been announced two days in advance, making it impossible for the protesters to comply even with a three-day notification time-limit for "pickets".'

> 'In the proceedings against each applicant the courts limited their assessment to establishing that she had taken part in a "picket" held without prior notification. They omitted to examine whether there had been special circumstances calling for an immediate response to a current event in the form of a spontaneous assembly, justifying derogation from the strict application of the notification time-limits.'

> 'the automatic and inflexible application of the notification time-limits without any regard to the specific circumstances of the present case amounted to an interference which was not justified under Article 11 § 2 of the Convention'

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding to identify — the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all.
