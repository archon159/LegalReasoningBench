I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The factual grounding is: "public assemblies" — drawn from the opening sentence: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."

**ii) Participation**
The factual grounding is the same sentence: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."

Note: This is drawn from the applicants' complaint, not from an independent factual finding by the Court. The Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact — it merely recites the complaint. Therefore, strictly speaking, there is **no independently established factual grounding** for participation beyond the applicants' own characterisation.

**iii) Peaceful**
There is **no factual grounding** in the reasoning text. The Court says nothing about the nature of the assemblies or the conduct of participants. This element is implied only by the Court's silence and its willingness to proceed to the interference and necessity analysis.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is stated as the substance of the complaint and is implicitly accepted by the Court as the factual basis when it refers to "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly."

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity finding is: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

However, the factual grounding for this conclusion is **not spelled out in the reasoning text**. The Court does not identify specific facts about, for example, the proportionality of the arrests, the conduct of authorities, or the severity of the convictions. Instead, it grounds its conclusion entirely by reference to prior case-law: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" and "Having regard to its case-law on the subject."

The factual grounding is therefore **delegated by reference** to the leading cases (*Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, *Kasparov and Others v. Russia*) rather than set out in this reasoning text itself. No specific facts from the present case are quoted or adopted in support of the necessity finding.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning text is notably sparse in its factual grounding. The Court relies almost entirely on a reference to prior case-law and a general statement that no new facts or arguments have been presented. The only concrete facts mentioned — the arrests, the dispersal of assemblies, and the administrative convictions — appear in the recitation of the applicants' complaint rather than in independent judicial findings of fact.
