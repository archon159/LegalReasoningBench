## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not itself make an independent factual finding on this point, but adopts the findings of the Beyoğlu Assize Court:

> "the participants had been gathering at a public place in order to make a press statement on issues of public interest"

and

> "the acts of the demonstrators, including the applicant, should be seen as an exercise of their democratic right to freedom of assembly"

**(ii) Participation of the applicant**

Again grounded in the adopted findings of the Beyoğlu Assize Court, as well as the Court's own description of the police action:

> "the police officers arrested the applicant, together with a number of other people taking part in the demonstration"

and from the domestic court findings:

> "the acts of the demonstrators, including the applicant, should be seen as an exercise of their democratic right to freedom of assembly"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

Grounded in the adopted findings of the Beyoğlu Assize Court:

> "It can also be seen from the judgment of the Beyoğlu Assize Court that the group did not present a danger to public order, or engage in acts of violence."

---

### (b) Interference

Grounded in the following factual description of the police action:

> "The Court considers that the intervention of the police which led to the subsequent arrest of the applicant for participating in the meeting constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicant's rights under Article 11."

The underlying facts of the police action are further elaborated through the adopted domestic court findings:

> "the police officers had stopped them, kept them in a circle and used force to disperse the group, without issuing a prior warning"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's finding is grounded solely in the identification of a legal provision:

> "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act"

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards, as these are not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the Government's submission on legitimate aim without independent factual grounding of its own:

> "the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder, and the Court finds no reason to differ."

No independent facts are cited by the Court to establish the aim. The Court simply does not contest the Government's characterisation. This is an acceptance of a legal characterisation rather than a factual finding.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court draws on the following facts, all derived from the adopted findings of the Beyoğlu Assize Court:

On the conduct of the demonstrators and the absence of lawbreaking:

> "the applicant and the other demonstrators did not breach this law, contrary to the allegations of the Government"

> "the participants had been gathering at a public place in order to make a press statement on issues of public interest"

> "the acts of the demonstrators, including the applicant, should be seen as an exercise of their democratic right to freedom of assembly, and it therefore acquitted them"

On the absence of violence or danger to public order:

> "the group did not present a danger to public order, or engage in acts of violence"

On the manner of the police intervention:

> "the police officers had stopped them, kept them in a circle and used force to disperse the group, without issuing a prior warning"

These facts collectively ground the Court's conclusion:

> "the forceful intervention of the police officers was disproportionate and unnecessary for the prevention of disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions positive obligations in general terms but does not ground this in any specific facts of the case, nor does it reach a separate factual conclusion on this element. The reference is purely doctrinal:

> "although the essential aim of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities with the exercise of the rights protected, there may in addition be positive obligations to secure the effective enjoyment of those rights"

No factual grounding can be identified for this element, as the Court does not apply it to the facts of the case.
