## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

No explicit factual grounding. The Court does not identify specific facts to establish that an assembly within the meaning of Article 11 existed. The classification of the event as an assembly is treated as a domestic legal question that the Court scrutinises, rather than an independent factual finding the Court makes for itself.

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds the applicant's role as organiser in the following:

> "the applicant submitted that he had organised solo demonstrations by his co-workers rather than a group event."

However, this is a party submission. The Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact in its own voice. What the Court does accept is that the domestic authorities treated him as the organiser and prosecuted him on that basis:

> "the impugned legislation was applied in 2009 for prosecuting the applicant for organising what the authorities classified as a group event rather than several solo demonstrations"

and:

> "for punishing the applicant as its organiser"

These passages reflect the Court's own characterisation of what happened, grounding participation/organisation in the fact of prosecution itself.

**iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds the peaceful nature of the assembly in the following factual observation:

> "they were merely standing in a peaceful and non-disruptive manner at a distance of some fifty metres from each other"

and:

> "Undoubtedly, no such considerations were at stake before or during Mr Romakhin's demonstration"

(the latter referring to the absence of any public safety or security risks).

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference in the following explicitly accepted facts:

> "It is common ground between the parties that the 'interference' in the present case consisted in the applicant being taken to the police station and held there for some time, and his prosecution for an administrative offence."

The Court accepts this as common ground, making it an established fact rather than merely a party submission.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds its foreseeability finding in the following facts:

First, the state of the law before 2012:

> "before 2012 section 7 of the Public Assemblies Act clearly stated that the requirement of prior notification did not apply to solo static demonstrations. The Act contained no specific rules relating to that type of public event."

Second, the timing of the legislative changes relative to the applicant's demonstration:

> "the specific distance to be observed between solo demonstrators was not set at regional level until December 2012, that is after Mr Romakhin's demonstration. At the relevant time, the federal legislation provided only guidance, stating that the distance to be specified at regional level could not be more than fifty metres."

Third, the basis on which the applicant was actually convicted:

> "the applicant's conviction was rather based on the finding that the common design of the event was that of a public assembly rather than two unrelated simultaneous solo [demonstrations]"

Fourth, the date of the demonstration relative to the legislative framework:

> "the impugned legislation was applied in 2009 for prosecuting the applicant for organising what the authorities classified as a group event rather than several solo demonstrations, despite the observance of certain (arguably, considerable) distance between demonstrators"

**ii) Accessibility**

No factual grounding identified. The Court does not discuss this sub-element.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse**

No factual grounding identified as a distinct sub-element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds its finding that no legitimate aim was genuinely pursued in the following factual observations:

> "no compelling consideration relating to public safety, prevention of disorder or protection of the rights of others was at stake."

and:

> "Undoubtedly, no such considerations were at stake before or during Mr Romakhin's demonstration."

and:

> "The only relevant consideration was the need to punish unlawful conduct. This is not a sufficient consideration in this context, in terms of Article 10 of the Convention, in the absence of any aggravating elements."

The Court also grounds this in the absence of any prior notification risk materialising:

> "the prior notification rule for a public assembly... may be intended to afford the authorities reasonable time in advance of the planned event to ponder various public safety, security or other risks and, where appropriate, to make arrangements to avert such risks. Undoubtedly, no such considerations were at stake before or during Mr Romakhin's demonstration."

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its necessity finding in several factual observations:

First, the distance maintained between demonstrators:

> "they were merely standing in a peaceful and non-disruptive manner at a distance of some fifty metres from each other"

and:

> "the impugned legislation was applied in 2009 for prosecuting the applicant for organising what the authorities classified as a group event rather than several solo demonstrations, despite the observance of certain (arguably, considerable) distance between demonstrators"

Second, the absence of any public safety or disorder concerns:

> "no compelling consideration relating to public safety, prevention of disorder or protection of the rights of others was at stake"

> "Undoubtedly, no such considerations were at stake before or during Mr Romakhin's demonstration."

Third, the failure of domestic courts to provide adequate reasoning:

> "The domestic courts failed to provide reasons, which would be sufficient, for classifying the events on 12 December 2009 as a single group event under the PEA and, foremost, for punishing the applicant as its organiser"

Fourth, the timing of the demonstration relative to the regulatory framework:

> "the specific distance to be observed between solo demonstrators was not set at regional level until December 2012, that is after Mr Romakhin's demonstration."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding identified. The Court does not discuss positive obligations.
