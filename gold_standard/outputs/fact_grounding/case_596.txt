I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**, namely their arrest in relation to the **dispersal of these assemblies**."

The Court does not elaborate further on the nature, size, location, or form of the assemblies.

**ii) Participation**
The only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies."

Again, no further factual detail is provided.

**iii) Peaceful character**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not mention any facts relating to whether the assemblies were peaceful or violent. This element is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

These are the only concrete facts stated: arrest and administrative conviction. No further factual detail about the circumstances of arrest or the nature of the administrative offences is provided in this reasoning section.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most explicitly discussed, yet it is striking that **no specific facts** from the instant case are cited in support of the necessity finding. The Court states:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has **not found any fact or argument** capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

And: "Having regard to its **case-law on the subject**, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely by reference to prior case-law — specifically *Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, and *Kasparov and Others v. Russia* — rather than by any facts from the instant case that the Court explicitly sets out and adopts. The phrase "having examined all the material submitted to it" acknowledges that material exists, but no specific facts from that material are quoted, adopted, or relied upon in the text.

---

**Summary observation**

Across all elements, the factual grounding in this reasoning section is extremely thin. The Court identifies only two concrete facts — arrest and administrative conviction — and relies for the rest on a reference back to prior leading cases. For the peaceful character of the assemblies and the necessity assessment, no independently stated and adopted facts can be identified in this text.
