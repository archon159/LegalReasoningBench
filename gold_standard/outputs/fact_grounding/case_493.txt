## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
No specific facts are identified by the Court to establish that the planned events constitute an assembly. The Court proceeds on this assumption without grounding it in explicit factual findings.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?**
For Mr Patyi:
> 'Mr Patyi was the organiser of the demonstrations planned and that he signed all the documents submitted to the competent authorities.'

For the other 47 applicants, the Court grounds its negative finding on the following facts:
> 'the intention of the other forty-seven applicants actually to participate in the banned demonstrations is not supported by any evidence. These applicants have based their complaints on the mere fact that they were amongst the thousands of people who lost money due to the insolvency at issue.'
> 'in Mr Patyi's submissions to the authorities, the number of prospective participants was consistently limited to twenty persons.'
> 'it has not been possible to determine which of the applicants would have been involved in a particular event.'

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court grounds its implied finding of peacefulness on the absence of contrary evidence:
> 'the Court notes that there is no evidence in the case file to suggest that the demonstrations would have been violent or would have represented a danger to public order.'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court does not independently identify facts to establish interference. It relies on the parties' agreement:
> 'The Government did not dispute that Mr Patyi could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11; nor did they deny that the ban on the demonstrations had interfered with the exercise of his rights under that provision. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise.'

No independent factual grounding is articulated by the Court beyond this agreed position.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its finding on one factual observation:
> 'the restriction imposed on Mr Patyi's freedom of peaceful assembly was based on sections 6, 8 and 14 of the Assembly Act, the wording of which is clear.'

No further facts are identified regarding accessibility or safeguards against abuse.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not independently establish facts to ground the legitimate aim. It accepts the aim on the basis of the Government's submissions, stating only:
> 'the Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others.'

No specific facts about actual traffic conditions, pedestrian flows, or disorder risks are identified by the Court at this stage as established. The factual grounding for the aim is not separately articulated here — the Court defers to the Government's framing without independently verifying the underlying facts at this step.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively fact-grounded element. The Court identifies the following established facts:

On the nature and scale of the planned demonstrations:
> 'Mr Patyi planned to organise demonstrations with twenty participants, whose only action would have been to stand silently in line on the pavement in front of the Prime Minister's house.'

On the physical space available:
> 'It is clear from the pictures attached to the case file that the space in question was wide enough – approximately five metres – to allow other pedestrians to walk by during a demonstration.'

On the Christmas Eve demonstration specifically:
> 'it appears unlikely that a limited number of demonstrators would have needed more space at the scene than the five-metre-wide pedestrian area, or that they would have significantly impeded the traffic, especially on Christmas Eve when the town buses ceased to run soon after 4 p.m.'

Note: The Court references 'the information produced by the Budapest Transportation Company' as supporting this finding, but does not quote its content directly in the reasoning text provided.

On the conduct of the authorities:
> 'the authorities, when issuing repetitive prohibitions on the demonstrations, mechanically relying on the same reasons and not taking into account Mr Patyi's factual clarifications, failed to strike a fair balance between the rights of those wishing to exercise their freedom of assembly and those others whose freedom of movement may have been frustrated temporarily, if at all.'

On the absence of violence or public order risk:
> 'there is no evidence in the case file to suggest that the demonstrations would have been violent or would have represented a danger to public order.'

On the general principle regarding disruption:
> 'any demonstration in a public place may cause a certain level of disruption to ordinary life.'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not develop a positive obligation analysis in its own reasoning. No factual grounding can be identified for this element, as the Court does not engage with it independently.
