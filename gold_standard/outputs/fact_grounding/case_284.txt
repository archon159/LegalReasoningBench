I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Participation**

The only factual grounding in the text is the following sentence from the opening paragraph:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**"

This is the sole factual basis for both the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation. It should be noted that this is framed as the applicants' complaint, not as a finding of fact by the Court. The Court does not independently state as an established fact that assemblies took place or that the applicants participated. There is no other factual grounding for these elements.

**iii) Peaceful character**

There is no factual grounding whatsoever for the peaceful character of the assemblies. The text contains no reference to any facts bearing on this question.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for interference is found in the opening paragraph:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

Again, this is framed as the applicants' complaint rather than an independent judicial finding of fact. The Court does not separately state these as established facts, though it proceeds on this basis.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding for the necessity assessment in this reasoning text. The Court does not identify or weigh any specific facts relating to the proportionality of the measures. Instead, it relies entirely on its prior case-law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The necessity finding is thus grounded not in specific facts recited in this section, but in the similarity of the present case to prior leading cases. No concrete facts about the proportionality of the arrests, dispersals, or convictions are spelled out in this reasoning section.
