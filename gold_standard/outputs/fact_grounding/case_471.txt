## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

> "KESK organised two demonstrations, on 7 and 25 June 2001 respectively."

> "On both occasions, while the president of KESK was making press statements, police officers intervened in the demonstrations and asked the participants to disperse."

**ii) Participation**

> "The Court notes that the applicants took part in demonstrations with a view to protesting a draft bill proposed in the Turkish Parliament which would amend the Act on Trade Unions."

> "the applicants and other demonstrators did not comply with these orders and attempted to force their way through"

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

> "it transpires that the demonstrators, including the applicants, wished to draw attention to a sensitive bill proposed in the Parliament and that their rally was initially peaceful."

> "there is no evidence to suggest that the group in question initially presented a serious danger to public order."

Note: The Court also acknowledges that violence eventually occurred ("Some demonstrators responded violently and turmoil ensued, resulting in the injury of five of the applicants, together with a number of police officers and other demonstrators"), but it treats the assembly as initially peaceful and attributes the escalation to the authorities' intervention rather than to the demonstrators' conduct.

---

### (b) Interference

> "their meetings were forcibly ended by the security forces on the ground that the location was not permitted by law."

> "the interference in the meetings and the force used by the police to disperse the participants, as well as the subsequent prosecution, could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants from taking part in similar meetings."

> "the police officers used truncheons and tear gas to disperse the crowd."

> "the Court considers that the applicants were negatively affected by the police intervention and that there has therefore been an interference with the exercise of their right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

> "the Court observes that the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of Law no. 2911 (the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act), and was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse, as the Court does not analyze them separately.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No independent factual grounding is provided by the Court. The Court simply accepts the Government's submission without elaboration:

> "the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting public safety, and the Court finds no reason to differ."

The Court does not itself identify facts that establish these aims; it merely declines to dispute the Government's characterization.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

> "there is no evidence to suggest that the group in question initially presented a serious danger to public order."

> "it is likely that they would have caused some disruption in a particularly busy square in central Ankara."

> "the demonstrators, including the applicants, wished to draw attention to a sensitive bill proposed in the Parliament and that their rally was initially peaceful."

> "the authorities' intervened swiftly with considerable force in order to disperse them, thereby causing tensions to rise, followed by clashes."

> "at the material time no authorisation was required for the holding of public demonstrations; however, notification was required seventy-two hours prior to the event."

> "the Court considers that in the instant case the forceful intervention of the police officers was disproportionate and was not necessary for the prevention of disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No specific facts are identified by the Court in support of the positive obligation point. The Court raises positive obligations as a general principle of Article 11 jurisprudence:

> "although the essential object of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities in the exercise of the rights protected, there may also be positive obligations to secure their effective enjoyment."

However, since the Court does not apply this principle to reach a separate finding on the facts, there is no factual grounding to identify for a positive obligation violation. The mention remains at the level of abstract doctrine.
