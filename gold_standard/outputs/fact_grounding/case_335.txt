## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly state facts grounding this finding, but the following passage treats the existence of a demonstration as an established fact:

> "a demonstration in a public place may cause some disruption to ordinary life, including disruption of traffic"

And in the interference section:

> "The Court considers that the intervention of the police officers, as well as the ill-treatment to which the applicant was subjected, constituted an interference with her rights under Article 11 of the Convention"

The Court proceeds throughout on the factual premise that a demonstration occurred, but does not explicitly identify the facts establishing this. **No explicit factual grounding identifiable.**

---

**(ii) Participation**

The Court does not explicitly ground this in identified facts. It is treated as a given throughout. **No explicit factual grounding identifiable.**

---

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court grounds its implicit finding of peacefulness in the following facts:

> "having regard to the absence of any criminal proceedings against the applicant, as well as the lack of any information to show that proceedings were initiated against any other demonstrator for an alleged breach of that Law, the Court observes that the applicant and many other demonstrators did not act violently"

> "the video footage (see paragraph 14 above), the report of the expert on that footage (see paragraph 15 above), statements taken from the six police officers who accepted having used unnecessary and excessive force against the demonstrators (see paragraph 20 above), and the conclusion reached by the trial court (see paragraphs 24-25 above) show that the demonstrators neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence requiring such a heavy-handed intervention"

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference in the following facts:

> "The Court considers that the intervention of the police officers, as well as the ill-treatment to which the applicant was subjected, constituted an interference with her rights under Article 11 of the Convention"

The specific factual content of the "intervention" and "ill-treatment" is not spelled out in the reasoning section itself — the Court refers to earlier paragraphs of the judgment (not reproduced here) for the underlying facts. Within the reasoning text, the factual grounding is limited to this characterisation. **The Court does not elaborate further on the specific facts within this section.**

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this finding in one fact:

> "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely the Law on Meetings and Demonstration Marches (see paragraphs 29-31 above)"

No further facts are identified. The sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not factually grounded because they are not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not independently identify facts grounding the legitimate aim. It simply accepts the Government's submission without elaboration:

> "the Government submitted that the interference pursued, among others, the legitimate aim of prevention of disorder and crime, and the Court finds no reason to differ"

No facts are cited by the Court in support of this conclusion. **No factual grounding identifiable beyond acceptance of the Government's characterisation.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

On the absence of violence by demonstrators:

> "having regard to the absence of any criminal proceedings against the applicant, as well as the lack of any information to show that proceedings were initiated against any other demonstrator for an alleged breach of that Law, the Court observes that the applicant and many other demonstrators did not act violently"

On the disproportionate nature of the police response:

> "the video footage (see paragraph 14 above), the report of the expert on that footage (see paragraph 15 above), statements taken from the six police officers who accepted having used unnecessary and excessive force against the demonstrators (see paragraph 20 above), and the conclusion reached by the trial court (see paragraphs 24-25 above) show that the demonstrators neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence requiring such a heavy-handed intervention"

On the chilling effect:

> "the brutality of the dispersal had an inevitable dissuasive effect on people's willingness to demonstrate"

Note: the Court does not spell out the specific content of the video footage, expert report, police statements, or trial court conclusion within this reasoning section — it references them by paragraph number. The factual grounding is thus present but partially delegated to earlier parts of the judgment not reproduced here.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted previously, the Court does not adopt a positive obligation framing in its own analysis. **No factual grounding to identify.**
