## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court does not explicitly establish this as a finding of fact, but proceeds on the basis of the following:
> 'the demonstration on 4 July 2002'
> 'his right to organise a peaceful demonstration and to take part in it'

These are drawn from the applicant's submissions and treated as uncontested background facts. The Court does not make an independent factual finding on this point.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?**
Again, no independent factual finding. The Court proceeds on the uncontested basis reflected in:
> 'the applicant was convicted on account of his actions during the demonstration'

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court does not make an explicit factual finding on peacefulness as a threshold matter. The closest factual grounding is the Court's observation in the general principles section:
> 'where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings'

This is stated as a general principle, not as an explicit factual finding about this assembly. No verbatim factual finding on peacefulness can be identified.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following fact:
> 'the fact that the applicant was convicted on account of his actions during the demonstration is sufficient to conclude that there was an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly'

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**(i) Foreseeability**
The Court grounds its finding on:
> 'the interference had a legal basis in national law, namely section 260(1) of the Criminal Code (this provision having already been held relevant in a similar situation, which occurred in October 1990 (see paragraphs 16 and 17 above)), and was thus "prescribed by law" with sufficient foreseeability within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention'

The factual grounding is the prior application of the same provision in a similar situation in October 1990, which the Court uses to establish foreseeability. The Court does not quote the content of that prior situation in this section but refers to paragraphs 16 and 17 of the judgment.

**(ii) Accessibility**
No factual grounding identified. Not discussed.

**(iii) Safeguards against abuse**
No factual grounding identified. Not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds its finding on:
> 'the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aim of the protection of the rights and freedoms of others, with a view to preventing disorder and maintaining the orderly circulation of traffic'

The factual grounding for this is the nature of the conduct itself — the blockade of traffic — which is established elsewhere in the judgment. The Court does not in this sentence cite a specific fact but relies on the established nature of the interference (traffic obstruction) as self-evidently engaging these aims.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's proportionality assessment is grounded on several explicitly stated facts:

**On spontaneity (absence thereof):**
> 'The ballot papers were, under statute, bound for destruction on 20 July 2002 pursuant to the relevant ministerial decree.'
> 'the domestic court's conclusion that the law did not leave any doubt about the relevant date'
> 'the impugned situation did not disclose special circumstances of the kind to which the only adequate response was an immediate demonstration'

**On the scale of disruption:**
> 'The domestic courts conducted a thorough examination of the consequences of the blockade erected by the applicant. They held that the assembly had developed into a massive traffic jam all over Budapest and that the magnitude of the traffic paralysis had been sufficient for them to justify the applicant's conviction of the offence of disturbance of public works.'

**On the applicant's failure to cooperate:**
> 'the applicant and other protesters failed to give evidence of flexibility and readiness to cooperate with the other road users, in particular in that the blockade of a central thoroughfare of the Hungarian capital was complete for several hours'

**On the authorities' tolerance:**
> 'it was rather the authorities who showed a certain degree of tolerance in the situation, in that they allowed the demonstration to continue for several hours, before dispersing it'

**On the national courts' proportionality assessment:**
> 'the national courts' assessment was not confined to a mere, formal application of the criminal law. They also engaged in an assessment of the conviction's proportionality in the light of the Court's and the Constitutional Court's case-law concerning freedom of assembly.'

**On the nature of the conviction:**
> 'he was convicted not for the participation in, or organisation of, the demonstration but for the obstruction of traffic'

**On the sentence:**
> 'the applicant's conviction and rather lenient sentence for the criminal offence does not appear disproportionate to the aims pursued'
