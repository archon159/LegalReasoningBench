I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the following sentence from the opening paragraph:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the situation, not merely a party submission. It establishes that public assemblies took place and that the applicants were organisers or participants.

- **Peaceful**: No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not state any facts supporting the conclusion that the assemblies were peaceful. The peacefulness of the assemblies is implied only by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any explicit factual finding.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited by the Court to ground its necessity assessment. The Court relies entirely on its prior case-law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court thus grounds its necessity finding not in specific facts from the present case, but in the similarity of the case to prior precedents. No individual facts from the case are quoted or adopted in support of the necessity conclusion.
