**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." The word "dispersal" implies that assemblies were taking place and were brought to an end by state action. Additionally: "organisers or participants of public assemblies" — the Court uses this characterization as an established description of the applicants' roles, not merely as a submission.

- **Peaceful:** No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character or conduct of the assemblies. The peaceful nature is implied only by the logical structure of the analysis, not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds interference in two concrete state acts, stated in the opening paragraph:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

These are the only facts explicitly identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet it is strikingly thin in terms of explicit factual grounding. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is the closest the Court comes to referencing a factual basis, but it is entirely non-specific — it refers to "all the material" without identifying any particular fact. The necessity conclusion is grounded not in facts stated in this reasoning section, but in the reference to prior case-law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

The factual basis for the necessity finding is thus effectively delegated to those prior judgments by analogy, rather than established through facts recited in this decision.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed.
