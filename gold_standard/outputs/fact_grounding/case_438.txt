**ii) Participation**
The factual grounding is the same sentence: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants or organisers of public assemblies."

Again, this is a bare characterisation rather than a factual description.

**iii) Peaceful character**
No factual grounding can be identified. The reasoning text contains no facts — not even a bare assertion — about whether the assemblies were peaceful or violent. This element is implied by the Court's proceeding to a violation finding, but there is no factual statement in the text to quote.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity finding is grounded not in facts specific to this case but in reference to prior case law: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. The necessity finding is thus grounded entirely in the similarity to prior cases, not in any independently stated facts about the present case. The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual content.
