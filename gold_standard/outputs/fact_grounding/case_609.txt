## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:
- "the demonstration which was held on 26 November 2005 in Galaba Square"
- "the demonstration took place without any police intervention from 3 p.m. to 5 p.m., which was the period notified by the organisers" — this is from the Government's submissions, but the Court implicitly adopts it as established fact by building its analysis on the timeline.
- "It is undisputed by the parties that the assembly was conducted peacefully until 5 p.m., the time scheduled for the end of the demonstration in the notification given."

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:
- "the demonstrators, including the applicant, did not disperse after the end of the allotted time for the demonstration"
- "the demonstrators, including the applicant, were informed by the police after 5 p.m. that the continuation of their demonstration was unlawful"
- "the demonstrators, including the applicant, wished to draw attention to a vital issue in a democratic society, namely alleged election irregularities"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court grounds this on:
- "The Court observes that the demonstration which was held on 26 November 2005 in Galaba Square was peaceful and was carried out according to the rules on holding of assemblies."
- "It is undisputed by the parties that the assembly was conducted peacefully until 5 p.m."
- "there is no evidence to suggest that in the instant case the applicant or other demonstrators presented a danger to public order. The Government failed to submit any evidence in that respect."
- "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence" — the Court applies this general principle to the present case, implying it found no acts of violence.

Note: The Court's finding of peacefulness extends to the period after 5 p.m. as well. The only basis for the police intervention after 5 p.m. was the failure to disperse, not any violence or disorder — this is grounded in: "the only reason advanced by the Government for the police intervention was the fact that the demonstrators, including the applicant, did not disperse after the end of the allotted time for the demonstration."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds this on:
- "there is no dispute between the parties, and the Court agrees, that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly."

No further specific facts are cited at this stage; the Court treats it as undisputed and moves on.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this on:
- "The potential dispersal of a demonstration by the police and the use of force by the latter in this respect were set out in Articles 7 and 14 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly, which were the legal basis for the police intervention in the present case."

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards, as the Court does not examine them individually.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not ground its acceptance of legitimate aim in any specific facts. It simply accepts the Government's characterisation — "preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others" — without tying this to particular factual findings. This element is accepted without factual elaboration.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on:

- **On the reason for intervention being insufficient:**
  "the only reason advanced by the Government for the police intervention was the fact that the demonstrators, including the applicant, did not disperse after the end of the allotted time for the demonstration, despite numerous warnings by the police."

- **On the peaceful nature of the demonstration:**
  "The Court observes that the demonstration which was held on 26 November 2005 in Galaba Square was peaceful and was carried out according to the rules on holding of assemblies."

- **On the absence of danger to public order:**
  "there is no evidence to suggest that in the instant case the applicant or other demonstrators presented a danger to public order. The Government failed to submit any evidence in that respect."

- **On the applicant's specific conduct:**
  "the Government have not submitted any evidence which could cast doubt on the facts submitted by the applicant, namely that she was about to leave the place and go home." — Note: the Court does not positively adopt this as an established fact, but accepts it by default due to the Government's failure to rebut it.

- **On the subject matter of the demonstration:**
  "the demonstrators, including the applicant, wished to draw attention to a vital issue in a democratic society, namely alleged election irregularities."

- **On the authorities' conduct:**
  "the Court is therefore particularly struck by the authorities' impatience in seeking to end the demonstration by forcible dispersal."
  "the Court sees no sign of tolerance in the present case."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted previously, the Court does not apply a positive obligation analysis in this case. No factual grounding is relevant here.
