## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "the applicants were fined for taking part in unauthorised demonstrations organised by the CDPP"

And from the quoted Christian Democratic People's Party findings:

> "gatherings which it had organised in order to express its disagreement with and protest against the Government's plans to make the study of Russian compulsory for school children, at that time the subject of a heated debate within Moldovan society"

> "the CDPP's gatherings were entirely peaceful, there were no calls to violent overthrow of the Government or any other acts undermining the principles of pluralism and democracy"

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?**

The Court grounds this on:

> "the applicants were fined for taking part in unauthorised demonstrations organised by the CDPP"

> "the present applicants were members and/or supporters of the CDPP organising and/or participating at the latter's peaceful gatherings"

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**

The Court grounds this on:

> "Since the CDPP's gatherings were entirely peaceful, there were no calls to violent overthrow of the Government or any other acts undermining the principles of pluralism and democracy"

> "the present applicants were members and/or supporters of the CDPP organising and/or participating at the latter's peaceful gatherings"

On the specific question of alleged calls to violence (one of the government's grounds), the Court grounds its rejection on:

> "the Court is not persuaded that the singing of a fairly mild student song could reasonably be interpreted as a call to public violence. Neither the Ministry of Justice nor the domestic courts have attempted to explain how the impugned line from the chorus of the song amounted to a call for violence."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the existence of an interference on:

> "the applicants were fined for taking part in unauthorised demonstrations organised by the CDPP"

And in the conclusion:

> "the interference with their freedom of peaceful assembly was not proportionate to the aim pursued"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not resolve this element definitively. The factual grounding for the doubt it raises is:

> "there was a dispute as to the applicability of the provisions of the Assemblies Act to the CDPP's gatherings. The Municipal Council, which was the only authority empowered to issue authorisations under that law, considered the legislation unclear and refused to apply it to the CDPP until Parliament had given its official interpretation"

> "Hence, it would appear questionable whether non-compliance with the legislation in those circumstances would justify such a serious measure as a temporary ban. However, even assuming that the legislation was clear..."

The Court then proceeds without resolving the question. No factual grounding can be identified for the sub-elements of accessibility or safeguards, as the Court does not discuss them.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

As noted previously, the Court does not explicitly identify a legitimate aim. No factual grounding can be identified for this element, as the Court does not discuss it.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court grounds its assessment on the following established facts:

**On the narrowed margin of appreciation:**

> "at the material time the CDPP was a minority parliamentary opposition party with approximately ten percent of the seats in Parliament, while the majority Communist Party had approximately seventy percent of the seats"

> "gatherings which it had organised in order to express its disagreement with and protest against the Government's plans to make the study of Russian compulsory for school children, at that time the subject of a heated debate within Moldovan society"

**On the first government ground (lack of authorisation):**

> "there was a dispute as to the applicability of the provisions of the Assemblies Act to the CDPP's gatherings. The Municipal Council, which was the only authority empowered to issue authorisations under that law, considered the legislation unclear and refused to apply it to the CDPP until Parliament had given its official interpretation"

> "the failure to comply with that legislation, which otherwise was punishable with an administrative fine of MDL 180-450 (EUR 16-40)"

**On the second government ground (presence of children):**

> "it has not been established by the domestic courts that they were there as a result of any action or policy on the part of the applicant party. Since the gatherings were held in a public place everyone, including children, could attend"

> "the gatherings were to protest against Government policy on schooling"

**On the third government ground (calls to violence):**

> "the Court is not persuaded that the singing of a fairly mild student song could reasonably be interpreted as a call to public violence. Neither the Ministry of Justice nor the domestic courts have attempted to explain how the impugned line from the chorus of the song amounted to a call for violence."

**On the overall proportionality conclusion:**

> "Since the CDPP's gatherings were entirely peaceful, there were no calls to violent overthrow of the Government or any other acts undermining the principles of pluralism and democracy, it cannot reasonably be said that the measure applied was proportionate to the aim pursued and that it met a 'pressing social need.'"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss this element at all.
