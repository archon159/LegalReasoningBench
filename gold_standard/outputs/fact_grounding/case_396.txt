Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element the Court discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterisation: *"public assemblies"* and *"dispersal of these assemblies."* These are the Court's own words, not party submissions. No further factual detail is provided.

**ii) Participation:**
The only factual grounding is: *"organisers or participants of public assemblies."* Again, this is the Court's own characterisation. No further factual detail is provided.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the character of the assemblies. Peacefulness is implied solely by the Court's proceeding to assess the interference without comment.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding in the following facts: *"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."* These are the only concrete facts stated, and they are presented as the Court's own characterisation of what occurred, not as party submissions adopted by the Court.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet it is strikingly thin in its factual grounding. The Court states: *"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."* This is the entirety of the factual engagement. The Court does not identify any specific facts from the case to ground its necessity assessment. Instead, it relies entirely on the analogy with prior cases (*Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, *Kasparov*) and its general case-law on proportionality (*Oya Ataman*, *Hyde Park*). The only case-specific facts implicitly underpinning the necessity finding are the same facts noted under interference: the arrest and conviction for administrative offences.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**Overall observation:** The reasoning section is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. This appears to be a repetitive-case judgment (sometimes called a "clone case" in ECtHR practice), where the Court disposes of the case by reference to established precedent rather than by conducting a fresh, fact-specific analysis. The only concrete facts stated anywhere in the reasoning are the arrest and conviction of the applicants in connection with the dispersal of public assemblies.
