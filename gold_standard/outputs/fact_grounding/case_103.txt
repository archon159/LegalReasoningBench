**(a)(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly analyze this element, but implies it by referring to the events as "demonstrations" and "assemblies." The factual grounding is found in the framing of the complaints themselves, which the Court accepts:

> "the dispersal of the demonstrations of 2 April and 17 April 2011 by the police and their arrests and convictions for administrative offences"

> "their arrests and convictions prior to the assemblies of 18 October 2011 and 20 October 2012"

These are the Court's own characterizations of the events, not merely party assertions.

**(a)(ii) Participation**

The Court implies participation throughout. The factual grounding is the same references to arrests at or in connection with the demonstrations:

> "the dispersal of the demonstrations of 2 April and 17 April 2011 by the police and their arrests and convictions for administrative offences"

> "their arrests and convictions prior to the assemblies of 18 October 2011 and 20 October 2012 had been measures used by the authorities to punish them for their political activity and to prevent them from participating in opposition protests"

Note that the second quote is framed as the applicants' complaint, not a finding of fact by the Court. However, the Court's subsequent reasoning — particularly its finding of a violation — implies it accepted that the applicants were participants or intended participants. No single sentence explicitly establishes this as a Court finding in this excerpt.

**(a)(iii) Peaceful assembly**

This is explicitly discussed and grounded in the following facts, which the Court itself establishes:

> "The Court notes from the outset that none of the official records or the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties suggests that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was violent."

This is a factual finding by the Court drawn from the official record. On this basis, the Court then accepts a party submission:

> "The Court therefore accepts the second applicant's assertion that that demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up until his arrest."

The Court explicitly adopts this assertion, so it qualifies as an accepted fact. Note that this explicit finding is limited to the 2 April 2011 demonstration. No equivalent explicit factual finding is made for the other demonstrations in this excerpt.

**(b) Interference**

The Court implies interference. The factual grounding consists of the following, which the Court treats as established facts:

> "the dispersal of the demonstrations of 2 April and 17 April 2011 by the police and their arrests and convictions for administrative offences"

> "The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies."

The first quote identifies the concrete acts (dispersal, arrest, conviction). The second is the Court's own characterization of the effect of those acts, grounding the interference finding in the chilling effect produced.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (implied)**

The Court does not conduct an explicit necessity analysis in this excerpt but implies disproportionality through the chilling effect finding. The factual grounding is:

> "The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate."

This is the Court's own assessment of the consequences of the established facts (arrests, administrative proceedings). No additional specific facts are cited here beyond those already noted under interference — the Court reasons from the nature of the measures themselves to their chilling effect.
