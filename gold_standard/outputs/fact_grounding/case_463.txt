**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the phrase: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." This establishes that assemblies existed and were dispersed, and that the applicants were present at them (since they were arrested). The Court also describes the applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies" — but this is characterization rather than a finding of fact based on evidence recited in this section.

- **Peaceful:** No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not cite any facts relating to the character or conduct of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These two concrete state actions — arrest and criminal conviction — are the only facts cited as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed and no facts are cited in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited as the basis for the necessity assessment. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a negative statement — it references the existence of submitted material but does not identify or quote any specific facts from it. The Court's conclusion rests entirely on the reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) rather than on any facts recited in this reasoning section.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding.

---

**Overall observation:** The reasoning section is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. The only concrete facts cited anywhere in the section are the arrest of the applicants and their conviction for administrative offences, both mentioned in the opening paragraph. The Court's violation finding is driven almost entirely by reference to precedent rather than by any detailed engagement with the specific facts of the instant case.
