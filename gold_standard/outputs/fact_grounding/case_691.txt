## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not independently establish this fact in the present judgment but relies on a prior finding:

> "The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Yaroslav Belousov v. Russia, nos. 2653/13 and 60980/14, §§ 168-72, 4 October 2016)."

No new facts are identified in this judgment to ground this conclusion; the Court simply imports the prior holding.

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on the following accepted fact:

> "It was not disputed that both applicants had participated in the assembly."

**(iii) Peaceful character**

The Court grounds this on the following accepted fact:

> "it is common ground that they were not among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on its prior finding under Article 3:

> "The Court notes that it has found a violation of Article 3 of the Convention on account of the applicants' ill-treatment by police during the public assembly."

And then draws the Article 11 consequence directly from that factual finding:

> "The Court considers that the intervention by the police and their conduct vis-à-vis the applicants also constituted an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11 of the Convention."

The specific facts underlying the Article 3 finding (the nature and circumstances of the ill-treatment) are not re-stated here but are incorporated by reference to paragraph 71.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court accepts the Government's submission on this point without independently establishing facts of its own:

> "The Government submitted in their observations that the Police Act allowed police officers to use force and special equipment in order to, inter alia, prevent a crime and suppress resistance shown towards a police officer. Thus, the Court accepts that the interference in question was 'prescribed by law'..."

The Court adopts the existence of the Police Act and its content as sufficient factual grounding, but does not independently verify or elaborate on the specific provisions. No further factual analysis is offered on foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not identify independent facts here. The legitimate aim finding flows directly and without separate factual analysis from the acceptance of the prescribed-by-law basis:

> "...and pursued the legitimate aim of the prevention of disorder and crime."

No specific facts about the actual disorder at Bolotnaya Square are cited at this point to ground the legitimate aim finding. The Court simply accepts it.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of a violation here on two factual elements:

**First**, the absence of any justification for using force against these specific applicants:

> "the Government have not submitted any explanations as to why force had to be applied in respect of the applicants, who were not arrested and did not engage in any acts of violence."

Note that the characterisation of the applicants as non-violent and non-arrested is treated here as an established fact (consistent with the earlier finding under scope), not merely a party submission.

**Second**, the Court imports its factual finding from the Article 3 analysis:

> "In the light of its finding that the force used in respect of the applicants was unnecessary and excessive and thus contrary to Article 3 of the Convention (see paragraph 74 above), it finds that it was 'not necessary in a democratic society' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

**Third**, the Court adds a consequential factual observation about effect:

> "Moreover, it could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants and others from taking part in similar public gatherings."

This last point is stated as a potential consequence rather than an established fact; the Court uses the conditional "could have had" rather than asserting it as proven.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
