I will go through each canonical element and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts it explicitly states or accepts:

> "in May 2003 the Lithuanian authorities issued the farmers with permits to hold peaceful assemblies in selected areas"

> "on 21 May 2003 the farmers' peaceful demonstration dispersed and resulted in major disruptions of traffic on three main roads"

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:

> "the applicants were convicted of an offence in connection to their actions during an assembly which did not involve any violence"

> "the applicants' conviction for their participation in the gathering at issue amounted to an interference with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly"

The Court does not separately set out a detailed factual account of each applicant's individual participation in this section. The factual grounding is implicit in the reference to their conviction and its connection to the assembly.

**iii) Peaceful nature**

The Court grounds this on:

> "the applicants were convicted of an offence in connection to their actions during an assembly which did not involve any violence"

> "the element of violence was clearly absent in the instant case"

> "the farmers who held a demonstration on Panevėžys-Pasvalys-Riga highway not only allowed through passenger vehicles and vehicles that carried dangerous substances, but also that vehicles which carried goods and cars had been allowed to go through ten at a time on each side of the road"

> "good faith negotiations between the farmers and the Government had been on-going during the demonstrations"

> "especially in the circumstances where the applicants gave evidence of their flexibility and readiness to cooperate with the other road users"

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the finding of interference on:

> "the applicants were convicted of an offence in connection to their actions during an assembly which did not involve any violence"

> "the Court shares the Government's view that the applicants were permitted to exercise their right to peaceful assembly. It notes, however, that the applicants were convicted of an offence in connection to their actions during an assembly which did not involve any violence. Accordingly, the Court finds that the applicants' conviction for their participation in the gathering at issue amounted to an interference with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

The factual grounding is thus the criminal conviction itself, combined with the peaceful nature of the assembly.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not resolve this element — it assumes it in the Government's favor. There is accordingly **no factual grounding offered by the Court** for this element. The Court simply states:

> "even assuming that such interference was therefore 'prescribed by law'..."

No facts are identified or relied upon in connection with this assumption.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Similarly, the Court assumes this element without resolving it:

> "even assuming that such interference was therefore 'prescribed by law' and in pursuit of legitimate aims, namely the 'prevention of disorder' and 'the protection of the rights and freedoms of others'"

**No factual grounding is offered** — the Court proceeds on assumption only.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

On the limited disruption caused by the demonstration:

> "on 21 May 2003 the farmers' peaceful demonstration dispersed and resulted in major disruptions of traffic on three main roads"

> "the farmers who held a demonstration on Panevėžys-Pasvalys-Riga highway not only allowed through passenger vehicles and vehicles that carried dangerous substances, but also that vehicles which carried goods and cars had been allowed to go through ten at a time on each side of the road"

> "only one carrier company sued the farmers for that reason"

On the absence of violence:

> "the element of violence was clearly absent in the instant case"

> "especially in the circumstances where the applicants gave evidence of their flexibility and readiness to cooperate with the other road users"

On the ongoing negotiations:

> "good faith negotiations between the farmers and the Government had been on-going during the demonstrations"

On the deficiency of the domestic courts' analysis:

> "in contrast with the facts in Barraco (cited above, §§ 12 and 19), the Lithuanian courts considered the case in the context of riot and that context did not allow for the proper consideration of proportionality of the restriction of the right of assembly and thus significantly restricted their analysis"

On the inconsistent treatment of A.D. compared to the applicants:

> "another farmer A.D., who had taken the other farmers to block the Kaunas-Marijampolė-Suvalkai highway in Kalvarija municipality and had himself been walking in the middle of the road, pushing a cart in front of him and thus obstructing the traffic during the same demonstration of 21 May 2003, had been charged with merely an administrative offence – a violation of road traffic rules. This fact has not been denied by the Government, and it also appears to have been supported by the Lithuanian courts"

> "the Court considers that the actions of the five applicants and those of A.D. appear to have been of similar nature and thus of similar danger to society. However, A.D. escaped with nothing but administrative punishment and a modest fine of LTL 40 (approximately EUR 12), whereas the five applicants had to go through the ordeal of criminal proceedings, and, as a result of criminal conviction, were given a custodial sentence."

On the severity of the sanction imposed on the applicants:

> "Although the execution of the sentences was suspended for one year, the applicants were also ordered not to leave their places of residence for more than seven days without the authorities' prior approval, that restrictive measure having lasted for an entire year"

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, the Court mentions the positive obligation only in passing as general context, citing Oya Ataman:

> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to lawful demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct"

The Court does not apply this to any specific facts of the case in the context of a positive obligation analysis. There is accordingly **no factual grounding offered** for a positive obligation finding — the Court does not pursue this line of reasoning to a conclusion.
