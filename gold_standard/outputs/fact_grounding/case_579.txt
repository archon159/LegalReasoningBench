**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the introductory paragraph: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The Court also describes the applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies." These are the only factual anchors, and they are stated very briefly without elaboration.

- **Peaceful:** There is no factual grounding whatsoever for the implied conclusion that the assemblies were peaceful. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the conduct of the assemblies or the behaviour of participants. This element is implied purely by the Court's proceeding to full Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the only concrete facts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in this reasoning section.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding for the necessity assessment in this reasoning section. The Court does not identify or analyse any specific facts to support its conclusion that the interference was not necessary in a democratic society. Instead, it relies entirely on a reference to prior case-law: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject..." The necessity conclusion is thus grounded in legal precedent (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others), not in any facts explicitly stated or adopted in this section of the judgment.

**Summary observation:**

The factual basis of this decision is strikingly thin within this reasoning section. The Court relies almost entirely on the repetition of a brief characterisation of the measures complained of ("arrest," "dispersal," "conviction for administrative offences") and on the authority of prior judgments. No facts are elaborated upon, no party submissions are explicitly adopted as established, and no case-specific factual analysis is conducted. This is characteristic of a "clone case" or repetitive case judgment, where the Court disposes of the matter by reference to leading cases rather than conducting a fresh factual analysis.
