## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly state a finding of fact that an assembly existed. The factual grounding is implied by the framing of the entire case. No verbatim factual finding can be quoted for this sub-element specifically.

**ii) Participation**
Again, no explicit factual finding is stated by the Court. The applicants are treated throughout as organisers, but the Court does not make a discrete factual finding on this point that can be quoted.

**iii) Peaceful**
The Court does not make an explicit factual finding that the assembly was or would have been peaceful. The closest the Court comes is in the general principle it recites: *'where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 is not to be deprived of all substance.'* This is stated as a general legal principle, not as a factual finding about this specific assembly. No discrete factual finding on peacefulness can be quoted.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following fact, which it accepts without dispute:

*'The Court notes that the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11; nor did they deny that the ban on the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of their rights under that provision. It sees no reason to hold otherwise.'*

The underlying factual basis — that a ban was imposed on organising the demonstration on the road leading to the airport — is stated in the opening of the section: *'The applicants complained that the ban on organising a demonstration on the road leading to the airport had resulted in a disproportionate interference with their right to freedom of assembly.'* The Court does not separately restate this as its own factual finding, but proceeds on this basis throughout.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this conclusion on one factual/legal finding:

*'The Court observes that the ban was based on section 8(1) of the Assembly Act (see paragraph 12 above) and considers that it was thus prescribed by law.'*

No further factual elaboration is provided. The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not grounded in any identified facts.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not elaborate on the factual basis for its finding of legitimate aim beyond the following statement:

*'the Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others.'*

No specific facts are cited to ground this conclusion. The reference to 'the rights of others' can be contextually linked to the Government's submission about other road users, but the Court does not explicitly adopt specific facts in support of this finding — it simply declares itself satisfied.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court draws on the following facts, which it explicitly accepts or establishes:

**On the traffic disruption being foreseeable but limited:**
*'It sees no reason to doubt the domestic courts' findings to the effect that the demonstration held next to the road leading to the airport would have affected the flow of traffic leading to the airport.'*

**On the organisers' undertakings — explicitly adopted by the Court as relevant established facts:**
*'the organisers of the demonstration had undertaken not to provoke a major disruption to traffic or significant inconvenience for road-users, explaining that the number of demonstrators would be limited to fifty to 100 persons and they would only use the hard shoulder of the road, that the event would not last longer than two hours, and that it would be called off immediately if the demonstrators started to act unlawfully or if it exceeded the scope of the permit issued by the authorities (see paragraphs 6 and 8 above).'*

**On the Government's emergency argument being speculative:**
*'the Government's contention that the demonstration could have endangered a rescue operation in the event of an emergency is largely a matter of speculation.'*

**On the absence of intended disruption:**
*'no disruption to ordinary life and traffic was intended by the organisers and it would have constituted only a side-effect of a demonstration held in a public place.'*

**On the symbolic importance of the location:**
*'the site where the applicants had intended to hold their demonstration had a symbolic importance for the employees of the airport, and it is for this reason that they insisted on organising the demonstration there.'*

**On the authorities' failure to consider alternatives:**
*'The domestic authorities nonetheless did not embark on an assessment of whether the employees of the airport had at their disposal alternative means of protecting their interest. Instead of considering measures which could have allowed the assembly to proceed in a manner that did not cause undue disturbance to road traffic in the proximity of the airport, the authorities imposed a ban on it. They resorted to the most radical measure of denying the applicants the possibility of exercising their right to freedom of assembly.'*

**On the basis of the domestic court decision:**
*'in the domestic court decision dealing with the case, the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues (see paragraphs 9 and 11 above).'*

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The factual grounding for the positive obligation finding overlaps substantially with the necessity analysis. The Court grounds the violation of the positive obligation specifically on:

*'it was the authorities' duty to reflect on possible alternative solutions and propose other arrangements to the organisers.'*

And the breach of that obligation is grounded in:

*'The domestic authorities nonetheless did not embark on an assessment of whether the employees of the airport had at their disposal alternative means of protecting their interest. Instead of considering measures which could have allowed the assembly to proceed in a manner that did not cause undue disturbance to road traffic in the proximity of the airport, the authorities imposed a ban on it.'*

And further contextualised by the symbolic importance finding:

*'the site where the applicants had intended to hold their demonstration had a symbolic importance for the employees of the airport, and it is for this reason that they insisted on organising the demonstration there.'*
