**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation**: The only factual grounding is the following sentence from the opening paragraph: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the complaints, not merely a party submission. However, it should be noted that the Court does not independently verify or elaborate on these facts — it simply describes what the applicants complained of. Beyond this sentence, there is no further factual grounding for the scope elements.

- **Peaceful character**: There is no factual grounding whatsoever in the reasoning text. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the conduct of the assemblies, the behaviour of participants, or the presence or absence of violence.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same sentence as above: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most directly addressed, yet the factual grounding is strikingly thin. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This sentence references "all the material submitted" in the abstract but does not quote, summarise, or adopt any specific facts from that material. The Court's necessity finding is grounded not in facts recited in this reasoning section, but entirely by reference to prior case-law: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

In short: the necessity conclusion is grounded in the similarity of the present case to prior cases, but no specific facts establishing that similarity are recited in this reasoning section. There is no independent factual analysis of proportionality in the text provided.

**Summary observation**

The reasoning text is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. Across all elements discussed or implied, the Court relies on a single factual sentence (arrest, dispersal, conviction for administrative offences), a general reference to unspecified "material submitted," and an appeal to prior case-law. No facts are quoted from party submissions in a manner that the Court explicitly adopts as established.
