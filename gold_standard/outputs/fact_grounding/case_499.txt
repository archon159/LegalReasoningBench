**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." No further facts about the nature, location, size, or purpose of the assemblies are provided in this reasoning section.

**ii) Participation**
Same factual grounding as above: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." Additionally: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The arrest and conviction facts imply the applicants were present.

**iii) Peaceful**
There is no factual grounding for this element. The Court does not describe the conduct of the assemblies or the applicants' behaviour. The peacefulness of the assemblies is implied structurally by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, but no facts are cited to support this.

The factual grounding is explicit: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

These are the two concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference: (1) arrest connected to dispersal, and (2) conviction for administrative offences.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity assessment is notably thin on independent factual grounding. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This sentence acknowledges that material was examined but does not identify any specific facts from that material. The necessity conclusion rests primarily on the reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) rather than on independently stated facts. The only facts that can be connected to the necessity finding are the same facts identified under interference — "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" — since these are the measures whose necessity is being assessed.
