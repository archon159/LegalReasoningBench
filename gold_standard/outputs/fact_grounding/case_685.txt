## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly establish this as a finding of fact, but the following factual references ground the implied conclusion:
- "she had participated in the reading out of a press statement on the anniversary of the arrest of Abdullah Öcalan"
- "the public gathering in question"

**ii) Participation**
Grounded in the same factual finding:
- "the applicant was convicted under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for the sole reason that she had participated in the reading out of a press statement on the anniversary of the arrest of Abdullah Öcalan"

**iii) Peaceful character**
Grounded in the following factual observations, all made by the Court itself (not merely adopted from party submissions):
- "there is nothing in the case file showing that the applicant had been involved in violent acts or incited or had the intention of inciting others to violence during the public gathering in question"
- "She was not involved in the clashes between the demonstrators and the police"
- "Nor did she chant any slogans or carry banners containing expressions which might be construed as encouraging violence, armed resistance or an uprising or capable of inciting to violence"

---

### (b) Interference

Grounded in:
- "the applicant's conviction, although its pronouncement was suspended, amounted to an 'interference' with the exercise of her freedom of assembly"

The specific factual basis for the conviction is:
- "the applicant was convicted under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for the sole reason that she had participated in the reading out of a press statement on the anniversary of the arrest of Abdullah Öcalan"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declines to examine this element. The only factual grounding offered is the identification of the legal basis:
- "the interference was based on section 7(2) of Law no. 3713"

No further factual grounding is provided, as the Court does not proceed to examine foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the legitimate aims only tentatively ("prepared to accept") and does not ground this acceptance in specific facts of the case. No particular facts are cited in support of this finding. The Court's acceptance appears to rest on a general concession rather than on established facts, so no factual grounding can be identified here beyond the bare reference to the nature of the conviction under anti-terrorism legislation.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court's finding of a violation rests on the following facts:

On the absence of reasoning by the national courts:
- "the first-instance court's judgment does not contain any information as to the reasons for which the applicant was found guilty of disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK"
- "The Adana Assize Court, however, does not appear to have given consideration to any of the above"
- "the national courts did not adduce 'relevant and sufficient' reasons justifying the applicant's criminal conviction under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713"

On the applicant's conduct:
- "there is nothing in the case file showing that the applicant had been involved in violent acts or incited or had the intention of inciting others to violence during the public gathering in question"
- "She was not involved in the clashes between the demonstrators and the police"
- "Nor did she chant any slogans or carry banners containing expressions which might be construed as encouraging violence, armed resistance or an uprising or capable of inciting to violence"

On the sole basis for conviction:
- "the applicant was convicted under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for the sole reason that she had participated in the reading out of a press statement on the anniversary of the arrest of Abdullah Öcalan"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
