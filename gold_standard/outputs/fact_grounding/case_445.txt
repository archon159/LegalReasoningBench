I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**, namely the **dispersal of these assemblies**"

The Court does not elaborate further on the nature, size, location, or form of these assemblies.

**ii) Participation**
The only factual grounding is again from the opening paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies"

**iii) Peaceful character**
There is no factual grounding at all. The Court states no facts from which peacefulness could be inferred. This element is implied purely by the legal logic of proceeding under Article 11, not by any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is from the opening paragraph:
> "namely the **dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence**"

These are the only concrete facts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the reasoning text provides **no independent factual grounding** specific to the individual cases. The Court's conclusion rests entirely on:

1. Reference to prior case-law:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts); Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

2. A general statement of having reviewed the material:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it"

3. A negative statement — the absence of distinguishing facts or arguments:
> "the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints"

4. A reference to its case-law:
> "Having regard to its case-law on the subject"

In other words, the necessity finding is grounded not in facts explicitly stated in this reasoning section, but in the similarity to prior cases and the absence of any new distinguishing material. The Court does not quote, describe, or adopt any specific facts about what happened at the assemblies, why they were dispersed, or what justification the government offered.
