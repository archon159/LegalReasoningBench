**Factual grounding for each implied or explicitly discussed element:**

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding in the text is the phrase from the complaint framing: 'the peaceful demonstration that had been planned for 24 May 2012.' The Court does not independently verify or establish this fact — it appears only in the description of the second applicant's complaint. The Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact; it simply proceeds on this basis without comment. Strictly speaking, there is no verbatim factual finding by the Court itself on this point.

**ii) Participation (attempted):**
The only grounding is again from the complaint framing: 'he had been arrested and convicted by the authorities to prevent him from participating.' Same caveat applies — this is the applicant's complaint, not an independent factual finding by the Court.

**iii) Peaceful character:**
Same source: 'the peaceful demonstration that had been planned for 24 May 2012.' Same caveat applies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The only factual grounding is: 'he had been arrested and convicted by the authorities to prevent him from participating in the peaceful demonstration that had been planned for 24 May 2012.' Again, this appears in the framing of the complaint. The Court does not separately state these facts as established. However, the Court's conclusion that 'the second applicant's right to freedom of assembly was breached' implicitly treats the arrest and conviction as established facts, since a breach cannot logically be found without an interference having occurred.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (e) Necessity, (f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified in this excerpt for any of these elements. The Court provides no facts — and no analysis — in respect of these elements. It simply states:

'the Court sees no special circumstances that could compel it to deviate from its findings in those judgments, and finds that the second applicant's right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined in the above-mentioned judgments'

The factual and legal reasoning underlying those elements is located in the referenced prior judgments (Huseynli and Others, Huseynov and Others, Damirov and Others), not in this text. This excerpt provides no independent factual grounding for these elements.
