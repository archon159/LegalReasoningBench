Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants or organisers of **public assemblies**, namely their arrest in relation to the **dispersal of these assemblies**"

The Court itself uses the word "assemblies" and refers to their dispersal, which constitutes the Court's own characterisation of the events.

**ii) Participation:**
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as **participants or organisers** of public assemblies"

The Court itself describes the applicants as participants or organisers.

**iii) Peaceful character:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. This element is implied only by the logical structure of the analysis, not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit:
> "their **arrest** in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These two concrete state acts — arrest and conviction — are the Court's own description of what happened and constitute the factual basis for the interference finding.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed, and no factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed, and no factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most notable finding: **no independent factual grounding can be identified** in this reasoning section. The Court does not cite any specific facts about, for example, the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, the nature of the offences charged, or the severity of the sanctions. Instead, the Court grounds its necessity assessment entirely by reference to prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of **Frumkin v. Russia**, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), **Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia**, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and **Kasparov and Others v. Russia**, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The necessity conclusion is thus reached by analogy with precedent, not by explicit engagement with the specific facts of the present case.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed, and no factual grounding can be identified.
