Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
However, this is drawn from the applicants' complaints, not from the Court's own factual findings. The Court does not independently establish the existence of assemblies as a factual matter. There is no passage where the Court explicitly adopts this as an established fact.

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." Again, this comes from the framing of the applicants' complaints. The Court does not independently verify or state as established fact that the applicants participated.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. This element is implied only by the logical structure of the analysis, not grounded in any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding in the following factual statement from the opening paragraph:

"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the closest the Court comes to identifying concrete facts underpinning the interference. However, as with scope, this is presented as a summary of the applicants' complaints rather than as an independent factual finding by the Court. The Court does not separately state "we find as a fact that the applicants were arrested and convicted."

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court provides no independent factual grounding for its necessity assessment in this text. It states:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a purely procedural statement about the absence of new material, not a factual finding. The Court's necessity conclusion rests entirely on its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov) rather than on any facts it identifies and establishes in this decision. No specific facts about the proportionality of the government's response are stated or adopted.

---

**Overall observation:**
This reasoning section is strikingly thin on factual grounding. The Court operates almost entirely by reference to prior case-law and a general reference to "all the material submitted," without identifying or adopting specific facts to support any element of the Article 11 analysis. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case procedure where the Court treats the matter as essentially resolved by precedent.
