**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope**

- **Assembly exists / Participation:** The only factual grounding is the phrase "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." These are the verbatim facts from which the Court implicitly derives that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. No further factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

- **Peaceful character:** There is no factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the conduct of the assemblies, the behaviour of participants, or the absence of violence. The peaceful character is implied purely by the Court's willingness to proceed, not by any stated fact.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the only concrete facts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual elaboration is provided.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding in this reasoning text. The Court's necessity finding is grounded entirely in its prior case-law — specifically the references to *Frumkin v. Russia*, *Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia*, and *Kasparov and Others v. Russia* — and the statement that it found "no fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." The Court does not identify any specific facts of the present case that bear on the proportionality assessment. The necessity conclusion is essentially derived by analogy with precedent, not from case-specific factual analysis.

**(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, these elements are not discussed. Consequently, there is no factual grounding to identify for any of them.
