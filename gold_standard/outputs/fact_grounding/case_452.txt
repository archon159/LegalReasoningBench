I will work through each canonical element where I found explicit discussion or implication, and identify the factual grounding the Court actually relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists / Participation:**
The only factual grounding comes from the introductory paragraph of section III:

"The applicants in applications nos. 37565/08, 37581/08, 42250/08, and 39189/09 complained about the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of the public events, as well as the allegedly unlawful arrest of Mr Nagibin and Mr Nikolayev during some of those public events."

This is thin — the Court characterises the subject matter of the complaints but does not itself make factual findings about what occurred at the assemblies. The Court does not quote or adopt specific facts about the assemblies themselves in the reasoning section reproduced here.

**Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court makes no factual finding on this point.

**Participation (negative case — Mr Nikolayev's arrest, application no. 42250/08):**
The Court does provide factual grounding for why the scope element was *not* satisfied for the specific complaint about the 24-26 November 2007 arrest:

"Unlike Mr Nagibin, the applicant in the present application (Mr Nikolayev) was not apprehended at the venue of the public event that he had allegedly intended to join on the day of the arrest. Moreover, in the administrative proceedings Mr Nikolayev did not argue that the measures taken in his respect had infringed his freedom of assembly."

These are the facts the Court relied upon to conclude it could not establish "a clear and acknowledged link between the measures taken against the applicant and his exercise of the freedom of peaceful assembly."

**(b) Interference**

The Court states: "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements."

The factual grounding for the existence of interference is found in the introductory paragraph:

"The applicants in applications nos. 37565/08, 37581/08, 42250/08, and 39189/09 complained about the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of the public events, as well as the allegedly unlawful arrest of Mr Nagibin and Mr Nikolayev during some of those public events."

Beyond this characterisation, the Court does not set out specific facts about the nature or content of the restrictions in the reproduced reasoning section. It relies on the material examined ("Having examined all the material submitted to it") without quoting or adopting specific facts from that material in the reasoning text.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court's finding is: "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements."

There is **no factual grounding** for this finding in the reproduced reasoning text. The Court simply refers back to the leading case of Lashmankin and Others:

"In the leading case of Lashmankin and Others (cited above, §§ 402-78), the Court found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in applications nos. 37565/08, 37581/08 and 42250/08. Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion in the present case."

The Court does not identify which specific legal provisions were at issue, what their wording was, or what features of those provisions failed the quality of law test. The factual and legal analysis is entirely delegated by reference to Lashmankin.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's finding is that the interference was "moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

Again, there is **no factual grounding** in the reproduced reasoning text. As with the prescribed-by-law element, the Court relies entirely on the reference to Lashmankin and the general statement that it has "not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." No facts about proportionality, the weight of the legitimate aim, or the intensity of the interference are set out.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**Overall observation:** The reasoning section reproduced here is notably sparse in its factual grounding. For the core merits findings (prescribed by law and necessity), the Court provides no independent factual analysis, relying entirely on the precedent of Lashmankin and Others by way of a formulaic reference. The only substantive factual reasoning in the reproduced text concerns the *inadmissibility* of Mr Nikolayev's specific complaint about his arrest.
