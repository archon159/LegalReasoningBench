I will go through each canonical element where the Court made a finding, and identify only the facts explicitly relied upon by the Court itself.

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly state a finding on this, but treats it as established from the framing. The factual grounding is implicit in the Court's own references to 'the demonstration of 2 April 2011' and 'the dispersal of the demonstrations by the police.' No specific factual passage grounds this as a distinct finding.

**(ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this in its own assessment by adopting the Gafgaz Mammadov analysis, specifically: 'there had been no acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention (ibid., §§ 63-64).' The factual basis for participation is implied by the Court's reference to the applicants' 'arrest and conviction for an administrative offence' in the context of the demonstrations. No separate explicit factual finding is made beyond this.

**(iii) Peaceful**
The Court explicitly grounds this finding in the following facts:

'The Court notes from the outset that none of the official records or the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties suggests that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was violent. Therefore the Court accepts the assertion of the applicants in applications nos. 67977/11, 69411/11 and 69421/11 that that demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and was conducted in a peaceful manner up to the point of their arrest.'

And further, adopted from Gafgaz Mammadov:

'there was a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner (ibid., § 61)'

The factual grounding is therefore: the absence of any indication of violence in official records or domestic court decisions.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not make a separate explicit factual finding establishing interference. The factual grounding is embedded in the framing of the complaint itself, which the Court adopts as its starting point: 'the dispersal of the demonstrations by the police and their arrest and conviction for an administrative offence.' The Court treats these as established facts throughout but does not isolate them as a distinct factual finding on interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds its concerns on the following, adopted from Gafgaz Mammadov:

'(i) there were serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed (ibid., § 55)'

'(ii) there were doubts about the credibility of the formal grounds, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the participant in an unauthorised demonstration (ibid., §§ 56 and 62)'

No additional independent factual findings are made by the Court in this judgment beyond these references to Gafgaz Mammadov. The Court does not itself examine the text of the legislation or the domestic court decisions on this point.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court makes no explicit factual finding of its own on this element. The Government's assertion that the dispersal 'pursued the aim of protecting public safety and preventing disorder or crime' is recorded in the parties' submissions section but is not explicitly adopted by the Court as an established fact. No factual grounding can be identified for this element in the Court's own assessment.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court grounds its finding on the following facts, adopted from Gafgaz Mammadov:

'(iii) the authorities had failed to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified (ibid., § 60)'

'(iv) there was a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner (ibid., § 61)'

'(v) there had been no acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention (ibid., §§ 63-64)'

Additionally, the Court's own finding on peacefulness — 'none of the official records or the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties suggests that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was violent' — feeds directly into the necessity assessment, as the absence of violence is the factual basis for finding no sufficient justification for dispersal.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
