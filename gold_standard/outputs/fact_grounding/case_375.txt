I will go through each element where the Court made a finding, and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists / Applicant participation:**
The Court does not explicitly ground these in stated facts. It simply notes the Government did not dispute the applicant's reliance on Article 11 guarantees and accepts this without further factual elaboration: 'It sees no reason to hold otherwise.' No specific facts are quoted by the Court to ground these elements.

**Peaceful character:**
The Court refers to the assembly as 'the planned peaceful assembly' and invokes the general principle that 'where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings.' However, the Court does not cite any specific facts establishing that this particular assembly was peaceful. The characterisation appears to be accepted without explicit factual grounding beyond the label itself.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference finding solely on the Government's non-denial: 'The Court notes that the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11; nor did they deny that the ban on the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of his rights under that provision. It sees no reason to hold otherwise.'

No independent factual findings are made by the Court on this element.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds this finding in one fact: 'The Court observes that the ban was based on section 8(1) of the Assembly Act and considers that it was thus prescribed by law.'

The sole factual grounding is the identification of the legal basis — section 8(1) of the Assembly Act. No further facts are cited.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court states: 'the Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others.'

The Court does not explicitly cite specific facts of its own to ground this. It appears to accept the Government's framing that the ban related to traffic concerns and the rights of other road users, but the Court does not explicitly adopt any particular factual submission as established. The finding is stated as a conclusion without identified factual grounding beyond the general context of a traffic-related ban.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most richly grounded in facts. The Court identifies the following:

**On the domestic proceedings:**
'The Court observes that in the domestic court decision dealing with the case, the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues.'

**On the Government's submissions (noted but not explicitly adopted as established facts — rather used to characterise the justification offered):**
'The Government's submissions were, in essence, confined to the affirmation that the demonstration would have seriously hampered the free flow of traffic in the area.'

**On the general principle applied to the facts:**
'a demonstration in a public place may cause a certain level of disruption to ordinary life including disruption of traffic. However, where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 is not to be deprived of all substance.'

**On the location:**
'the Court bases its findings on those in the Körtvélyessy case (see paragraphs 29 and 30 of that judgment, cited above), which concerned a similar problem relating to the very same location.'

The Court does not reproduce the Körtvélyessy factual findings verbatim here, but explicitly incorporates them by reference as the factual basis for its conclusion: 'For essentially the same reasons as in that case, the Court finds that the reason for banning the planned peaceful assembly was, if at all relevant, not sufficient to meet any pressing social need.'

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
