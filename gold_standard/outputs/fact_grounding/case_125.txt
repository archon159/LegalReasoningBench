## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope — implied

**Assembly exists / Applicant participated / Peaceful character:**

The Court does not explicitly ground these elements in specific facts. The reasoning text contains no verbatim factual findings about the nature of the assembly, the applicant's role in it, or its peaceful character. The scope elements are implied solely by the Court's legal characterisation ("his right to freedom of peaceful assembly") rather than by any identified underlying facts. **No factual grounding can be quoted.**

---

### (b) Interference — explicitly discussed

The Court grounds its finding of interference in one concrete fact:

> "the fine imposed on the applicant by the decision of the President of the Criminal and Military Court of Appeal of 1 March 2003 undoubtedly interfered with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

The factual basis is thus the imposition of a fine by a specific judicial decision on a specific date. No further underlying facts about the circumstances of the assembly or the applicant's conduct are cited by the Court in this connection.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law — explicitly discussed

The Court's assessment rests on the following factual findings:

**On the legal basis invoked:**
> "the applicant was convicted under Article 180.1 of the CAO for 'violation of the prescribed rules for organising or holding assemblies, rallies, street marches and demonstrations'."

Note: this is drawn from the Government's submission, but the Court accepts it as the established legal basis for the conviction and reasons from it directly.

**On the inapplicability of the USSR Law:**
> "there was no legal act applicable in Armenia which contained the 'prescribed rules' referred to in that provision. The USSR Law of 28 October 1988 was no longer applicable..."

**On the absence of a replacement law at the material time:**
> "a new law on assemblies and rallies was adopted only on 28 April 2004."

**On the temporal relationship between the interference and the legal gap:**
> "the interference in the present case similarly took place before the enactment of a new law on assemblies and rallies."

These four factual findings together ground the Court's conclusion that the interference was not prescribed by law.
