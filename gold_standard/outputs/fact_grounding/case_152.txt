I will work through each doctrinal element in turn, identifying only facts that the Court itself relied upon (not merely party assertions), quoting verbatim from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly ground this in stated facts. It is implied by references such as "the demonstration of 11 March 2011" and "the dispersal of the demonstrations in which they had participated," but the Court makes no factual finding about the existence of an assembly as a distinct doctrinal step. No verbatim quote can be offered as a Court finding on this point.

**Participation**
For the first applicant in his first application, the Court grounds participation in the following facts it accepts:
> "the applicant's affiliation with the opposition was likewise generally known, as he was a member of the PFPA, he had participated in various protests held by the opposition, and he had promoted public participation in the demonstration of 11 March 2011 on online social networks"

For the second applicant and first applicant in the second application, participation is implied by the framing of the complaints ("the dispersal of the demonstrations in which they had participated") but the Court makes no separate factual finding on this as a doctrinal step.

**Peaceful**
For the second applicant and first applicant in the second application, the Court grounds this in the following accepted fact (by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov):
> "there had been a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which the organisers had intended to be peaceful and which had been conducted in a peaceful manner"

For the first applicant in his first application, no explicit factual grounding for peacefulness is provided.

---

**(b) Interference**

For the first applicant in his first application, the Court grounds interference in the following facts:

> "the applicant was arrested on dubious grounds shortly before the demonstration of 11 March 2011"

> "the domestic courts failed to establish facts that were disputed between the parties – the police and the applicant – by carrying out an objective and thorough examination. They ignored the applicant's submissions that the arrest had been politically motivated, and merely recapitulated the circumstances and the charges as presented by the police"

> "the proceedings against the applicant in the present case and the ensuing administrative detention were arbitrary and unlawful measures aimed at punishing the applicant for his political activity, including his previous participation in opposition protests, and preventing him from participating in or promoting such protests"

> "The applicant's arrest and administrative detention could not but have had the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate"

For the second applicant and first applicant in the second application, interference is grounded in:
> "the dispersal of the demonstration and his arrest and conviction" (by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov, adopted by the Court as applicable to the present cases)

> "both applicants' right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined above"

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

**Foreseeability and safeguards against abuse**
The Court grounds these findings exclusively by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov, which it explicitly adopts as applicable:

> "there were serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being arbitrarily banned or dispersed"

> "there were doubts about the credibility of the formal grounds, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the participant in an unauthorised demonstration"

> "there had been no acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration had itself been protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

**Accessibility**
No factual grounding identified. The Court makes no finding on this sub-element.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court makes no factual finding accepting any legitimate aim. The Government's assertion that the dispersal "pursued the aim of protecting the rights of others" is recorded only as a submission and is not adopted by the Court. No verbatim quote can be offered as a Court finding on this point.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

For the second applicant and first applicant in the second application, the Court grounds the necessity finding in the following facts, adopted from Gafgaz Mammadov:

> "the authorities had failed to take into consideration the fact that notification had been given about the demonstration"

> "there had been a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which the organisers had intended to be peaceful and which had been conducted in a peaceful manner"

> "there had been no acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration had itself been protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

For the first applicant in his first application, the Court grounds its equivalent finding (framed as arbitrariness rather than formal necessity) in:

> "the applicant was arrested on dubious grounds shortly before the demonstration of 11 March 2011"

> "the domestic courts failed to establish facts that were disputed between the parties – the police and the applicant – by carrying out an objective and thorough examination. They ignored the applicant's submissions that the arrest had been politically motivated, and merely recapitulated the circumstances and the charges as presented by the police"

> "the proceedings against the applicant in the present case and the ensuing administrative detention were arbitrary and unlawful measures aimed at punishing the applicant for his political activity"

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding identified. The Court makes no finding on positive obligations in this judgment.
