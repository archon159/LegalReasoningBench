**Factual grounding for each element discussed or implied:**

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists — implied**

The factual grounding is: "public assemblies" and "dispersal of these assemblies." Verbatim: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(ii) Participation — implied**

The factual grounding is the same passage: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(iii) Peaceful — implied**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not refer to any facts establishing the peaceful nature of the assemblies. The implication that the assemblies were peaceful rests purely on the legal logic of proceeding under Article 11, not on any stated facts in this reasoning section.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." Verbatim: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, but the factual grounding is notably thin in this reasoning section. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints." This is a reference to the totality of the material, but no specific facts are quoted or identified in support of the necessity finding. The Court relies instead on its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) by analogy: "the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case." The only concrete facts stated in the entire reasoning section — the arrest, dispersal, and conviction for administrative offences — are thus the implicit factual basis, but the Court does not explicitly map these facts onto the necessity analysis.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed.
