## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this implicitly in:
> "the applicant and others clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia."

**(ii) Participation**

Grounded in:
> "the applicant and others clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

**(iii) Peaceful character**

The Court does not separately establish facts going to the peaceful/non-peaceful character for scoping purposes. The violent character is addressed under necessity (see below). No distinct factual finding is made at the scope stage. **No factual grounding identifiable at this stage.**

---

### (b) Interference

Grounded in:
> "The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's conclusion is grounded in the identification of legal provisions:
> "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 28-29 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law (see paragraph 32 above)."

And on the principle regarding "TRNC" laws:
> "when, as in the Foka case, an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law for the purposes of the Convention."

No further facts are cited going to foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards, consistent with the finding above that these sub-elements were not separately analyzed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not make an explicit, separate factual finding to ground acceptance of a legitimate aim. However, the factual findings made under necessity (see below) implicitly support the aims of prevention of disorder, prevention of crime, and public safety. The Court's statement that connects facts to justification is:

> "interferences with the right guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention are in principle justified for the prevention of disorder or crime and for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others where, as in the instant case, demonstrators engage in acts of violence."

The phrase "as in the instant case" links this legal principle to the factual findings on violence made immediately prior, but the Court does not isolate a distinct factual finding specifically for the legitimate aim stage. The factual grounding is shared with the necessity analysis.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court draws on the following established facts:

On the violent character of the demonstration, adopting the Commission's findings:
> "in the case of Chrysostomos and Papachrysostomou, the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."

> "The Court sees no reason to depart from these findings."

On the specific conduct of demonstrators, adopting the UN Secretary General's report:
> "in his report, the UN Secretary General stated that the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."

On the entry into "TRNC" territory:
> "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory."

On the reason for intervention:
> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

The proportionality conclusion is then grounded in all of the above:
> "In these conditions and having regard to the wide margin of appreciation left to the States in this sphere, the Court holds that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly was not, in the light of all the circumstances of the case, disproportionate for the purposes of Article 11 § 2."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions the positive obligation as a general legal principle only:
> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens."

> "However, they cannot guarantee this absolutely and they have a wide discretion in the choice of the means to be used."

**No specific facts are identified or applied by the Court in relation to this obligation.** It is invoked as background jurisprudence, not applied to concrete findings of fact. No factual grounding identifiable.
