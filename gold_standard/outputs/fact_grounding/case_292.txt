**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding in the reasoning text is notably thin. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. No specific facts from the present case are quoted or adopted as established in support of the necessity finding.

**Overall observation:**
The reasoning text is extremely sparse in its factual grounding. The Court relies almost entirely on precedent and a general reference to "all the material submitted," without identifying or adopting any specific facts from the record. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case procedure (likely a well-established case-law application under Rule 28 or similar), where the Court treats the facts as sufficiently analogous to prior leading cases without restating them.
