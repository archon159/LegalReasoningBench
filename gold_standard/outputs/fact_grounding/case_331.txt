## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

#### (i) Has there been an assembly?
The Court's finding that the events fall within Article 11's scope is grounded in the following facts, accepted by the Court:

The Court accepted the characterisation of the events as assemblies by distinguishing the present case from *Kartal v. Turkey*:
> "The Court observes that the indictment dated 8 May 2007 did not contain any charge against the applicant on account of alleged acts of violence... the applicant was not convicted of committing acts of violence in the judgments of 30 November 2007 and 28 April 2009."

The nature of the events themselves is described in the merits section, where the Court accepts as established:
> "He was sentenced to six years and three months in prison on the grounds that he had attended the funeral of four PKK militants, had walked in front of one of the coffins during the funeral and made a 'V' sign, and that he had applauded while other demonstrators chanted slogans in support of Abdullah Öcalan during a gathering at his university."

And:
> "his attendance at two public meetings, which, according to the first-instance court, were held in line with the instructions by the PKK, and his acts therein, that is to say, walking close to coffins and making a 'V' sign during the funeral and applauding during the demonstration."

---

#### (ii) Did the applicant participate in the assembly?
The Court grounds this in the same factual findings accepted as established:

> "He was sentenced to six years and three months in prison on the grounds that he had attended the funeral of four PKK militants, had walked in front of one of the coffins during the funeral and made a 'V' sign, and that he had applauded while other demonstrators chanted slogans in support of Abdullah Öcalan during a gathering at his university."

And:
> "his attendance at two public meetings... and his acts therein, that is to say, walking close to coffins and making a 'V' sign during the funeral and applauding during the demonstration."

---

#### (iii) Was the assembly peaceful?
The Court grounds its finding of peacefulness in the absence of charges or convictions for violence:

> "The Court observes that the indictment dated 8 May 2007 did not contain any charge against the applicant on account of alleged acts of violence... the applicant was not convicted of committing acts of violence in the judgments of 30 November 2007 and 28 April 2009."

And in the merits section, the Court characterises the applicant's conduct directly:
> "on account of his conviction, for acts which fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention, there remained no distinction between the applicant, a peaceful demonstrator, and an individual who had committed offences within the structure of the PKK."

And:
> "the very essence of the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and, thereby, the foundations of a democratic society, was undermined when the applicant was held criminally liable under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 of the Criminal Code for the mere fact of attending a public meeting and expressing his views therein."

---

### (b) Interference
The Court grounds the finding of interference in the criminal conviction and sentence imposed on the applicant:

> "In the instant case, the Court considers that there has been an interference with the applicant's exercise of his right to freedom of assembly on account of his conviction for membership of an illegal organisation under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code based on his participation in a funeral and a demonstration."

And:
> "the applicant was given a prison sentence of six years and three months under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code and was imprisoned for a period of four years and eight months in total."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

#### (i) Accessibility
The Court's finding of accessibility is not grounded in specific facts about the applicant's situation. The Court simply states:
> "The Court considers that there is no doubt that Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code was accessible."

No specific factual basis beyond the existence of the provision is articulated.

---

#### (ii) Foreseeability and (iii) Safeguards against abuse
These two elements are treated together by the Court. The factual grounding is extensive.

**On the text of the provision and its operation:**
> "the text of Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code tied the status of membership of an illegal organisation to the mere fact of a person having acted 'on behalf' of that organisation, without the prosecution having to prove the material elements of actual membership. Furthermore, the wording of Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code did not itself define the meaning of the expression 'on behalf of an illegal organisation'."

**On the Court of Cassation's interpretation:**
> "The Court of Cassation also held that if a person participated in a demonstration which had been called by an illegal armed organisation, there was no need for such calls to target particular individuals. It found that under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code, any offence during such a demonstration meant the offender should be considered as having acted on behalf of the organisation concerned, with its knowledge and in line with its wishes and, as a result, be punished for being a member of that organisation."

**On how the provision was applied to the applicant specifically:**
> "He was convicted of membership of the PKK on the basis of Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code. He was sentenced to six years and three months in prison on the grounds that he had attended the funeral of four PKK militants, had walked in front of one of the coffins during the funeral and made a 'V' sign, and that he had applauded while other demonstrators chanted slogans in support of Abdullah Öcalan during a gathering at his university. The courts considered that since both the funeral and the demonstration had been held following calls and instructions issued by the PKK, the applicant, who had participated in those events, should be considered as having acted on behalf of that organisation."

**On the contrast with how Article 314 operates when applied alone:**
> "when Article 314 of the Criminal Code is applied alone, the domestic courts must have regard to the 'continuity, diversity and intensity' of the acts of the accused... whereas when the same Article was applied in connection with Article 220 § 6, in the applicant's case, he was convicted of membership of an armed organisation merely on account of his attendance at two public meetings, which, according to the first-instance court, were held in line with the instructions by the PKK, and his acts therein, that is to say, walking close to coffins and making a 'V' sign during the funeral and applauding during the demonstration."

And:
> "when Article 314 is applied alone, the courts assess whether an accused person has committed offences within the 'hierarchical structure' of an armed organisation. On the other hand, when the same Article was applied with reference to Article 220 § 6 in the applicant's case, the question of acting within a hierarchy became irrelevant and he was convicted of membership of an armed organisation simply because he was considered to have acted 'on behalf of' the PKK."

**On the severity of the sanction and its chilling effect:**
> "when demonstrators, such as the applicant, face the charge of membership of an illegal armed organisation, they risk an additional sentence of between five and ten years in prison, a sanction which is strikingly severe and grossly disproportionate to their conduct."

And:
> "the application of the provision at issue was not only likely to deter those who were found criminally liable from re-exercising their rights under Articles 10 and 11 of the Convention, but also had a great deal of potential to deter other members of the public from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate."

**Overall conclusion on the factual basis:**
> "the array of acts that potentially constitute a basis for the application of a severe criminal sanction in the form of imprisonment, under Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code, are so vast that the wording of the provision, including its extensive interpretation by the domestic courts, do not afford a sufficient measure of protection against arbitrary interferences by the public authorities."

---

### (d) Legitimate aim
As noted in the prior analysis, the Court made no finding on legitimate aim. No factual grounding can be identified for this element from the Court's own assessment.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society
The Court did not formally assess necessity as a separate element. No factual grounding can be identified for a standalone necessity assessment. The proportionality observations made by the Court appear within the prescribed by law analysis rather than as an independent necessity finding.

---

### (f) Positive obligation
Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.
