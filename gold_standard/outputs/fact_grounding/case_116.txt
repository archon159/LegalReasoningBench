## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

> "the group in question concerned around seventy to eighty people. It appears that they were carrying banners and chanting slogans to protest against the killing of their fellow student."

> "the gathering had been a peaceful one"

> "According to the video footage, the demonstration lasted just over eleven minutes."

**ii) Participation**

> "the applicants were prosecuted - and convicted at first instance - on account of the mere fact of their participation in an unauthorised but peaceful demonstration."

> "the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the demonstration"

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

> "on the basis of the video footage of the demonstration, the domestic courts have already established that the gathering had been a peaceful one and that neither the applicants nor any other student had resorted to any acts of violence in the course of the demonstration."

---

### (b) Interference

> "the gendarmes' intervention, the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the demonstration, and the prolonged criminal proceedings against the applicants"

> "the criminal proceedings against the applicants are still pending in Turkey"

> "the applicants were prosecuted - and convicted at first instance - on account of the mere fact of their participation in an unauthorised but peaceful demonstration."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

> "the legal basis for the interference was section 32 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act"

The Court goes no further in its factual grounding here. It states the legal basis and immediately concludes the interference was prescribed by law, without grounding the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards in any specific facts.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No specific facts are cited. The Court simply states it "is prepared to accept" the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting public safety. This is a bare acceptance, not grounded in any specific factual findings. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's assessment of necessity is grounded in several distinct factual findings:

**On the peaceful nature of the gathering:**
> "the domestic courts have already established that the gathering had been a peaceful one and that neither the applicants nor any other student had resorted to any acts of violence in the course of the demonstration."

**On the absence of danger to public order:**
> "the Government gave no particular reasons, such as the specificities of the location of the demonstration, to show that this group represented a danger to public order or public safety."

> "Having particular regard to the fact that the gathering took place on the grounds of a university... the Court finds no such evidence of its own motion."

**On the nature of the prosecution:**
> "the applicants were prosecuted - and convicted at first instance - on account of the mere fact of their participation in an unauthorised but peaceful demonstration."

**On the brevity of the demonstration:**
> "the dispersal was quite prompt. According to the video footage, the demonstration lasted just over eleven minutes. Consequently, it is not satisfied that the applicants had sufficient time – together with their fellow demonstrators – to manifest their views."

**On the unauthorised but non-violent character:**
> "the group in question concerned around seventy to eighty people. It appears that they were carrying banners and chanting slogans to protest against the killing of their fellow student."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
