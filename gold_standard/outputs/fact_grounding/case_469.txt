## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i. Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly analyze this as a threshold question, but the following facts ground the implied finding:

> "undeterred by the authorities' refusal to approve the location in the town centre chosen by them, the protesters gathered at that location and remained there for more than two months"

> "The protesters were not prevented from re-erecting the tents and from remaining at the location for as long as they wished. They were able to display placards and banners stating their criticisms and demands for the duration of the demonstration."

**ii. Participation**

The Court does not analyze this as a threshold question, but grounds it implicitly in:

> "none of the protesters was brought to liability in connection with his participation in the demonstration"

The applicants are referred to throughout as participants, but no specific fact is stated by the Court establishing their individual participation beyond this reference.

**iii. Peaceful character**

The Court does not formally analyze this as a threshold question, but the following facts ground the implied finding:

> "the protesters had not chanted any slogans or made any other noise. Their act of protest had consisted of displaying placards criticising the authorities for their unlawful actions."

Note: this is drawn from the applicants' submissions. The Court does not explicitly state that it accepts these facts as established. However, the Court's own reasoning proceeds on the basis that the assembly was peaceful, as evidenced by its application of the tolerance principle:

> "where participants do not engage in acts of violence it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful assemblies"

The Court applies this principle to the present case without qualification, implying it accepted the peaceful character of the assembly. Additionally:

> "even though the protesters refused to comply with the dispersal order, the police did not resort to force to disperse them"

This fact, stated by the Court as established, is consistent with and supports the peaceful character of the assembly, though it describes police conduct rather than protester conduct directly.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference in the following explicitly stated facts:

> "the authorities' refusal to approve the location chosen by the applicants, the judicial decision declaring the demonstration unlawful, the order to disperse and the subsequent dismantling of the protesters' tents by the police constituted an interference with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly"

> "on at least one occasion, about two weeks after the beginning of the demonstration, the police requested the protesters to disperse and dismantled their tents"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

No factual grounding is provided. The Court simply notes the matter is uncontested:

> "It is not contested that the interference was 'prescribed by law'"

No facts relating to the quality, accessibility, or foreseeability of the legal basis are stated by the Court.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No specific facts are provided beyond the Court's acceptance of the uncontested position:

> "It is not contested that the interference was 'prescribed by law' and 'pursued a legitimate aim', that of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others, for the purposes of Article 11 § 2."

The Government's submissions about terrorist activity and disruption to public life are noted by the Court but are not explicitly adopted as established facts. The Court merely "takes note" of them:

> "The Court takes note of the Government's arguments that the demonstration in which the applicants participated was unlawful and moreover disrupted the everyday life of the town centre."

The phrase "takes note of the Government's arguments" falls short of an explicit adoption of those facts as established, and so they are not quoted here as factual grounding.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding, all explicitly stated by the Court:

On the duration of the demonstration and the authorities' tolerance:

> "undeterred by the authorities' refusal to approve the location in the town centre chosen by them, the protesters gathered at that location and remained there for more than two months"

> "the Court notes that in the present case the authorities showed tolerance towards the applicants' demonstration despite the fact that it had been declared unlawful"

On the police conduct during the dispersal attempt:

> "on at least one occasion, about two weeks after the beginning of the demonstration, the police requested the protesters to disperse and dismantled their tents. However, even though the protesters refused to comply with the dispersal order, the police did not resort to force to disperse them."

On the protesters' continued ability to demonstrate:

> "The protesters were not prevented from re-erecting the tents and from remaining at the location for as long as they wished. They were able to display placards and banners stating their criticisms and demands for the duration of the demonstration."

On the absence of sanctions:

> "none of the protesters was brought to liability in connection with his participation in the demonstration"

These facts collectively ground the Court's conclusion:

> "the applicants' demonstration lasted sufficiently long for them to express their position of protest and to draw the attention of the public to their concerns"

> "it cannot be said that the authorities overstepped the margin of appreciation afforded to them in that particular sphere or that the measures taken against the applicants' demonstration were disproportionate to the legitimate aims pursued"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
