# Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

## (a) Scope

### i) Has there been an assembly?

The Court's conclusion that the events fall within the scope of Article 11 is grounded in the following facts:

- The applicants had permission to protest and used it: "the applicants had permission to organise a meeting on the premises of the University on 3 July 2006 and that they had availed themselves of that opportunity. During the first phase of their protest on that day they gathered, as duly authorised by the University administration, in one of the lecture halls."
- The subsequent movement to the Rector's office: "They moved, however, soon afterwards to the acting Rector's office, protesting against the ongoing University reform and demanding his resignation."
- The nature of the conduct: "although not an uncommon occurrence in the context of the exercise of freedom of assembly in modern societies, physical conduct purposely obstructing the ordinary course of life in order to seriously disrupt the activities carried out by others is not at the core of that freedom as protected by Article 11 of the Convention."
- The overall character of the protest: "the applicants' protest on 3 July 2006, viewed as a whole, was not of such a nature and degree as to exclude them from the scope of protection under Article 11 of the Convention, read in the light of Article 10."

### ii) Participation

No specific factual grounding is identifiable beyond what is stated under (i) above. The Court does not separately ground participation as a distinct finding.

### iii) Was the assembly peaceful?

The Court's finding of sufficient peacefulness is grounded in:

- Absence of established violence: "the applicants were not held responsible for using violence."
- The tense but non-violent character: "While the events at issue happened in a situation of tension, the applicants' conduct was not established to have been of a violent nature."
- The acting Rector's own pre-trial statement, which the Court explicitly notes as "noteworthy": "the pre-trial statement of the acting Rector himself, who noted that he had not been personally insulted and that no foul language had been used by protesters in his office, either with respect to him or to his colleagues."

---

## (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in:

- The physical removal: "the applicants were removed by police from the office of the acting Rector of the University."
- The administrative proceedings: "Subsequently, they were charged and found responsible for several administrative offences in connection with what had happened in the University."
- The Court's conclusion: "their removal and administrative responsibility constituted an interference with their right to freedom of assembly."

---

## (c) Prescribed by law

### Overall lawfulness (removal)

- Legal basis accepted: "The Court accepts that the impugned interference had a basis in domestic law."
- Police acted on request: "the police acted at the request of the acting Rector of the University."
- Prior warnings given: "The removal was preceded by G.Kh. and other administrative staff members, and then by the police, explicitly and repeatedly asking the applicants to leave the acting Rector's office."

### Overall lawfulness (administrative sanctions)

- The Court states simply: "the Court considers that the interference by way of imposing administrative sanctions in the current case was prescribed by law." No specific facts are cited beyond the general context already established.

### i) Foreseeability

- The Court grounds foreseeability on the nature of the applicants' conduct and the domestic courts' interpretation of Article 166 CAO: "the applicants could have foreseen, to a degree reasonable in the circumstances, that their actions entailing disruption to the functioning of the University, could have been deemed to amount to a minor breach of public order attracting the application of Article 166 of the CAO."
- The disruption itself: "the Court cannot overlook the fact that the applicants' conduct did indeed disrupt public order on the premises of the University."
- The domestic courts' non-arbitrary interpretation: "the interpretation of this provision given by the domestic courts in the present case was not arbitrary."

### ii) Accessibility

No factual grounding identifiable. Not discussed.

### iii) Safeguards against abuse

No factual grounding identifiable. Not discussed.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

### Removal

The Court's acceptance of legitimate aim is grounded in:

- The escalation of events as established by the national court: "on 3 July 2006, after the impugned 'election' of the second applicant as the new Rector of the University, the situation at the University escalated, with the applicants entering by force the office of the acting Rector and many more protesters chanting anti-G.Kh. slogans in the corridor of the University and in its courtyard."
- The disruption to the University: "in view of the scale and duration of the protest, also having regard to the content of the video recording at the disposal of the Court, the disruption to the work of the University was noticeable."
- The effect on employees and students: "the behaviour of the applicants − intensified by the number of protesters in the corridors of the building – intimidated the employees and students and disrupted the normal functioning of the educational establishment."
- The impediment to the Rector's work: "the applicants' protest at the very least impeded the work of the acting Rector and his immediate colleagues for about two hours."

### Administrative sanctions

The Court does not separately ground the legitimate aim for the sanctions in additional facts beyond those already established for the removal. It states: "the legitimate aim of the interference was the prevention of disruption to public order and to the interests of others," relying on the same factual context.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

### Removal

The Court grounds its proportionality finding on the following established facts:

- The applicants were allowed to protest beforehand: "the applicants were allowed to proceed, uninterruptedly, with a pre-authorised gathering in the Grand Hall of the main University building on the very same day for several hours."
- Duration of the unauthorised protest: "they had protested for about two hours in the office of the acting Rector."
- Tolerance shown by administration and police: "the administration of the University (including the acting Rector) − and subsequently the police − showed the necessary tolerance."
- No physical force used: "No physical force was used by the police against the applicants."
- Police negotiated before acting: "police officers negotiated with the applicants for more than an hour for their peaceful removal from G.Kh.'s office."
- Continued protest permitted after removal: "after their removal from the office of the acting Rector, they were allowed to stay on the premises of the University and continue with their protest."

### Administrative sanctions

The Court grounds proportionality on:

- Discontinuation of criminal proceedings: "the criminal proceedings for breach of public order were discontinued."
- Modest level of fines: "The administrative proceedings conducted against the applicants resulted in the imposition of fines in the amount of GEL 100."
- No deprivation of liberty: "None of the applicants were arrested or detained."
- Prior tolerance of protest: "the applicants were allowed to protest against the ongoing University reform for months, by, among other ways, holding meetings on the premises of the University."
- Nature of the culminating conduct: "the nature of the protest on 3 July 2006 which culminated with the forceful entry of the applicants into the Rector's office, the disruption to the work of the University administration, and the refusal to obey explicit and reiterated requests of the police for them to leave the office of G.Kh."
- Duration of resistance to police: "it took the police about one hour to negotiate the applicants' removal from G.Kh.'s office."
- Ultimately voluntary compliance: "at the end of those negotiations the applicants left the office voluntarily" — though the Court notes this did not prevent the finding of resistance under Article 173 CAO.
