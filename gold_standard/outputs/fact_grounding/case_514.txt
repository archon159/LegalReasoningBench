## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope — implied

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Applicant's participation / iii) Peaceful character**

The Court does not ground these elements in any independently stated facts. The only textual basis is the Court's acceptance of the parties' common position:

> "it was not in dispute between the parties that there had been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly – and it has no reason to hold otherwise."

The Court does not itself identify or describe the assembly, the applicant's role, or the peaceful nature of the event by reference to any specific facts. The implied acceptance of all three scope elements rests entirely on this single sentence, which is itself a statement about the parties' agreement rather than a factual finding.

---

### (b) Interference — explicit

The factual grounding is again the same sentence, which the Court explicitly adopts as its own finding:

> "it was not in dispute between the parties that there had been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly – and it has no reason to hold otherwise."

Beyond this, the Court does not describe the specific act of interference (e.g., the nature of the ban, by whom it was issued, when) in the reasoning section provided. No further factual detail is quoted or adopted by the Court.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law — explicit

This element has the richest factual grounding in the text. The Court grounds its assessment on the following facts, which it explicitly identifies:

**The content of the relevant legal provision:**
> "section 8(1) of the Assembly Act provides for two reasons for banning a planned demonstration: where the planned event 'seriously endangers the proper functioning of the representative bodies or courts', or where 'the circulation of traffic cannot be secured by another route'."

**The authorities' actual conduct — not relying on those reasons:**
> "the authorities dealing with the applicant's case did not rely on any of these reasons. In particular, the court hearing the applicant's complaint based its decision on the residents' right to respect for their private life and free movement, as well as the right of official dignitaries to statutory protection."

**The Constitutional Court's ruling:**
> "these considerations do not figure in the exhaustive list contained in section 8(1) of the Assembly Act, for which reason the Constitutional Court reached the conclusion that the lower court had unlawfully expanded the grounds for banning a demonstration."

**The legal consequence the Court draws from that ruling:**
> "this ruling has effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis of the impugned measure."

---

### (d) Legitimate aim — not decided

The Court explicitly declines to examine this element. No factual grounding is relevant or identifiable, as no assessment was made.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society — not decided

Same as above. The Court explicitly declines to examine this element. No factual grounding is relevant or identifiable.

---

### (f) Positive obligation — not discussed

No factual grounding identifiable or relevant.
