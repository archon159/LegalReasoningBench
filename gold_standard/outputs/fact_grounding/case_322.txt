Here is the factual grounding for each element the Court explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The Court grounds this on the following fact, which it states as undisputed:
> 'both the applicant's presence at the construction site and his apprehension by the police were directly related to his participation in the protest against the construction works in the park'

**ii) Participation:**
Grounded on the same passage:
> 'both the applicant's presence at the construction site and his apprehension by the police were directly related to his participation in the protest against the construction works in the park'

**iii) Peaceful assembly:**
The Court grounds this on the absence of any allegation of violent behaviour:
> 'it was not alleged that the applicant had manifested any violent behaviour prior to his apprehension by the police'

Note: The Government's submission that the apprehension was 'related to his aggressive behaviour' is implicitly rejected here — the Court does not adopt it as established fact, and indeed contradicts it with the above finding. It is therefore not quoted as factual grounding.

The Court also grounds the continued protection under Article 11 despite the absence of prior notification on the following fact:
> 'the staging of a demonstration without prior authorisation where such authorisation is required under domestic law'

This is treated as an established factual circumstance (the protest was not officially notified), but the Court draws the legal conclusion that this does not per se remove Article 11 protection.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the interference on the following facts, stated as undisputed:
> 'both the applicant's presence at the construction site and his apprehension by the police were directly related to his participation in the protest against the construction works in the park'

And, from the opening paragraph (which the Court adopts as the framing of the complaint it proceeds to assess):
> 'his forceful removal from the public assembly on 20 April 2013, his ensuing deprivation of liberty for several hours and his conviction for an administrative offence'

These are treated by the Court as the established acts constituting the interference, since the Court proceeds to assess them without disputing their occurrence.

---

**(c) Lawfulness (implicitly raised)**

The Court grounds its finding on the following fact concerning the domestic courts' conduct:
> 'The domestic courts found the applicant guilty of non-compliance with a lawful police order without analysing whether the police had in fact given him any order prior to having forced him into their car, and, if so, what that order had been and whether it could be regarded lawful.'

This is the sole factual basis for the violation finding. No further facts are identified by the Court in relation to this element.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**
No factual grounding can be identified — the Court does not discuss this element.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**
No factual grounding can be identified — the Court does not discuss this element.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**
No factual grounding can be identified — the Court does not discuss this element.
