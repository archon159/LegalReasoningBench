## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court's treatment of the events as an assembly is grounded in the following facts, drawn from the description of the administrative proceedings:

> 'the administrative offence record indicated that the applicant had committed the offence under Article 19.3 of the CAO because she held a non-authorised demonstration'

> 'the court described the circumstances of the offence stating that the applicant had failed to end an unlawful demonstration'

> 'the appeal court found it pertinent to amend the description of the circumstances, stating that the applicant had held an unlawful demonstration and held a banner containing a very negative assessment of the professional activity of high-ranking public officials'

**(ii) Participation**

The applicant's participation is grounded in the same domestic findings quoted above, and additionally:

> 'the applicant had refused (i) to stop this "non-authorised" demonstration, and (ii) to show her identity document and to follow the police to the police station'

**(iii) Peaceful**

The Court does not explicitly identify specific facts establishing peacefulness. It treats the assembly as peaceful by presumption, stating:

> 'the immediate dispersal of the peaceful demonstration (which is presumed to be lawful for the reasons stated above)'

No specific factual findings (e.g., absence of violence) are quoted by the Court to ground this characterisation. The peaceful nature appears to rest on the absence of any contrary finding in the domestic proceedings rather than on positively established facts. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the presumption itself.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference in the following facts:

> 'Having regard to the context of the present case, the dispersal of the demonstration, the findings made by the domestic authorities and the penalty imposed on the applicant, the Court considers that the relevant facts disclose an interference which was sufficiently linked to the applicant's exercise of her right to freedom of peaceful assembly'

More specifically, the factual basis for the interference is drawn from the domestic proceedings:

> 'the arrest record referred to the alleged refusal to comply with orders given by the police (Article 19.3 of the CAO) whereas the administrative offence record indicated that the applicant had committed the offence under Article 19.3 of the CAO because she held a non-authorised demonstration'

> 'the court described the circumstances of the offence stating that the applicant had failed to end an unlawful demonstration and to comply with a lawful order by police officers (to show an identity document and to follow the police to the police station)'

> 'It follows that the applicant was arrested and the demonstration was dispersed because the applicant had refused (i) to stop this "non-authorised" demonstration, and (ii) to show her identity document and to follow the police to the police station.'

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declines to rule on this element. No factual grounding is developed for it. The Court merely notes the parties disagreed:

> 'The parties disagreed as to whether the interference was prescribed by law and served a legitimate aim. However, the Court decides to dispense with ruling on the issue of lawfulness because, in any event, the interference fell short of being necessary in a democratic society.'

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not engage with the substance of this element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Similarly, the Court declines to rule on legitimate aim. However, it does make one factual observation that bears negatively on one of the Government's proposed aims:

> 'the Court cannot deal with the Government's argument suggesting that the demonstration had to be dispersed in order to put an end to unsubstantiated accusations in respect of judges and in order to protect their reputation and honour. This justification was never mentioned in the domestic proceedings and did not justify the interference with the applicant's rights.'

The factual grounding here is the absence of this justification in the domestic proceedings — a negative fact. No positive factual grounding for any legitimate aim is established by the Court.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court draws on the following established facts:

**On the contradictory nature of the domestic proceedings:**

> 'both the findings of the national courts and the Government's observations before the Court contain contradictory submissions as to the nature of the actions held against the applicant in the administrative offence proceedings'

> 'The arrest record referred to the alleged refusal to comply with orders given by the police (Article 19.3 of the CAO) whereas the administrative offence record indicated that the applicant had committed the offence under Article 19.3 of the CAO because she held a non-authorised demonstration.'

> 'the appeal court found it pertinent to amend the description of the circumstances, stating that the applicant had held an unlawful demonstration and held a banner containing a very negative assessment of the professional activity of high-ranking public officials. It was also noted in the appeal decision that the applicant had refused to comply with lawful orders to end the violation of the public order and threatened police officers with prosecution, thereby committing the offence of resisting a lawful order by the police.'

**On the absence of domestic findings regarding notification requirements and location:**

> 'the domestic decisions do not contain any clear findings that, as suggested by the respondent Government before the Court, the applicant had breached the requirements of the Public Gatherings Act, for instance on account of the notification by telegram, her alleged omission to provide sufficient contact details, or whether the demonstration had indeed taken place in the area expressly prohibited by section 8 of the Act, that is, in the "immediate vicinity" of "court buildings". The appeal court merely mentioned this provision, without drawing any conclusions relating to the circumstance of the applicant's case under Article 19.3 of the CAO. Nor do the domestic decisions contain any findings that the above alleged breaches justified the dispersal of the demonstration.'

**On the small size of the demonstration:**

> 'the demonstration involved only three persons. For the Court, it is difficult to conceive that such an event could have generated the gathering of a significant crowd warranting specific (for instance, security) measures on the side of the authorities.'

**On the discontinuation of proceedings against other participants:**

> 'the proceedings against the other two participants in the demonstration were discontinued, inter alia, because no related violations were established.'

**On the absence of the judges' reputation justification from domestic proceedings:**

> 'This justification was never mentioned in the domestic proceedings and did not justify the interference with the applicant's rights.'

**On the absence of domestic assessment of the conditions for administrative arrest:**

> 'under the CAO administrative arrest required "exceptional circumstances" relating to the need for a proper and expedient examination of the administrative case. The domestic courts did not assess this issue in any noticeable way.'

**On the failure of domestic courts to apply Convention standards:**

> 'the national courts in the administrative offence procedure in the present case did not assess the applicant's situation, including the penalty to be imposed, taking into account the relevant principles under Articles 10 and 11 of the Convention.'

> 'It cannot be said that the national authorities adduced reasons which could be accepted as "relevant and sufficient".'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding exists for this element, as it is entirely absent from the reasoning.
