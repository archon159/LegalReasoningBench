## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this as a separate element. The factual grounding is implied by:
> "The applicant and other persons clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia."

**(ii) Participation**
Same passage as above:
> "The applicant and other persons clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

**(iii) Peaceful character**
The Court does not make an explicit finding on peacefulness at the scope stage. At the justification stage, it grounds its assessment of the violent character on the following facts it explicitly adopts:

From the Commission's findings, which the Court explicitly endorses ("The Court sees no reason to depart from these findings"):
> "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."

From the UN Secretary General's report, which the Court explicitly quotes and relies upon:
> "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on:
> "The applicant and other persons clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this finding on the identification of specific legal provisions:
> "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 35-36 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law (see paragraph 39 above)."

And on the principle established in the Foka case:
> "when, as in the Foka case, an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law for the purposes of the Convention."

No facts are cited going to foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse, as the Court does not discuss these sub-elements.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds its acceptance of legitimate aims on the following facts it explicitly adopts:

The Commission's findings (explicitly endorsed by the Court):
> "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."

The UN Secretary General's report (explicitly quoted and relied upon):
> "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."

The entry into 'TRNC' territory, where the Court notes it has no basis to doubt witness statements:
> "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory."

The Court then draws the following factual conclusion:
> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its proportionality assessment on the same core facts as for legitimate aim, and adds the following legal principle as a structural element of the analysis:

On the violent character of the demonstration (Commission findings and UN report, already quoted above under (d)).

On the border violation:
> "the demonstrators had forced their way into the UN buffer zone. According to the 'TRNC' authorities, they also entered into 'TRNC' territory, thus committing offences punishable under the 'TRNC' laws."

Note: the 'TRNC' authorities' characterization of entry into their territory is cited here, but the Court's own acceptance of this is grounded on the witness statement passage already quoted above.

On the causal link between the facts and the intervention:
> "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."

The Court also invokes the wide margin of appreciation as a structural consideration:
> "having regard to the wide margin of appreciation left to the States in this sphere."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions the positive obligation in general terms but does not ground it in any specific facts of the case. It is stated as a general legal principle only:
> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens. However, they cannot guarantee this absolutely and they have a wide discretion in the choice of the means to be used."

No case-specific facts are mapped onto this element, and no distinct conclusion is reached on it. **No factual grounding can be identified for this element beyond its invocation as background doctrine.**
