**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited by the Court in support of its necessity assessment. The Court instead grounds its conclusion entirely on prior case-law:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

And:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found **any fact or argument** capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion"

The Court thus explicitly acknowledges that it examined factual material but does not identify or quote any of it. The necessity conclusion is grounded in the similarity to prior cases, not in any independently stated facts about the present case.

**Overall observation:**
The factual basis of this decision is strikingly thin as presented in this reasoning section. The Court relies almost entirely on its prior case-law through a repetitive case mechanism, and does not set out the specific facts of the individual applicants' situations that ground its legal conclusions. This is characteristic of a repetitive case judgment where the Court treats the matter as already settled by leading judgments.
