I will go through each canonical element and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**Peaceful assembly:**
The Court's finding rests on a single factual observation: "It has not been argued by the Government that the organisers and the participants in the events planned by UMO Ilinden – PIRIN had violent intentions. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise."

The factual grounding here is essentially the absence of any contrary evidence — the Court does not cite specific facts establishing peacefulness, but rather the absence of any allegation of violent intent. No further factual detail is quoted by the Court in support.

---

**(b) Interference**

**First ban:** The Court grounds this on the Government's own non-contestation: "The Government have not disputed that the first rally planned by UMO Ilinden – PIRIN was effectively banned by the authorities and that this ban constituted an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly."

**Second ban:** The Court grounds its finding on the following facts:
- "under the Constitution of 1971 the Executive Committee of the People's Council was the executive authority within the municipality... in 1991 it was superseded by the mayor, not by the Municipal Council"
- "The failure of a nonexistent body to rule on the applicants' appeal was therefore only natural, whereas, contrary to what was suggested by the Government, the Municipal Council was apparently under no obligation to examine the appeal."
- "it may hardly be concluded that the ban had in reality ceased to have effect by reason of the fact that the Municipal Council had not examined it."

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds its positive finding on two facts:
- "the mayor... referred to an alleged threat to public order, which under domestic law could justify an interference with the right to peaceful assembly"
- "the bans were imposed in accordance with the procedure laid down in the Meetings and Marches Act"

No further factual breakdown is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. As noted previously, these sub-elements are not individually examined.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not ground its analysis on established facts here. It proceeds on an assumption — "even assuming that the legitimate aims pursued were public safety and the prevention of disorder" — without making a factual finding that these aims were actually pursued. There is no factual grounding to quote for a positive finding on legitimate aim, because the Court makes none.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court grounds its finding that necessity was NOT satisfied on the following facts:

First, on the inadequacy of the reasons given for the bans: "both mayoral bans only made brief references to an alleged threat to public order, without stating the basis for such a conclusion or going into further detail." The Court also notes: "in their observations the Government did not specify any particular reasons to justify the bans, but merely stated that the authorities had acted in conformity with national law and that their actions had not been arbitrary."

Second, on the separatist context: "Even if it was not unreasonable for the authorities to suspect that certain leaders of UMO Ilinden – PIRIN (which was later declared unconstitutional), or small groups which had developed from it, harboured separatist views and had a political agenda that included the notion of autonomy for the region of Pirin Macedonia or even secession from Bulgaria and could hence expect that separatist slogans would be broadcast by some participants during the planned rallies, such a probability could not per se justify their banning."

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, the Court does not address positive obligations in its own assessment. There is no factual grounding to identify here.
