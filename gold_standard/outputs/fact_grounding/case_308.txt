## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following fact:
> "the fact that he had attended an assembly at Yerevan's Freedom Square in February 2008 following the disputed presidential election"

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:
> "the fact that he had attended an assembly at Yerevan's Freedom Square in February 2008 following the disputed presidential election"

And further, in the necessity section, the nature of that participation is specified:
> "the applicant's participation in the said assembly was purely passive and without any expression of political opinions"

And:
> "the applicant was officially on leave when he attended the rallies in question"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court does not establish this from fresh facts found in the present case. Instead, it grounds this on a prior judgment:
> "The Court is mindful that it has already scrutinised the circumstances of that assembly and found that it had been peaceful, without any incitement to violence or acts of violence (see Mushegh Saghatelyan v. Armenia, no. 23086/08, §§ 230-33, 20 September 2018)."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the existence of interference on:
> "one of the grounds on which the applicant, a civil servant, was dismissed from his post was the fact that he had attended an assembly at Yerevan's Freedom Square in February 2008 following the disputed presidential election"

The dismissal itself is thus the factual basis for the interference finding. The Court also draws on the legal framework it articulates — that punitive measures taken after an assembly count as restrictions — but the factual anchor is the dismissal.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its acceptance that the interference was prescribed by law on:
> "the applicant's dismissal was based on sections 24 § 1(d) and 33 § 1(j) of the Civil Service Act"

This is the only factual/legal grounding offered. The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not grounded in any identified facts, as the Court does not discuss them.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not ground the legitimate aim of "preventing disorder" in any specific facts of the case. It simply accepts the aim as plausible, noting that the applicant's arguments against it would be better addressed under necessity:
> "while the applicant argued that the interference had not pursued a legitimate aim, the Court considers that these arguments would be more appropriately addressed under the necessity of the interference and is prepared to accept that the interference had pursued the legitimate aim of preventing disorder"

No specific facts are cited to establish that preventing disorder was actually the aim pursued. The Court's acceptance appears to be a formal concession rather than a fact-grounded finding.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's necessity assessment is grounded in several specific facts:

**The applicant's role and conduct at the assembly:**
> "the applicant's participation in the said assembly was purely passive and without any expression of political opinions"

**The applicant's status at the time:**
> "the applicant was officially on leave when he attended the rallies in question"

**The nature of the assembly:**
> "the applicant was sanctioned for the mere fact of taking part in a political demonstration which, as already noted above, was of a peaceful nature"

**The domestic court's own finding:**
> "The Court observes that the question whether there were grounds to sanction the applicant on the basis of section 24 § 1(d) of the Civil Service Act was examined by the Administrative Court which held that no such grounds existed, finding that the applicant's behaviour had not breached the principle in question"

**The severity of the sanction:**
> "it cannot be said that the sanction imposed on the applicant, especially such a severe one as his dismissal, was a proportionate measure within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention"

**The applicant's position:**
> "the applicant was a civil servant holding a post in the Lori Regional Administration"

**The dates of participation:**
> "On 26 and 28 February 2008 he took part in the assembly at Freedom Square which was organised by the political opposition in the wake of the disputed presidential election of 19 February 2008."

**The reason for dismissal:**
> "This became one of the reasons why the applicant was considered to have breached the principle of political restraint of civil servants enshrined in section 24 § 1(d) of the Civil Service Act and was dismissed from his post."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
