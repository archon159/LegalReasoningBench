**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists (implied)**

The Court's implied acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in the following phrase:
> 'the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013'

This is the Court's own characterisation, appearing in the applicants' complaint as framed by the Court: 'their arrests and convictions immediately after the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013.'

**Participation (implied)**

The factual grounding for the implied finding that the applicants participated is:
> 'their arrests and convictions immediately after the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013'

The temporal and causal link between the demonstration and the arrests implies the applicants were present and participating.

**Peaceful (implied)**

The factual grounding is again:
> 'the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013'

The Court's own description of the demonstration as 'authorised' is the only factual basis offered. No facts suggesting violence or disorder are mentioned anywhere in the reasoning text.

---

**(b) Interference (implied)**

The factual grounding for the implied finding of interference is:
> 'their arrests and convictions immediately after the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013'

and:

> 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies.'

These are the Court's own statements, not merely party submissions. The arrests, convictions, and administrative proceedings are treated as established facts underpinning the interference finding.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (implied negative — i.e. not necessary)**

The factual grounding for the implied finding that the interference was not necessary is:

> 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'

Additionally, the Court grounds its overall assessment in the similarity of the facts to prior cases:

> 'Having regard to the facts of the present cases and their similarity to those of the Huseynli and Others case and the Huseynov and Others case on all relevant and crucial points, the Court sees no particular circumstances that could compel it to deviate from its findings in those judgments.'

The Court also accepts the applicants' characterisation of the purpose of the measures:

> 'their arrests and convictions immediately after the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013 had been measures used by the authorities to punish them for their political activity and to prevent them from participating in opposition protests.'

However, it should be noted that this last quotation comes from the framing of the applicants' complaint. The Court does not explicitly state that it adopts this characterisation as an established fact; rather, it is the premise of the complaint. The more clearly Court-endorsed factual grounding remains the chilling effect passage quoted above.
