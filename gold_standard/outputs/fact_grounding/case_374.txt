## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, but the following facts ground the implied acceptance that assemblies existed:

- "a public meeting at the Klin Ice Palace"
- "three protest actions on the road leading to the landfill site"
- "four notices of public events submitted to the authorities"
- "the meeting concerning the environment organised by him and authorised by the authorities that took place at the Klin Ice Palace"
- "their gathering on the road leading to Aleksinskiy Quarry and at the quarry itself"

**(ii) Participation**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element. The following facts ground the implied acceptance that the applicants participated:

- "the applicant had 'failed to ensure the safety of the participants' during the meeting" (regarding the Klin Ice Palace applicant as organiser)
- "the applicants were arrested, taken to a police station and charged with administrative offences because their gathering on the road leading to Aleksinskiy Quarry and at the quarry itself had not been authorised"
- "the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assembly"

**(iii) Peaceful**
The Court explicitly grounds this finding on the following facts:

- "The applicant was not found guilty of any violent acts."
- "Nor was it established that any disorder had erupted among the large congregation of people at the venue of the meeting"
- "there is nothing in the present case file to indicate that the protest actions on the road were violent or that any of the applicants engaged in violent behaviour."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court states this is "not disputed." No specific facts are identified by the Court as grounding this finding beyond the description of what happened to the applicants:

- "they had been arrested, taken to a police station and convicted of administrative offences"
- "the applicants were arrested, taken to a police station and charged with administrative offences"
- "the three notices of public events submitted by the applicant were not approved by the authorities"
- "they further did not approve his notice of intention to hold a protest car rally"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court states this is "not disputed" and provides no factual grounding beyond this concession. No facts are quoted or identified by the Court in support of this element. **No factual grounding can be identified.**

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Again, the Court states this is "not disputed" and accepts the aim of "providing safety to citizens and keeping public order." No specific facts are identified by the Court as grounding this acceptance. **No factual grounding can be identified beyond the bare concession.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element is the most extensively grounded in facts. The Court draws on the following:

**For the Klin Ice Palace meeting (Section B.1):**

- "there is nothing in the case material, including the judgment of the domestic court, to indicate that the safety of the participants or other people at that venue was in any way compromised, or that anyone was injured or at any time risked being injured."
- "The applicant was not found guilty of any violent acts."
- "Nor was it established that any disorder had erupted among the large congregation of people at the venue of the meeting or that the actual number of participants had exceeded the number indicated in the notice of the event."
- "neither the applicant's arrest six days after the meeting nor his subsequent conviction for an administrative offence corresponded to a 'pressing social need'"
- "the domestic courts did not establish exactly which actions or inaction of the applicant had put the safety of the participants or other people at risk, stating only that 'he had let a significant group of participants congregate in the hallway, obstructing access to the facilities of Klin Ice Palace'."
- "it does not appear from the case material that the police assisted the applicant in any way to ensure its orderly conduct"
- "the domestic courts summarily dismissed his argument concerning this, without any further inquiry."
- "considering the rather cursory examination of the circumstances of the applicant's case by the domestic courts"

**For the protest actions on the road (Section B.2):**

- "there is nothing in the present case file to indicate that the protest actions on the road were violent or that any of the applicants engaged in violent behaviour."
- "the applicants were arrested, taken to a police station and charged with administrative offences because their gathering on the road leading to Aleksinskiy Quarry and at the quarry itself had not been authorised and allegedly blocked the passage of transport."

Note: The applicants' submission that "the authorities had failed to point out even one vehicle that had been blocked from accessing the quarry and what other operations of Kombinat LLC had exactly been disrupted by their protest" is noted by the Court but is a party submission. The Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact; rather, it proceeds on the basis of what is and is not in the case file.

**For the refusal to approve notices (Section B.3):**

- "the three notices of public events submitted by the applicant were not approved by the authorities because another public event had already been planned to take place at the location chosen by the organiser and that they therefore suggested that he change the location."
- "they further did not approve his notice of intention to hold a protest car rally because it had been planned on a road that had only one lane in each direction."
- The Court grounds its finding on the absence of evidence: "the refusal to approve a public event was issued in the absence of evidence indicating that both events to be held simultaneously could not be adequately managed by the exercise of policing powers or that the authorities had considered ways of minimising disruption to ordinary life, for example by organising a temporary diversion of traffic on alternative routes or by taking other similar measures."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court's implied reference to a positive obligation is grounded in the following fact:

- "it does not appear from the case material that the police assisted the applicant in any way to ensure its orderly conduct"
- "the domestic courts summarily dismissed his argument concerning this, without any further inquiry."

These facts are identified by the Court itself from the case material, not merely asserted by a party.
