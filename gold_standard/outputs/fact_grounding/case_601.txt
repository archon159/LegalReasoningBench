I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the introductory paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and/or participants of **public assemblies**"

The Court does not elaborate further on the nature or circumstances of these assemblies. No additional facts are provided.

**ii) Participation**
The only factual grounding is again:
> "measures taken against them as organisers and/or **participants** of public assemblies"

No further facts are stated about the nature of the applicants' participation.

**iii) Peaceful character**
There is **no factual grounding** in the reasoning text. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the peaceful or non-peaceful character of the assemblies. This element is implied solely by the Court's legal reasoning (proceeding under Article 11 and referencing leading cases involving peaceful assemblies), not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court identifies the following concrete acts as constituting the interference:
> "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences"

These are the only facts stated. The Court does not elaborate on the circumstances of the dispersal, the arrests, or the convictions beyond this description.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central element, yet the factual grounding in the reasoning text is notably thin. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it and having taken into account the issue of compliance with the six-month time-limit under Article 35 § 1 of the Convention [...] the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This sentence references "all the material submitted" but does not quote or specify any particular facts from that material. The necessity finding is grounded not in explicitly stated facts but in:

1. The reference to the leading cases:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia [...], Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia [...], and Kasparov and Others v. Russia [...], the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present cases**."

2. The absence of distinguishing facts:
> "the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion"

In short: the necessity assessment is grounded in the **similarity to prior cases** and the **absence of any distinguishing facts**, rather than in any specific facts about the proportionality of the measures that are explicitly stated in this reasoning section.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. For most elements — including scope, peaceful character, prescribed by law, and legitimate aim — the Court either implies the element without stating supporting facts, or omits the element entirely. Even for the one element explicitly decided (necessity), the Court relies on analogy to prior case-law and the absence of distinguishing material rather than on explicitly recited facts. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case or "clone case" judgment, where the Court treats the facts as sufficiently established by reference to leading cases rather than restating them in full.
