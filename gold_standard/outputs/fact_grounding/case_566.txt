## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "The applicant in the present case held a picket in front of a regional court."

> "the applicant submitted the picket notice eight days before the planned event"

> "the town administration did not consider the alleged delay in notification relevant or important. Not only did it promptly acknowledge receipt of the picket notice, but also gave orders to the police five days in advance to ensure public order at the event."

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on:

> "The applicant in the present case held a picket in front of a regional court. Some time later he was found administratively liable and fined for having breached the procedure for organising and holding a public assembly."

> "the applicant submitted the picket notice eight days before the planned event"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court does not make an explicit finding on peacefulness as a threshold matter. The closest factual grounding is implied by:

> "The materials distributed by the applicant and the ideas he advocated during the picket were not shown to contain any defamatory statements, incitement to violence or rejection of democratic principles."

> "it is creditable that the applicant diligently complied with the officials' request and without further argument descended the stairs onto the pavement."

No explicit factual finding on peacefulness as a scope question is made.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on:

> "The applicant in the present case held a picket in front of a regional court. Some time later he was found administratively liable and fined for having breached the procedure for organising and holding a public assembly."

> "The term 'restrictions' in paragraph 2 of Article 11 must be interpreted as including both measures taken before or during the public assembly, and those, such as punitive measures, taken after the meeting."

The factual basis is thus the administrative prosecution and fine imposed after the picket. No further factual elaboration is provided at this stage; the Court moves immediately to justification.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this on:

> "The administrative liability for failure to comply with the established procedure for organising or holding a public assembly was foreseen by Article 20.2 §§ 1 and 2 of the Russian Code of Administrative Offences, which was the legal basis for the applicant's conviction in the present case."

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Court simply accepts the requirement as satisfied on the basis of the existence of the statutory provision.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not ground the legitimate aim finding in specific facts. It simply accepts the aims asserted without factual elaboration:

> "The Court accepts that the interference was 'prescribed by law' and that it pursued 'legitimate aims' within the meaning of paragraph 2 of Articles 10 and 11, those of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others."

No factual grounding can be identified for this element beyond the bare acceptance of the Government's position.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively fact-grounded element. The Court addresses each of the three charges separately.

**On belated notification:**

> "it is undisputed that in the instant case the applicant submitted the picket notice eight days before the planned event, whereas the applicable regulations stipulated a ten-day notification."

> "it does not appear that the two-day difference in any way impaired the authorities' ability to make necessary preparations for the picket."

> "the town administration did not consider the alleged delay in notification relevant or important. Not only did it promptly acknowledge receipt of the picket notice, but also gave orders to the police five days in advance to ensure public order at the event."

> "The delay was not held against the applicant in any official documents and did not affect the lawfulness of the picket."

> "that transgression surfaced for the first time in the report on the administrative offence, which was compiled six weeks after the assembly."

**On obstruction of passage:**

> "the picket, in which just a few persons participated, began at about 9 a.m. at the top of the stairs leading to the entrance to the Sverdlovsk Regional Court. Shortly afterwards the court bailiffs and a police officer approached the participants and asked them to go down the stairs. They complied with the request and continued to picket in front of the staircase. The witnesses who arrived at 9.30 a.m. saw the applicant and his colleagues, who were already on the pavement downstairs."

> "it is undisputed that there were no complaints by anyone, whether individual visitors, judges or court employees, about the alleged obstruction of entry to the court-house by the picket participants."

> "it is creditable that the applicant diligently complied with the officials' request and without further argument descended the stairs onto the pavement."

> "the alleged hindrance was of an extremely short duration."

**On the distributed materials:**

> "the domestic courts omitted to indicate any factual basis for their findings on that issue. Their judgments did not contain any analysis as to what the alleged differences were between the declared aims of the picket and the contents of the article which the applicant had distributed during the picket."

> "The materials distributed by the applicant and the ideas he advocated during the picket were not shown to contain any defamatory statements, incitement to violence or rejection of democratic principles."

> "the alleged discrepancy between the aims of the picket and the disseminated materials was raised for the first time in the letter by the deputy President of the Sverdlovsk Regional Court, that is the official reporting directly to the President of the Regional Court who had been the target of criticism in the distributed publications."

> "The terms employed in the deputy president's letter, such as the statement that the picket participants had 'committed thereby an administrative offence', prejudged the assessment of the facts by the competent judicial authority and expressed the opinion that the applicant was guilty even before he had been proved guilty according to law."

**On the nature of the speech:**

> "the purpose of the picket was to attract public attention to the alleged dysfunction of the judicial system in the Sverdlovsk Region. This serious matter was undeniably part of a political debate on a matter of general and public concern."

**Overall conclusion:**

> "In the light of the above considerations, the Court finds that the Russian authorities did not adduce any 'relevant and sufficient' reasons which could have justified the interference with the applicant's rights to freedom of expression and assembly. That the amount of the fine was relatively small does not detract from the fact that the interference was not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted previously, the Court does not make a positive obligation finding. No factual grounding is therefore applicable.
