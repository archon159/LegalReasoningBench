## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court does not explicitly state a separate factual finding that an assembly existed. The factual grounding is implied by the Court's treatment of the applicant's notification as triggering Article 11 protection. The closest factual grounding is found in the interference finding: *"the dismissal by the police of the applicant's notification of his intention to hold a demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of his rights under Article 11 of the Convention."* No richer factual description of the assembly itself is provided in the reasoning section reproduced.

**ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate?**
The same passage provides the factual grounding by implication: *"the dismissal by the police of the applicant's notification of his intention to hold a demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of his rights under Article 11 of the Convention."* The Court does not separately articulate this as a factual finding.

**iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court grounds this on the absence of any contrary indication: *"In the present case, there is no indication – nor has it been argued by the Government – that the applicant or other potential participants would have had violent intentions."* This is a negative factual finding — the Court relies on the absence of evidence of violence rather than on positive facts establishing peacefulness.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on two facts:

First, the police dismissal of the notification: *"the dismissal by the police of the applicant's notification of his intention to hold a demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of his rights under Article 11 of the Convention."*

Second, the Court grounds the significance of this interference — and why it could not be remedied — on the temporal facts of the situation: *"the President of Hungary had five days – that is to say, until 16 March 2013 – to assent to the Amendment of 11 March 2013 and that the area in front of the President's residence was closed down for the entirety of this period."*

The Court also grounds the chilling effect on the practical consequence of the interference: *"the only option left to the applicant would have been to hold an unauthorised gathering, that is, without a presumption of legality, such a presumption constituting a vital aspect of the effective and unhindered exercise of freedom of assembly and freedom of expression. Inevitably, the state of affairs complained of resulted in a chilling effect on the freedom of assembly."*

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's conclusion that the interference was not prescribed by law is grounded on two factual findings:

First, the Police Department's stated justification: *"the Police Department in essence argued that the fact that the Anti-Terror Centre had closed that area down for the period in question meant that it was excluded from the scope of the law on peaceful assembly."*

Second, and decisively, the domestic court's ruling: *"on 18 March 2013 the Budapest Administrative and Labour Court quashed the impugned police decision, reproaching the Police Department for failing to provide the precise reasons for the underlying measure taken by the Anti-Terror Centre."*

From these two facts the Court draws its legal conclusion: *"In the Court's view, that court ruling effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis of the impugned measure... This consideration is sufficient to enable the Court to conclude that the interference was devoid of a basis in domestic law and cannot as such be regarded as 'prescribed by law'."*

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding is provided. The Court explicitly declines to examine this element: *"It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or its necessity in a democratic society."*

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

No factual grounding is provided. The Court explicitly declines to examine this element alongside legitimate aim, as quoted above.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding is provided or applicable. The Court does not engage with this element at all.
