## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i. Assembly exists / ii. Applicant participated**

The Court does not explicitly state facts about the assembly or the applicant's participation. The only factual grounding is implicit in the Court's framing of the interference finding. No facts are quoted by the Court as established in its own voice regarding the assembly itself. The Court simply proceeds on the basis that a conviction was imposed, which it treats as undisputed:

> "It has not been disputed between the parties that the applicant's conviction constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

The existence of the assembly and the applicant's participation are thus grounded solely in this undisputed fact of conviction — the Court does not separately establish them.

**iii. Peaceful**

No factual grounding identifiable. The Court uses the phrase "peaceful assembly" throughout but never grounds this characterisation in any specific fact. Cannot identify a factual basis for this element beyond the absence of any dispute about it.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on two facts:

First, the undisputed fact of conviction:

> "It has not been disputed between the parties that the applicant's conviction constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

Second, the Court grounds the legal characterisation of that conviction as a "restriction" within Article 11 § 2 on the following doctrinal point applied to the facts, citing *Ezelin v. France*:

> "The Court recalls that the term 'restrictions' used in Article 11 § 2 cannot be interpreted as not including measures – such as punitive measures – taken not before or during but after a meeting."

The factual grounding is thus the imposition of a sanction (the conviction and fine) after the meeting. The Court does not separately quote the facts of the fine's imposition in its own voice in this section, but it refers to it again in the prescribed-by-law section:

> "The Court notes that the fine was imposed on the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO, which prescribed a penalty for the violation of the prescribed rules for organising and holding rallies and street processions."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**Accessible (CAO)**

The Court grounds its finding of accessibility of the CAO solely on the absence of dispute:

> "It has not been disputed that the CAO was accessible and the Court does not have reason to doubt that."

No further factual grounding is provided.

**Foreseeable**

The Court's foreseeability finding is grounded in several specific facts, all stated in the Court's own voice:

*1. Dispute over whether any valid legal act prescribed the rules:*

> "The Court notes that it is in dispute between the parties whether at the material time there was any legal act in Armenia which envisaged the 'prescribed rules' referred to in Article 180.1 of the CAO."

*2. Ambiguity of the relevant domestic and international documents:*

> "the Court notes that these documents allow various interpretations as to the applicability of the former USSR laws. There is no domestic provision which clearly stated whether the former USSR laws remained or did not remain in force on the territory of Armenia."

*3. No explicit decision adopting the Law or Decree in Armenia:*

> "While the Decision of the Supreme Council of Armenia of 26 February 1992, as pointed out by the Government, explicitly allowed the application of two former USSR laws on the territory of Armenia, no such decision was ever adopted in respect of the Law or the Decree."

*4. Ambiguity of the Declaration of Independence provisions:*

> "It is not obvious, however, from the wording of these provisions whether the same applied to the former USSR laws."

*5. Absence of domestic case-law:*

> "the Court would draw attention to the absence of any domestic case-law concerning the disputed matter. Notwithstanding the Court's request, the Government failed to submit any examples of domestic practice, such as copies of any court decisions or judgments which would clarify this issue or at least make references to any former USSR laws, in general, or the Law and the Decree, in particular."

*6. Domestic courts' failure to refer to any legal provision:*

> "the domestic courts in the present case also failed to refer to any legal act which prescribed the rules for holding rallies and street processions which the applicant was found to have violated."

*7. Absence of any applicable legal act and the thirteen-year delay:*

> "The Court notes that following the dissolution of the USSR there was no legal act applicable in Armenia which contained these rules and the relevant law was adopted only on 28 April 2004. The Court accepts that it may take some time for a country to establish its legislative framework in a transition period, but it cannot accept the delay of almost thirteen years to be justifiable, especially when such a fundamental right as freedom of peaceful assembly is at stake."
