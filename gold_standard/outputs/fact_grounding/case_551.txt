Here is the factual grounding for each element discussed or implied, drawn only from what the Court itself stated or explicitly adopted:

---

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The factual grounding is found in the Court's own statement:

> "the applicant's conviction for participation in an unauthorised march"

This single phrase provides the factual basis for all three implied sub-elements: it identifies an assembly (a march), the applicant's participation in it, and — by the Court's uncontested characterisation of it as falling within "freedom of peaceful assembly" — its peaceful character.

---

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same phrase, which the Court itself uses as the basis for its interference finding:

> "the applicant's conviction for participation in an unauthorised march undoubtedly interfered with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly"

The Court identifies the conviction itself as the governmental act constituting the interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law (explicit)**

The Court's assessment is grounded in the following facts, which it explicitly adopted from the Mkrtchyan precedent and applied to the present case:

On the absence of an applicable legal act:
> "there was no legal act applicable in Armenia which contained the 'prescribed rules' referred to in that provision. The USSR Law of 28 October 1988 was no longer applicable and a new law on assemblies and rallies was adopted only on 28 April 2004."

On the temporal connection to the present case:
> "the interference in the present case similarly took place before the enactment of a new law on assemblies and rallies"

These are the only facts the Court itself relies upon. The Government's assertion that the rules "were accessible and formulated with sufficient precision" is not adopted by the Court and therefore is not included here.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim, (e) Necessity, (f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified for these elements. The Court explicitly declined to examine legitimate aim and necessity, and positive obligations were not discussed at all. There are accordingly no facts in the reasoning text that the Court grounded these assessments upon.
