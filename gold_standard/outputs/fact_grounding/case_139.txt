This is an important question, and the honest answer is that **the reasoning text is almost entirely devoid of specific factual grounding**. The Court does not describe, analyze, or rely upon any concrete facts of the individual cases in its reasoning. Below is a systematic account of each element:

---

**(a) Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful character)**

The only factual grounding offered is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

This is the sole factual statement in the text. It establishes that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated in them, and that dispersal and arrest occurred. However, it reads more as a summary of the applicants' complaints than as the Court's own established findings of fact. The Court does not independently verify or narrate these facts — it simply records what the applicants complained of.

There is **no factual grounding whatsoever** for the peaceful character of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The same passage provides the only factual grounding:

> "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

Again, this comes from the characterization of the applicants' complaints, not from an independent factual finding by the Court.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or could exist in this text.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or could exist in this text.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's conclusion on necessity is:

> "the Court considers that in the instant case the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was not 'necessary in a democratic society'"

This conclusion is **not grounded in any specific facts of the instant case**. Instead, it rests entirely on:

1. A reference to prior leading cases (*Frumkin*, *Navalnyy and Yashin*, *Kasparov*) in which similar issues were found to violate Article 11.
2. A general statement that the Court "has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."

The Court does not identify which facts of the present case it considered, nor does it explain how those facts map onto the necessity analysis. The reasoning is essentially one of analogy to precedent, with no case-specific factual elaboration.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or could exist in this text.

---

**Overall observation:** This decision follows the pattern of a **repetitive case** or **clone case** judgment, where the Court disposes of cases by reference to leading judgments without conducting an independent factual analysis. The reasoning text contains almost no original factual findings — it is essentially a procedural confirmation that the present cases fall within the scope of already-established violations.
