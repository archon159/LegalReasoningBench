Here is the factual grounding for each element the Court discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:**
> "the applicant's protest was staged in accordance with the law concerning assemblies"

This is the Court's own statement, directly grounding the existence of a legally recognized assembly.

**(ii) Participation (implied):**
The same sentence grounds the implication that the applicant was a participant:
> "the applicant's protest was staged in accordance with the law concerning assemblies"

The possessive "the applicant's protest" establishes him as the person conducting the assembly.

**(iii) Peaceful assembly:**
> "he remained peaceful and did not disturb public order in any way"
> "He continued to be peaceful and polite even after being manhandled by the police and did not resist the abusive arrest in any way."

---

**(b) Interference:**
> "the applicant's arrest constituted 'interference by [a] public authority' with his right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11"

The Court does not spell out additional facts here beyond naming the arrest as the act of interference. The Government's submission about the reason for arrest is noted but explicitly rejected, so it does not constitute factual grounding adopted by the Court.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law:**
The Court grounds its negative conclusion on the following facts:

> "the applicant's protest was staged in accordance with the law concerning assemblies"

> "he remained peaceful and did not disturb public order in any way"

> "Nor was his filming the encounter with the police officers contrary to the law."

> "He continued to be peaceful and polite even after being manhandled by the police and did not resist the abusive arrest in any way."

These facts collectively support the conclusion that there was no lawful basis for the arrest under domestic law, since the applicant had done nothing unlawful. The Court's conclusion follows directly:
> "the interference with his right of assembly cannot be considered lawful under domestic law"

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society:** Not discussed; no factual grounding to identify.

**(f) Positive obligation:** Not discussed; no factual grounding to identify.
