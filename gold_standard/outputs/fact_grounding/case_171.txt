I will go through each element in turn, identifying only facts that the Court itself states or explicitly adopts.

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this as a doctrinal element, but the factual grounding for the implied finding is:
> 'the authorities banned the planned march and several of the stationary assemblies'
> 'the assemblies were eventually held on the planned dates'

**(ii) Participation**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this as a doctrinal element, but the factual grounding for the implied finding is:
> 'the applicants took a risk in holding them given the official ban in force at that time'
> 'the applicants were negatively affected by the refusals to authorise them'

**(iii) Peaceful character**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this as a doctrinal element and does not itself state any facts establishing the peaceful character of the assemblies. The applicants argued this, but the Court does not explicitly adopt this submission as an established fact. **No factual grounding can be identified in the Court's own reasoning.**

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following facts:

> 'the authorities banned the planned march and several of the stationary assemblies'

> 'the assemblies were eventually held on the planned dates. However, the applicants took a risk in holding them given the official ban in force at that time.'

> 'The assemblies were held without a presumption of legality, such a presumption constituting a vital aspect of effective and unhindered exercise of freedom of assembly and freedom of expression.'

> 'the refusals to give authorisation could have had a chilling effect on the applicants and other participants in the assemblies. It could also have discouraged other persons from participating in the assemblies on the grounds that they did not have official authorisation and that, therefore, no official protection against possible hostile counter-demonstrators would be ensured by the authorities.'

> 'legal remedies available to them could not ameliorate their situation as the relevant decisions were given in the appeal proceedings after the date on which the assemblies were held.'

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds its finding that the interference was not prescribed by law on the following facts:

> 'on 22 August 2005 the Local Government Appeals Board found the decision of 3 June 2005 unlawful'

> 'on 17 June 2005 the Mazowsze Governor quashed the refusals of 9 June 2005, finding that they had breached the applicants' freedom of assembly'

> 'the judgment of the Constitutional Court given on 18 January 2006. That court found that the provisions of the Road Traffic Act as applied in the applicants' case were incompatible with the constitutional guarantees of freedom of assembly. It observed that the restrictions on the exercise of this freedom imposed by the impugned provisions were in breach of the proportionality principle applicable to all restrictions imposed on the exercise of rights guaranteed by the Constitution'

The Court also explicitly notes a limitation on the weight of this last fact:
> 'The Court is well aware that under the applicable provisions of the Constitution these provisions lost their binding force after the events concerned in the present case. However, it is of the view that the Constitutional Court's ruling that the impugned provisions were incompatible with the freedom of assembly guaranteed by the Constitution cannot but add force to its own above conclusion concerning the lawfulness of the interference complained of in the present case.'

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court articulates the positive obligation principle in general terms but does not apply it to a separate factual finding of violation. The only fact the Court connects to the positive obligation discussion is:

> 'This obligation is of particular importance for persons holding unpopular views or belonging to minorities, because they are more vulnerable to victimisation.'

This is stated as a general principle rather than grounded in specific facts about the applicants. The Court does not explicitly state facts establishing that the applicants belonged to a minority or held unpopular views in the context of applying the positive obligation doctrine. **No case-specific factual grounding can be identified for the positive obligation element.**
