I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists / Participation:**
The only factual grounding in the text is the following phrase:
>'The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**'

This is the sole factual basis offered. Importantly, this is drawn from the applicants' complaints, not from the Court's own findings of fact. The Court does not explicitly adopt this characterisation as an established fact — it merely recites what the applicants complained of. Therefore, strictly speaking, there is **no factual grounding that the Court itself explicitly accepted** for the scope elements.

**Peaceful assembly:**
There is **no factual grounding** in the text. The Court says nothing about the nature or conduct of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding offered is:
>'disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely the **dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence**'

Again, this is drawn from the recitation of the applicants' complaints. The Court does not separately and explicitly adopt these as established facts. However, the Court does use the word 'interferences' as its own term in its legal assessment ('the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly'), which implies it accepted that these events occurred. That said, **no independent factual findings** are articulated by the Court itself.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

**No factual grounding** — this element is neither discussed nor implied in a way that is grounded in any facts from the text.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

**No factual grounding** — the Court does not identify any facts relating to the aim pursued by the authorities.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's conclusion that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society is grounded not in specific facts but in a reference to prior case law:
>'In the leading cases of **Frumkin v. Russia**, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), **Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia**, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and **Kasparov and Others v. Russia**, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**.'

And:
>'Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints.'

The necessity finding is thus grounded in the similarity to prior cases rather than in any independently articulated facts about what happened in the present case. **No specific facts** about the proportionality of the dispersal, arrest, or conviction are identified in the text.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

**No factual grounding** — this element is not discussed.

---

**Overall observation:** The reasoning text is strikingly thin on explicit factual findings. The Court relies almost entirely on the similarity to leading cases and does not independently articulate the facts it considers established. The only facts mentioned appear in the recitation of the applicants' complaints, and the Court does not formally adopt them as its own findings.
