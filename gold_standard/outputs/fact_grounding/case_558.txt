Here is the factual grounding for each element, drawn only from facts the Court itself accepted or stated:

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element. However, the following facts imply an assembly existed:

> "the applicants had obtained prior authorisation from the Adıyaman Governor to celebrate May Day in the Adıyaman Amphitheatre."

> "While they were walking along the pavement, the police stopped them and used force to disperse the group"

The reference to "the group" and the authorised celebration implies an assembly, but the Court makes no explicit factual finding on this point.

**(ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this implicitly in:

> "the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the meeting constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11."

No separate factual finding is made about participation; it is embedded in the interference finding.

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**
Grounded explicitly in:

> "The Court also notes from the decision of the Public Prosecutor that the group had not presented a danger to public order, or engaged in acts of violence."

---

**(b) Interference**
Grounded in:

> "the police intervention and the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the meeting constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11."

And further factual detail:

> "the police stopped them and used force to disperse the group, without issuing a prior warning. The applicants were subsequently arrested, but released the following day."

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**
The Court's finding is grounded solely in the identification of a legal provision:

> "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of the Assemblies and Marches Act (Law no. 2911), and was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

No further facts are cited regarding foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The sub-elements are not factually grounded because they are not discussed.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**
The Court accepts the aim of preventing public disorder but provides no independent factual grounding of its own. It simply states:

> "the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder and the Court finds no reason to differ."

The Court adopts the Government's characterisation without identifying specific facts that substantiate the aim. There is no independent factual grounding from the Court itself.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**
This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on:

> "the applicants had obtained prior authorisation from the Adıyaman Governor to celebrate May Day in the Adıyaman Amphitheatre."

> "While they were walking along the pavement, the police stopped them and used force to disperse the group, without issuing a prior warning."

> "The applicants were subsequently arrested, but released the following day."

> "the group had not presented a danger to public order, or engaged in acts of violence."

These facts collectively ground the conclusion that:

> "the forceful intervention of the police was disproportionate and was not necessary for the prevention of disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention."

---

**(f) Positive obligation**
The Court mentions positive obligations as part of its general framework but makes no specific factual findings in connection with them. The positive obligation is stated in abstract terms:

> "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to lawful demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens."

> "there may in addition be positive obligations to secure the effective enjoyment of these rights."

No facts are identified by the Court as grounding a separate positive obligation analysis, and no violation finding is made on this basis. There is no factual grounding to report here.
