## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly state facts establishing the existence of an assembly as a distinct finding. The closest factual grounding is the following, which the Court itself accepts:

> "the Court is ready to accept that that protest was an immediate response of the people who had gathered near the Baku Court of Serious Crimes to the judgment of that court against certain Nida members."

This is the Court's own acceptance of the factual circumstances, not merely a party submission.

**ii) Participation**

The Court does not make an explicit factual finding on participation as a distinct step. The closest grounding is the Court's own statement:

> "The dispersal of the assembly and the applicants' arrest and conviction could not but have the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies."

This presupposes participation but does not state it as an explicit factual finding. No verbatim factual finding on participation can be identified beyond this implication.

**iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds its implicit acceptance of the peaceful nature of the assembly on its dismissal of the Government's contrary argument. The relevant factual findings are:

> "none of the documents before it suggest that the first applicant insulted any particular people during the protest of 6 May 2014."

> "The transcript of the first-instance court hearing merely mentions that she stated that she had participated in the protest and 'had rightfully used improper language' in the course of it. Neither the transcript of the hearing nor any other document in the applicant's case file give any further details as to the words used by applicant or to whom she addressed those words."

> "the Court dismisses the Government's argument that the arrest of the first applicant was necessitated by the fact that she had insulted public authorities during the protest."

The Court then proceeds to refer to the assembly as a "peaceful assembly" without further factual elaboration, treating peacefulness as established by the absence of evidence to the contrary.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court does not make a discrete factual finding on interference as a formal step. The factual grounding is embedded in the description of what happened, which the Court treats as undisputed:

> "the dispersal of the protest by the police and their arrest and conviction for an administrative offence"

> "The dispersal of the assembly and the applicants' arrest and conviction could not but have the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies."

> "the sanctions applied to the applicants under Article 298.2 of the CAO were harsh. They were sentenced to thirty and twenty days of administrative detention respectively."

These are the Court's own statements, not merely party submissions.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds its doubts about lawfulness on the following facts, which it itself identifies or accepts:

> "the organisers had not submitted a prior notice about the protest of 6 May 2014 to the relevant authority, the Baku City Executive Authority."

> "the Court is ready to accept that that protest was an immediate response of the people who had gathered near the Baku Court of Serious Crimes to the judgment of that court against certain Nida members. Those circumstances warranted a spontaneous protest on that particular date, and overrode the obligation to submit a prior notice about an assembly."

> "according to Article 5 § IV of the Law on Freedom of Assembly, no prior written notification was required for 'spontaneous assemblies'."

The Court does not make further factual findings on the sub-elements of accessibility or safeguards, consistent with the absence of any discussion of those sub-elements noted above.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

As noted above, the Court makes no formal finding on legitimate aim. The Government's claimed aims are stated in the parties' submissions section but are not adopted by the Court as established facts. No factual grounding can be identified for this element, because the Court does not engage with it as a matter of its own assessment.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its necessity finding on the following facts:

On the absence of relevant and sufficient reasons:

> "the Court concludes that the authorities in the present cases have not adduced relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the protest."

> "The authorities also failed to acknowledge that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention."

On the harshness of the sanctions:

> "the sanctions applied to the applicants under Article 298.2 of the CAO were harsh. They were sentenced to thirty and twenty days of administrative detention respectively."

On the chilling effect:

> "The dispersal of the assembly and the applicants' arrest and conviction could not but have the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies."

> "The measures applied in the present cases, and the fear of sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies, undoubtedly had a chilling effect on the exercise of freedom of assembly. The chilling effect deters opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate."

It should be noted that the Court's necessity analysis is largely conducted by reference to the earlier *Gafgaz Mammadov* judgment ("for the same reasons as those outlined in that judgment"), meaning the factual grounding in the present text is relatively sparse and partly delegated to that prior case.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all.
