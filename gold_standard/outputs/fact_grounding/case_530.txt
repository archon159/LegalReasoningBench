**ii) Participation**

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is found in the opening paragraph:

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most explicitly discussed, yet the factual grounding is notably thin. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it"

and

> "the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints"

and

> "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'"

However, **no specific facts** from the case are quoted or identified by the Court as the basis for its necessity assessment. No particular facts about the proportionality of the dispersal, the conduct of authorities, or the circumstances of the arrest and conviction are cited in support of the necessity finding.

**Overall observation**

The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. The Court relies almost entirely on references to prior case-law and a formulaic statement that it has examined the material, without identifying specific facts that ground its assessments. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case or "clone case" judgment, where the Court applies established findings from leading cases without conducting a fresh factual analysis.
