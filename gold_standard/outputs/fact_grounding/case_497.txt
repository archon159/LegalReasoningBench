**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The Court does not explicitly analyze this element, but the factual grounding is implicit in repeated references to the event itself. The Court refers to 'the meeting' and 'the demonstration' and 'a peaceful demonstration' throughout, but these are characterizations rather than findings of fact quoted from the reasoning section provided. No discrete factual finding about the nature of the gathering is explicitly stated in this reasoning section.

**ii) Participation:**
The factual grounding is: 'the police officers arrested four of the applicants, together with a number of other people taking part in the demonstration.' This establishes participation as a fact accepted by the Court.

**iii) Peaceful:**
The factual grounding is drawn from the domestic court's findings, which the Court explicitly adopts: 'having regard to the findings of the Malatya Criminal Court of First Instance, the Court observes that the applicants and the other demonstrators did not act in breach of this law' and 'the decision adopted by the Malatya Criminal Court of First Instance also shows that the group neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence.'

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: 'the intervention of the police officers, which led to the subsequent arrests of the four applicants for participating in the meeting, as well as the ill-treatment to which they were subjected.' Two distinct facts ground the interference finding: the arrests and the ill-treatment.

For Ms İspir's exclusion, the factual grounding is: 'the applicant Refika Meltem İspir failed to substantiate her allegation that she had been sprayed with pepper gas' and 'unlike the remaining four applicants, Ms İspir was not arrested.' The absence of these facts means no interference was found for her.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The factual grounding is: 'the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely sections 23 and 24 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act.' This is the sole factual basis offered. As noted previously, the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not discussed, and accordingly no further factual grounding exists for them.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court states 'the Court finds no reason to differ' from the Government's submission that the aim was preventing public disorder. The Court does not independently identify facts grounding this finding — it simply accepts the Government's characterization without elaboration. There is no independently stated factual basis in the reasoning text beyond this acceptance.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court draws on the following facts:

First, from the domestic court's findings, explicitly adopted: 'having regard to the findings of the Malatya Criminal Court of First Instance, the Court observes that the applicants and the other demonstrators did not act in breach of this law, contrary to the allegations of the Government.'

Second, again from the domestic court's findings: 'the decision adopted by the Malatya Criminal Court of First Instance also shows that the group neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence.'

Third, regarding the prosecution: 'the four applicants were prosecuted – albeit subsequently acquitted – on account of the mere fact of their participation in a peaceful demonstration.'

These three factual findings together ground the Court's conclusion that the interference was 'disproportionate and not necessary for preventing disorder.'

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, the Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding can be identified for this element.
