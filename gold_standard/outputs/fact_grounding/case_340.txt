## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**Assembly exists / Participation:**
The Court does not explicitly state facts establishing these elements. They are implied by the Court's unquestioned treatment of the applicant's planned event as a public assembly. The closest factual grounding is the Court's own framing: *"It has not been disputed between the parties that the proposal to change the location of the applicant's public event amounted to an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly."* The Court adopts this as an uncontested starting point, which implicitly establishes that an assembly existed and that the applicant was its organiser.

**Peaceful:**
No specific facts are cited by the Court to establish this. It is implied by the absence of any suggestion to the contrary and by the Court's use of the phrase "freedom of peaceful assembly" without qualification. No factual grounding can be identified beyond this.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding entirely on the uncontested nature of the parties' positions, not on independently stated facts:

*"It has not been disputed between the parties that the proposal to change the location of the applicant's public event amounted to an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly (see Lashmankin and Others, cited above, §§ 404-09)."*

No additional independent factual grounding is provided by the Court for this element.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court does not resolve this element. However, it does identify the legal provisions relied upon by the domestic authorities as a factual matter:

*"The Court notes that the domestic authorities relied on two legal provisions prohibiting public events at the location chosen by the applicant: while the town administration relied on a regional law prohibiting public events in Stefanovskaya Square in Syktyvkar (see paragraphs 9 and 34 above), the domestic courts referred to the town administration's decree delimiting the perimeter of the zone in the immediate vicinity of the Constitutional Court of the Komi Republic (see paragraphs 17 and 35 above), where the holding of public events was prohibited by section 8 of the Public Events Act."*

Beyond identifying these provisions, the Court makes no factual findings on foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards as such. The implicit safeguards concern is grounded in the following factual observation, though framed under necessity:

*"the general ban under section 8 of the Public Events Act on holding public events in the vicinity of court buildings is incompatible with Article 11 § 2, taking into account its absolute nature coupled with the local executive authorities' wide discretion in determining what is considered to be 'in the immediate vicinity'."*

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not resolve this element and makes no independent factual findings in support of or against a legitimate aim. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court's assessment rests on the following facts, stated or adopted by the Court itself:

**On the vicinity-of-court ban:**

*"the general ban under section 8 of the Public Events Act on holding public events in the vicinity of court buildings is incompatible with Article 11 § 2, taking into account its absolute nature coupled with the local executive authorities' wide discretion in determining what is considered to be 'in the immediate vicinity'."*

*"The refusal to approve the location chosen by the applicant for his public event by reference to the fact that it was to be held in the vicinity of the Constitutional Court of the Komi Republic could not, therefore, be regarded as being 'necessary in a democratic society' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."*

**On the regional law ban on Stefanovskaya Square:**

The Court grounds its necessity analysis on the following facts it itself identifies or accepts:

1. The content and character of the regional law's justification:
*"the regional law establishing that ban simply paraphrased the Public Events Act – stating that the holding of public events there might result in disruptions in the functioning of public utilities, transport, social or communications services and the obstruction of pedestrians or traffic or the hindering of citizens' access to residential buildings or transport and social services."*

2. The absence of any location-specific justification:
*"neither the regional law itself, nor any other legal provision or judicial interpretation explained which of the above circumstances were relevant to Stefanovskaya Square and why."*

3. The absence of any argument regarding disorder or security:
*"No arguments regarding risk of public disorder or security considerations have ever been advanced to justify the general ban on holding public events in Stefanovskaya Square."*

4. The absence of any explanation for why a general ban was preferred over case-by-case examination:
*"Nor has any explanation been given as to why a general ban was a more feasible means of achieving the aim of preventing serious disruptions of people's ordinary life than a provision allowing a case-by-case examination, taking account of the possibility of minimising such disruption, for example by organising a temporary diversion of traffic on alternative routes or by taking other similar measures, and at the same time accommodating the organisers' legitimate interests in assembling at their chosen location."*

5. The absence of argument that relaxing the ban would risk abuse:
*"it was never argued that the general ban could not be relaxed without a risk of abuse, significant uncertainty, discrimination or arbitrariness."*

6. The unlimited temporal and categorical scope of the ban:
*"the general ban is not limited in time and applies to all public events, irrespective of the number of participants and the event's potential to cause disruptions of people's ordinary life."*

7. The absence of parliamentary and judicial scrutiny:
*"The general ban at issue is therefore not specifically circumscribed to address a precise risk of serious disruption with a minimum impairment of the right to freedom of assembly. It has not therefore been shown that the general ban on holding public events in Stefanovskaya Square has been the subject of an exacting parliamentary and judicial review."*

**Final conclusion grounded in the above facts:**

*"the refusal to approve the location of the applicant's public event by reference to the general ban on holding public events in the vicinity of court buildings or by reference to the ban, established by a regional law, on holding public events in Stefanovskaya Square in Syktyvkar, without any consideration to the specific circumstances of the case, could not therefore be regarded as being 'necessary in a democratic society' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."*

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding exists, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations in this case.
