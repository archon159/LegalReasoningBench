## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, but the following facts ground the implicit conclusion that an assembly existed:
- "the group of demonstrators was informed by the police that their march was unlawful"
- "the rally began at about noon and ended with the group's arrest within half an hour at 12.30 p.m."
- "the group in question consisted of forty-six persons"

**ii) Participation**
- "by participating in this meeting, the applicants aimed to draw attention to F-type prison conditions, which was a topical issue at the time"

**iii) Peaceful**
- "there is no evidence to suggest that the group presented a danger to public order, apart from possibly blocking the tram line"
- "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence" — the Court uses this formulation in a way that treats the present demonstrators as falling within this category, though it is stated as a general principle rather than an explicit factual finding

---

### (b) Interference

- "there is no dispute between the parties as to the initial existence of an interference with the applicants' right of assembly"
- "the interference in the meeting, the force used by the police to disperse the participants and the subsequent prosecution could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants from taking part in similar meetings"
- "the applicants were negatively affected by the police intervention and subsequent criminal proceedings brought against them, irrespective of the final result"
- "this decision [acquittal] was delivered on 19 September 2005, almost 5 years after the incident"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

- "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of Law No. 2911 (Assemblies and Marches Act)"

No further facts are cited by the Court to ground the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Court simply identifies the legal basis and moves on.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No independent facts are cited by the Court to ground this finding. The Court states "the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder and the Court finds no reason to differ" — but this is an acceptance of the Government's legal characterisation, not a finding grounded in specific facts. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the general context of an unnotified demonstration.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively fact-grounded element. The relevant facts are:

- "the group in question consisted of forty-six persons, who wished to draw attention to a topical issue, namely the F-type prison conditions"
- "the rally began at about noon and ended with the group's arrest within half an hour at 12.30 p.m."
- "there is no evidence to suggest that the group presented a danger to public order, apart from possibly blocking the tram line"
- "the group of demonstrators was informed by the police that their march was unlawful and would disrupt public order at a busy time of the day, and had been ordered to disperse. The applicants and other demonstrators did not comply with these orders and attempted to continue their march" — the Court acknowledges this fact but does not treat it as sufficient to justify the intervention
- "although no notification had been given, the authorities had prior knowledge... that such a demonstration would take place on that date and could have therefore taken preventive measures"
- "at the material time no authorisation was required for the holding of public demonstrations; however, notification was required seventy-two hours prior to the event"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court states the positive obligation principle in general terms — "there may in addition be positive obligations to secure the effective enjoyment of these rights" — but does not ground this in any specific facts of the case, nor does it apply it to reach a factual conclusion. No factual grounding can be identified for this element as applied to the present case.
