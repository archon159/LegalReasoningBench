Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
However, this is drawn from the applicants' complaints, not from the Court's own factual findings. The Court does not independently establish the existence of assemblies as a factual matter. There is no separate Court-adopted factual statement confirming assemblies took place.

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." Again, this is framed as the applicants' complaint. The Court does not independently verify or adopt a factual finding on participation.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court makes no factual statement — adopted or otherwise — about the peaceful nature of the assemblies.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court's factual grounding for interference is:
"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

Again, this appears in the framing of the applicants' complaints. However, the Court implicitly adopts these facts by proceeding to analyze them as "interferences" — notably using the word "interferences" as an established fact in its own analytical conclusion: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." By using the definite article and the noun "interferences" without qualification, the Court treats the arrests, dispersals, and convictions as established facts grounding the interference finding.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court provides no independent factual analysis grounding its necessity assessment. It states:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a purely procedural/comparative statement — the Court is saying that nothing in the present case distinguishes it from the leading cases (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov). The factual grounding for the necessity finding is therefore entirely borrowed by reference to those prior cases, rather than established through any facts quoted or adopted from the present record. No specific facts from the present case are cited to support the necessity conclusion.

---

**Summary observation:**

The reasoning is strikingly thin on factual grounding throughout. The Court relies almost entirely on the pattern-matching technique of referring to leading cases and stating that no distinguishing facts have been found. The only facts that appear — arrests, dispersal, and administrative convictions — are drawn from the applicants' framing of their complaints, and are adopted only implicitly by the Court's use of the term "interferences" in its own conclusion.
