## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

No explicit factual grounding. The Court implies this without identifying specific facts. No quotable passage establishes this as a reasoned factual finding.

**ii) Participation (applicant as organiser)**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "Although when notifying the mayor about the rally planned for 30 September 2006 and when seeking judicial review of his decision to ban it the applicant purported to act on behalf of the unregistered Macedonian Initiative Committee, he was one of the rally's organisers (see paragraphs 9 and 11 above)."

And in the merits section:

> "The applicant was not proposing to hold a 'solo demonstration' but to take part in a gathering with others."

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

No explicit factual grounding. The Court does not identify any facts to establish this. It is implied by the absence of any contrary finding.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on the same facts as the participation element, referring back to its earlier finding:

> "The Court has already found that the applicant, who was one of the rally's organisers, was personally affected by the mayor's decision to ban it (see paragraph 39 above). For the same reasons, the Court considers that the ban amounted to a 'restriction' of the applicant's right to peaceful assembly under Article 11 of the Convention."

The factual basis is therefore the existence of the ban itself, as established earlier in the admissibility section through the applicant's role as organiser and his dealings with the authorities.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declines to make a factual finding on this element. No factual grounding is identified or needed, as the Court assumes the element in the applicant's favour without deciding it.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Same as above. The Court assumes without deciding. No factual grounding is established by the Court.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding. The Court relies on the following established facts:

**The justifications offered by the domestic authorities:**

> "The mayor and the court which upheld his decision justified the ban by reference to the holding of a municipal event at the same time and place and by the need to protect the participants in that event from being exposed to controversial statements on historical issues seen as sensitive (see paragraphs 10 and 12 above)."

**The absence of detail from the domestic authorities:**

> "The domestic authorities provided no details regarding the logistical or security difficulties which two parallel events might have posed."

**The precedent of a prior rally being permitted alongside a counter-demonstration:**

> "on a previous occasion when the authorities did not ban a rally organised by Ilinden, they allowed a counter-demonstration to proceed on the same day (see United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden and Ivanov, cited above, § 115)."

**The pattern of prior identical cases:**

> "In four materially identical cases such grounds have been found to be insufficient for the purposes of Article 11 § 2 (see Stankov and the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden, §§ 106-07; United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden and Ivanov, §§ 113-14; United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden and Ivanov (no. 2), § 133; and Singartiyski and Others, § 46, all cited above)."

Note: this last point is grounded in prior case law rather than facts of the instant case, but the Court treats it as directly relevant to the assessment of necessity here.

**The Government's failure to distinguish the present case:**

> "the Government have not explained why justifications considered insufficient in previous cases should suffice in the instant one."

This is a procedural/argumentative fact — the absence of a justification — rather than a substantive fact about the rally itself, but the Court explicitly relies on it.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
