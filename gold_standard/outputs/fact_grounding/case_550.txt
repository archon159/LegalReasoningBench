## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court's implicit acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in the following facts:

> "the group in question concerned around 30-35 people. It appears that they were carrying banners and candles and that they wanted to read out a press statement regarding Israel's actions against Palestine, a topical issue, at Tünnel square."

> "According to the incident report in the present case, drafted at 9.15 p.m., the demonstration began at 8 p.m."

**ii) Participation of the applicant**

The Court does not explicitly ground the applicant's participation in specific facts beyond the general statement that the police intervention and arrest constituted interference with *his* rights. The only factual grounding is implicit in the reference to his arrest:

> "the police intervention and the subsequent arrest of the applicant constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicant's rights under Article 11 of the Convention."

No further specific facts are quoted or adopted by the Court to establish the applicant's participation as a distinct finding.

**iii) Peaceful nature of the assembly**

The Court does not explicitly make a factual finding that the assembly was peaceful. However, the closest factual grounding for the implied finding is:

> "the group in question concerned around 30-35 people. It appears that they were carrying banners and candles and that they wanted to read out a press statement regarding Israel's actions against Palestine, a topical issue, at Tünnel square."

> "It appears that the demonstrators decided to disband the demonstration after the police had ordered them, several times, to disperse."

The Court also applies the principle about non-violent demonstrators to this case, which implies a factual finding of peacefulness, but does not state this finding in explicit terms grounded in named facts.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in:

> "the police intervention and the subsequent arrest of the applicant constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicant's rights under Article 11 of the Convention."

The Court does not elaborate further on the specific facts underlying this finding beyond the bare reference to police intervention and arrest. No additional facts are explicitly adopted here.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court's finding is grounded in the identification of a legal basis:

> "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely Article 24 of the Assemblies and Marches Act (Law no. 2911)."

Additionally, the Court makes a factual finding relevant to the lawfulness question:

> "the Court notes that the official documents submitted by the parties refer to the absence of authorisation, which is not a requisite under Turkish law, and not to the absence of any prior notification."

> "the applicant has neither argued that a prior notification had been given to the authorities, in accordance with Article 10 of Law no. 2911, nor that the situation in Palestine called for a spontaneous demonstration overriding this obligation."

> "the Court will presume that the reference to the absence of authorisation in the official documents implied the absence of prior notification."

No facts are identified going to foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards, as the Court does not discuss those sub-elements.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court makes no independent factual findings to ground the legitimate aim conclusion. It simply states satisfaction that the aims of preventing disorder and protecting public safety were pursued, without grounding this in specific facts. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the bare assertion.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element.

**On the nature and scale of the demonstration:**
> "the group in question concerned around 30-35 people. It appears that they were carrying banners and candles and that they wanted to read out a press statement regarding Israel's actions against Palestine, a topical issue, at Tünnel square."

**On the absence of demonstrated danger:**
> "the Government gave no particular reasons, such as the specificities of the location of the demonstration, to show that this group represented a danger to public order or public safety. The Court finds no such evidence on its own motion."

**On the promptness of dispersal:**
> "According to the incident report in the present case, drafted at 9.15 p.m., the demonstration began at 8 p.m."

> "the applicant claimed that the police intervention was immediate, a fact not disputed by the Government."

> "taking into account that the police had already returned to the Beyoğlu police station by 9.15 p.m., the Court considers that the dispersal was quite prompt."

**On the applicant's inability to manifest his views:**
> "it is not satisfied that the applicant had sufficient time - together with his fellow demonstrators - to manifest his views."

**On the demonstrators' conduct:**
> "It appears that the demonstrators decided to disband the demonstration after the police had ordered them, several times, to disperse."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
