I will go through each element for which I found explicit discussion or implication, and identify the factual grounding in the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The factual grounding is: "the public assembly" — this phrase appears in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of the public assembly."
The Court uses the definite article ("the public assembly"), treating the existence of an assembly as an established fact, but provides no further factual elaboration.

**ii) Participation**
The factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of the public assembly."
The applicants are described as "participants," which the Court treats as given, but again no further facts are provided.

**iii) Peaceful**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not mention the character of the assembly at all. The peaceful nature is implied structurally by the Court's willingness to apply Article 11, but there is no fact in the reasoning text that grounds this.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit: "the dispersal of the assembly, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences."

This appears in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of the public assembly, in particular about the dispersal of the assembly, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences."

The Court refers back to these facts when it speaks of "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly," treating the dispersal, arrest, and conviction as the established acts of interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is neither discussed nor implied in the reasoning text.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is neither discussed nor implied in the reasoning text.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The factual grounding is thin. The Court does not conduct an independent factual analysis but instead grounds its necessity assessment on two things visible in the text:

First, the nature of the measures: "the dispersal of the assembly, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences" — these are the measures whose necessity is being assessed.

Second, the Court grounds its conclusion by reference to prior case-law rather than fresh factual findings: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And: "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."

So the necessity finding is grounded not in specific facts about this assembly, but in the similarity of the issues to prior cases and the absence of any distinguishing facts or arguments. The Court does not quote or adopt any specific factual submissions from the parties in support of this conclusion.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.
