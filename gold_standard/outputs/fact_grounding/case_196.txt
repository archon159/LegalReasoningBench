Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**Assembly exists / Participation:**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase:
> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

and

> "organisers or participants of public assemblies"

These are the only facts the Court states. They establish that assemblies took place and that the applicants were involved as organisers or participants. No further factual detail is provided.

**Peaceful:**
There is no factual grounding at all for the peacefulness of the assemblies. The Court states no facts bearing on this question. As noted in the prior analysis, peacefulness is implied by the Court's proceeding to assess the case under Article 11, but there is no factual statement in the reasoning that supports this inference.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is:
> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

These are the only concrete facts stated. The arrest, dispersal, and administrative conviction are the acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided about the circumstances of the arrests or the nature of the convictions.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding for the necessity assessment in this reasoning text. The Court does not set out any facts about, for example, the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the police response, the behaviour of participants, or the reasons given by domestic courts. Instead, the Court grounds its necessity finding entirely by reference to prior case-law:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The reference to "all the material submitted to it" indicates the Court reviewed factual submissions, but it does not identify, adopt, or quote any specific facts from those submissions. The necessity finding is therefore grounded in the prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) rather than in any facts explicitly stated in this reasoning section.

---

**Summary observation:**
The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. Across all elements discussed or implied, the only concrete facts explicitly stated are (1) that there were public assemblies, (2) that the applicants were organisers or participants, (3) that the assemblies were dispersed, (4) that the applicants were arrested, and (5) that they were convicted of administrative offences. All substantive legal conclusions rest primarily on the prior case-law rather than on facts set out in this reasoning.
