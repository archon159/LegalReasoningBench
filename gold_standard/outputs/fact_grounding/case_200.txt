Below I work through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding the Court relied upon.

---

**(a)(iii) Peaceful nature of the assembly**

The Court's assessment is grounded in three distinct factual bases, all explicitly stated:

1. *"the applicants' protests were staged in accordance with the old and new laws concerning assemblies"*
2. *"they remained peaceful"*
3. *"they did not disturb public order in any way"*

The Court then explicitly identifies the evidentiary basis for these factual conclusions:

4. *"This conclusion is supported by the videos attached to the case-file and by the conclusions of the domestic courts which acquitted the applicants."*

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court's assessment is grounded in one explicit fact:

1. *"the applicants' arrest constituted 'interference by [a] public authority' with their right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11"*

The arrest is thus the sole factual anchor for the interference finding. No further elaboration of the circumstances of the arrest is provided in this passage.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court's negative conclusion on lawfulness is grounded in the same facts established under (a)(iii) above, which the Court explicitly links to the lawfulness assessment:

1. *"the applicants' protests were staged in accordance with the old and new laws concerning assemblies"*
2. *"they remained peaceful"*
3. *"they did not disturb public order in any way"*

The Court then draws the legal conclusion directly from these facts: *"In such circumstances, the interference with their right of assembly cannot be considered lawful under domestic law."*

The acquittals by domestic courts and the video evidence, cited above, also serve as factual grounding here, since the Court uses them to support the overall factual picture from which the unlawfulness conclusion flows.

---

**(a)(i) Existence of an assembly and (a)(ii) Participation**

As noted in the prior analysis, these elements are implied rather than explicitly discussed. The Court refers to *"the applicants' protests"* and *"their right of assembly"* and *"their arrest,"* but provides no separate factual analysis grounding these elements. **No distinct factual grounding can be identified for these implied elements beyond the general references to "the applicants' protests" and their arrest.**

---

**(d) Legitimate aim, (e) Necessity, (f) Positive obligation**

These elements were not discussed, explicitly or by implication. **No factual grounding can be identified, as the Court did not reach these stages of the analysis.**
