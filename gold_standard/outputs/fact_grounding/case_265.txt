## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly analyze this, but grounds its implicit acceptance of an assembly in the following factual reference:
> "the sole ground for the applicant's conviction under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK was his participation in **the demonstration in question**"

No further factual description of the demonstration is provided in the reasoning text.

**ii) Participation**
Grounded in the same passage:
> "the sole ground for the applicant's conviction under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK was **his participation in the demonstration in question** and his alleged incitement of the group who was shouting slogans and threw stones at the police"

Note: the incitement allegation is attributed to the domestic courts' basis for conviction, not independently verified by the Court as established fact.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**
No factual grounding identifiable. The Court does not discuss this sub-element.

---

### (b) Interference

Grounded in:
> "the applicant's conviction amounted to an 'interference' with the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly"

The specific factual basis for the interference is the conviction itself:
> "the applicant alleged that he had been convicted for disseminating propaganda in favour of the PKK due to his activities falling within the scope of Article 11"

And more precisely, the nature of that conviction:
> "his conviction under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court merely assumes this element without grounding it in any specific facts:
> "It is further prepared to assume that the interference was prescribed by law"

No factual grounding is provided beyond the implicit reference to section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 as the legal basis for the conviction. The Court does not analyze the qualities of that provision.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

Again, the Court assumes rather than firmly establishes this, and provides no independent factual grounding. The only factual anchor is the nature of the conviction itself (under anti-terrorism legislation), which the Court treats as sufficient to assume the aims of:
> "protecting national security and preventing disorder and crime"

No specific facts are cited to ground these aims beyond the implicit character of the legislation invoked.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following facts:

First, the sole basis for the conviction:
> "the sole ground for the applicant's conviction under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713 for disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK was his participation in the demonstration in question and his alleged incitement of the group who was shouting slogans and threw stones at the police"

Second, the failure of the domestic courts to connect those facts to the offence charged:
> "the trial court did not demonstrate any connection or relevance between those actions and the scope and purpose of the offence of disseminating propaganda under section 7(2) of Law no. 3713"

Third, the absence of any other factual basis in the domestic reasoning:
> "bearing in mind that in their reasoned judgments the domestic courts failed to indicate any other actions which could be regarded as disseminating propaganda in support of the PKK"

Fourth, the nature of the sanction imposed:
> "the imposition of a prison sentence amounted to a disproportionate interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
