I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

The only factual grounding in the text is the following description from the opening paragraph:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

and

> "organisers or participants of public assemblies"

These phrases provide minimal factual grounding for the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation. There is no factual grounding for the peaceful nature of the assemblies — the Court implies this but grounds it in nothing stated in this reasoning section.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

This is the Court's own characterisation of what happened, not merely a party submission. It identifies arrest, dispersal, and criminal conviction as the concrete acts constituting interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only element explicitly decided, yet it is striking that **no specific facts** are cited in support of the necessity finding. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

and

> "Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely in the reference to prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) and the general statement that no new fact or argument was found. **No case-specific facts are cited** to support the necessity assessment. The Court relies on the similarity of the present case to the leading cases rather than on any independently stated factual analysis.

---

**Summary observation**

The factual grounding across all discussed or implied elements is remarkably thin. The only facts stated in the reasoning text are the bare descriptions of arrest, dispersal, and conviction. The Court does not set out the circumstances of the assemblies, the conduct of the authorities, the legal basis invoked, or any other case-specific facts. This is consistent with the judgment being a repetitive case decided by reference to leading judgments, where detailed factual analysis is not reproduced in the reasoning section provided.
