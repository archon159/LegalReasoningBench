**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following verbatim passage:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the situation, not merely a party submission. It establishes that there were "public assemblies" and that the applicants were "organisers or participants" of them. However, it should be noted that this is framed as a description of what the applicants "complained of," rather than an unambiguous judicial finding of fact. There is no factual grounding in the text for the sub-element of peacefulness — the Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies.

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same passage:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

Again, this is the Court's own characterisation. The specific acts constituting interference — arrest, dispersal of assemblies, and conviction for administrative offences — are identified here. No additional factual detail is provided in the reasoning text.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is not discussed, and no facts are recited in relation to it.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is not discussed, and no facts are recited in relation to it.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is the central conclusion of the reasoning, but it is striking that **no specific facts are cited to ground it**. The Court's assessment rests entirely on a reference to prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court does not identify which specific facts it examined, nor does it explain how the facts of the present case map onto the leading cases. The necessity conclusion is therefore factually ungrounded in the text of this reasoning section as reproduced.

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified. The element is not discussed.
