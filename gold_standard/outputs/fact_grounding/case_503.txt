Here is the analysis of factual grounding for each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**, namely their arrest in relation to the **dispersal of these assemblies**"

No further facts about the nature, location, size, or purpose of the assemblies are provided in this reasoning section.

**ii) Participation:**
> "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies"

No further individual facts about any specific applicant's participation are provided.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court does not describe the character of the assemblies. This element is implied purely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is:
> "namely their **arrest** in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These are the only concrete governmental acts identified. No further facts about the circumstances of arrest, the nature of the administrative offences, or the penalties imposed are provided in this section.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the element most explicitly decided, yet it has **no independent factual grounding** in this reasoning section. The Court does not cite any specific facts about the conduct of the authorities, the proportionality of the response, or the circumstances of the dispersals to support its necessity finding. Instead, the Court grounds its conclusion entirely in:

1. A reference to prior case-law:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

2. A general reference to submitted material without specifying what it contained:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it..."

3. A conclusion stated without factual elaboration:
> "the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

So the necessity finding is grounded in **analogical reasoning from precedent** and a general review of unspecified material, not in any explicitly stated facts about the present case.

---

**Summary observation:**
The reasoning section is notably sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. It functions essentially as a repetition/clone judgment, relying on the similarity to leading cases rather than independent factual analysis. The only concrete facts stated anywhere in the section are the arrests and administrative convictions of the applicants as organisers or participants of public assemblies.
