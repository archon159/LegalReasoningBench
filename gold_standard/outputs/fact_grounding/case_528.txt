## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:
> "the event of 5 May 2018 has been seen by its organisers, the authorities and participants as a single public event"

> "what was then classified as a separate non-approved demonstration"

> "the meeting and what was then classified as a separate non-approved demonstration"

**ii) Participation**

> "The applicants were not in charge of organising or running any assembly on 5 May 2018, and, a fortiori, did not intentionally fail to abide by the requirement to lodge a prior notice about a planned event. They were convicted as ordinary demonstrators."

**iii) Peaceful**

> "The event and their participation in it were peaceful."

> "There were no signs of an imminent outbreak of violence or increase in the level of disturbance."

---

### (b) Interference

> "The interferences in the form of prosecution for failing to comply with those orders to disperse"

> "The applicants were convicted for failing to comply with the police orders to cease their participation in that non-approved event."

> "No other act was held against them under Article 20.2 § 5 of the CAO together with section 6 § 3 of the PEA."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**Foreseeability (left open)**

The Court grounds its decision to leave this open on the following facts:

> "The Public Events Act expressly provided for a possibility to hold or participate in what could be classified as a hybrid public event. That type of event could, for instance, include movement of demonstrators from one venue to another."

> "The Government have not referred to any other regulations or jurisprudence clarifying the approach concerning a hybrid event."

> "the factual elements in the file strongly indicate that the event of 5 May 2018 has been seen by its organisers, the authorities and participants as a single public event"

The foreseeability question specifically concerned whether applicants could have anticipated prosecution in *separate proceedings* for non-compliance with a dispersal order issued after what was reclassified as a separate non-approved demonstration. The Court does not resolve this factually, explicitly leaving it open.

**Accessibility and safeguards:** No factual grounding identified, as these sub-elements were not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not make any factual findings in support of or against a legitimate aim. It merely uses the phrase "even assuming that the reasons relied on by the courts were relevant," which is a legal assumption, not a factual finding.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding:

**On the absence of real risk or disruption:**
> "The courts did not substantiate that there had been any real risk of disruption to ordinary life due to the actions of the applicants or their fellow protestors that, in the concrete circumstances, would have exceeded the level of minor disturbance that follows from normal exercise of the right of peaceful assembly in a public place."

> "There were no signs of an imminent outbreak of violence or increase in the level of disturbance."

**On the sole basis for the dispersal order:**
> "The only specific act held against them was their non-compliance with an order to disperse that was issued solely on the basis that that event had not received official approval from the competent authority."

**On the absence of balancing by domestic courts:**
> "The courts did not ascertain that, in exercising the discretion afforded to them by the domestic law by way of issuing orders to disperse, the police had acted in a manner compatible with the essence of the right to freedom of peaceful assembly, and with due recognition of the privileged protection under the Convention of political speech, debate on questions of public interest and the peaceful expression of opinions on such matters."

> "Nor does it appear that the courts in the CAO cases struck a fair balance between those interests, on the one hand, and those of the applicants in exercising their right to freedom of peaceful assembly, on the other."

> "At the time the authorities were not required to carry out such a balancing assessment"

**On the absence of substantive justification:**
> "The interferences in the form of prosecution for failing to comply with those orders to disperse were not justified with reference to specific and averred substantive grounds."

**On the conduct of the applicants at the scene:**
> "it also appears that the police did not consider it necessary to levy any sanctions upon the applicants at the event venue, as they left it sometime after the orders to disperse had been given"

**On the systemic nature of the problem:**
> "The shortcomings mentioned above have been identified in numerous cases where the Russian police intervened for the sole reason that the public event had not received official approval, and in which that formal unlawfulness had been put forward as the only justification, including for convicting the applicants of administrative offences"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding identified, as this element was not discussed.
