## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, but the following facts ground the implied finding that assemblies existed:

> "on every occasion when Ilinden wished to hold a rally at Yane Sandanski's grave near the Rozhen Monastery, the Mayor of Sandanski imposed restrictions on the timing and manner of organisation of its events"

> "the Mayor of Petrich systematically banned or imposed restrictions on the events which Ilinden sought to organise in the Samuilova Krepost area"

> "in Blagoevgrad, between 2004 and 2007 the Mayor systematically banned the events planned by Ilinden"

**(ii) Participation**

The Court does not explicitly discuss this element. The following facts ground the implied finding that the applicants participated or attempted to participate:

> "on a number of occasions, the Blagoevgrad police issued orders to members and supporters of Ilinden not to organise or take part in rallies"

> "on several occasions, the Blagoevgrad police arrested participants in rallies and seized materials from them"

> "on one occasion, participants in a rally were fined"

**(iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court does not explicitly discuss this element. The following fact grounds the implied finding that the assemblies were peaceful:

> "Even if there was a risk of clashes between participants in Ilinden's rallies and counter-demonstrators, it was the task of the police to stand between the two groups and to ensure public order, as was shown in fact to be possible."

This implies the violence risk came from counter-demonstrators, not from the applicants' assemblies themselves. No facts are cited by the Court establishing violent conduct on the part of Ilinden's participants.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in the following facts:

> "on every occasion when Ilinden wished to hold a rally at Yane Sandanski's grave near the Rozhen Monastery, the Mayor of Sandanski imposed restrictions on the timing and manner of organisation of its events"

> "In April 2007 the Regional Governor of Blagoevgrad imposed an outright ban on the rally"

> "the Mayor of Petrich systematically banned or imposed restrictions on the events which Ilinden sought to organise in the Samuilova Krepost area"

> "in Blagoevgrad, between 2004 and 2007 the Mayor systematically banned the events planned by Ilinden"

> "on a number of occasions, the Blagoevgrad police issued orders to members and supporters of Ilinden not to organise or take part in rallies"

> "on several occasions, the Blagoevgrad police arrested participants in rallies and seized materials from them"

> "on one occasion, participants in a rally were fined"

The Court then draws the legal conclusion directly from these facts:

> "Those measures were clearly aimed at hindering or even altogether preventing the events planned by Ilinden. While in many cases not actually impeding the holding of those events, they undoubtedly had a chilling effect on the individuals concerned and on the other participants in the rallies and thus amounted, in themselves, to interferences with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**(i) Foreseeability**

The Court's misgivings about foreseeability are grounded in the following facts:

> "no legal basis has been cited for the arrests and seizures made by the police during certain rallies"

> "on a number of occasions the Mayor of Blagoevgrad relied on the fact that Ilinden was not registered to ban its rallies... even though this was not an express requirement of the law"

> "the 1990 Meetings and Marches Act envisages no role for regional governors in the policing of rallies; moreover, in banning the rally the Regional Governor likewise relied on some clearly irrelevant grounds, such as UMO Ilinden – PIRIN's lack of registration"

> "section 62(1) of the 1997 Ministry of Internal Affairs Act, which served as a basis for the orders issued by the police, was formulated very generally and gave no indication of the circumstances in which the police could use the power conferred on them... The same goes for section 55(1) of the 2006 Ministry of Internal Affairs Act, which superseded that provision on 1 May 2006"

> "the fines imposed by the mayor of Blagoevgrad were later annulled by the courts"

**(ii) Accessibility**

No factual grounding identified. The Court does not discuss this element.

**(iii) Safeguards against abuse**

The following fact grounds the implied concern about absence of safeguards:

> "section 62(1) of the 1997 Ministry of Internal Affairs Act, which served as a basis for the orders issued by the police, was formulated very generally and gave no indication of the circumstances in which the police could use the power conferred on them"

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court explicitly declines to determine whether a legitimate aim was pursued. No facts are identified or accepted by the Court as establishing any particular legitimate aim. No factual grounding can be mapped to this element.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element is the most extensively grounded in facts. The Court's finding of non-necessity rests on the following:

**Repetition of previously condemned grounds:**

> "The mayors of Blagoevgrad and Petrich relied on essentially the same grounds as those given to ban such rallies previously: the organisation's unregistered status, conjectural threats to public order, the holding of events of other organisations and/or the authorities at the same time and place, and lastly, Ilinden's controversial statements on issues perceived as sensitive"

> "The fact that the national authorities should rely on grounds which the Court had, at the time when those authorities made their decisions, already found problematic is indicative of a troubling disregard for the Court's judgments and the applicants' right to freedom of peaceful assembly"

**Absence of reasons for restrictions:**

> "on those occasions when the mayors did not ban the rallies, but only imposed restrictions as to their time, duration, place and manner, they either did not give any reasons, or briefly referred to the holding of other events at the same time and place without specifying why the simultaneous holding of two events would be impossible or why the specific restrictions envisaged were considered necessary"

**Police orders without reasons:**

> "on a number of occasions the police, without giving any reasons, ordered members and supporters of Ilinden to refrain from organising or taking part in commemorative rallies"

**Counter-demonstrator risk insufficient justification:**

> "Even if there was a risk of clashes between participants in Ilinden's rallies and counter-demonstrators, it was the task of the police to stand between the two groups and to ensure public order, as was shown in fact to be possible. That reason for interfering with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly could not therefore be considered as relevant and sufficient."

**Escalation of police conduct:**

> "in September 2007 and in May and September 2009 the police in Blagoevgrad toughened their approach, arresting participants in Ilinden's rallies without citing any grounds for the arrests and without any violent behaviour warranting such measures, and, on one occasion, fining them"

> "While the fines were later set aside by the courts, they doubtlessly had a chilling effect and discouraged those concerned from taking part in similar rallies in the future"

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court's implicit rejection of the Government's positive obligation argument is grounded in the following fact:

> "Even if there was a risk of clashes between participants in Ilinden's rallies and counter-demonstrators, it was the task of the police to stand between the two groups and to ensure public order, as was shown in fact to be possible."

This establishes both that the State had a positive obligation to protect the assemblies from disruption, and that this obligation was in fact capable of being fulfilled. The Court does not separately identify facts establishing a breach of positive obligation — rather, the positive obligation dimension is subsumed into the necessity analysis, and the overall violation is grounded in the facts listed under (e) above.
