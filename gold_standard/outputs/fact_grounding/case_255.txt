## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists (private café gathering covered by Article 11)**

The Court's finding that Article 11 applies to this gathering rests on the legal proposition that the right covers private meetings, but the factual grounding is the nature of the gathering itself. The Court does not quote specific facts about the gathering's character beyond its location. The admissibility section states the applicable legal principle and then simply finds "this provision is applicable in the present case" — without spelling out which specific facts about the café gathering drove that conclusion. The factual grounding is therefore **thin and not explicitly articulated** beyond the implicit fact that the gathering took place in a private café.

**ii) Participation**

The Court does not explicitly articulate the facts grounding the applicant's participation. It is implied throughout. The closest the Court comes is the framing in the interference section: "there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly on account of the dispersal by the police of the gathering of 14 June 2008." No specific facts about what the applicant was doing at the gathering are quoted or relied upon explicitly.

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court characterizes the gathering as peaceful — "the dispersal of a peaceful gathering in a private property" — but does **not explicitly identify which facts** support this characterization. No evidence of violence or threat of violence is mentioned, and the peaceful nature appears to be treated as uncontested. The factual grounding is **implied by the absence of any contrary assertion**, but is not explicitly articulated.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference in the following explicitly stated and accepted fact:

> "there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly on account of the dispersal by the police of the gathering of 14 June 2008."

This is described as "undisputed between the parties," and the Court explicitly agrees with it. The fact is: **the police dispersed the gathering on 14 June 2008.** This is the sole factual basis stated, and the Court adopts it as established.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

This is the most factually grounded element in the decision. The Court relies on the following explicitly stated and accepted facts:

**1. The Government offered no applicable legal basis:**
> "the Government failed to refer to any domestic law or provisions which could constitute the legal basis for the dispersal of a gathering held in places which are in private ownership."

**2. The Law on Freedom of Assembly explicitly excluded private gatherings:**
> "under Azerbaijani law the legal basis for the police intervention in an assembly and its potential dispersal is set out in Articles 7 and 14 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly. However, Article 4.1 of the same Law clearly provided that the right to hold gatherings in places which are in private ownership is not regulated by this Law."

**3. The Government conceded no notification requirement applied:**
> "The Government also did not contest that an assembly held in a private café is not subject to any requirement of notification."

**4. The Government's stated reason (neighbour complaints) was unsupported by evidence:**
> "the Government failed to submit any evidence in support of their argument."

**5. The Government's stated reason was contradicted by the police's own account:**
> "the Head of Nasimi District Police Station no. 22 stated that the police had intervened in order to identify the persons gathered and to establish the purpose of the gathering, without referring to any complaint from neighbours."

**6. The Ministry of Internal Affairs' account also contradicted the Government's stated reason:**
> "The spokesman for the Ministry of Internal Affairs also indicated in his statement of 17 June 2008 that the police had intervened in order to interrupt an unauthorised gathering and to identify its participants, without referring to any complaint from neighbours."
