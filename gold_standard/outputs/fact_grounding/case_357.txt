**Factual grounding for each discussed element:**

---

**(a) Scope**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following passage from the opening paragraph:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences."

This provides minimal factual grounding for:
- *Assembly exists*: "public assemblies" and "these assemblies"
- *Participation*: "participants of public assemblies"
- *Peaceful*: No factual grounding. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies. The peaceful nature is implied only by the Court's willingness to proceed with Article 11 analysis, not by any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The same passage provides the factual grounding:

> "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences."

The specific acts constituting interference are identified here: dispersal, arrest, and conviction for administrative offences.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed and no facts are stated in relation to it.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the reasoning text provides no independent factual grounding for it. The Court's conclusion rests entirely on reference to prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts); Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court does not articulate which specific facts of the present case ground the necessity finding. It relies on the similarity to leading cases and the absence of distinguishing facts or arguments, but does not quote or adopt any particular factual submission. The necessity conclusion is therefore jurisprudentially grounded rather than factually grounded in the text before us.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding — this element is not discussed.
