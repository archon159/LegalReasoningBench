Here is the factual grounding for each element, drawn only from what the Court itself established or accepted:

---

**(a) Scope — implied**

**Assembly exists / Participation / Peaceful:**
The factual grounding for all three implied scope elements is the same passage, in which the Court accepts as undisputed that a prohibition on participating in public gatherings was applied:

> "the prohibition imposed on the applicants on participating in public gatherings amounted to an 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11"

The Court does not separately identify a specific assembly, describe its peaceful character, or detail the applicants' attempts to participate. The scope elements are implied solely by the Court's acceptance of this characterisation, not by any separately stated facts about the assembly itself.

---

**(b) Interference — explicitly discussed**

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the same factual basis:

> "the prohibition imposed on the applicants on participating in public gatherings amounted to an 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicants' right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11"

The Court also notes the temporal scope of the measure as an established fact:

> "the measure imposed on them on 26 April 2016 and prolonged until 12 March 2019"

This is drawn from the applicants' argument, but the Court does not explicitly adopt these dates as established facts in its own voice in the merits section — it merely recites them as the applicants' submission. Therefore, strictly speaking, the only Court-endorsed factual grounding for interference is the first quote above.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law — explicitly discussed**

The Court grounds its finding that the measure was not prescribed by law on two factual elements:

First, the absence of a legal basis in the identified domestic provision:

> "Article 191 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (see paragraph 23 above) does not provide for such a surety measure"

Second, the Government's failure to point to any alternative legal basis:

> "the Government did not indicate to any other domestic legal provisions which would allow such a measure being imposed on a person released pending trial"

The Court treats the Government's silence as a relevant fact, and draws on it directly in its own reasoning. Together, these two facts ground the conclusion:

> "the Court concludes that the interference in question was not lawful under domestic law"

---

**(d) Legitimate aim — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify. The Court explicitly declined to examine this element.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify. The Court explicitly declined to examine this element.

---

**(f) Positive obligation — not discussed**

No factual grounding to identify.
