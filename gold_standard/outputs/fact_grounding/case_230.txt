I'll work through each canonical element where the Court made a finding, identifying only the facts the Court itself relied upon (not merely party assertions).

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly state a finding on this sub-element. Its conclusion is implied by the finding of interference. The factual grounding is the same as for participation (see below). No separate factual statement can be quoted for this sub-element specifically.

**ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this on the following facts, stated in its own voice:

> "The Court notes that the applicant was a member of a group of aliens without valid residence permits who decided to take collective action to draw attention to the difficulties they were having in obtaining a review of their immigration status in France. Their campaign culminated on 28 June 1996 with a decision to occupy St Bernard's Church, in which the group took up residence for approximately two months."

**iii) Peaceful**
The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "Neither the priest nor the parish council of the church objected to their presence and the religious services and various ceremonies proceeded as planned and without incident."

And later, in the necessity section, the Court reaffirms this characterisation as a factual premise:

> "even though it was peaceful and did not in itself entail any disturbance of public order or prevent churchgoers from attending services..."

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the same core facts established in section A:

> "The Court notes that the applicant was a member of a group of aliens without valid residence permits who decided to take collective action to draw attention to the difficulties they were having in obtaining a review of their immigration status in France. Their campaign culminated on 28 June 1996 with a decision to occupy St Bernard's Church, in which the group took up residence for approximately two months. Neither the priest nor the parish council of the church objected to their presence and the religious services and various ceremonies proceeded as planned and without incident."

Followed by the conclusion:

> "Consequently, the Court finds that the evacuation of the church amounted to an interference in the exercise of the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

The factual grounding is thus the peaceful nature of the assembly combined with the fact of evacuation. The evacuation itself is treated as an established fact but is not separately quoted as a Court-found fact — it is referenced only in the conclusion. No additional independent factual statement about the evacuation is made by the Court in its own voice beyond this.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court grounds its finding on one specific legal fact:

> "in particular, under the Law of 9 December 1905 it is the local authorities, not the parish priest, who are responsible for supervising religious ceremonies and they are empowered to act either at the priest's request or on their own initiative."

This is a statement of legal fact (the content of domestic law) rather than a circumstantial fact. No further factual grounding is provided. The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not factually grounded because the Court does not address them separately.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court grounds its finding on the following fact, stated in its own voice:

> "The Court notes that the evacuation was ordered to put an end to the continuing occupation of a place of worship by persons, including the applicant, who had broken French law."

This is the sole factual basis the Court itself articulates for the legitimate aim finding. The Court does not adopt the Government's additional stated reasons (health, traffic, public order outside the church) as established facts at this stage.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's proportionality assessment is grounded on several facts stated in its own voice:

On the conditions that made some restriction necessary:

> "even though it was peaceful and did not in itself entail any disturbance of public order or prevent churchgoers from attending services, after two months the continued occupation of the church by illegal immigrants, including the applicant, had developed into a situation – described in a report drawn up by a bailiff on the instructions of the Commissioner of Police – in which the hunger-strikers' health had deteriorated and sanitary conditions become wholly inadequate."

On the methods used:

> "the methods used by the police in an intervention that came without warning and was indiscriminate, went beyond what it was reasonable to expect the authorities to do when curtailing the freedom of assembly."

On the lawfulness of intervention without the priest's request:

> "the police could quite lawfully intervene under domestic law without a request from the parish priest"

On the reasonableness of the authorities' fear:

> "the authorities' fear that the situation might deteriorate rapidly and could not be allowed to continue much longer was not unreasonable."

On the duration of tolerance:

> "the symbolic and testimonial value of the applicant's and other immigrants' presence had been tolerated sufficiently long enough in the instant case for the interference not to appear, after such a lengthy period, unreasonable."

One factual element the Court explicitly does **not** accept as a sufficient justification:

> "The Court does not share the Government's view that the fact that the applicant was an illegal immigrant sufficed to justify a breach of her right to freedom of assembly, as that freedom had already been exercised for two months without intervention on the part of the authorities and peaceful protest against legislation which has been contravened does not constitute a legitimate aim for a restriction on liberty within the meaning of Article 11 § 2."

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

As noted previously, the Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding can be identified for this element.
