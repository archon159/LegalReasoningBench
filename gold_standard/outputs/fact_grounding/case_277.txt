## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

> "fifty to sixty persons, including the applicants, had gathered in a square in Istanbul to protest against the invasion of Iraq."

**ii) Participation**

> "fifty to sixty persons, including the applicants, had gathered in a square in Istanbul to protest against the invasion of Iraq."

> "the applicants' arrest and the use of force against two of the applicants constituted an interference with their rights under that provision."

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

> "the Government have not sought to argue that the applicants or any of the other demonstrators had presented a danger to public order or engaged in acts of violence."

> "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, as was the case in the present application..."

Note: The first of these is a statement about what the Government did not argue, but the Court explicitly adopts it as a factual characterization of the situation by immediately following it with "as was the case in the present application" — confirming it as an accepted fact, not merely an uncontested submission.

---

### (b) Interference

> "the applicants' arrest and the use of force against two of the applicants constituted an interference with their rights under that provision."

> "The police warned the demonstrators with loudspeakers that they were disturbing the flow of traffic and unsuccessfully asked them to disperse, before resorting to using force to arrest the applicants and a number of other persons. Their intervention resulted in the injury of, among others, two of the applicants."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court accepts the legal basis exists but states only: "the Government submitted that the interference had a legal basis, namely the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act, and was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention... The Court agrees with these submissions."

No independent factual grounding is provided by the Court beyond accepting the Government's identification of the legal instrument. The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not discussed, and no facts are marshalled in relation to them.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

As with prescribed by law, the Court simply agrees with the Government's submission without providing independent factual grounding: "The Court agrees with these submissions." No specific facts are cited by the Court to ground the finding of a legitimate aim independently.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This element has the richest factual grounding:

> "fifty to sixty persons, including the applicants, had gathered in a square in Istanbul to protest against the invasion of Iraq."

> "The police warned the demonstrators with loudspeakers that they were disturbing the flow of traffic and unsuccessfully asked them to disperse, before resorting to using force to arrest the applicants and a number of other persons. Their intervention resulted in the injury of, among others, two of the applicants."

> "the police officers arrested the applicants on the ground that they had breached the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act. However, the Bakırköy Assize Court found that the applicants had not breached this law, contrary to the allegations of the police and the submissions of the Government. The Assize Court found that the applicants had exercised their democratic rights and had not committed any offences."

> "the Government have not sought to argue that the applicants or any of the other demonstrators had presented a danger to public order or engaged in acts of violence."

> "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, as was the case in the present application, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention is not to be deprived of all substance."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
