## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not explicitly reason about this. The factual grounding is implicit in the Court's framing of the events as "the demonstration of 20 October 2012" and "the assembly of 6 May 2014." No specific facts are marshalled to establish this element.

**(ii) Participation**
The Court grounds this in the following facts:
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 20 October 2012"
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the assembly of 6 May 2014"

No further factual elaboration is provided beyond these characterisations.

**(iii) Peaceful**
For the first case, the Court grounds this by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov:
> "the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner"

For the second case:
> "the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

The Court does not identify specific facts from the record of the present case to establish peacefulness independently — it relies on the characterisation carried over from Gafgaz Mammadov for the first case, and uses the descriptor "peaceful" without grounding it in specific facts for the second case.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds interference in the following facts:
- "the dispersal of the protests by the police and his arrest and conviction for an administrative offence"
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 20 October 2012"
- "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the assembly of 6 May 2014"

For the second case specifically, the Court also notes:
> "the police authorities had not intervened in the assembly until the participants had started to disturb public order"

However, this is drawn from the Government's submissions and the Court does not explicitly adopt it as an established fact. The interference itself (dispersal, arrest, conviction) is treated as uncontested.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**(i) Foreseeable**
The Court grounds its concerns about foreseeability in the following:
> "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed"

This is stated by reference to Gafgaz Mammadov (§ 55) and applied to the first case. For the second case, the Court grounds its doubts about lawfulness in the following specific fact:
> "The Court observes that the organisers did not give the BCEA prior notice about the protest of 6 May 2014. However, it is ready to accept that that protest was an immediate response by people who had gathered near the Baku Court of Serious Crimes to the judgment of that court against certain members of the opposition group, Nida. The spontaneous nature of the protest was warranted by those circumstances and thus the obligation to submit prior notice was overridden."

And further:
> "under Article 5 § IV of the Law on Freedom of Assembly, no prior written notification was required for 'spontaneous assemblies'"

These two facts together — the spontaneous nature of the protest and the existence of a legal exemption for spontaneous assemblies — ground the Court's conclusion that there are doubts as to whether the dispersal was prescribed by law.

**(ii) Accessible**
No factual grounding identified. Not discussed by the Court.

**(iii) Safeguards against abuse**
The Court grounds this in:
> "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, § 55)

And:
> "doubts about the credibility of the formal ground, namely, Article 310.1 of the CAO, invoked by the authorities for arresting and convicting the participant in an unauthorised demonstration" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, §§ 56 and 62)

And:
> "the failure by the authorities to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, § 60)

These facts — the dubious invocation of Article 310.1 CAO and the disregard of prior notification — ground the Court's concern about insufficient safeguards against abuse.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding identified. The Court does not make any finding on legitimate aim and does not adopt any facts in support of one. The Government's general assertions are noted in the parties' submissions section but are not adopted by the Court.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of a violation of necessity in the following facts:

First, the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons for dispersal:
> "the lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration, which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, § 61)

> "the authorities in the present case have not adduced relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the protest"

Second, the failure to acknowledge the protected status of participation:
> "the lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention" (referencing Gafgaz Mammadov, §§ 63-64)

> "The authorities also failed to acknowledge that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention"

Third, the chilling effect:
> "The dispersal of the assemblies and the applicant's arrests and convictions could not but have the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies. The measures applied in the present cases and the fear of sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies undoubtedly have a chilling effect on the exercise of freedom of assembly. This deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
