**Factual grounding for each discussed or implied element:**

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Existence of an assembly:**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "the dispersal of these assemblies." These are the Court's own characterisations, not party submissions adopted by the Court. Verbatim: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."

**ii) Participation:**
Same passage as above: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The Court characterises the applicants themselves as organisers or participants.

**iii) Peacefulness:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court says nothing about the character of the assemblies — whether they were violent, disruptive, or peaceful. The peacefulness of the assemblies is implied only by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is explicit and verbatim: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the two concrete state acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

As noted in the prior analysis, this element is not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court's necessity finding is notably thin in its factual grounding. The Court states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints." This is a reference to the totality of submitted material, but no specific facts are quoted or adopted. The Court grounds its necessity finding primarily by reference to prior case-law — specifically Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, and Kasparov — rather than on independently stated facts from the present case. The only concrete facts underpinning the necessity assessment are therefore the same facts identified under interference: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." No additional facts are stated or adopted by the Court to support the necessity analysis.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.
