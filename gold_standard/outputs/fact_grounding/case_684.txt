## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "all the applicants participated in the meetings"

> "the applicants gathered to demonstrate in an area that had been prohibited by the relevant authorities, their intention was to participate in a debate on matters of public interest, namely the transfer of SSK hospitals to the Ministry of Health"

> "It appears from the evidence before the Court that the groups of demonstrators were informed by the police that their gatherings were illegal and that they were ordered to disband for the sake of public order and safety."

**(ii) Participation**

> "all the applicants participated in the meetings and two of the three applicants read out the press statements"

> "the applicants and other demonstrators did not comply with those orders and continued to read out the press statements"

**(iii) Peaceful**

> "The participants held a peaceful demonstration and did not cause any disruptions in the entrance of the hospitals; they also allowed patients to enter the hospitals. Moreover, there is no evidence to suggest that the demonstrators either presented a danger to public order or engaged in acts of violence."

---

### (b) Interference

> "the prosecution and conviction of the applicants could have had a chilling effect and discouraged them from taking part in other similar meetings"

> "the imposition of fines on the applicants constituted an interference with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly"

The Court does not separately enumerate additional facts here beyond those already established under scope — the interference finding flows directly from the fact of prosecution and conviction, which is stated as:

> "the three applicants were prosecuted and subsequently sentenced to pay administrative fines on account of the mere fact of their participation in a peaceful demonstration"

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

As noted in the prior analysis, the Court declines to reach a final conclusion on this element. It does, however, identify the following factual context relevant to the question:

> "administrative fines were imposed on the applicants under section 32 of the Misdemeanours Act (Law No. 5326)"

Beyond this, the Court does not make any factual findings of its own on the lawfulness question. It notes the parties' competing submissions (applicants: circulars not published in Official Gazette; Government: press bulletins were published in the Official Gazette based on the relevant circular and statutory provisions) but does not adopt either as established fact, precisely because it declines to resolve the issue. **No factual grounding can be identified for a concluded assessment on this element.**

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the Government's submission on legitimate aim without making independent factual findings. It states:

> "The Court accepts that the measure pursued the legitimate aims cited by the Government."

The aims cited were "maintaining public order, public safety and public health." The Court does not ground this acceptance in specific facts from the record — it is a bare acceptance of the Government's characterisation. **No independent factual grounding can be identified beyond this acceptance.**

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court draws on the following facts:

On the nature of the demonstration:

> "the applicants gathered to demonstrate in an area that had been prohibited by the relevant authorities, their intention was to participate in a debate on matters of public interest, namely the transfer of SSK hospitals to the Ministry of Health"

> "The participants held a peaceful demonstration and did not cause any disruptions in the entrance of the hospitals; they also allowed patients to enter the hospitals. Moreover, there is no evidence to suggest that the demonstrators either presented a danger to public order or engaged in acts of violence."

On the police orders and the applicants' response:

> "It appears from the evidence before the Court that the groups of demonstrators were informed by the police that their gatherings were illegal and that they were ordered to disband for the sake of public order and safety. The applicants and other demonstrators did not comply with those orders and continued to read out the press statements."

On the sanction imposed:

> "the three applicants were prosecuted and subsequently sentenced to pay administrative fines on account of the mere fact of their participation in a peaceful demonstration"

On the domestic courts' reasoning:

> "it was not established in the reasoning of the domestic courts' judgments whether the domestic courts had engaged with the proportionality of the interference and the balancing of rights of the applicants on account of freedom of assembly"

This last factual finding is particularly significant: the Court grounds its necessity conclusion not only in the nature of the demonstration but in the failure of the domestic courts to conduct any proportionality balancing at all.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted previously, the Court does not make a positive obligation finding. **No factual grounding is applicable.**
