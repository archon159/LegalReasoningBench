I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Participation**

The only factual grounding for both of these implied elements is the following sentence from the opening paragraph:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**"

This is the Court's own characterisation of the situation, not merely a party submission. It is the sole factual basis from which the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation are implied.

**iii) Peaceful**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not refer to any facts establishing the peaceful nature of the assemblies. The peacefulness is implied structurally (by the Court proceeding to assess interference and necessity without excluding the assemblies from Article 11's scope), but no facts are cited to support this.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding for the interference finding is explicitly stated in the opening paragraph:

> "their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These are the concrete governmental acts the Court identifies as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are cited by the Court in support of its necessity finding. The Court grounds its conclusion entirely by reference to prior case-law:

> "In the leading cases of **Frumkin v. Russia**... **Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia**... and **Kasparov and Others v. Russia**... the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion"

The Court thus relies on the similarity to prior cases rather than setting out any case-specific facts to ground its necessity assessment. No particular facts from the instant case are quoted or adopted as the basis for the conclusion.

---

**Summary observation**

The reasoning text is notably sparse in its factual grounding. The Court operates almost entirely by reference to prior case-law and a general characterisation of the applicants' situation, without setting out the specific facts of each applicant's case or explaining how those facts map onto the doctrinal elements. This is characteristic of a repetitive-case judgment where the Court treats the matter as already settled by leading cases.
