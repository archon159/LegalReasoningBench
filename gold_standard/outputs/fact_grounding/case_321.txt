**Factual grounding for each element discussed**

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding available in the text is the following description from the opening paragraph:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the Court's own characterization of the situation, not merely a party submission. It establishes that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants. However, it must be noted that this sentence describes what the applicants "complained of" — it is framed as a summary of the complaint rather than as an independent factual finding by the Court. The Court does not separately state as an established fact that the assemblies were peaceful. There is no factual grounding in the text for the peaceful character of the assemblies beyond the implied acceptance that flows from the Court proceeding to assess the case under Article 11.

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same passage:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

Again, this is framed as a description of the applicants' complaints rather than as an independent judicial finding of fact. The Court does not separately articulate a factual finding that these acts occurred and constituted interference — it uses the word "interferences" in its legal assessment without separately establishing the underlying facts in this excerpt.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

There is no independent factual grounding in the text for the necessity assessment. The Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This sentence references the existence of "material submitted" but does not identify or quote any specific facts from that material. The necessity conclusion is grounded entirely by reference to prior case-law (Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, Kasparov and Others v. Russia, and the general principles cases), not by any specific facts recited in this excerpt. The Court does not explain which facts of the present case it weighed, or how it balanced them.

**Overall observation**

The reasoning section provided is extremely compressed. It functions as a repetition/clone judgment, relying on the leading cases rather than conducting an independent factual analysis. As a result, the factual grounding for most elements is either absent or only minimally present in the form of the complaint summary. No facts are quoted from party submissions that the Court explicitly adopted as established.
