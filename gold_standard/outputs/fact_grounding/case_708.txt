## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts, stated as established:

> "the applicant's criminal conviction under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code was indisputably directed at activities falling within the scope of freedom of assembly, and that he was sanctioned for participating in the demonstration held on 26 October 2008."

The demonstration of 26 October 2008 is treated as an established fact throughout, including in the merits section where the Court states:

> "The applicant in the instant case was convicted under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code on the sole basis of his participation in the demonstration of 26 October 2008, covering his face during that demonstration and chanting slogans supporting the PKK and its leader."

**(ii) Participation**

The Court grounds its finding of participation on the facts as established by the national courts, explicitly stating:

> "the Court must examine the case on the basis of the facts as established by the national courts."

And the factual basis for participation as established by those courts is:

> "The applicant in the instant case was convicted under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code on the sole basis of his participation in the demonstration of 26 October 2008, covering his face during that demonstration and chanting slogans supporting the PKK and its leader."

The Court also notes the applicant's own later submission as a supporting fact:

> "in his submissions dated 7 August 2018 the applicant contended that he had attended the demonstration of 26 October 2008 and exercised his rights to freedom of expression and freedom of assembly."

**(iii) Peaceful (implied)**

The Court does not identify specific facts establishing peacefulness as a discrete finding. The implied treatment of the assembly as peaceful is grounded in the following factual characterisation:

> "The applicant in the instant case was convicted under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code on the sole basis of his participation in the demonstration of 26 October 2008, covering his face during that demonstration and chanting slogans supporting the PKK and its leader."

The phrase "on the sole basis of" implies that no acts of violence or other non-peaceful conduct were established as the basis for conviction. This is reinforced by the Court's adoption of the Işıkırık reasoning:

> "there remained no distinction between him, a peaceful demonstrator, and an individual who had committed offences within the structure of the PKK."

And:

> "the very essence of the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and, thereby, the foundations of a democratic society, had been undermined when Mr Işıkırık had been held criminally liable under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 of the Criminal Code for the mere fact of attending a public meeting and expressing his views therein."

No specific facts are quoted by the Court establishing peacefulness as a positive finding in its own right.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on the following established facts:

> "the applicant's criminal conviction under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code was indisputably directed at activities falling within the scope of freedom of assembly, and that he was sanctioned for participating in the demonstration held on 26 October 2008."

And:

> "the applicant's criminal conviction for membership of the PKK as well as the imposition of a prison sentence upon him, constituted an interference with the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly as guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention."

The factual basis for the severity of the interference is also noted:

> "between 18 August 2009 and 12 July 2012 the applicant served his prison sentence arising from the judgment of the Adana Assize Court in part. Thus, he was deprived of his liberty for two years and eleven months within the context of the criminal proceedings brought against him."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**(i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds its finding on the following facts about how the law was applied in the present case:

> "The applicant in the instant case was convicted under Articles 220 § 6 and 314 § 2 of the Criminal Code on the sole basis of his participation in the demonstration of 26 October 2008, covering his face during that demonstration and chanting slogans supporting the PKK and its leader."

The Court then grounds the foreseeability failure in the structural characteristics of the legal provision as applied, drawing on Işıkırık:

> "when applied in connection with Article 220 § 6, the criteria for a conviction under Article 314 § 2 were extensively applied to the detriment of the applicant."

> "the array of acts that potentially constituted a basis for the application of a severe criminal sanction in the form of imprisonment, under Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code, were so vast that the wording of the provision, including its extensive interpretation by the domestic courts, did not afford a sufficient measure of protection against arbitrary interferences by the public authorities."

> "such an extensive interpretation of a legal norm could not be justified when it had the effect of equating mere exercise of fundamental freedoms with membership of an illegal organisation in the absence of any concrete evidence of such membership."

The Court confirms these facts apply to the present case:

> "the Court finds that its considerations in the above-mentioned case of Işıkırık also apply in the present case and that there is nothing in the case file that would require it to reach a different conclusion from the one reached in that case."

**(ii) Accessibility**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not address this sub-element in its assessment.

**(iii) Safeguards**

The factual grounding is embedded within the foreseeability analysis and is not separately grounded in distinct facts. The same facts apply as under (i) above, particularly:

> "the wording of the provision, including its extensive interpretation by the domestic courts, did not afford a sufficient measure of protection against arbitrary interferences by the public authorities."

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not address this element in its assessment.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

No factual grounding can be identified as a standalone element. The Court does not conduct a necessity analysis. The observations embedded in the prescribed by law analysis that touch on proportionality-adjacent concerns are grounded in the same facts as (c)(i) above, particularly the sole basis of conviction being participation in a demonstration and the chilling effect of the provision:

> "when demonstrators faced the charge of membership of an illegal armed organisation, they risked an additional sentence of between five and ten years in prison."

> "Article 220 § 6 of the Criminal Code would inevitably have a particularly chilling effect on those who were found criminally liable on re-exercising their rights under Articles 10 and 11 of the Convention."

But these are not grounded in facts specific to this applicant beyond his conviction and sentence; they are structural observations about the provision itself.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not address this element.
