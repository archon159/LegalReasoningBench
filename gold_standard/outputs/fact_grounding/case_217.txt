I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope (implied)**

The only factual grounding in the reasoning text is the following description of the complaints:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the sole factual basis from which the Court implies the existence of assemblies, the applicants' participation, and (by proceeding to assess the case under Article 11 without exclusion) the peaceful nature of those assemblies. No further facts are stated in the reasoning text to ground these elements.

---

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is the same passage:

> "namely their **arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies** and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference. No further factual elaboration is provided in the reasoning text.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is the central finding, but the reasoning text provides **no independent factual grounding** specific to the necessity assessment. The Court does not describe, for example, the conduct of the authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, the nature of the offences charged, or the severity of the sanctions. Instead, the Court grounds its necessity finding entirely by reference to prior case law:

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

And:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court thus signals that it has reviewed the material but does not articulate any specific facts from that material in the reasoning text. The necessity conclusion rests on the similarity to prior cases, not on any explicitly stated facts about the present case.
