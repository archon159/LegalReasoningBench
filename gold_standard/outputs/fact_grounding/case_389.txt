## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following established facts:

> 'It has not been disputed between the parties that the facts of the present application do not relate, as such, to the duly notified demonstration organised by Asociación parad@s en movimiento Valladolid but to the gathering that took place afterwards, when some fifty to sixty participants continued marching spontaneously after the end of the demonstration.'

**ii) Participation**

The Court grounds this on the following established facts:

> 'The Court notes that the applicant's conduct was not established to have been of violent character either during the official demonstration or at the informal gathering afterwards, but she was injured during the police dispersal of the latter.'

> 'no proceedings have been instituted against the applicant. She was not arrested or detained, nor has she been held responsible of any impugned conduct in respect of the protest.'

> 'the applicant has never been arrested or prosecuted for any violent actions during the protests, and her name was not even mentioned in the reports of the facts from the day.'

**iii) Peaceful**

The Court grounds this on the following established facts:

> 'there was no indication whatsoever that the applicant, as one of the protesters, engaged in actions that were violent or not peaceful... or undermining the foundations of a democratic society.'

> 'the authorities dispersed the spontaneous gathering despite the fact that it had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up to that point.'

The Court also draws on the finding of the domestic court, which it explicitly adopts:

> 'Valladolid criminal judge no. 3, after having examined evidence, concluded that the protesters had been violently repressed without any prior warning, despite the fact that they had not blocked traffic, that it had not been proved that they had been trying to enter the restaurant... where some politicians had been having lunch, and that they had not provoked the confrontation with the police officers.'

And the Court notes the negative confirmation from police records:

> 'the applicant has never been arrested or prosecuted for any violent actions during the protests, and her name was not even mentioned in the reports of the facts from the day.'

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the finding of interference on the following established facts:

> 'The Court notes that the applicant's conduct was not established to have been of violent character either during the official demonstration or at the informal gathering afterwards, but she was injured during the police dispersal of the latter.'

> 'A situation of tension and violence was then created and some of the people who participated in the gathering were injured, including the applicant, as were some police officers.'

> 'It is not in dispute between the parties that the applicant sustained injuries on 2 February 2014. There also appears to be common ground between the parties that such injuries were inflicted during the police intervention to dissolve the protest, and that police officers were implicated in one way or another in this situation.'

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds this solely on the character of the legislative text:

> 'the restriction imposed on the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly was based on domestic legislation (section 5 of the Right of Assembly Act and section 23 of the Protection of Public Safety Act...), the wording of which was clear. Therefore, the requirement of foreseeability was satisfied.'

No further factual grounding is provided. The Court does not point to any specific facts about how the law was applied or communicated to the applicant.

**ii) Accessibility and iii) Safeguards against abuse**

As noted previously, these sub-elements are not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court does not establish any facts in support of or against a legitimate aim. It explicitly declines to resolve the question, proceeding only on the assumption that a legitimate aim existed:

> 'even assuming that the forceful terminating of the gathering pursued a legitimate aim.'

No factual grounding for a definitive finding can be identified, because no definitive finding was made.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following established facts:

On the peaceful character of the gathering and absence of justification for dispersal:

> 'the authorities dispersed the spontaneous gathering despite the fact that it had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up to that point.'

> 'It has not been argued or demonstrated that it would have been difficult for the police to contain or redirect the protesters, or control the situation otherwise, protect public safety and prevent any possible disorder or crime. Nor has it been shown, either at the domestic level or before the Court, that the demonstration posed a high level of disruption of public order.'

On the minor nature of the disruption:

> 'the nuisance caused by the applicant and her fellow protesters, on a Sunday morning and mostly concentrated on pedestrian streets, caused a certain disruption to ordinary life, but it did not in those specific circumstances exceed the level of minor disturbance that follows from normal exercise of the right of peaceful assembly in a public place.'

On the applicant's individual conduct:

> 'the applicant has never been arrested or prosecuted for any violent actions during the protests, and her name was not even mentioned in the reports of the facts from the day.'

> 'nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the applicant committed any reprehensible act during the demonstration.'

On the outcome of domestic proceedings, explicitly adopted by the Court:

> 'the proceedings in respect of certain protesters ended with acquittals being issued by criminal judge no. 3, either for withdrawal of the charges against them or for lack of evidence.'

> 'Valladolid criminal judge no. 3, after having examined evidence, concluded that the protesters had been violently repressed without any prior warning, despite the fact that they had not blocked traffic, that it had not been proved that they had been trying to enter the restaurant... where some politicians had been having lunch, and that they had not provoked the confrontation with the police officers.'

> 'the protesters' behaviour and the harmlessness of their slogans and placards did not justify the force deployed by the police.'

On the injuries sustained:

> 'It is not in dispute between the parties that the applicant sustained injuries on 2 February 2014. There also appears to be common ground between the parties that such injuries were inflicted during the police intervention to dissolve the protest, and that police officers were implicated in one way or another in this situation.'

---

### (f) Positive obligation

As noted previously, the Court does not discuss positive obligations. No factual grounding can be identified for this element, as it is entirely absent from the reasoning.
