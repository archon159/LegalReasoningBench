I will work through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is the phrase: "public assemblies." No further facts about the nature, size, location, or organisation of the assemblies are provided in this reasoning section.

**ii) Participation**
The factual grounding is: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." This identifies the applicants' roles but provides no further factual detail.

**iii) Peaceful**
There is no factual grounding for this element. The Court does not describe the character of the assemblies or state that they were peaceful. The peaceful nature is implied structurally (by the Court applying Article 11 protection at all), but no facts are cited to support this.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

This is the only factual basis provided. The Court does not elaborate on the circumstances of the arrests, the nature of the dispersals, or the content of the administrative convictions.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

There is no independent factual grounding for this element in the reasoning text. The Court does not cite any specific facts about the proportionality of the measures, the conduct of authorities, the behaviour of participants, or the weight of the legitimate aim pursued. Instead, the Court grounds its necessity finding entirely by reference to prior case-law:

"In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

And: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

The Court thus acknowledges that material was submitted but does not identify or quote any specific facts from it. The necessity conclusion rests on the similarity to prior cases, not on independently stated facts in this text.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning section is extremely sparse in its factual grounding across all elements. The Court operates almost entirely by reference to prior case-law and a general statement that no new facts or arguments were found to justify a different conclusion. The only concrete facts stated are: (1) the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies; and (2) they were arrested in connection with the dispersal of those assemblies and convicted of administrative offences. All doctrinal elements beyond interference and necessity are either implied without factual grounding or not discussed at all.
