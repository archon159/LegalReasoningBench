Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied:

---

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase: "public assemblies" and "the dispersal of these assemblies." Verbatim: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."

**(ii) Participation:**
The same sentence provides the factual grounding: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."

**(iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the character of the assemblies (e.g., absence of violence, conduct of participants). The implication that the assemblies were peaceful is not grounded in any explicitly stated fact in this reasoning section.

---

**(b) Interference:**
The factual grounding is explicit: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the concrete governmental acts identified as constituting the interference.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society:**
This is the most striking finding: **there is no factual grounding whatsoever** in this reasoning section for the necessity assessment. The Court does not cite any specific facts about the conduct of authorities, the proportionality of the arrests, the nature of the dispersals, or the circumstances of the convictions. Instead, it relies entirely on a reference to prior case law: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case." The necessity conclusion is reached by analogy to precedent, not by reasoning from the facts of the present case. The only other statement is: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a negative statement — it confirms the absence of distinguishing facts — rather than a positive identification of facts grounding the assessment.
