## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court's implicit acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in the following facts stated by the Court:

> "the competent authorities refused to approve the location of the public event planned by the applicant, and proposed alternative locations."

> "the applicant, considering that the authorities' proposals did not answer the purpose of the demonstration, decided to hold it as initially planned."

> "the demonstration had not been dispersed and had taken place at the location chosen by the applicant."

Note: The last quote is drawn from the Government's submissions. The Court does not explicitly adopt this as an established fact in its own voice, so it should be treated with caution. The Court's own factual statements are the first two quotes.

**ii) Participation**

The Court's implicit finding that the applicant participated is grounded in:

> "the applicant, considering that the authorities' proposals did not answer the purpose of the demonstration, decided to hold it as initially planned. Afterwards, he was arrested and convicted, inter alia, of an administrative offence under Article 20.2 § 2 of the Code of Administrative Offences."

**iii) Peaceful**

No facts are explicitly stated by the Court in its own voice to establish that the assembly was peaceful. The applicant asserted that "the authorities had not provided any evidence to show that the demonstration had not been peaceful," but the Court does not explicitly adopt this assertion as an established fact. There is therefore no identifiable factual grounding in the Court's own reasoning for this element, beyond the implicit inference drawn from the Court's willingness to proceed with the analysis.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in the following facts stated by the Court:

> "the competent authorities refused to approve the location of the public event planned by the applicant, and proposed alternative locations."

> "he was arrested and convicted, inter alia, of an administrative offence under Article 20.2 § 2 of the Code of Administrative Offences."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court acknowledges a legal basis without fully analyzing it. The factual grounding for this acknowledgment is:

> "The applicant's conviction under Article 20.2 § 2 of the Code of Administrative Offences on account of holding the demonstration opposite the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Tatarstan had its basis in the statutory ban on holding public events in the vicinity of court buildings."

No further facts are stated by the Court in relation to the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not discuss legitimate aim in its own voice, and does not adopt any party's submissions on this point as established facts.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's finding that the interference was not necessary is grounded in the following facts stated by the Court:

> "The applicant's conviction under Article 20.2 § 2 of the Code of Administrative Offences on account of holding the demonstration opposite the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Tatarstan had its basis in the statutory ban on holding public events in the vicinity of court buildings."

> "the applicant's conviction, by relying solely on the ban, without any consideration of the specific circumstances of the case, cannot be regarded as necessary within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

The second quote is the Court's legal conclusion, but it simultaneously identifies the key operative fact: that the conviction relied *solely* on the ban, with *no consideration of specific circumstances*. The Court also grounds this in its prior holding in Lashmankin, characterizing the ban as "so broadly drawn that it cannot be accepted as compatible with Article 11 § 2," though this is a legal finding from precedent rather than a fact found in the present case.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding can be identified, as this element is not discussed.
