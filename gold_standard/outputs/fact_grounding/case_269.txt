I will go through each element that was found to be either explicitly discussed or implied, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Participation**

The only factual grounding in the text is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

and:

> "organisers or participants of public assemblies"

These are the Court's own characterisations, not merely party submissions. They establish that assemblies took place and that the applicants were organisers or participants.

**iii) Peaceful**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court makes no factual statement about the character of the assemblies (peaceful or otherwise). The peaceful nature is implied structurally by the Court's willingness to proceed under Article 11, but there is no factual statement in the text to quote.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is the same passage as above:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

This is the Court's own description of what happened to the applicants, and it constitutes the factual basis for the finding that an interference occurred.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

As noted in the prior analysis, this element is not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the critical finding, but the factual grounding here is notably thin. The Court does not set out specific facts of its own in this excerpt to support the necessity finding. Instead, it grounds its conclusion in two ways:

First, by reference to prior cases raising "issues similar to those in the present case":

> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts); Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

Second, by a general statement that no new material warranted a different conclusion:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints"

These are the only factual anchors offered. The Court does not independently set out the specific facts of the present case (e.g., the conduct of authorities, the nature of the dispersal, the severity of the sanctions) as the basis for its necessity assessment. The factual grounding is effectively delegated to the cited precedents rather than articulated in this text.

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding can be identified.
