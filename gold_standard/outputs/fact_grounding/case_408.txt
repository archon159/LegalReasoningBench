**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court does not separately establish the facts of the assembly's existence through its own fact-finding in this section. It relies on a prior judgment: *'The Court has previously held that the assembly in Baku on 2 April 2011 had been intended to be peaceful and therefore fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Ahad Mammadli [Committee] nos. 69456/11 and 48271/13, § 34, 16 June 2016).'* The factual basis is thus incorporated by reference rather than stated anew.

**ii) Participation**
The Court does not separately establish participation through explicit fact-finding. It is implied by the following statement, which treats participation as given: *'it is apparent from the domestic decisions that the applicants were arrested, detained and convicted for participation in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration and chanting non-violent slogans.'*

**iii) Peaceful character**

For the first group, the Court grounds the peaceful character on two factual findings:

First, the absence of violent intent or initial aggression by the applicants: *'it does not transpire from the case files that the applicants had violent intentions and were responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's peaceful character.'*

Second, the reason for dispersal: *'It follows from the material in the case files that the authorities dispersed the demonstration because it was being held in a place not agreed with them, and not because of the violent acts of some demonstrators.'*

Third, the nature of the conduct during the demonstration: *'the applicants were arrested, detained and convicted for participation in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration and chanting non-violent slogans. Nothing in the material before the Court suggests that the applicants committed any reprehensible acts during the demonstration.'*

For the second group (Alishli and Guliyev), the Court acknowledges some violent conduct but grounds its assessment on the following facts:

*'the criminal files did not disclose any pre-existing intention by the applicants to participate in violent acts, and no such intention was established by the domestic courts.'*

*'the applicants were not responsible for the deterioration of the assembly's peaceful character, which was rather caused by the forceful intervention of the police to disperse and arrest demonstrators, the majority of whom had been peaceful.'*

*'The applicants' violent conduct was sporadic in nature and appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police to arrest protestors.'*

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the finding of interference on the following facts:

*'the applicants' arrest and ensuing prosecution and criminal conviction for participation in the said demonstration constituted an interference with the exercise of freedom of assembly.'*

No further granular factual elaboration is provided at this point; the interference is treated as self-evidently following from the arrest, prosecution and conviction.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly declines to examine this element. No factual grounding is identified or needed, as the Court proceeds on an assumption. No facts are quoted.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not establish the legitimate aim as a matter of fact; it proceeds on an assumption only. No factual grounding is identified.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This element has the richest factual grounding. The facts relied upon differ somewhat between the two groups.

**For the first group:**

On the peaceful nature of the demonstration and the unjustified dispersal: *'the Court has already found that the dispersal of the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was not justified under Article 11 of the Convention having regard, in particular, to the fact that the authorities had been notified of the demonstration and it had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner until the moment of its dispersal by the police.'*

On the absence of reprehensible individual conduct: *'Nothing in the material before the Court suggests that the applicants committed any reprehensible acts during the demonstration.'*

On the lack of factual basis for the accusations: *'the accusation that the applicants behaved aggressively during the demonstration and disobeyed the orders of the police was not supported by any specific individual facts.'*

On the severity of the sanctions: *'the situation in the present cases is aggravated by the fact that the applicants were subjected to criminal prosecution, pre-trial detention and prison terms.'*

On the chilling effect: *'the measures in question were disproportionate and could not but have the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Undoubtedly, those measures had a serious potential also to deter other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'*

**For the second group:**

On the specific conduct of Mr Alishli: *'Mr Arif Alishli was found guilty of damaging property owned by a private individual. His conviction was based on the testimonies of police officers involved in the dispersal of the demonstration and video recordings.'*

On the specific conduct of Mr Guliyev and the evidentiary basis: *'Mr Ulvu Guliyev, he was convicted for punching a police officer who had been involved in his arrest but who had not sustained any injuries as a result. His guilty verdict was based exclusively on the submissions of police officers and was not corroborated by any other evidence.'*

On the absence of pre-existing violent intent: *'the criminal files did not disclose any pre-existing intention by the applicants to participate in violent acts, and no such intention was established by the domestic courts.'*

On the cause of the deterioration of the assembly: *'the applicants were not responsible for the deterioration of the assembly's peaceful character, which was rather caused by the forceful intervention of the police to disperse and arrest demonstrators, the majority of whom had been peaceful.'*

On the nature of the violent conduct: *'The applicants' violent conduct was sporadic in nature and appeared to be a spontaneous reaction to the force used by the police to arrest protestors, the majority of whom had been peaceful. It therefore did not attain the degree of violence which the Court has found in other cases that would call for lengthy prison terms.'*

On the severity of the sanctions: *'both applicants were sentenced to three years' imprisonment.'*

On the chilling effect: *'Their convictions, and especially the severity of their sentences, could not but have had the effect of discouraging them and other opposition supporters, as well as the public at large, from attending demonstrations and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'*

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
