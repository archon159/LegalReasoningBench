## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, but the following facts ground the implied conclusion that an assembly existed or was planned:

> "the municipality's decision in relation to the static demonstration"

> "holding a public event as planned"

> "the demonstration near the local FSB office"

> "The goal of the demonstration was related to the second applicant's arrest and his ongoing detention on remand in relation to an unspecified offence."

**ii) Participation**

The following facts ground the implied conclusion that the first applicant participated or attempted to participate:

> "the applicant was required to abstain, under the threat of prosecution, from holding a public event as planned and was then prosecuted in that connection"

> "the first applicant's CAO case"

**iii) Peaceful character**

No facts are explicitly stated in the reasoning to ground a finding on the peaceful character of the assembly. The Court makes no factual finding on this point. The only relevant contextual fact is:

> "The goal of the demonstration was related to the second applicant's arrest and his ongoing detention on remand in relation to an unspecified offence."

This describes the purpose of the demonstration but does not address its conduct or character. No factual grounding for the peaceful character element can be identified beyond this.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in the following facts:

> "The prosecution under the CAO amounted to an 'interference' under Article 11 § 2."

> "In so far as the applicant was required to abstain, under the threat of prosecution, from holding a public event as planned and was then prosecuted in that connection, the municipality's decision of 23 November 2009 amounted to an 'interference' too."

> "The applicant was convicted of breaching sections 8 and 12 § 2 of the Public Events Act."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court explicitly declines to make factual findings on this element. The only relevant statement is:

> "The Court does not need to ascertain whether, as the courts adjudged, section 8 authorised restrictions relating to a venue of a public event in view of any 'specificities of the objects [объекты]' located in the vicinity of that venue, or banned such events near FSB buildings."

No factual grounding is established by the Court for this element, as it is deliberately left unresolved.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court's negative finding on legitimate aim is grounded in the following facts:

On the aim of **preventing crime**:

> "When 'inviting' the first applicant, under a threat of prosecution, to abstain from the demonstration near the local FSB office, the municipality inferred, without any further assessment or substantiation, that that event would interfere with the administration of justice in the second applicant's criminal case."

> "The municipality assessed no circumstances, which would justify the existence of any real and immediate risk of the first applicant interfering with the activities of a court with the aim to impede the administration of justice."

On the aims of **public safety** and **prevention of disorder**:

> "Its decision was not specifically circumscribed to address any precise risk to public safety or a precise risk of disorder with the minimum impairment of the right of peaceful assembly."

On the aim of **protecting the rights of others**:

> "Nor did that decision contain any reasoning as to protecting the judicial process in a specific case from outside influence, and thereby protecting the rights of others, namely the parties to judicial proceedings."

On the aim of **maintaining the authority and impartiality of the judiciary** (explicitly excluded as a listed aim):

> "Article 11 § 2 does not contain such aim as 'maintaining the authority and impartiality of the judiciary'."

On the conduct of the **domestic courts** in the CAO proceedings:

> "Similarly, the courts in the first applicant's CAO case provided no reasoning as to why holding the demonstration near the FSB building interfered with the administration of justice or what legitimate aim was pursued under Article 11 § 2 by prosecuting him for holding it there."

> "The courts had no regard to any statements made during that demonstration and did not rely on any factual circumstances concerning any court proceedings then pending or, at least, completed in respect of the second applicant."

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's negative finding on necessity is grounded in the same facts as those identified under legitimate aim above (since the Court treats the absence of relevant reasoning and factual assessment as dispositive for both elements), plus the following overarching finding:

> "Faced with the domestic authorities' failure to provide relevant and sufficient reasons to justify the 'interferences', the Court finds that they cannot be said to have applied standards which were in conformity with the principles embodied in Article 11 or to have based themselves on an acceptable assessment of the relevant facts."

The specific factual deficiencies identified are:

> "the municipality inferred, without any further assessment or substantiation, that that event would interfere with the administration of justice in the second applicant's criminal case."

> "The municipality assessed no circumstances, which would justify the existence of any real and immediate risk of the first applicant interfering with the activities of a court with the aim to impede the administration of justice."

> "The municipality did not rely, at least in substance, on another legitimate aim."

> "the courts in the first applicant's CAO case provided no reasoning as to why holding the demonstration near the FSB building interfered with the administration of justice or what legitimate aim was pursued under Article 11 § 2 by prosecuting him for holding it there."

> "The courts had no regard to any statements made during that demonstration and did not rely on any factual circumstances concerning any court proceedings then pending or, at least, completed in respect of the second applicant."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding is identifiable, as the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all in this reasoning.
