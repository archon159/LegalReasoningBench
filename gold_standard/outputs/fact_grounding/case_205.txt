## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
No explicit factual grounding stated by the Court itself. The existence of an assembly is treated as uncontested and assumed throughout. The Court refers to "the applicants' assembly" and "their spontaneous demonstration" descriptively but does not ground this in any explicit factual finding of its own.

**ii) Participation**
No explicit factual grounding stated by the Court itself. Same observation as above — participation is assumed rather than established through a discrete factual finding.

**iii) Peaceful**
The Court grounds this on the following facts it explicitly accepts or states:

- "there is no evidence to suggest that the applicants represented a danger to public order beyond the level of the minor disturbance which is inevitably caused by an assembly in a public place."
- "a decision to disband the ensuing, peaceful assembly solely because of the absence of the requisite prior notice, **without any illegal conduct by the participants**"
- The domestic courts "did not take into account other aspects of the case, in particular, **the peaceful nature of the event**" — the Court notes this as a failing of the domestic courts, implicitly accepting that the event was indeed peaceful.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds its finding of interference on the following:

- "the dispersal of the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of the applicants' rights under that provision. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise."

The factual basis is the dispersal itself. The Court does not independently elaborate on the facts of the dispersal beyond accepting the Government's non-denial. No additional factual detail is quoted by the Court as its own finding.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**
The Court grounds this on:

- "the restriction imposed on the applicants' freedom of peaceful assembly was based on **section 14 of the Assembly Act**, the wording of which is clear."

This is the sole factual/legal grounding offered. The Court accepts the parties' agreement on this point: "The Court sees no reason to depart from the parties' view."

**ii) Accessibility**
No factual grounding identifiable — not discussed.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse**
No factual grounding identifiable — not discussed.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court's own conclusion is: "the Court is satisfied that the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others."

However, the Court does not independently state the facts underpinning this conclusion. It follows the Government's submissions on this point without explicitly adopting specific factual assertions from them as established. The conclusion appears to rest on the general nature of a prior-notification requirement rather than on any specific facts the Court itself identifies. No verbatim factual finding by the Court can be quoted here beyond the conclusion itself.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following explicitly stated facts:

- On the legal basis for dispersal: "The Court finds, in the domestic court decisions dealing with the lawfulness of the events, that **the legal basis for the dispersal of the applicants' assembly lay exclusively in the lack of prior notice**. The courts based their decision to declare the police measures lawful solely on that argument and did not take into account other aspects of the case, in particular, the peaceful nature of the event."

- On the special circumstances creating a dilemma for the applicants: "the failure to inform the public sufficiently in advance of the Prime Minister's intention to attend the reception left the applicants with the option of either **foregoing their right to peaceful assembly altogether, or of exercising it in defiance of the administrative requirements**."

- On the absence of danger: "there is no evidence to suggest that the applicants represented a danger to public order beyond the level of the minor disturbance which is inevitably caused by an assembly in a public place."

- On the absence of illegal conduct: "a decision to disband the ensuing, peaceful assembly solely because of the absence of the requisite prior notice, **without any illegal conduct by the participants**, amounts to a disproportionate restriction on freedom of peaceful assembly."

- On the nature of the trigger for the assembly: "in special circumstances when an **immediate response, in the form of a demonstration, to a political event** might be justified" — the Court treats the spontaneous, reactive character of the assembly as a relevant fact.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding identifiable from the Court's own reasoning — the Court does not adopt this element as part of its analysis.
