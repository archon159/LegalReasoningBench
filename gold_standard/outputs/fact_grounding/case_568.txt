**(a) Scope**

**i) Existence of an assembly**
The only factual grounding is the following phrase from the opening paragraph:
> "the dispersal of these assemblies"

and:
> "organisers and participants of public assemblies"

These are the only facts stated. The Court does not describe the assemblies in any further detail (location, date, size, purpose, etc.).

**ii) Participation**
The factual grounding is:
> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies"

No further facts are provided about the nature of the applicants' participation.

**iii) Peacefulness**
There is no factual grounding whatsoever for this element. The Court does not mention any facts relating to the conduct of the assemblies or the behaviour of participants. This element is implied solely by the Court's proceeding with the analysis, not by any stated facts.

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is:
> "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence"

These are the only concrete facts stated. The Court does not elaborate further on the circumstances of the dispersal, the arrest, or the conviction. No additional facts are provided or adopted from party submissions.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is notably thin. The Court states:
> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is a reference to the totality of the material, but no specific facts are quoted or identified. The Court grounds its necessity finding not in stated facts but in its prior case law:
> "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts); Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014; and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case."

The necessity conclusion is thus grounded in the similarity to prior cases, not in any independently stated facts about the present case. No specific facts from the present case are identified as the basis for the necessity finding.

**Summary observation**

The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. Across all elements, the only concrete facts stated are: (1) the applicants were organisers and participants of public assemblies; (2) those assemblies were dispersed; (3) the applicants were arrested and convicted of an administrative offence. Beyond these three facts, the Court's reasoning rests entirely on reference to prior case law and a general statement that it has examined "all the material submitted to it" — without specifying what that material contains or which facts it found established.
