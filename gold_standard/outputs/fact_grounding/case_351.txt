I will go through each canonical element and identify the factual grounding the Court itself relied upon.

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts it treats as established regardless of which version is accepted:

> "irrespective of whether Mr Kasparov, Mr Tarasov and Mr Toropov were heading to an authorised public gathering, as they alleged, or were already in the process of conducting an unauthorised rally, as the Government claimed"

And from the domestic court judgments, which the Court quotes as established findings:

> "according to the domestic judgments, the applicants were 'walking in a big group of people shouting anti-government slogans', that this constituted a public demonstration that had not been approved by the Moscow authorities"

**ii) Participation**

The Court's factual grounding for participation of the three admitted applicants is implicit in the same passage — they were present and active in the group. For the other applicants, the Court grounds the *absence* of established participation on their own denials:

> "these applicants denied that they had had any intention of taking part in a public assembly on 14 April 2007, either on Tverskaya Street, at the Griboyedov monument or elsewhere. They all claimed before the domestic courts and in their applications to the Court to have been arrested by mistake, simply because they had accidentally and by mischance found themselves in the midst of the attempts to break up the demonstration on Tverskaya Street. They have therefore not made out a prima facie case of interference with their freedom of expression or freedom of assembly."

**iii) Peaceful assembly**

The Court grounds this on two facts it treats as established:

> "the undeniably peaceful character of the march"

And:

> "the modest size of the group (estimated by the police as fifty to sixty persons)"

The Court also grounds it implicitly in the domestic court findings, which referred only to chanting slogans and walking, with no finding of violence.

---

**(b) Interference**

The Court grounds the finding of interference on two established facts:

> "their arrest constituted an interference with their right of peaceful assembly, as did the ensuing administrative charges brought against them."

And it notes that this was not even contested:

> "the Government did not dispute the existence of the interference with the right to peaceful assembly in the present case."

---

**(c) Prescribed by law**

As noted previously, the Court does not resolve this element separately. The only factual grounding it offers for declining to do so is:

> "The Court has already found that the domestic courts made no attempt to establish the precise circumstances of the applicants' arrest, such as the time and the place, in order to verify whether it had been necessary to stop them."

And:

> "In the absence of sufficient factual material to resolve this controversy the Court is unable to accept either party's version of events as a basis for deciding whether the authorities acted lawfully."

These are the facts (or rather, the absence of established facts) that lead the Court to fold the lawfulness question into the necessity analysis. No sub-element factual grounding can be identified because the Court does not proceed to resolve them.

---

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court accepts the legitimate aim without detailed factual analysis. It simply accepts the Government's characterisation:

> "the Court will accept that the applicants' arrest and their conviction of an administrative offence had pursued the legitimate aim of maintaining public order, as the Government claimed."

No independent factual grounding is provided by the Court beyond this acceptance. The Court does not identify specific facts establishing that public order was actually at risk.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court relies on the following established facts:

**On the sole reason for arrest being lack of authorisation:**

> "according to the domestic judgments, the applicants were 'walking in a big group of people shouting anti-government slogans', that this constituted a public demonstration that had not been approved by the Moscow authorities and that the applicants were therefore in breach of Article 20.2 of the Code of Administrative Offences. It follows that in the domestic courts' view it was the lack of permission to stage a public demonstration that served as grounds for charging them with administrative offences."

**On the implausibility of the Red Square security threat:**

> "the police reports did not mention any such risk and, in any event, it did not form part of the reasoning given by the Justice of the Peace or the Tverskoy District Court."

> "the place of arrest set out in the judgments, 19 Tverskaya Street, is about 1.4 km away from the nearest approach to Red Square."

> "the modest size of the group (estimated by the police as fifty to sixty persons) and the undeniably peaceful character of the march"

**On the implausibility of the Government's claim of being unprepared:**

> "a policeman testified that the authorities had anticipated a rally at the time and place where the applicants were arrested, and measures had been taken to cordon off the area in advance of the march"

> "Clear instructions had been given to the police officers who took part in the riot control operation specifically planned for the event."

> "the documents submitted by the Government reveal that reinforcements from the riot police had been brought in for the day from twenty-nine regions of Russia precisely because of the expected demonstration"

**On the other justifications raised by the Government:**

> "Other considerations put forward by the Government in their observations, such as the nuisance caused by the protesters walking on the pavement and on the road and obstructing pedestrians and traffic, were not mentioned in the police reports or domestic judgments either. It therefore appears that they had no impact on the decisions taken in the applicants' case."

**Conclusion drawn from these facts:**

> "It follows that the applicants were arrested and charged with administrative offences for the sole reason that the authorities perceived their demonstration to be unauthorised."

---

**(f) Positive obligation**

No factual grounding can be identified because the Court does not discuss positive obligations at all in this case.
