## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> "prior notice had been given of the demonstration in question and that it was not prohibited"

> "In joining it, the applicant availed himself of his freedom of peaceful assembly."

(The procession of 12 February 1983 is also referenced throughout as an established fact, though the Court does not quote a single passage formally establishing it — it is treated as uncontested background.)

---

**ii) Participation**

> "In joining it, the applicant availed himself of his freedom of peaceful assembly."

---

**iii) Peaceful assembly (from the applicant's perspective)**

> "neither the report made by the Chief Superintendent of the Basse-Terre police nor any other evidence shows that Mr Ezelin himself made threats or daubed graffiti."

The Court also grounds the peaceful character of the applicant's participation negatively, by reference to what the domestic courts actually found against him — not that he committed acts, but that he failed to dissociate:

> "The Court of Appeal found the charge of not having 'dissociate[d] himself from the demonstrators' offensive and insulting acts or [left] the procession' proven."

> "The Court of Cassation noted that at no time did he 'express his disapproval of these excesses or leave the procession in order to dissociate himself from these criminal acts'."

These facts confirm that the charge was based on omission, not on any reprehensible act by the applicant himself, which is the factual basis for the Court's later conclusion in B.3:

> "so long as the person concerned does not himself commit any reprehensible act on such an occasion."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding on two sets of facts:

First, the timing of the sanction (post-event):

> "The term 'restrictions' in paragraph 2 of Article 11 (art. 11-2) ... cannot be interpreted as not including measures - such as punitive measures - taken not before or during but after a meeting."

Second, the nature of the charge — that the applicant was sanctioned not for his own acts but for failing to dissociate:

> "The Court of Appeal found the charge of not having 'dissociate[d] himself from the demonstrators' offensive and insulting acts or [left] the procession' proven."

> "The Court of Cassation noted that at no time did he 'express his disapproval of these excesses or leave the procession in order to dissociate himself from these criminal acts'."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**i) Foreseeability**

The Court grounds its finding of sufficient foreseeability on:

> "Article 106 of the relevant Decree of 9 June 1972 provides unequivocally that any avocat, even in his non-professional activities, has special obligations"

> "the Court of Cassation has held that these include the respect due to the judicial authorities"

These two facts together — the text of the Decree and the interpretive gloss provided by the Court of Cassation — form the factual basis for the conclusion that the law was sufficiently precise to be foreseeable.

**ii) Accessibility**

No factual grounding identifiable. The Court does not discuss this sub-element.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse**

No factual grounding identifiable. The Court does not discuss this sub-element.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds the legitimate aim finding on:

> "It is apparent from the evidence that Mr Ezelin incurred the punishment because he had not dissociated himself from the unruly incidents which occurred during the demonstration."

> "the authorities took the view that such an attitude was a reflection of the fact that the applicant, as an avocat, endorsed and actively supported such excesses."

Note: the second sentence reflects the authorities' view, which the Court accepts as the factual basis for the aim pursued, without necessarily endorsing the characterisation of the applicant's conduct as actual endorsement.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its proportionality assessment on several facts:

On the relative lightness of the penalty (acknowledged but found insufficient to save the measure):

> "Admittedly, the penalty imposed on Mr Ezelin was at the lower end of the scale of disciplinary penalties given in Article 107 of the Decree of 9 June 1972; it had mainly moral force, since it did not entail any ban, even a temporary one, on practising the profession or on sitting as a member of the Bar Council."

On the nature of the assembly:

> "the freedom to take part in a peaceful assembly - in this instance a demonstration that had not been prohibited - is of such importance that it cannot be restricted in any way, even for an avocat, so long as the person concerned does not himself commit any reprehensible act on such an occasion."

On the basis of the sanction (omission rather than act):

The Court implicitly draws on the already-established facts that the applicant committed no reprehensible act himself (see above under scope/peaceful), and that the sanction rested solely on his failure to dissociate. No new facts are introduced at this stage; the Court applies the proportionality principle to the facts already established.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

No factual grounding identifiable. This element is not discussed.
