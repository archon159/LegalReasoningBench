Here is the analysis of factual grounding for each element the Court addressed (explicitly or by implication):

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:**
The only factual grounding is the applicant's complaint itself, as described by the Court: *'The applicant association complained of a violation of its right to peaceful assembly. It claimed that the ban imposed on it on holding a demonstration had not been in accordance with the law.'*
The Court does not independently establish the existence of the assembly through its own factual findings. It does not explicitly adopt any party's account of the facts as established.

**ii) Participation:**
The same passage implies the applicant was the entity seeking to hold the demonstration: *'The applicant association complained of a violation of its right to peaceful assembly. It claimed that the ban imposed on it on holding a demonstration...'*
Again, this is drawn from the framing of the complaint, not from an independent factual finding by the Court.

**iii) Peaceful:**
The Court uses the phrase *'its right to peaceful assembly'* when describing the complaint, but makes no independent factual finding that the assembly was or would have been peaceful. No factual grounding beyond the label used in the complaint.

---

**(b) Interference:**

The Court's acceptance of interference is grounded in the Government's concession, which the Court implicitly adopts by proceeding on that basis: *'In their observations lodged on 15 September 2008 the Government agreed that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly.'*

Note, however, that the Court does not explicitly state that it independently finds interference established — it simply proceeds on the basis of the concession. The underlying fact giving rise to the interference — the ban — is described only through the applicant's complaint: *'the ban imposed on it on holding a demonstration.'* The Court does not independently verify or describe the ban in its own factual terms.

---

**(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (e) Necessity in a democratic society:**

No factual grounding can be identified. The Court makes no factual findings relevant to these elements. The Government's initial submissions on legitimate aim (referencing the Orthodox Christian majority and popular petitions) are recorded descriptively — *'The Government pointed out that 98% of the Moldovan population was Christian Orthodox... The Government attached requests sent by the Moldovan population to the Chişinău Mayor's Office'* — but the Court does not adopt or accept these as established facts. They are simply noted as party submissions and are never engaged with in the merits analysis.

---

**(f) Positive obligation:**

No factual grounding. The element is not discussed at all.

---

**Overall observation:** The factual basis of this judgment is strikingly thin. The Court's conclusion of a violation rests almost entirely on the Government's own concession (*'The Government have admitted that there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention. In the circumstances of the case, the Court finds no reason to hold otherwise'*), without independently grounding its assessment in established facts for any of the canonical doctrinal elements.
