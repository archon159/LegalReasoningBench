## Factual Grounding for Each Discussed/Implied Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Existence of an assembly**

The only factual grounding is the Court's own characterisation of the complaints:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**"

No further factual detail about the assemblies (location, date, size, purpose) is provided in this reasoning section.

**(ii) Participation**

Again, the only grounding is:

> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies"

**(iii) Peaceful character**

**No factual grounding can be identified.** The Court does not mention any facts bearing on whether the assemblies were peaceful. The peaceful character is implied structurally by the Court's proceeding to the interference and necessity analysis, but no facts are cited to support this.

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the existence of interference in the following facts:

> "their **arrest** in relation to the **dispersal** of these assemblies and their **conviction for administrative offences**"

These three concrete governmental acts — arrest, dispersal, and conviction — are the factual basis for the interference finding.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

**No factual grounding can be identified**, as this element is neither discussed nor implied.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

**No factual grounding can be identified**, as this element is neither discussed nor implied.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court's necessity assessment is notably **thin on independent factual grounding**. The reasoning rests almost entirely on legal precedent rather than case-specific facts. The only factual elements that can be identified as underpinning the necessity conclusion are the same facts cited under interference:

> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

Beyond this, the Court states:

> "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion"

This sentence acknowledges that material was submitted, but the Court does not identify or quote any specific facts from that material. It is a negative statement — no new facts displaced the conclusion drawn from precedent — rather than a positive factual finding.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

**No factual grounding can be identified**, as this element is not discussed.
