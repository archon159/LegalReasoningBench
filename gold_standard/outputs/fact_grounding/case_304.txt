## Factual Grounding for Each Doctrinal Element

---

### (a) Scope

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court does not explicitly discuss this element, and there is no specific factual finding grounding it. The planned event is described throughout as a "mourning march" but the Court makes no factual finding that this constitutes an assembly for Article 11 purposes. No factual grounding can be identified beyond the bare description of the event.

**(ii) Did the applicant try to participate?**
The Court's finding of interference is grounded in the following factual statement:
> "the applicant organisation was prevented by the police from holding the planned march, apparently on the basis of the Mayor's ban."

Note: the Court does not explicitly adopt the applicant's submission that "police officers, who had been informed of the Mayor's ban, prohibited the applicant organisation and the assembled persons from doing so" — it uses the word "apparently," indicating some caution. However, the finding of interference is clearly grounded in this fact.

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court's implicit acceptance that the assembly was peaceful is grounded in the following factual finding made during the necessity analysis:
> "nothing suggests that the organisers or the participants of the planned march had violent intentions or that the march might for any other reason degenerate into mass disorder."

And further:
> "The purpose of the planned march had nothing to do with politics, elections or the confrontation between the opposition and the government and was instead to raise awareness on a matter of public concern, namely the death of a person while in a police station."

---

### (b) Interference

The Court's finding of interference is grounded in two factual findings:

First, regarding the Mayor's decision:
> "The decision in question banned the applicant organisation from holding the event and the fact that a copy of it had not been served on the applicant organisation did not in any way nullify that decision."

Second, regarding police action:
> "the applicant organisation was prevented by the police from holding the planned march, apparently on the basis of the Mayor's ban."

The Court also explicitly rejects the Government's factual argument that no interference occurred because the applicant had a default right to proceed:
> "The Court does not, however, accept the Government's line of reasoning. The decision in question banned the applicant organisation from holding the event and the fact that a copy of it had not been served on the applicant organisation did not in any way nullify that decision."

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court states only: "nothing suggests that the interference was not prescribed by law." There is no specific factual grounding offered for this conclusion. The Court does not identify any facts about the legal basis, its accessibility, or its foreseeability. No factual grounding can be identified.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court states only: "it can accept that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing disorder or crime." The Court does not explicitly ground this in specific facts. However, this acceptance appears to be informed by the Government's stated reason for the ban, which the Court implicitly acknowledges when it describes it in the necessity analysis:
> "the reason for banning the march in question was that the post-election rallies had resulted in clashes and human casualties, there was still an on-going investigation and not all the offenders had been identified or weapons found."

This is the only factual context the Court provides that relates to the aim, and it is stated in the necessity section rather than the legitimate aim section. The Court accepts the aim without detailed factual analysis.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most extensively factually grounded element. The Court identifies three distinct factual bases for its conclusion that the reasons for the ban were not "relevant or sufficient":

**First factual ground — temporal distance from the post-election clashes:**
> "the march was supposed to take place more than two months after the post-election clashes and more than a month after the expiry of the state of emergency declared by the President of Armenia"

**Second factual ground — no link between organisers/participants and prior disorder:**
> "there is no evidence to suggest that the organisers or the participants of the planned march were in any way involved or implicated in the post-election disorder or violence."

**Third factual ground — no evidence of violent intentions or risk of disorder, and the insufficiency of the Mayor's reliance on the NSS opinion:**
> "nothing suggests that the organisers or the participants of the planned march had violent intentions or that the march might for any other reason degenerate into mass disorder. The Court notes in this respect that the purpose of the planned march had nothing to do with politics, elections or the confrontation between the opposition and the government and was instead to raise awareness on a matter of public concern, namely the death of a person while in a police station."

And:
> "The Court has no reason to doubt this allegation. It notes that the Mayor's reference to the official opinion of the National Security Service alleging that the march posed a danger is couched in general terms. It is not even clear whether the 'credible and verified data' referred to were ever presented to the Mayor or what such data were or whether such data even existed. The Mayor's finding in this respect therefore appears not to be supported by any concrete, clear and convincing evidence."

Note on the last point: the Court here accepts the applicant's characterisation of the march as "a quiet march marking the anniversary of a tragic event" — but only to the extent of saying "The Court has no reason to doubt this allegation," which is a qualified rather than a full adoption of the submission. The factual grounding is therefore primarily the absence of evidence on the Government's side rather than a positive finding based on the applicant's submission.

---

### (f) Positive obligation

Not discussed. No factual grounding exists or can be identified for this element.
