## Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

### (a) Scope

**i) Assembly exists**
The Court's implicit acceptance that an assembly existed is grounded in:
> "the applicants participated in a gathering on 31 July 2000 in a park in order to protest against F-type prisons regime and the violent clashes that had occurred in Bergama Prison."

**ii) Participation**
The same factual statement grounds participation:
> "the applicants participated in a gathering on 31 July 2000 in a park in order to protest against F-type prisons regime and the violent clashes that had occurred in Bergama Prison."

Additionally, the conviction itself is treated as factual confirmation of participation:
> "the subsequent conviction of the applicants for participating in the meeting constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11."

**iii) Peaceful assembly**
The Court grounds the peaceful character of the assembly in the following facts:
> "It appears that the protestors, including the applicants, wished to draw attention to a sensitive subject concerning the conditions of detention in prisons and that the gathering was initially peaceful."

> "there is no evidence to suggest that the group in question initially presented a serious danger to public order."

The Court also attributes the subsequent violence to the police intervention rather than to the demonstrators:
> "the authorities intervened swiftly with considerable force in order to disperse them, thereby causing tensions to rise, followed by clashes."

And:
> "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention is not to be deprived of all substance."

(This last passage is normative rather than factual, but it is the Court's direct characterization of the demonstrators' conduct as non-violent.)

---

### (b) Interference

The Court grounds the interference finding in two distinct facts:

> "the police intervention and the subsequent conviction of the applicants for participating in the meeting constituted, in itself, an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11."

No further factual elaboration is provided at this stage; the Court treats these two facts — police intervention and criminal conviction — as sufficient in themselves to establish interference.

---

### (c) Prescribed by law

The Court grounds this conclusion in one fact:
> "the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of the Assemblies and Marches Act (Law no. 2911)."

No further factual grounding is provided for the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards, as the Court does not analyze them separately.

---

### (d) Legitimate aim

The Court accepts the Government's submission on legitimate aim without independently identifying supporting facts:
> "the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder and the Court finds no reason to differ."

There is no independent factual grounding offered by the Court for this conclusion beyond accepting the Government's characterization. The Court does not point to specific facts establishing that disorder was actually threatened.

---

### (e) Necessity in a democratic society

This is the most factually grounded element. The Court draws on the following facts:

On the absence of initial danger:
> "there is no evidence to suggest that the group in question initially presented a serious danger to public order."

On the likely but limited disruption:
> "it is likely that they would have caused some disruption in a central park in Ankara."

On the nature and intent of the gathering:
> "It appears that the protestors, including the applicants, wished to draw attention to a sensitive subject concerning the conditions of detention in prisons and that the gathering was initially peaceful."

On the manner of police intervention and its consequences:
> "the authorities intervened swiftly with considerable force in order to disperse them, thereby causing tensions to rise, followed by clashes."

On the notification requirement and its context:
> "at the material time no authorisation was required for the holding of public demonstrations; however, notification was required seventy-two hours prior to the event."

On the severity of the sanction:
> "the sanction imposed on some of the applicants, namely one year and three months' imprisonment, cannot be considered proportionate in the circumstances of the case."

---

### (f) Positive obligation

The Court mentions positive obligations in general terms but does not ground them in any specific facts of the case. The passage is purely doctrinal:
> "although the essential object of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities in the exercise of the rights protected, there may also be positive obligations to secure their effective enjoyment."

No factual grounding can be identified for this element, consistent with the earlier finding that the Court does not reach a separate conclusion on positive obligations in this case.
