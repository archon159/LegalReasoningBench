Here is the analysis of the factual grounding for each element found to be implied or explicitly discussed:

---

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists / ii) Participation:**
The only factual grounding is the following sentence from the opening paragraph:
> "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants of public assemblies**."

This is the Court's own characterisation of the applicants' situation, not merely a party submission. It is the sole factual basis for the implied conclusions that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated.

**iii) Peaceful:**
No factual grounding can be identified. The Court does not refer to any fact — whether from its own assessment or adopted from party submissions — that speaks to the peaceful nature of the assemblies. The implication that the assemblies were peaceful rests entirely on the logical structure of the analysis, not on any stated fact.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is found in the opening paragraph:
> "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences"

This is the Court's own description of the measures taken, and it constitutes the factual basis for the finding that an interference occurred.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

No specific facts are identified by the Court to ground its necessity assessment. The Court does not point to any particular circumstance of the arrests, the dispersals, or the convictions to explain why the interference was disproportionate. Instead, the finding rests entirely on:

1. Reference to prior leading cases: "the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia... Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia... and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."
2. A general statement: "Having examined all the material submitted to it... the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."

No concrete facts from the present case are quoted or adopted by the Court to support the necessity finding. The assessment is grounded in precedent and a general reference to the case file, rather than in any explicitly stated facts specific to these applicants.
