**Factual grounding for each element discussed:**

**(a) Scope (implied)**

- **(i) Assembly exists:** The only factual grounding is the phrase "public assemblies" in: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." No further facts about the nature, size, location, or organisation of the assemblies are provided in this reasoning section.

- **(ii) Participation:** The only factual grounding is again: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." No further facts about the applicants' individual roles or conduct are provided.

- **(iii) Peaceful character:** No factual grounding whatsoever. The Court provides no facts about the conduct of the assemblies or the applicants' behaviour. The peaceful character is implied structurally by the Court's application of Article 11, but no facts are cited to support it.

**(b) Interference (explicit)**

The factual grounding is: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." These are the only facts cited. No further detail is provided about the circumstances of the arrests, the dispersals, or the nature of the administrative convictions.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)**

This is notable: the Court provides **no independent factual grounding** for its necessity assessment in this reasoning section. The conclusion rests entirely on the reference to prior case law: "In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014, and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of issues similar to those in the present case." The Court then states it "has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion," but does not identify what facts it examined or how they map onto the necessity analysis. The factual basis for the necessity finding is thus not articulated in this section.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. No factual grounding to identify.
