I will go through each element that was found to be either implied or explicitly discussed, and identify the factual grounding from the reasoning text.

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists**
The only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of **public assemblies**, namely their arrest in relation to the **dispersal of these assemblies**."

The Court does not elaborate further on the nature of the assemblies. No additional facts are provided.

**ii) Participation**
The only factual grounding is: "disproportionate measures taken against them as **organisers or participants** of public assemblies."

Again, no further facts are provided.

**iii) Peaceful character**
There is no factual grounding at all. The Court does not mention any facts relating to whether the assemblies were peaceful. This element is implied solely by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis, not by any stated facts.

---

**(b) Interference**

The factual grounding is: "their **arrest** in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their **conviction for administrative offences**."

These are the only concrete facts the Court identifies as constituting the interference. No further factual detail is provided.

---

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central finding, yet the factual grounding is notably thin. The Court states:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has **not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion** as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."

This is the entirety of the factual grounding for the necessity assessment. The Court does not identify any specific facts from the case to support its conclusion. Instead, it grounds its assessment entirely in its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov), stating:

"In the leading cases of Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, ECHR 2016 (extracts), Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, no. 76204/11, 4 December 2014 and Kasparov and Others v. Russia, no. 21613/07, 3 October 2013, the Court already found a violation in respect of **issues similar to those in the present case**."

The phrase "issues similar to those in the present case" implies a factual similarity but no specific facts are identified or quoted. The necessity conclusion is thus grounded in precedent rather than in any case-specific factual analysis.

---

**Overall observation**

The reasoning text is strikingly sparse in its factual grounding. Across all elements — whether implied or explicitly discussed — the Court relies almost entirely on a reference to prior case-law and a bare assertion that no new facts or arguments have been presented. The only concrete facts mentioned anywhere in the text are the arrest of the applicants, the dispersal of the assemblies, and the conviction for administrative offences. No facts are quoted from party submissions in a manner that the Court explicitly adopts as established.
