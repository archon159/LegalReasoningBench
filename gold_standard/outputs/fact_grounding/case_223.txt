# Factual Grounding for Each Canonical Element

---

## (a) Scope

### (i) Assembly exists

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> 'a peaceful manifestation organised by the applicant as a leader of the Chumatskiy Shlyakh Association was dispersed pursuant to a court order and the Association itself was enjoined to refrain from organising any further "pickets" in the city for an indefinite period of time.'

> 'the picket at issue was intended to draw local residents' attention to the purported shortcomings and allegedly "unhealthy" practices within the regional authority'

### (ii) Participation

The Court grounds this on the following facts:

> 'The applicant in the present case was the organiser and a participant of the protest. He had also signed personally all the relevant documents submitted to the competent authorities and represented the Association during the court proceedings. The appeals signed by him as chairman of the Association were admitted for consideration.'

### (iii) Peaceful

The Court does not explicitly make a factual finding that the assembly was peaceful in the sense of conducting an evidentiary assessment. It treats the assembly as peaceful throughout, and the domestic courts' allegations of misconduct are treated as unsubstantiated. The factual grounding is therefore the Court's rejection of the contrary allegations:

> 'the judicial authorities did not explain, by reference to the particular circumstances of the relevant incidents, exactly how the protesters had been "breaching public order"; in which way they had "acted arrogantly"; and what specific actions or phrases uttered by them were "offending other citizens"... The conclusions that the protesters had "obstructed the passage of pedestrians" and "endangered road users" were likewise not supported by any factual references. No estimate was even made of the number of protesters or the size of the area they had allegedly blocked.'

> 'the fact that V.Ch. and Y.S lodged complaints with the police about the protesters' alleged misconduct does not change this conclusion, as those complaints themselves did not describe any particular incident.'

## (c) Prescribed by law

### (i) Foreseeability

The Court grounds its doubts on the following facts:

> 'the impugned provision stipulated that the competent authorities had the right to lodge a court action with a view to enjoining those planning a demonstration to refrain from holding it "immediately upon receipt of a notification" announcing their intention to organise the event. In addition, paragraph 2 of the Article provided that "an action received on the date when the ... event is scheduled to take place ... shall be left without examination"'

> 'in the absence of any explanations from the Government, the Court has doubts that, given the above-mentioned wording, the applicant could reasonably have foreseen that the procedure provided for by Article 182 would be applied for disbanding an on-going picket.'

Regarding the prohibition on future pickets:

> 'the Court of Appeal neither explained the meaning of the term "picket" and its correlation with the term "peaceful assembly", nor referred to any legal acts which could clarify that distinction. Likewise, no definition of the term "picket" in the domestic legal order can be discerned from any other material which has been made available by the parties.'

### (ii) Accessibility

The Court grounds its positive finding on the following fact:

> 'The aforementioned Acts, which constituted the legal basis for the interference complained of, had been published and there is no dispute as to their accessibility to the general public.'

### (iii) Safeguards

The Court grounds its finding that safeguards were not applied on the following facts:

> 'the plaintiff's allegations concerning obstructive and inappropriate conduct of the picketers were taken at face value, without any effort having been taken to verify the underlying facts, for instance, by questioning witnesses or employing other appropriate procedural means, notwithstanding the applicant's requests in this respect.'

> 'The Court of Appeal endorsed its factual findings and its order to disperse the picket, without giving reasons for dismissing the arguments raised in the applicant's appeal.'

Note: The Court accepts these facts as established. The first derives from applicant submissions that 'were not contested by the Government'; the second is drawn directly from the Court's own characterisation of the appellate judgment.

---

## (d) Legitimate aim

The Court grounds the (provisional) finding of legitimate aim on the following facts:

> 'the interference in question, which was based on the findings that the picket was disruptive, offensive and potentially dangerous, pursued legitimate aims: the prevention of disorder and the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.'

The Court immediately qualifies this by casting doubt on the actual motives, grounded in the same factual deficiencies identified under necessity (see below), but does not make a separate factual finding on this point beyond cross-referencing paragraphs 51–56.

---

## (e) Necessity in a democratic society

The Court grounds its finding of non-necessity on the following facts:

**Lack of substantiation of alleged disorder:**

> 'the judicial authorities did not explain, by reference to the particular circumstances of the relevant incidents, exactly how the protesters had been "breaching public order"; in which way they had "acted arrogantly"; and what specific actions or phrases uttered by them were "offending other citizens"'

> 'The conclusions that the protesters had "obstructed the passage of pedestrians" and "endangered road users" were likewise not supported by any factual references. No estimate was even made of the number of protesters or the size of the area they had allegedly blocked.'

> 'an allegation that the protesters might "repeatedly breach public order" in future was purely speculative.'

> 'the fact that V.Ch. and Y.S lodged complaints with the police about the protesters' alleged misconduct does not change this conclusion, as those complaints themselves did not describe any particular incident.'

**Disproportionate nature of the measures:**

> 'not only were the protesters dispersed, but a blanket ban was imposed on the defendant Association's ability to hold any other "pickets" on any street or square in the entire city of Vinnytsia for an undefined period of time.'

**Political nature of the assembly:**

> 'the picket at issue was intended to draw local residents' attention to the purported shortcomings and allegedly "unhealthy" practices within the regional authority'

**Deficiencies in decision-making compounding the disproportionality:**

> 'the plaintiff's allegations concerning obstructive and inappropriate conduct of the picketers were taken at face value, without any effort having been taken to verify the underlying facts, for instance, by questioning witnesses or employing other appropriate procedural means, notwithstanding the applicant's requests in this respect.'

> 'The Court of Appeal endorsed its factual findings and its order to disperse the picket, without giving reasons for dismissing the arguments raised in the applicant's appeal.'

> 'The only substantive change introduced as a result of the appeal proceedings was the replacement of a sweeping injunction on organising any "peaceful assemblies" in future with a supposedly more narrow prohibition on holding "pickets".'
