**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The key passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a breach of Article 11 of the Convention."

These two sentences together confirm a finding of violation of Article 11.

**2. Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicants participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court's statement that "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly" occurred implies that the scope requirements are satisfied — there must have been an assembly, the applicants must have participated (as "organisers or participants of public assemblies" is stated in the opening paragraph), and the assembly must have been peaceful (otherwise Article 11 would not apply). These are therefore implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- **Assembly exists:** The opening paragraph states the applicants were "organisers or participants of public assemblies," implying an assembly existed. Implied, conclusion: yes.
- **Participation:** The opening paragraph explicitly identifies the applicants as "organisers or participants," implying participation. Implied, conclusion: yes.
- **Peaceful:** Not discussed explicitly, but implied by the Court's application of Article 11 protections. Implied, conclusion: yes.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly refers to "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly" in the penultimate paragraph: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The opening paragraph also describes the measures: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." Interference is thus explicitly acknowledged. Conclusion: yes.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor does it address foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse. Since the Court proceeds directly to the necessity analysis (and finds a violation there), it implicitly accepts that the interference was prescribed by law — otherwise it would have found a violation at that earlier stage. However, this is not discussed at all in the text provided, so it is best characterized as not_discussed (the Court jumps straight to necessity without addressing this element). Given the Court references leading cases and finds the interference not necessary, it appears to have assumed prescribed by law without discussion.

- **Prescribed by law (overall):** not_discussed, conclusion: null
- **Foreseeable:** not_discussed, conclusion: null
- **Accessible:** not_discussed, conclusion: null
- **Safeguards:** not_discussed, conclusion: null

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim. No aims are identified or analyzed. The Court moves directly from acknowledging the interference to the necessity conclusion. This element is not discussed. Conclusion: null, aims_found: [].

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only substantive element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." This is an explicit finding that the necessity requirement was not met (i.e., the government did not adequately balance the intensity of the intervention with the weight of the legitimate aim). Conclusion: no (necessity was not satisfied), discussed: explicit.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this reasoning section. Not discussed, conclusion: null.
