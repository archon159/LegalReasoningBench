**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The relevant passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a breach of Article 11 of the Convention."

The Court reaches this conclusion by reference to its leading cases (Frumkin v. Russia, Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia, Kasparov and Others v. Russia) and finds no reason to depart from those findings.

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (existence of an assembly, participation, and peacefulness). However, scope is implied: the Court refers to "the applicants" as "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and discusses "interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly," which logically presupposes that there was an assembly, that the applicants participated (or tried to), and that the assembly fell within the protection of Article 11. The peacefulness of the assemblies is not explicitly addressed but is implied by the framing of the case as one involving Article 11 protection.

- **scope_assembly_exists**: Implied — the text refers to "public assemblies" and "dispersal of these assemblies," presupposing their existence.
- **scope_participation**: Implied — the applicants are described as "organisers or participants of public assemblies."
- **scope_peaceful**: Implied — the Court treats the case as falling within Article 11 protection without any suggestion that the assemblies were violent or outside the scope of the right.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference. The passage states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The earlier passage also describes the measures: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The existence of interference is thus explicitly acknowledged.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor does it address the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. These elements are not discussed at all. The Court jumps directly to the necessity analysis, relying on its prior case-law. Since the Court finds a violation on necessity grounds, it does not need to address prescribed by law. These sub-elements are not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim. No specific aim from Article 11(2) is identified. The Court proceeds directly to the necessity analysis without addressing legitimate aim. This element is not_discussed, and no aims are identified.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only substantive element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The Court reaches this conclusion by reference to its prior case-law (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov and Others) and finds no new facts or arguments to depart from those findings. The conclusion is that necessity was NOT satisfied (i.e., the interference was not necessary), which is why Article 11 was violated.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government. The case is framed entirely in terms of negative obligations (unjustified interference). This element is not_discussed.
