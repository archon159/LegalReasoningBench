**Question 1: Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The relevant passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a breach of Article 11 of the Convention."

These two sentences together confirm the violation finding.

**Question 2: Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (existence of an assembly, participation, and peacefulness). However, scope is implied. The Court refers to "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies" and speaks of "interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly." This language implies that: (i) an assembly existed (the Court refers to "public assemblies"); (ii) the applicants participated (they are described as "organisers and participants"); and (iii) the assembly was peaceful (the Court proceeds to assess the interference and necessity without suggesting the assembly fell outside Article 11 protection). All three sub-elements are therefore implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- **scope_assembly_exists**: implied yes — the Court refers to "public assemblies" and "dispersal of these assemblies" without questioning whether an assembly existed.
- **scope_participation**: implied yes — the applicants are described as "organisers and participants of public assemblies."
- **scope_peaceful**: implied yes — the Court proceeds to assess the interference and necessity, which logically presupposes the assembly was peaceful and within the scope of Article 11.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly acknowledges interference. The passage states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The word "interferences" is used explicitly, and earlier the complaint is described as concerning "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies, and their conviction for administrative offences." Interference is therefore explicitly discussed and confirmed.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor any of its sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards). The Court jumps directly to the necessity assessment. These sub-elements are not discussed, even by implication, since the Court's reasoning does not require a finding on prescribed by law before reaching its necessity conclusion in this abbreviated reasoning. They are not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim. No specific aim from Article 11(2) is identified. The Court proceeds directly to the necessity assessment without addressing legitimate aim. This element is not_discussed.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only substantive element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The Court reaches a negative conclusion on necessity (i.e., the interference was NOT necessary), which is the basis for the violation finding. This is explicitly discussed.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government. This element is not_discussed.
