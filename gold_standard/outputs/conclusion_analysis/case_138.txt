**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court finds a violation of Article 11 in two separate instances, one for each demonstration.

For the demonstration of 7 April 2003, the Court concludes: "The Court reiterates that the very essence of the right to freedom of peaceful assembly would be impaired, if the State was not to prohibit a demonstration but was then to impose sanctions on its participants, even one at the lower end of the scale of penalties, for the mere fact of attending it, without committing anything reprehensible, as happened in the applicant's case... The Court therefore concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not 'necessary in a democratic society'. Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

For the demonstration of 9 April 2003, the Court concludes: "In the light of the above, the Court concludes that the reasons adduced by the domestic court were not sufficient to justify the interference with the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly, especially in the form of such a harsh penalty as five days of detention... Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly discusses the existence of an assembly. For 7 April 2003: "The Court notes that it is apparent from the court decision that the applicant was convicted for violating public order during a demonstration and, more specifically, the demonstration of 7 April 2003 held on the Mashtots Avenue." The Court also references findings from Galstyan that "the demonstration in question was neither intended to be not peaceful nor was it prohibited." For 9 April 2003, the existence of a demonstration is not disputed. Conclusion: yes, explicit.

**(ii) Participation:** The Court explicitly addresses participation. For 7 April 2003: "Thus, by joining the demonstration, the applicant availed herself of her right to freedom of peaceful assembly." The applicant's conviction was "for her participation at a lawful demonstration." For 9 April 2003, the Court notes "the applicant's conviction of 9 April 2003 undoubtedly amounted to an interference with her freedom of peaceful assembly," implying participation. Conclusion: yes, explicit.

**(iii) Peaceful:** The Court explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assembly. For 7 April 2003: "In that case, the Court established that the demonstration in question was neither intended to be not peaceful nor was it prohibited." The Court also notes that "the loud noise made by the applicant, there is similarly no suggestion that this noise involved any obscenity or incitement to violence." For 9 April 2003, the Court discusses whether the applicant committed reprehensible acts but ultimately finds the domestic court's findings insufficient, and notes that the obstruction "did not go beyond the level of disruption to ordinary life inherent in any peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes, explicit.

**(b) Interference**

For 7 April 2003, the Court explicitly discusses and concludes there was an interference: "The Court accordingly concludes that the applicant's conviction for her participation at a lawful demonstration amounted to an interference with her right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

For 9 April 2003: "It was not in dispute between the parties that there had been an interference with the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly. It considers that the applicant's conviction of 9 April 2003 undoubtedly amounted to an interference with her freedom of peaceful assembly."

Conclusion: yes, explicit.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly discusses this element for both demonstrations.

For 7 April 2003: "The Court notes that the applicant was convicted for an offence envisaged by Article 172 of the CAO. It further reiterates that this norm was formulated with sufficient precision to satisfy the requirements of Article 11 (see Galstyan, cited above, § 107). It follows that the interference was prescribed by law."

For 9 April 2003: "the applicant was convicted under the same Article of the CAO as on 7 April 2003. Therefore the interference was prescribed by law."

Conclusion: yes, explicit.

**(i) Foreseeable:** The Court's reference to the norm being "formulated with sufficient precision" in the context of Article 172 of the CAO addresses foreseeability. This is the standard foreseeability/precision requirement. Conclusion: yes, explicit (though briefly, by reference to Galstyan).

**(ii) Accessible:** The Court does not separately discuss accessibility of the law. It is implied by the overall finding that the interference was "prescribed by law," but accessibility as a distinct sub-element is not explicitly addressed. Conclusion: null, implied.

**(iii) Safeguards:** The Court does not discuss safeguards against abuse as a distinct sub-element. Conclusion: null, not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

For 7 April 2003: "The Court notes that it is apparent that the applicant incurred the sanction for actions which were qualified by the authorities as violating public order. The interference was therefore in pursuit of a legitimate aim, namely 'the prevention of disorder'."

For 9 April 2003: "The Court further considers that the interference pursued the legitimate aims of 'the prevention of disorder' and 'the protection of the rights and freedoms of others'."

Conclusion: yes, explicit. Aims found: prevention_of_disorder (both demonstrations), rights_of_others (9 April demonstration).

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

For 7 April 2003, the Court explicitly discusses necessity at length, concluding: "The Court therefore concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

For 9 April 2003, the Court similarly discusses necessity: "the Court concludes that the reasons adduced by the domestic court were not sufficient to justify the interference with the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly."

Conclusion: no (not necessary), explicit.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation of the state in this judgment. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (unjustified interference). Conclusion: null, not_discussed.
