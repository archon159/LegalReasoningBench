**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The key passage is in the final paragraph of the merits section: "the Court therefore dismisses the Government's preliminary objection regarding the applicants' alleged lack of victim status and concludes that there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The substantive finding leading to this conclusion is: "the Court considers that in the instant case the forceful intervention of the police officers was disproportionate and was not necessary for the prevention of disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

*i) Assembly exists:* The Court explicitly discusses the existence of assemblies/demonstrations. It states: "The Court notes that the applicants took part in demonstrations with a view to protesting a draft bill proposed in the Turkish Parliament which would amend the Act on Trade Unions." It also notes: "KESK organised two demonstrations, on 7 and 25 June 2001 respectively." This is explicitly discussed and the conclusion is affirmative.

*ii) Participation:* The Court explicitly states that "the applicants took part in demonstrations" and later refers to "the applicants and other demonstrators." This is explicitly discussed and the conclusion is affirmative.

*iii) Peaceful assembly:* The Court explicitly addresses this: "it transpires that the demonstrators, including the applicants, wished to draw attention to a sensitive bill proposed in the Parliament and that their rally was initially peaceful." It also states: "there is no evidence to suggest that the group in question initially presented a serious danger to public order." The Court further notes: "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings." The assembly was initially peaceful, and the Court treats it as a peaceful assembly for Article 11 purposes. Conclusion: yes (peaceful), explicitly discussed.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference under the heading "1. Existence of any interference with the applicants' rights." It concludes: "the Court considers that the applicants were negatively affected by the police intervention and that there has therefore been an interference with the exercise of their right to freedom of peaceful assembly." The Court also notes the chilling effect: "the interference in the meetings and the force used by the police to disperse the participants, as well as the subsequent prosecution, could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants from taking part in similar meetings." Conclusion: yes, explicitly discussed.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly addresses this: "the Court observes that the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely section 22 of Law no. 2911 (the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act), and was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention." The overall conclusion is yes, explicitly discussed.

*i) Foreseeable:* The Court does not separately analyze foreseeability of the legal provision. It simply states the interference had a legal basis. Not explicitly discussed; the overall "prescribed by law" finding is made without breaking it into sub-elements. Conclusion: null, not_discussed (the sub-element is not separately addressed).

*ii) Accessible:* Similarly, accessibility of the provision is not separately analyzed. Conclusion: null, not_discussed.

*iii) Safeguards against abuse:* Not separately analyzed. Conclusion: null, not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court explicitly discusses legitimate aim: "As concerns legitimate aims, the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting public safety, and the Court finds no reason to differ." Conclusion: yes, explicitly discussed. Aims found: public_safety and prevention_of_disorder.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court extensively discusses necessity under the heading "2. Justification for the interference." Key passages include:
- "the Court refers in the first place to the fundamental principles underlying its judgments relating to Article 11"
- "the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to lawful demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens"
- "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention is not to be deprived of all substance"
- "the Court considers that in the instant case the forceful intervention of the police officers was disproportionate and was not necessary for the prevention of disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention."

Conclusion: no (the interference was NOT necessary), explicitly discussed.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court explicitly mentions positive obligations: "although the essential object of Article 11 is to protect the individual against arbitrary interference by public authorities in the exercise of the rights protected, there may also be positive obligations to secure their effective enjoyment (see Djavit An, cited above, § 57; Oya Ataman, cited above, § 36)." It also states: "States must not only safeguard freedom of peaceful assembly, but must also refrain from applying unreasonable indirect restrictions upon that right." However, the Court does not make a separate finding that the government violated a positive obligation — the violation is found on the basis of disproportionate interference (negative obligation). The positive obligation is mentioned as part of the general principles but not applied to reach a separate conclusion. Conclusion: null (mentioned but no separate finding of violation of positive obligation), explicitly discussed (the concept is raised).
