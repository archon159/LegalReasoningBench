**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The final paragraph of the reasoning states: "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The basis for this conclusion is found in the 'prescribed by law' analysis: "The foregoing considerations are sufficient to enable the Court to conclude that the ban on Kossuth Square at the material time was devoid of a basis in domestic law and cannot as such be regarded as 'prescribed by law'. It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or necessity in a democratic society."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

*Assembly exists:* The Court does not explicitly discuss whether an assembly existed, but it is implied by the entire framing of the case. The applicant "complained that the police measure in question had prevented him from exercising his right to peaceful assembly," and the Court proceeds to examine the complaint under Article 11 without questioning whether an assembly was planned. The existence of an intended demonstration (at Kossuth Square) is treated as given.

*Participation:* Similarly implied. The Court notes "the non-acquiescence by the police in the demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of the applicant's rights under that provision," which presupposes the applicant was attempting to participate. Not explicitly discussed as a separate sub-element.

*Peaceful:* The Court refers to the applicant's "right to peaceful assembly" and the complaint concerns a "peaceful assembly," but does not explicitly analyze whether the assembly was peaceful. This is implied — the Court proceeds on the assumption that it was peaceful, as there is no suggestion of violence.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed: "The Court considers that the non-acquiescence by the police in the demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of the applicant's rights under that provision, as the individualised application of the original police decision referred to by the Government." The Court finds interference in the affirmative.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed at length. The Court states: "As regards the question whether the non-availability of Kossuth Square for the purposes of the intended demonstration was 'prescribed by law', the Court notes that the police declared it a 'security operational zone' in 2006, and it remained so throughout the material period. However, on 11 November 2010 the Budapest Regional Court quashed the underlying police decisions, reproaching those authorities for failing to assess the necessity and proportionality of the measure as maintained subsequent to 22 November 2006. Consequently, on 4 April 2011 the Budapest Commander carried out the requisite scrutiny and found that the proportionality of the measure had not been proved... For the Court, these court rulings have effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis of the impugned measure."

The Court concludes: "the ban on Kossuth Square at the material time was devoid of a basis in domestic law and cannot as such be regarded as 'prescribed by law'."

The Court does not separately analyze the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. Instead, it focuses on the absence of a legal basis altogether (the domestic courts retroactively removed the legal basis). The sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards are not discussed — the analysis stops at the threshold question of whether there was any valid legal basis at all. The conclusion on 'prescribed by law' is negative (no), and the sub-elements are not_discussed individually.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. The Court explicitly states: "It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or necessity in a democratic society." Since the measure failed the 'prescribed by law' test, the Court did not proceed to examine legitimate aim.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed, for the same reason: "It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or necessity in a democratic society."

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The Court's analysis is framed entirely in terms of a negative obligation (the state interfered with the applicant's right). No positive obligation is spelled out or examined.
