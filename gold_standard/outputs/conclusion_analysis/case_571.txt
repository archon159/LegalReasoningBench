**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The final paragraph of the merits section states: "Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The key reasoning leading to this conclusion is found in the section on 'prescribed by law,' where the Court quotes extensively from Vyerentsov v. Ukraine and states: "The Court sees no reasons to depart from those conclusions in the present case, which concerns the same legal framework and in which the parties had raised similar arguments to those advanced in the case of Vyerentsov cited above." The Court then notes: "Having reached the conclusion that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law, the Court does not need to verify whether the other two requirements (legitimate aim and necessity of the interference) set forth in Article 11 § 2 have been complied with."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The scope elements are implied rather than explicitly discussed. The Court proceeds directly to interference, noting it as "undisputed by the parties," which implies that the assembly existed, the applicant participated, and it was peaceful. The phrase "the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful assembly" in the interference section confirms all three sub-elements by implication.

- **(i) Assembly exists:** Implied. The Court refers to "a peaceful assembly" and "the picket he had organised" without separately analyzing whether an assembly existed. Conclusion: yes (implied).

- **(ii) Participation:** Implied. The applicant is described as having "organised and held" the assembly. Conclusion: yes (implied).

- **(iii) Peaceful:** Implied. The Court explicitly uses the phrase "peaceful assembly" in the interference section: "the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful assembly constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes (implied, though the word 'peaceful' is used explicitly in context of interference rather than as a separate scope analysis).

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed. Under section "a) Whether there was an interference," the Court states: "The Court considers, as undisputed by the parties, that the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful assembly constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes (explicit).

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed in detail under section "b) Whether the interference was justified."

The Court sets out the general standard: "The law should be accessible to the persons concerned and formulated with sufficient precision to enable them – if need be, with appropriate advice – to foresee, to a degree that is reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences which a given action may entail."

- **(i) Foreseeable:** Explicitly discussed. The Court focuses on foreseeability, quoting Vyerentsov: "it cannot be concluded that the 'procedure' referred to in Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences was formulated with sufficient precision to enable the applicant to foresee, to a degree that was reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences of his actions." Conclusion: no (explicit).

- **(ii) Accessible:** Explicitly discussed but found satisfied. The Court states: "It has not been disputed that the Code was accessible and the Court does not have reason to doubt that." Conclusion: yes (explicit).

- **(iii) Safeguards against abuse:** Not discussed. The Court does not address whether the legal framework contained adequate safeguards against abuse. Conclusion: null (not discussed).

Overall 'prescribed by law' conclusion: no (explicit) — "The Court thus concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law."

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed by the Court. The Court explicitly states: "Having reached the conclusion that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law, the Court does not need to verify whether the other two requirements (legitimate aim and necessity of the interference) set forth in Article 11 § 2 have been complied with." The Government had argued the aim of "protecting the rights and freedoms of others" and public safety, but the Court did not assess these. Conclusion: null (not discussed by the Court). Aims found: none (the Court did not make a finding).

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed by the Court, for the same reason stated above. Conclusion: null (not discussed).

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The Court does not frame the case in terms of positive obligations. Conclusion: null (not discussed).
