**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The relevant passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a breach of Article 11 of the Convention."

These two sentences together confirm the violation finding.

**2. Canonical Elements of Article 11 Doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicants participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court's reference to "the applicants' freedom of assembly" and to them as "organisers or participants of public assemblies" implies that all three sub-elements of scope are satisfied. The Court would not have proceeded to analyze interference and necessity without implicitly accepting that the assemblies existed, that the applicants participated (or tried to), and that the assemblies were peaceful (since the leading cases cited, such as Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, and Kasparov, all involved peaceful assemblies).

- **scope_participation**: Implied as yes — the Court describes the applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies."
- **scope_peaceful**: Implied as yes — the Court proceeds to analyze interference and necessity, which logically requires the assemblies to have been peaceful. No suggestion of violence is made.

The Court explicitly acknowledges interference: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The opening paragraph also describes the measures: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This is explicit acknowledgment of interference, though the Court does not separately analyze whether interference occurred as a standalone step — it is treated as given.
