**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of the assessment: "Accordingly, the interference in the present case was not 'necessary in a democratic society', which is why there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

Additionally, the penultimate paragraph states: "the Court considers that the domestic courts failed to strike a fair balance between the legitimate aim in question and the requirements of freedom of assembly."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly refers to "the demonstration" and "an unlawful demonstration" throughout the judgment. The existence of an assembly (a demonstration) is treated as an established fact. The Court states: "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration..." This is explicitly discussed and affirmed.

**(ii) Participation:** The Court explicitly states: "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration amounted to an interference with the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly." The applicant's participation (indeed, organisation and participation) is explicitly confirmed.

**(iii) Peaceful assembly:** The Court explicitly addresses this: "There is no indication in the case file that the demonstration was not peaceful or that the demonstrators, including the applicant, engaged in acts of violence or presented a danger to public order beyond the level of minor disturbance." Further: "nor could the applicant's speech be construed as encouraging any violent behaviour." And: "he was nevertheless faced with the threat of a sanction of one year and three months' imprisonment on account of his involvement in a peaceful demonstration." The assembly is explicitly found to be peaceful.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed and affirmed: "The applicant's criminal conviction for having organised and participated in an unlawful demonstration amounted to an interference with the exercise of his right to freedom of assembly, even though the pronouncement of the judgment was suspended."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court states: "That interference was prescribed by section 28(1) of Law no. 2911." This is an explicit affirmation that the interference was prescribed by law. However, the Court does not separately discuss the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse. The conclusion that the measure was "prescribed by law" is stated as a single finding without breaking it into sub-elements. The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are therefore not individually discussed — they are implied by the overall finding that the interference was prescribed by law.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Explicitly discussed: "That interference was prescribed by section 28(1) of Law no. 2911 and pursued at least one of the legitimate aims set out in Article 11 § 2 of the Convention, namely the prevention of disorder." The aim of prevention of disorder is explicitly found.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Explicitly and extensively discussed. The Court examines proportionality: "As to the proportionality of the interference..." It notes the demonstration was considered unlawful only due to absence of prior notice, that it was peaceful, that slogans did not constitute incitement to violence, and that the applicant faced a threat of imprisonment. The Court concludes: "the domestic courts failed to strike a fair balance between the legitimate aim in question and the requirements of freedom of assembly" and "the interference in the present case was not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The necessity test is explicitly failed (i.e., the answer to whether the interference was necessary is 'no').

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (the State interfered with the applicant's rights through criminal conviction). Positive obligations are not discussed at all.
