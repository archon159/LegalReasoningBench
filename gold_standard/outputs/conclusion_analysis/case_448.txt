**Question 1: Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated in respect of the second applicant. The relevant passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

'Having regard to the facts of the present case and their clear similarity to those of Huseynli and Others (cited above, §§ 97-101) and Huseynov and Others (cited above, §§ 45-51) on all relevant and crucial points, the Court sees no special circumstances that could compel it to deviate from its findings in those judgments, and finds that the second applicant's right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined in the above-mentioned judgments and in Damirov and Others v. Azerbaijan ([Committee], nos. 38158/12 and 5 others, §§ 16-17, 3 December 2020).'

And: 'There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention in respect of the second applicant.'

The Court reaches this conclusion by reference to prior judgments (Huseynli and Others, Huseynov and Others, Damirov and Others), finding no special circumstances to deviate from those findings.

**Question 2: Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful character) in this excerpt. However, the complaint itself — that the second applicant 'had been arrested and convicted by the authorities to prevent him from participating in the peaceful demonstration that had been planned for 24 May 2012' — implies that all three scope elements are satisfied: there was a planned assembly (a 'demonstration'), the applicant sought to participate in it, and it was described as 'peaceful.' These elements are implied rather than explicitly analyzed, as the Court proceeds directly to a finding of violation by reference to prior case law without re-examining scope.

- scope_assembly_exists: implied yes (the complaint references 'the peaceful demonstration that had been planned for 24 May 2012')
- scope_participation: implied yes (the applicant was 'prevent[ed] from participating')
- scope_peaceful: implied yes (the demonstration is described as 'peaceful')

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not explicitly analyze interference as a separate element. However, it is implied: the complaint states the applicant 'had been arrested and convicted by the authorities to prevent him from participating,' and the Court finds a breach of the right to freedom of assembly. The arrest and conviction constitute interference, which is implied by the overall finding.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor its sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards). The reasoning is entirely by reference to prior judgments. In those prior judgments (Huseynli and Others, Huseynov and Others, Damirov and Others), these elements were presumably addressed, but they are not discussed in this excerpt. Accordingly, these elements are not discussed in the present text.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim. Again, the reasoning is by reference to prior case law. Not discussed in this excerpt.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court does not explicitly discuss necessity. The finding of a violation is made by reference to prior judgments without re-examining this element. Not discussed in this excerpt.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this excerpt. Not discussed.

**Summary:** The Court's reasoning in this excerpt is highly compressed, relying entirely on the similarity of facts to prior cases (Huseynli and Others, Huseynov and Others, Damirov and Others) to conclude there has been a violation. None of the canonical doctrinal elements (scope, interference, prescribed by law, legitimate aim, necessity, positive obligation) are explicitly analyzed. Scope, participation, and peaceful character are implied by the framing of the complaint. Interference is implied by the finding of a violation. The remaining elements (prescribed by law and its sub-elements, legitimate aim, necessity, positive obligation) are not discussed at all in this excerpt.
