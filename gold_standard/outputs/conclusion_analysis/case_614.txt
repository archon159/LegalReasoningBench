The reasoning section is brief and relies entirely on a cross-reference to the earlier case of Berkman, which concerned the same public event. The Court states: 'It therefore finds, for the reasons advanced in Berkman (ibid., §§ 50-63), that there has been, in respect of both applicants, a violation of the respondent State's positive obligations under Article 11 of the Convention taken alone and in conjunction with Article 14, a violation of the respondent State's negative obligations under Article 11 of the Convention, and no violation of Article 14 taken in conjunction with negative obligations under Article 11 of the Convention.'

**1. Violation of Article 11:**
Yes, Article 11 has been violated. The Court explicitly states this in the paragraph quoted above (the single merits paragraph under B). It finds: (i) a violation of positive obligations under Article 11 taken alone; (ii) a violation of positive obligations under Article 11 in conjunction with Article 14; and (iii) a violation of negative obligations under Article 11. The Court does not find a violation of Article 14 taken in conjunction with negative obligations under Article 11.

**2. Canonical elements:**

Because the Court's reasoning is entirely by reference to Berkman §§ 50-63, the present text does not itself discuss any of the canonical elements explicitly. However, the Court's conclusions allow us to infer certain elements:

**(a) Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful):** Not explicitly discussed in this text. However, the finding of both positive and negative obligation violations under Article 11 implies that the assembly fell within the scope of Article 11 — i.e., there was an assembly, the applicants participated (or tried to), and it was peaceful. These are implied by the violation findings.

**(b) Interference:** Not explicitly discussed. The finding of a violation of negative obligations under Article 11 implies that there was an interference with the applicants' rights. This is implied.

**(c) Prescribed by law (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards):** Not discussed at all in this text. The Court does not address these sub-elements here, and since the violation is found on necessity/proportionality grounds (as is typical in such cases, per the Berkman reference), these sub-elements are not_discussed in this excerpt.

**(d) Legitimate aim:** Not discussed in this text. Not_discussed.

**(e) Necessity:** Not explicitly discussed. The finding of a violation of negative obligations implies the interference was not necessary in a democratic society, but this is only implied by the reference to Berkman.

**(f) Positive obligation:** Explicitly discussed and found violated. The Court states: 'there has been, in respect of both applicants, a violation of the respondent State's positive obligations under Article 11 of the Convention taken alone and in conjunction with Article 14.' This is explicit.

In summary: The only element explicitly discussed in this text is the positive obligation (found violated) and the overall violation of negative obligations under Article 11. All other canonical elements are either implied or not discussed in this excerpt, with the Court deferring entirely to the reasoning in Berkman §§ 50-63.
