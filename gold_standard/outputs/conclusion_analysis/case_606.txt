**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of the reasoning section: "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The Court reaches this conclusion on the basis that the ban on Kossuth Square was not "prescribed by law," as stated in the preceding paragraph: "The foregoing considerations are sufficient to enable the Court to conclude that the ban on Kossuth Square at the material time was devoid of a basis in domestic law and cannot as such be regarded as 'prescribed by law'."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:** The Court does not explicitly discuss whether an assembly existed, but it is implied. The Court proceeds directly to discuss interference with the applicant's rights under Article 11, which logically presupposes that there was an assembly. The Government also did not dispute that the applicant could rely on Article 11 guarantees: "The Court observes that the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11." This implies the existence of an assembly (an intended political demonstration at Kossuth Square).

**ii) Participation:** Similarly implied. The Court notes "the non-acquiescence by the police in the demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of the applicant's rights under that provision," which implies the applicant sought to participate in the assembly.

**iii) Peaceful:** Not explicitly discussed. However, the assembly is referred to throughout as a "demonstration" and there is no suggestion of violence. The Court's willingness to proceed under Article 11 implies it was peaceful, but this is not explicitly addressed.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed: "The Court observes that the Government did not dispute that the applicant could rely on the guarantees contained in Article 11. It considers that the non-acquiescence by the police in the demonstration effectively interfered with the exercise of the applicant's rights under that provision, as the individualised application of the original police decision referred to by the Government." The Court thus explicitly finds interference.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed and found in the negative. The Court states: "As regards the question whether the non-availability of Kossuth Square for the purposes of the intended demonstration was 'prescribed by law', the Court notes that the police declared it a 'security operational zone' in 2006... However, on 11 November 2010 the Budapest Regional Court quashed the underlying police decisions... For the Court, these court rulings have effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis of the impugned measure." And: "The foregoing considerations are sufficient to enable the Court to conclude that the ban on Kossuth Square at the material time was devoid of a basis in domestic law and cannot as such be regarded as 'prescribed by law'."

**i) Foreseeable:** Not explicitly discussed as a sub-element. The Court's analysis of "prescribed by law" focuses on the retroactive removal of the legal basis rather than foreseeability per se. Not discussed as a distinct sub-element.

**ii) Accessible:** Not explicitly discussed.

**iii) Safeguards against abuse:** Not explicitly discussed as a distinct sub-element, though the Court notes the domestic courts found the authorities failed to assess necessity and proportionality, which touches on the absence of adequate safeguards. However, this is not framed as a "safeguards" analysis under the prescribed-by-law test.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. The Court explicitly states: "It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or necessity in a democratic society." Since the measure failed the "prescribed by law" test, the Court did not proceed to examine legitimate aim.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed. As stated above, the Court explicitly declined to examine necessity: "It is therefore not necessary to embark on an examination of its legitimate aim or necessity in a democratic society."

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The Court's analysis is framed entirely in terms of a negative obligation (the state interfered with the applicant's right). No positive obligation is spelled out or examined.
