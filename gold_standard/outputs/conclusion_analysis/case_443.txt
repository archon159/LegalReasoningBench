**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The final conclusion is stated in the last paragraph of the Court's assessment: "Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The key finding leading to this conclusion is: "The Court concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements:**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicant participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court's explicit discussion of interference with the applicant's "right to freedom of peaceful assembly" implies that the assembly existed, that the applicant participated, and that it was peaceful. These elements are implied rather than explicitly addressed.

- scope_assembly_exists: implied (yes) — the Court refers to the applicant's "right to freedom of peaceful assembly" being interfered with, which presupposes an assembly existed.
- scope_participation: implied (yes) — the applicant's conviction for violating rules on holding rallies implies his participation.
- scope_peaceful: implied (yes) — the Court refers throughout to "peaceful assembly" without questioning its peaceful nature.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed. The Court states: "It has not been disputed between the parties that the applicant's conviction constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly. The Court recalls that the term 'restrictions' used in Article 11 § 2 cannot be interpreted as not including measures – such as punitive measures – taken not before or during but after a meeting (Ezelin v. France, judgment of 26 April 1991, Series A no. 202, § 39). The Court concludes that there has been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly and extensively discussed. The Court finds the interference was NOT prescribed by law.

- prescribed_by_law (overall): The Court states: "The Court concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law." Conclusion: no.

- prescribed_by_law_foreseeable: Explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the Court considers that the law in question, namely the 'prescribed rules' referred to in Article 180.1 of the CAO, was not formulated with such precision as to enable the applicant to foresee, to a degree that was reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences of his actions." The Court also notes "the domestic courts' lack of reference to any legal provision prescribing the rules in question and to the absence of any case-law concerning the applicability of the former USSR laws in Armenia following its independence." Conclusion: no.

- prescribed_by_law_accessible: Explicitly discussed but only partially. The Court states: "It has not been disputed that the CAO was accessible and the Court does not have reason to doubt that. It remains, therefore, to be determined whether this provision was foreseeable." The Court thus finds the CAO itself accessible, but the deeper question of whether the underlying 'prescribed rules' (the Law and Decree) were accessible is subsumed into the foreseeability analysis. The applicant argued the Law was not accessible since he could not find it. The Court does not make a definitive finding on accessibility of the Law/Decree separately, focusing instead on foreseeability. Conclusion: null (the CAO was found accessible, but the underlying rules' accessibility was not separately resolved — the Court moved to foreseeability as the decisive issue).

- prescribed_by_law_safeguards: Not discussed. Conclusion: null.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. The Court explicitly states: "Having reached this conclusion, the Court does not need to verify whether other two requirements (legitimate aim and necessity of the interference) set forth in Article 11 § 2 have been complied with." Conclusion: null, aims_found: [].

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed, explicitly bypassed. Same statement as above. Conclusion: null.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The Court's analysis is entirely framed in terms of a negative obligation (unjustified interference). Conclusion: null.
