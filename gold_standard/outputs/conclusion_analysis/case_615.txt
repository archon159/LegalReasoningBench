**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of section (b)(ii): "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

The Court reaches this conclusion primarily through its necessity analysis, having already found an interference and declining to decide on prescribed by law and legitimate aim.

**2. Analysis of canonical elements:**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly discusses the existence of an assembly. It refers to "the assembly at Freedom Square" throughout, and in the admissibility section states: "it has already examined and dismissed a similar claim by the Government, finding that there was not sufficient and convincing evidence to conclude that the organisers and the participants of the assembly at Freedom Square had had violent intentions and that the assembly in question had not been peaceful (see Mushegh Saghatelyan v. Armenia, no. 23086/08, §§ 229-233, 20 September 2018)." The existence of an assembly is thus explicitly confirmed.

**(ii) Participation:** The Court explicitly addresses the applicant's participation: "This conclusion undoubtedly applies to the applicant, who was the main leader of the demonstrations held at Freedom Square and was, moreover, on site during the dispersal." This is explicit.

**(iii) Peaceful:** The Court explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assembly. The Government argued that Article 11 was not applicable because the assembly was not peaceful. The Court rejected this: "it has already examined and dismissed a similar claim by the Government, finding that there was not sufficient and convincing evidence to conclude that the organisers and the participants of the assembly at Freedom Square had had violent intentions and that the assembly in question had not been peaceful." The Court therefore rejects the Government's objection, implicitly confirming the assembly was peaceful. This is explicit.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference in section (b)(i): "The Court notes at the outset that the Government did not dispute the existence of an interference, other than arguing that the assembly had not been peaceful. It further notes that it has already held that the dispersal of the assembly at Freedom Square had interfered with the right to freedom of assembly of its participants (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, § 234). This conclusion undoubtedly applies to the applicant, who was the main leader of the demonstrations held at Freedom Square and was, moreover, on site during the dispersal. The Court concludes that there has been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes, interference found. Discussed: explicit.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly declines to decide this element: "In the present case, the Court does not consider it necessary to decide whether the interference was prescribed by law and pursued a legitimate aim having regard to its conclusions set out below, regarding the necessity of the interference." The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are therefore not discussed. The overall prescribed by law question is mentioned but left unresolved (null conclusion). Discussed: explicit (but unresolved).

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Similarly, the Court explicitly declines to decide whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim: "the Court does not consider it necessary to decide whether the interference was prescribed by law and pursued a legitimate aim having regard to its conclusions set out below, regarding the necessity of the interference." Conclusion: null. Discussed: explicit (but unresolved). No aims found.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court explicitly discusses necessity. It reiterates the general principles and then states: "The Court notes that it has already examined the necessity of the interference with the assembly at Freedom Square and concluded that its dispersal was without sufficient justification and took place under somewhat dubious circumstances, apparently without warnings to disperse and with unjustified and excessive use of force, and that it was a disproportionate measure which went beyond what it was reasonable to expect from the authorities when curtailing freedom of assembly (see Mushegh Saghatelyan, cited above, §§ 240-248). The Court sees no reasons to depart from that conclusion in the present case." Conclusion: no (the interference was not necessary). Discussed: explicit.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this judgment. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (unjustified interference). Conclusion: null. Discussed: not_discussed.
