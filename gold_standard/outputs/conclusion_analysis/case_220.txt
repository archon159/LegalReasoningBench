**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The final paragraph of the Court's assessment states: "The Court finds that the temporary ban on the CDPP's activities was not based on relevant and sufficient reasons and was not necessary in a democratic society. Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements:**

**(a) Scope**

**i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly discusses whether the gatherings constituted assemblies protected under Article 11. In the Applicability section, the Court notes: "The Court first notes that the Government have failed to substantiate their allegations of violence. The domestic courts examined twice the issue of the legality of the CDPP's gatherings... and never found them to have been violent. Lastly, it appears from the video submitted by the Government that the gatherings were peaceful." The existence of assemblies (gatherings) is treated as given and not disputed — the Government only challenged whether they were peaceful and authorised. The assembly's existence is implied by the entire discussion.

**ii) Participation:** The applicant party organised and participated in the gatherings. This is implied throughout — the ban was imposed on the CDPP's activities as a result of gatherings it had organised. The Court notes: "The ban was imposed on the applicant party's activities as a result of gatherings it had organised in order to express its disagreement with and protest against the government's plans..." Participation is implied rather than explicitly discussed as a separate sub-element.

**iii) Peaceful assembly:** This is explicitly discussed. The Government argued the gatherings were not peaceful and not authorised. The Court states: "The Court first notes that the Government have failed to substantiate their allegations of violence. The domestic courts examined twice the issue of the legality of the CDPP's gatherings... and never found them to have been violent. Lastly, it appears from the video submitted by the Government that the gatherings were peaceful. It follows that the Government's objection must be dismissed." Later, the Court reiterates: "Since the CDPP's gatherings were entirely peaceful, there were no calls to violent overthrowing of the government or any other acts undermining the principles of pluralism and democracy..."

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed and agreed upon: "The parties agreed that the imposition of a temporary ban on the applicant party's activities amounted to interference with the CDPP's rights guaranteed by Article 11. That view is shared by the Court."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly discusses this element but leaves it unresolved, finding it unnecessary to decide given its conclusions on necessity. The Court notes: "The applicant party was not, therefore, informed in the warning letter of all the acts imputed to it, which reduced its ability to foresee all the consequences which might ensue if it continued to hold meetings. This in itself might be sufficient basis for the conclusion that the impugned measures were not 'prescribed by law'. However, the Court does not consider it necessary to decide this issue having regard to its conclusions set out below."

**i) Foreseeable:** Explicitly discussed. The Court raises concerns about foreseeability: "The applicant party was not, therefore, informed in the warning letter of all the acts imputed to it, which reduced its ability to foresee all the consequences which might ensue if it continued to hold meetings." The Court suggests this might not be satisfied but declines to decide.

**ii) Accessible:** Not explicitly discussed. The Court references the legal basis (section 29 of the Parties and other Socio-Political Organisations Act) but does not separately address accessibility.

**iii) Safeguards:** Not explicitly discussed as a separate sub-element.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Explicitly discussed but left unresolved. The Court states: "The Government did not make any particular submissions in this respect and the applicant party argued that the interference did not pursue any legitimate aim. The Court, for the reasons set out below, does not consider it necessary to decide this point either." No legitimate aim is found or confirmed. The Government's submissions referenced prevention of disorder and protection of children's rights, but the Court did not formally identify any legitimate aim.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Explicitly and extensively discussed. The Court concludes the interference was NOT necessary in a democratic society. Key passages include:
- "only very compelling reasons would have justified the interference with the CDPP's right to freedom of expression and assembly"
- On the first ground (lack of authorisation): "the Court is not convinced that the failure to comply with that legislation, which otherwise was punishable with an administrative fine of MDL 180-450 (EUR 16-40), could be considered as a relevant and sufficient reason for imposing a temporary ban on the activities of an opposition party."
- On the second ground (children present): "the Court is not satisfied that this reason was relevant and sufficient."
- On the third ground (calls to violence): "the Court is not persuaded that the singing of a fairly mild student song could reasonably be interpreted as a call to public violence... this reason cannot be considered as relevant and sufficient either."
- Final conclusion: "The Court finds that the temporary ban on the CDPP's activities was not based on relevant and sufficient reasons and was not necessary in a democratic society."

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (interference by the State with the applicant's rights). There is no mention of any positive obligation on the part of the government.
