**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The final paragraph of the merits section states: "Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The key finding leading to this conclusion is: "The Court thus concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements:**

**(a) Scope**

*Assembly exists:* The Court implicitly accepts that an assembly existed. The Court states: "The Court considers that the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful demonstration constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly." The reference to "organising and holding a peaceful demonstration" implies an assembly existed, though this is not separately analyzed.

*Participation:* The Court implies the applicant participated (indeed organized) the assembly. The same sentence — "the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful demonstration" — implies his participation/organization, though not explicitly analyzed as a separate element.

*Peaceful:* The Court explicitly characterizes the demonstration as peaceful: "the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful demonstration constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly." This is stated explicitly but not analyzed as a separate sub-element requiring proof.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly finds interference: "The Court considers that the applicant's punishment for organising and holding a peaceful demonstration constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly." This is explicit.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly and extensively discusses this element.

*Accessible:* "The Court has no reason to doubt that the Code was accessible." This is explicitly discussed and answered affirmatively.

*Foreseeable:* The Court extensively analyzes foreseeability and concludes negatively: "it cannot be concluded that the 'procedure' referred to in Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences was formulated with sufficient precision to enable the applicant to foresee, to a degree that was reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences of his actions." The Court points to the inconsistency of domestic court practice, the outdated 1988 Decree incompatible with the Ukrainian Constitution, and the absence of a parliamentary Act. This is explicitly discussed and answered negatively.

*Safeguards:* The Court does not explicitly discuss safeguards against abuse as a separate sub-element. However, the discussion of the 1988 Decree's incompatibility with the Constitution (e.g., requiring permission from local administration rather than court-ordered restrictions) touches on structural deficiencies that could be read as implying inadequate safeguards. This is at best implied, not explicitly addressed as a standalone sub-element.

*Overall prescribed by law:* The Court concludes: "The Court thus concludes that the interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly was not prescribed by law." — explicitly discussed, conclusion: no.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court explicitly states it does not need to examine this element: "Having reached this conclusion, the Court does not need to verify whether the other two requirements (legitimate aim and necessity of the interference) set forth in Article 11 § 2 have been complied with." Therefore, legitimate aim is not discussed and no aims are found.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Same as above — the Court explicitly declines to examine necessity: "Having reached this conclusion, the Court does not need to verify whether the other two requirements (legitimate aim and necessity of the interference) set forth in Article 11 § 2 have been complied with." Not discussed.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not frame its analysis in terms of a positive obligation. There is no discussion of a positive obligation on the part of the government. Not discussed.
