**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is found in the final paragraphs of the merits assessment:

"It concludes that the security measures implemented at the site of the demonstration at Bolotnaya Square, its early termination and the dispersal amounted to a violation of the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly."

And confirmed by: "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention in respect of the applicant."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
Explicitly discussed. The Court states: "The Court has previously held that the protest in Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Yaroslav Belousov, cited above, §§ 168-71). Noting that the present case contains no element capable of altering this conclusion, the Court adheres to that finding." Conclusion: yes.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?**
Explicitly discussed. The Court examines whether the applicant personally could rely on Article 11: "As to whether the applicant could personally rely on the provisions of Article 11..." and concludes "The Court therefore considers that she enjoyed the protection of Article 11 in the present case." Conclusion: yes.

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
Explicitly discussed. The Court addresses the peaceful nature of the applicant's participation: "peaceful participants of a protest tarnished by isolated acts of violence committed by other participants do not cease to enjoy the right to peaceful assembly... It does not appear from any of the submissions that the applicant was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression... Apart from that, the applicant's behaviour remained strictly peaceful." Conclusion: yes (as to the applicant's participation).

**(b) Interference**
The interference is implied rather than explicitly resolved as a standalone finding. The Government argued "there had been no interference with the exercise of the applicant's right to peaceful assembly," but the Court proceeds to find a violation based on the security measures, early termination, dispersal, and arrest, treating these as interferences. The Court does not explicitly state "there was an interference" as a separate step, but the entire merits analysis presupposes it, and the conclusion that these measures "amounted to a violation" implies interference was found. Conclusion: yes (implied).

**(c) Prescribed by law**
Not explicitly discussed. The Court does not separately analyze whether the measures were prescribed by law. The Government claimed the measures "had complied with domestic law," but the Court does not engage with this sub-question in its own assessment. Conclusion: null (not discussed).

- Foreseeable: not discussed.
- Accessible: not discussed.
- Safeguards: not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**
Not explicitly resolved by the Court. The Government argued the measures were necessary "for the prevention of disorder or crime" and "for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others." The Court references these aims in the context of the domestic balancing exercise that was not carried out: "no domestic balancing exercise was carried out, which would have allowed the Russian courts to weigh considerations of 'prevention of disorder' or 'protection of the rights and freedoms of others' against the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly." However, the Court does not itself make a finding on whether a legitimate aim was pursued. Conclusion: null (not explicitly resolved by the Court, though aims are mentioned in passing).

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**
Explicitly discussed, though framed through the lens of the absence of a domestic balancing exercise. The Court states: "no domestic balancing exercise was carried out, which would have allowed the Russian courts to weigh considerations of 'prevention of disorder' or 'protection of the rights and freedoms of others' against the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly." The Court also finds a "chilling effect" from the dispersal and arrest. The Court concludes the measures were not justified, finding a violation. Conclusion: no (the interference was not necessary/proportionate).

**(f) Positive obligation**
Explicitly discussed. The Court states: "The Court found in Frumkin (cited above, §§ 100-30) that the domestic authorities had failed to discharge their positive obligation to ensure the peaceful conduct of the assembly at Bolotnaya Square. The Government's submissions in this case were identical to those in Frumkin, and the Court sees no reason to reach a different conclusion on the same point." Conclusion: yes (positive obligation exists and was violated).
