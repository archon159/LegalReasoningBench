**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The key passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

'Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints. Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the measures applied in respect of the nine applicants as peaceful participants in the public assembly did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society.'

And: 'There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention in respect of all applicants, except Mr Shchurov, Mr Skorokhod, and Mr Maslov.'

The violation is found in respect of all applicants except three named individuals.

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Has there been an assembly?**
The Court explicitly addresses this: 'The Court refers to its finding that the gathering in question fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Navalnyy v. Russia [GC], nos. 29580/12 and 4 others, §§ 110-12, 15 November 2018).' This is an explicit finding that an assembly existed, by cross-reference to the leading case. The conclusion is yes.

**(ii) Did the applicant (try to) participate in the assembly?**
This is implied by the Court's statement that 'the applicants' intentions and conduct were also covered by the notion of peaceful assembly, even though the participants' initial purpose was to attend a court hearing and not to hold an assembly.' The Court treats the applicants as participants, so participation is implied as established. The conclusion is yes.

**(iii) Was the assembly peaceful?**
The Court explicitly states: 'the Court considers that in the present case the applicants' intentions and conduct were also covered by the notion of peaceful assembly.' This is an explicit finding that the assembly was peaceful. The conclusion is yes.

**(b) Interference**
The Court explicitly states: 'It finds that the applicants' arrests, transfer to the police station and the ensuing sanctions constituted an interference with the right to freedom of peaceful assembly under Article 11 § 2.' This is an explicit finding of interference. The conclusion is yes.

**(c) Prescribed by law**
The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor does it address the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Government's submissions mention that the applicants were convicted under Articles 19.3 § 1 and 20.2 § 5 of the CAO with reference to the Public Events Act, but the Court does not engage with whether these provisions satisfy the 'prescribed by law' requirement. The Court jumps directly to necessity. Given that the Court finds a violation on necessity grounds without needing to resolve the prescribed-by-law question, this element is not discussed. All three sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are likewise not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**
The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim. It proceeds directly to the necessity analysis. No legitimate aim is identified or discussed. The aims_found list is therefore empty.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**
The Court explicitly addresses this: 'the Court considers that in the instant case the measures applied in respect of the nine applicants as peaceful participants in the public assembly did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society.' This is an explicit finding that the interference was not necessary. The conclusion is no (i.e., the interference was not necessary, which contributes to the violation finding).

**(f) Positive obligation**
The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this reasoning section. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation (interference) case. The conclusion is null/not discussed.
