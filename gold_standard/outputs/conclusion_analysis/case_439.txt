**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The relevant passage is in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a breach of Article 11 of the Convention."

These two sentences together confirm the violation finding.

**2. Canonical Elements of Article 11 Doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicants participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court's statement that "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly" occurred implies that the scope conditions are satisfied — there must have been a (peaceful) assembly in which the applicants participated, otherwise there would be no freedom of assembly to interfere with. These elements are therefore implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- *scope_assembly_exists*: implied yes — the Court refers to "public assemblies" in the opening paragraph: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
- *scope_participation*: implied yes — the applicants are described as "organisers or participants of public assemblies."
- *scope_peaceful*: implied yes — the Court proceeds to analyze the interference with freedom of assembly without questioning the peaceful nature of the assemblies.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly acknowledges interference. The opening paragraph states: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." Furthermore, the Court explicitly states: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." The word "interferences" confirms that interference is found. This is explicit.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor does it address foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards against abuse. These sub-elements are not discussed at all. The Court jumps directly to the necessity analysis, relying on its prior case-law. These elements are not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim. No specific aim is identified or analyzed. This element is not_discussed, and no aims are found.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only substantive element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'." This is an explicit finding that the necessity requirement was not met (i.e., the government did not adequately balance the intensity of the intervention with the weight of the legitimate aim). The conclusion is "no" (necessity was not satisfied).

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government. This element is not_discussed.
