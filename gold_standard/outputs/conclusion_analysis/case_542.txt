**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of Section B (Merits):

"There has therefore been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention in respect of each applicant."

The Court also states in the preceding paragraph: "Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements, and was moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicants participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court's entire analysis presupposes that Article 11 is engaged — it proceeds directly to discuss interference, prescribed by law, and necessity. The complaint is described as concerning "restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of public events," which implies that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. The peaceful nature is implied by the fact that the Court applies Article 11 without excluding the applicants from its protection. These elements are therefore implied rather than explicitly discussed.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly refers to interference: "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements." This is an explicit acknowledgment that there was an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly. The nature of the interference is described earlier as "restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of public events."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly addresses the "prescribed by law" requirement: "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements." This is an explicit finding that the prescribed-by-law requirement was NOT satisfied. The Court does not separately and explicitly discuss the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards against abuse. However, by referencing the leading case of Lashmankin and Others (§§ 402-78), where those sub-elements were analyzed in detail, the Court implies that the same analysis applies. The reference to "quality of law" requirements failing is a composite finding. Given the Court's reliance on Lashmankin, the sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are implied rather than explicitly discussed individually in this text.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim. Given that the Court found the interference failed the "prescribed by law" test and the necessity test, it did not need to make a definitive finding on legitimate aim. No specific aim is identified in the reasoning text. This element is not discussed.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court explicitly addresses this: "was moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'." This is an explicit finding that the necessity requirement was NOT satisfied. The Court does not elaborate on the balancing exercise in detail, instead relying on its findings in Lashmankin and Others.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this reasoning. This element is entirely absent from the text.

**Summary of findings:**
- Scope: implied (yes) — the Court proceeds on the basis that Article 11 is engaged
- Assembly exists: implied (yes)
- Participation: implied (yes)
- Peaceful: implied (yes)
- Interference: explicit (yes)
- Prescribed by law (overall): explicit (no — quality of law requirements not met)
- Prescribed by law — foreseeable: implied (no)
- Prescribed by law — accessible: implied (no — but not individually discussed)
- Prescribed by law — safeguards: implied (no — but not individually discussed)
- Legitimate aim: not discussed (null)
- Necessity: explicit (no)
- Positive obligation: not discussed
