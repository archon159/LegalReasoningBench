**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. The key passages are:

- "Accordingly, the Court considers that in the instant case the interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11 of the Convention was disproportionate and was not necessary for preventing disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of that provision."
- "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

These appear in the final two paragraphs of the Merits section.

**2. Canonical Elements**

**(a) Scope**

*i) Assembly exists:* The Court explicitly discusses the existence of an assembly. It refers to "the demonstration," "the gathering," and describes "around seventy to eighty people" carrying banners and chanting slogans. The domestic courts had already established that "the gathering had been a peaceful one." This is explicitly discussed and affirmed.

*ii) Participation:* The Court explicitly discusses the applicants' participation: "the applicants were prosecuted - and convicted at first instance - on account of the mere fact of their participation in an unauthorised but peaceful demonstration." The interference finding also rests on "the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the demonstration." Participation is thus explicitly confirmed.

*iii) Peaceful assembly:* The Court explicitly addresses this: "on the basis of the video footage of the demonstration, the domestic courts have already established that the gathering had been a peaceful one and that neither the applicants nor any other student had resorted to any acts of violence in the course of the demonstration." Further: "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, as was the case in the present application..." The assembly is explicitly found to be peaceful.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly finds interference: "The Court considers that the gendarmes' intervention, the subsequent arrest of the applicants for participating in the demonstration, and the prolonged criminal proceedings against the applicants constitute an interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11 of the Convention."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly addresses this: "the legal basis for the interference was section 32 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act, and the interference was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention."

However, the Court does not separately discuss the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. It simply states the conclusion that the interference was prescribed by law without breaking it down into those sub-components. These sub-elements are therefore not discussed (neither explicitly nor by clear implication).

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court explicitly addresses this: "As regards a legitimate aim, the Court is prepared to accept that the interference pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder or protecting public safety."

The aims found are: prevention of disorder and public safety.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court explicitly and extensively discusses necessity. Key passages include:
- "Turning to the question of whether the interference was 'necessary in a democratic society'..."
- The Court notes the peaceful nature of the gathering, the lack of evidence of danger to public order, the prompt dispersal (just over eleven minutes), and the prosecution for mere participation in an unauthorised but peaceful demonstration.
- "the Court considers that in the instant case the interference with the applicants' rights under Article 11 of the Convention was disproportionate and was not necessary for preventing disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of that provision."

The Court concludes that necessity was NOT satisfied (i.e., the interference was not necessary).

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this judgment. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation (interference by state authorities). No positive obligation is spelled out or addressed.
