**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is found in the final two paragraphs of the reasoning section:

"Having regard to its case-law on the subject, the Court considers that in the instant case the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society."

And: "These complaints are therefore admissible and disclose a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (assembly exists, participation, peacefulness) as separate analytical steps. However, the Court implicitly affirms all three by referring to "the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies." This phrase implies: (i) there were public assemblies (assembly exists); (ii) the applicants participated in them (participation); and (iii) the assemblies were peaceful (peaceful). These are implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- scope_assembly_exists: implied yes — "public assemblies" referenced throughout
- scope_participation: implied yes — "measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies"
- scope_peaceful: implied yes — "peaceful participants in the public assemblies" and earlier "participants of peaceful public assemblies"

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not explicitly analyze interference as a separate step, but it is implied by the structure of the reasoning. The Court refers to "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of peaceful public assemblies, namely their arrest followed by their conviction for an administrative offence" and to "the measures applied to the applicants." The existence of an interference is thus implied — the arrest and conviction constitute the interference. The Court proceeds directly to necessity, which logically presupposes that interference, prescribed by law, and legitimate aim have been established (or are assumed).

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor any of its sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards). These are not_discussed. The Court jumps directly to necessity, relying on its leading case findings.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim. This element is not_discussed. No specific aim is identified in the reasoning.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the only substantive element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society." This is an explicit finding that the necessity requirement was not satisfied (i.e., the interference was not justified), leading to the violation finding.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this reasoning. This element is not_discussed.
