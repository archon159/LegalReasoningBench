**Question 1: Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. The relevant passage states: "Having examined all the material before it, the Court concludes that they also disclose violations of the Convention in the light of its findings in Frumkin v. Russia, no. 74568/12, §§ 81-142, 5 January 2016, as regards disproportionate measures taken by the authorities against organisers and participants of public assemblies, and Tsvetkova and Others v. Russia, nos. 54381/08 and 5 others, §§ 84-138, 10 April 2018, as regards unlawful administrative arrest."

The violation is found by reference to well-established case-law, specifically Frumkin v. Russia (disproportionate measures against organisers and participants of public assemblies) and Tsvetkova and Others v. Russia (unlawful administrative arrest). No specific paragraph numbers are given in this excerpt beyond the general section heading "III. OTHER ALLEGED VIOLATIONS UNDER WELL-ESTABLISHED CASE-LAW."

**Question 2: Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope:**
The scope is not explicitly discussed in this excerpt. However, the reference to "organisers and participants of public assemblies" implies that there was an assembly, that the applicants participated (or tried to), and that the assembly was peaceful enough to fall within Article 11 protection. These elements are implied by the Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated.

- Assembly exists: implied (reference to "public assemblies")
- Participation: implied (reference to "organisers and participants")
- Peaceful: implied (the Court finds a violation, which presupposes the assembly was peaceful)

**(b) Interference:**
Not explicitly discussed in this excerpt, but implied. The Court references "disproportionate measures taken by the authorities against organisers and participants" and "unlawful administrative arrest," both of which constitute interferences with the right to freedom of assembly. The finding of a violation implies that an interference was established.

**(c) Prescribed by law:**
Not explicitly discussed. The reference to "unlawful administrative arrest" (from Tsvetkova) implies that the interference was not lawful, suggesting the "prescribed by law" requirement was not met. However, the sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not individually discussed. The unlawfulness finding is implied.

**(d) Legitimate aim:**
Not explicitly discussed in this excerpt. No aims are identified.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society:**
Not explicitly discussed as a standalone element, but the reference to "disproportionate measures" (from Frumkin) implies that the necessity/proportionality test was addressed and found to be violated — i.e., the measures were not necessary in a democratic society. This is implied rather than explicit in this excerpt.

**(f) Positive obligation:**
Not discussed at all in this excerpt.

**Summary:**
The reasoning is extremely compressed, relying entirely on cross-references to Frumkin and Tsvetkova. The only elements that can be inferred are: scope (implied), interference (implied), prescribed by law (implied as not satisfied, via "unlawful"), and necessity (implied as not satisfied, via "disproportionate"). No element is explicitly discussed with independent reasoning in this excerpt.
