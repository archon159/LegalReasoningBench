**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is established in the final two paragraphs of the merits section:

- "In the light of the foregoing, the Court considers that the intervention of the police officers in the demonstration was disproportionate and not necessary for preventing disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention."
- "There has accordingly been a violation of that provision."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

*i) Assembly exists:* The Court explicitly describes the factual situation: "fifty to sixty persons, including the applicants, had gathered in a square in Istanbul to protest against the invasion of Iraq." This constitutes an explicit (if brief) acknowledgment that an assembly took place. The Court proceeds to analyze Article 11 without questioning whether an assembly existed, implying it accepted this as given.

*ii) Participation:* The Court notes that the applicants were among those gathered and were arrested during the demonstration. The Court states "the applicants' arrest and the use of force against two of the applicants constituted an interference with their rights under that provision," implying their participation is accepted. This is implied rather than explicitly discussed as a separate sub-element.

*iii) Peaceful assembly:* The Court explicitly addresses this: "the Government have not sought to argue that the applicants or any of the other demonstrators had presented a danger to public order or engaged in acts of violence." Further: "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, as was the case in the present application..." This is an explicit finding that the assembly was peaceful.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference: "The Government did not dispute that there had been an interference with the applicants' enjoyment of their rights under Article 11 of the Convention. In any event, the Court considers that the applicants' arrest and the use of force against two of the applicants constituted an interference with their rights under that provision." Conclusion: yes, there was an interference.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly addresses this: "the Government submitted that the interference had a legal basis, namely the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act, and was thus 'prescribed by law' within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention... The Court agrees with these submissions." The Court accepts the prescribed-by-law requirement is satisfied, but does so briefly without separately analyzing foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are not individually discussed — the Court simply agrees with the Government's submission that the legal basis exists. These sub-elements are therefore not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court explicitly discusses legitimate aim: "As regards a legitimate aim, the Government submitted that the interference pursued the legitimate aim of preventing public disorder. The Court agrees with these submissions." The aim found is prevention of disorder (and public safety is mentioned in the quoted Article 11 text, but the Court specifically agrees with 'preventing public disorder'). Conclusion: yes, legitimate aim established — prevention of disorder.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court explicitly and extensively discusses necessity. It notes the Bakırköy Assize Court found the applicants had not breached the law and had exercised their democratic rights. It notes the Government did not argue the demonstrators presented a danger or engaged in violence. It reiterates the tolerance principle: "where demonstrators do not engage in acts of violence, as was the case in the present application, it is important for the public authorities to show a certain degree of tolerance towards peaceful gatherings if the freedom of assembly guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention is not to be deprived of all substance." It concludes: "the intervention of the police officers in the demonstration was disproportionate and not necessary for preventing disorder within the meaning of the second paragraph of Article 11 of the Convention." Conclusion: no, the interference was not necessary in a democratic society.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this judgment. The case is analyzed entirely through the lens of a negative obligation (unjustified interference). Conclusion: not discussed.
