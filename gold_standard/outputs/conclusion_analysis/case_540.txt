**Question 1: Has Article 11 been violated?**

Yes, Article 11 has been violated in two distinct ways, both found in the reasoning section:

1. **Negative obligations (interference):** "the Court finds a breach of the applicants' right to liberty under Article 5 § 1 of the Convention and a violation of the State's negative obligations under Article 11 taken alone (see Berkman, cited above, §§ 32-38 and 59-62)." This concerns Mr Fedorov, Mr Prokopenko and Ms Levina, who were apprehended and detained, preventing them from participating in the public event.

2. **Positive obligations:** "the Court finds that the domestic authorities failed to comply with their positive obligations under Article 11 of the Convention taken alone and read in the light of Article 14 (see Berkman, cited above, §§ 45-58, and Women's Initiatives Supporting Group and Others, cited above, §§ 79-84)." This concerns all eight applicants (Mr Romanov, Mr Lebedev, Mr Nasonov, Mr Starov, Ms Pitenova, Mr Fedorov, Mr Prokopenko and Ms Levina), who complained that authorities failed to duly facilitate the demonstrations by deterring homophobic verbal attacks and physical violence by counter-demonstrators.

**Question 2: Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The scope is not explicitly discussed in the reasoning text, but it is implied. The Court proceeds to find violations of Article 11, which logically presupposes that: (i) there was an assembly (the text refers to "planned demonstrations" and a "public event"); (ii) the applicants participated or tried to participate (they were prevented from participating, or were present at demonstrations); and (iii) the assembly was peaceful (the Court applies Article 11 without excluding the applicants from its protection on grounds of violence). These are all implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- **Assembly exists:** Implied — the text refers to "the planned demonstrations" and "the public event," and the Court applies Article 11 without questioning whether an assembly existed.
- **Participation:** Implied — the applicants complained they were "prevented from participating in the public event" and that authorities failed to facilitate "the conduct of the planned demonstrations." The Court's finding of a violation implies participation or attempted participation.
- **Peaceful:** Implied — the Court applies Article 11 protection without any suggestion that the applicants' assembly was not peaceful. The violence came from counter-demonstrators, not the applicants.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed. The Court finds "a violation of the State's negative obligations under Article 11 taken alone" in relation to the apprehension and detention of Mr Fedorov, Mr Prokopenko and Ms Levina, which "prevented them from participating in the public event." This constitutes an explicit finding of interference with the right to freedom of assembly.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Not discussed. The reasoning text does not address whether the interference was prescribed by law, nor its foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. The Court proceeds by reference to its well-established case-law (Berkman) without separately analyzing these sub-elements.

- **Foreseeable:** Not discussed.
- **Accessible:** Not discussed.
- **Safeguards:** Not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. The Court does not identify or analyze any legitimate aim pursued by the interference. It proceeds directly to a finding of violation by reference to established case-law.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed explicitly. The Court does not engage in a proportionality or necessity analysis in the text provided. It relies on its well-established case-law (Berkman) without spelling out the necessity test.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Explicitly discussed. The Court explicitly finds: "the domestic authorities failed to comply with their positive obligations under Article 11 of the Convention taken alone and read in the light of Article 14 (see Berkman, cited above, §§ 45-58, and Women's Initiatives Supporting Group and Others, cited above, §§ 79-84)." The positive obligation concerned the duty to duly facilitate the conduct of the planned demonstrations by deterring homophobic verbal attacks and physical violence by counter-demonstrators. The Court concludes that this positive obligation was violated.

**Summary of findings:**
- Article 11 is violated (both negative and positive obligations).
- Scope is implied (assembly, participation, peaceful nature all implied).
- Interference is explicitly found (negative obligations violated).
- Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, and necessity are not discussed (the Court relies on established case-law by reference).
- Positive obligation is explicitly found to have been violated.
