**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final two sentences of the merits section: 'Thus, the measure was not necessary in a democratic society. Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention.'

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicant participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, these elements are implied by the Court's acceptance that there was an interference with 'the applicant's right to freedom of assembly' — which logically presupposes that the situation falls within the scope of Article 11 (i.e., there was a planned assembly, the applicant was seeking to organise/participate in it, and it was peaceful in nature). The Court states: 'The Court observes that it is not in dispute between the parties that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly, and it has no reason to hold otherwise.' This implies scope is satisfied.

- **scope_assembly_exists**: Implied as yes. The applicant sought to organise a demonstration, which constitutes an assembly. Not explicitly discussed as a separate question.
- **scope_participation**: Implied as yes. The applicant was the organiser seeking to hold the demonstration. Not explicitly discussed.
- **scope_peaceful**: Implied as yes. The assembly is treated throughout as a peaceful demonstration; no suggestion of violence. Not explicitly discussed.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed and confirmed: 'The Court observes that it is not in dispute between the parties that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly, and it has no reason to hold otherwise.' The interference was the refusal of the application to organise a demonstration.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed but only at the level of the overall conclusion, without sub-element analysis: 'The Court further notes that it is not in dispute between the parties either that the interference was prescribed by law, namely, by section 8(1) of the Assembly Act.' The Court accepts this without further analysis. The sub-elements (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not individually discussed.

- **prescribed_by_law_foreseeable**: Not discussed explicitly or implicitly as a sub-element.
- **prescribed_by_law_accessible**: Not discussed explicitly or implicitly as a sub-element.
- **prescribed_by_law_safeguards**: Not discussed explicitly or implicitly as a sub-element.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Explicitly discussed and confirmed: 'The Court further notes that it is not in dispute between the parties either that the interference was prescribed by law...and that it pursued the legitimate aims of public safety and the rights and freedoms of others. Again, it has no reason to hold otherwise.' The Government had also argued the aim of 'securing the unimpeded operation of the representative bodies of the people (that is, public safety and the protection of the rights and freedoms of others).'

Aims found: public_safety and rights_of_others.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Explicitly and extensively discussed. The Court finds that the interference was NOT necessary in a democratic society. Key passages:
- 'It remains to be ascertained whether the interference was necessary in a democratic society.'
- The Court applies the 'relevant and sufficient reasons' and proportionality test.
- 'the Court finds unconvincing the Government's argument that the restriction on the applicant's rights was necessitated by the requirement to secure the unimpeded work and movement of the MPs. This consideration cannot be regarded as a relevant or sufficient reason, especially since on the date of the event planned by the applicant no parliamentary activity was underway.'
- 'the Court cannot but conclude that the prohibition of the demonstration did not respond to a pressing social need...Thus, the measure was not necessary in a democratic society.'

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government. The case is framed entirely as a negative interference case. Not discussed.
