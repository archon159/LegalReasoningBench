**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court finds two separate violations of Article 11 ECHR in this case.

First violation (positive obligation / conduct of assembly): "The authorities have thus failed to discharge their positive obligation in respect of the conduct of the assembly at Bolotnaya Square. There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention on that account."

Second violation (applicant's arrest, detention and charges): "There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention on account of the applicant's arrest, pre-trial detention and administrative penalty."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly treats the event at Bolotnaya Square as a public assembly throughout the judgment. The applicant "alleged a violation of his right to peaceful assembly" and the Court proceeds on the basis that a public assembly took place on 6 May 2012. This is explicit from the outset: "The applicant alleged a violation of his right to peaceful assembly. He complained in particular of disruptive security measures implemented at the site of the meeting at Bolotnaya Square, the early termination of the assembly, and his own arrest followed by his conviction for an administrative offence." The existence of an assembly is not contested and is treated as given.

**(ii) Participation:** The Court explicitly discusses the applicant's participation: "He arrived at the site of the public event with the intention of taking part in the meeting; however, this became impossible because the meeting was disrupted and then cancelled, and the main speakers were arrested." And further: "the applicant remained within the perimeter of the cordoned meeting venue and that his behaviour remained, by all accounts, strictly peaceful."

**(iii) Peaceful character:** The Court explicitly discusses whether the assembly was peaceful, both generally and with respect to the applicant personally. On the general assembly: "The Government submitted that the intervention of the police had been justified since the assembly had ceased to be 'peaceful' within the meaning of Article 11 of the Convention." The Court notes that "the sit-in was strictly peaceful" and that "the area within the cordoned perimeter of the meeting venue at Bolotnaya Embankment remained strictly peaceful for the whole duration, even during the disorder outside that perimeter, at Malyy Kamennyy Bridge." As to the applicant personally: "the Court finds that the applicant remained within the perimeter of the cordoned meeting venue and that his behaviour remained, by all accounts, strictly peaceful. Moreover, it does not appear from any submissions that he was among those who manifested even 'passive resistance'."

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference. The Government argued "there had been no interference with the exercise of the applicant's right to peaceful assembly," which the Court implicitly rejects by finding violations. More explicitly, the Court notes: "applying security measures in the course of a public assembly constitutes, on the one hand, a restriction on the exercise of the right to freedom of assembly" and discusses the termination of the assembly, the applicant's arrest, pre-trial detention and administrative conviction as measures affecting his Article 11 rights. The Court also states: "even after the assembly was officially terminated, the guarantees of Article 11 continued to apply in respect of the applicant" and "any measures taken against him in the given situation had to have complied with the law, pursued a legitimate aim and been necessary in a democratic society within the meaning of Article 11 § 2 of the Convention." This implies an affirmative finding of interference.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court does not explicitly analyze the "prescribed by law" requirement in a structured way. It briefly references domestic law (section 15.3 and 17.3 of the Public Events Act) in the context of the termination procedure: "It does not appear that the assembly was suspended before being terminated, as required by section 15.3 of the Public Events Act." However, the Court does not conduct a full "prescribed by law" analysis with sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, and safeguards. The Government argued the measures "complied with domestic law" but the Court does not make an explicit finding on this element, instead proceeding directly to proportionality/necessity. The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court briefly addresses legitimate aim in the context of the applicant's arrest. It states: "even assuming that the applicant's arrest, pre-trial detention and administrative sentence complied with domestic law and pursued one of the legitimate aims enumerated in Article 11 § 2 of the Convention – presumably, public safety – the measures taken against him were grossly disproportionate to the aim pursued." The Government also argued the measures were "necessary 'for the prevention of disorder or crime' and 'for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others'." The Court does not make a definitive finding that a legitimate aim was established; it proceeds on an assumption for the sake of argument. The aims referenced are public safety (assumed), prevention of disorder, and protection of rights of others.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court explicitly discusses necessity/proportionality, particularly regarding the applicant's arrest. It finds: "the severity of the measures applied against the applicant is entirely devoid of any justification" and "even assuming that the applicant's arrest, pre-trial detention and administrative sentence complied with domestic law and pursued one of the legitimate aims enumerated in Article 11 § 2 of the Convention – presumably, public safety – the measures taken against him were grossly disproportionate to the aim pursued. There was no 'pressing social need' to arrest the applicant and to escort him to the police station. There was especially no need to sentence him to a prison term, albeit a short one." The Court also discusses the chilling effect: "the arrest, detention and ensuing administrative conviction of the applicant could not have failed to have the effect of discouraging him and others from participating in protest rallies." Regarding the termination of the assembly, the Court finds it was not convinced "that the termination of the meeting at Bolotnaya Square was inevitable" and that the authorities had not shown they attempted to separate the turbulent sector. The necessity element is found not satisfied.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court explicitly and extensively discusses positive obligations. It states in the general principles: "there may be positive obligations to secure the effective enjoyment of this right" and "The States have a duty to take reasonable and appropriate measures with regard to lawful demonstrations to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens." In the application section, the Court frames the first issue as: "whether the authorities took all reasonable measures to ensure that the meeting at Bolotnaya Square was conducted peacefully." It concludes: "the authorities made insufficient efforts to communicate with the assembly organisers to resolve the tension caused by the confusion regarding the venue layout. The failure to take simple and obvious steps at the first signs of the conflict allowed it to escalate, leading to the disruption of the previously peaceful assembly." And: "the authorities in this case did not comply with even the minimum requirements in their duty to communicate with the assembly leaders, which was an essential part of their positive obligation to ensure the peaceful conduct of the assembly, to prevent disorder and to secure the safety of all the citizens involved." The Court concludes: "The authorities have thus failed to discharge their positive obligation in respect of the conduct of the assembly at Bolotnaya Square. There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention on that account." The positive obligation is found to have been violated.
