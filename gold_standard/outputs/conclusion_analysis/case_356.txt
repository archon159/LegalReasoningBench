**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of the assessment section: "In these circumstances, the Court finds a violation of Article 11 of the Convention." The reasoning leading to this conclusion is found in the paragraphs discussing necessity: "the Court concludes that the authorities in the present cases have not adduced relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the protest" and "The authorities also failed to acknowledge that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention." Additionally, the Court notes the chilling effect of the sanctions: "The measures applied in the present cases, and the fear of sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies, undoubtedly had a chilling effect on the exercise of freedom of assembly."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

*i) Assembly exists:* The Court implicitly treats the gathering near the Baku Court of Serious Crimes on 6 May 2014 as an assembly. This is implied throughout the reasoning, particularly where the Court states: "the Court is ready to accept that that protest was an immediate response of the people who had gathered near the Baku Court of Serious Crimes to the judgment of that court against certain Nida members." The existence of an assembly is never questioned — it is treated as a given. The Government also refers to it as an "assembly" in their submissions. So scope_assembly_exists is implied (yes).

*ii) Participation:* The applicants' participation is also implied throughout. The Court notes "the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11" and the applicants were arrested and convicted in connection with the protest. Their participation is never in dispute. So scope_participation is implied (yes).

*iii) Peaceful:* The Court explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assembly. The applicants argued "the protest had been held in a peaceful manner." The Court itself refers to "unauthorised peaceful assembly" multiple times, e.g.: "the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention" and "the fear of sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies." The Court also dismisses the Government's argument that the first applicant insulted public authorities: "the Court dismisses the Government's argument that the arrest of the first applicant was necessitated by the fact that she had insulted public authorities during the protest." This confirms the assembly was peaceful. So scope_peaceful is explicitly discussed (yes).

**(b) Interference**

Interference is implied throughout the judgment. The Court discusses "the dispersal of the protest by the police and their arrest and conviction for an administrative offence" as the subject of the complaint, and the entire analysis proceeds on the basis that there was an interference. The Court explicitly states: "the dispersal of the assembly of 6 May 2014 and the applicants' conviction" and "The dispersal of the assembly and the applicants' arrest and conviction could not but have the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies." Interference is thus implied as clearly established (yes).

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The Court explicitly discusses the lawfulness of the interference: "the Court has doubts as to whether the dispersal of the assembly of 6 May 2014 was prescribed by law." The Court notes that the protest was spontaneous and that under Article 5 § IV of the Law on Freedom of Assembly, no prior written notification was required for "spontaneous assemblies." However, the Court declines to resolve this question definitively: "given that a more conspicuous problem arises with respect to the necessity of the interference, the Court considers that it is not appropriate to limit its examination under Article 11 of the Convention to only the lawfulness of the interference." So prescribed_by_law is explicitly discussed but left unresolved (null conclusion).

*Sub-elements of prescribed by law:* The Court's discussion of lawfulness focuses on whether the legal basis existed for dispersing a spontaneous assembly (touching on foreseeability — whether the law clearly required notification for spontaneous assemblies). However, the Court does not separately analyze accessibility or safeguards. The foreseeability sub-element is implicitly touched upon (the Court notes the law itself exempted spontaneous assemblies from notification requirements, casting doubt on whether dispersal was lawful/foreseeable), but the Court does not reach a firm conclusion. Accessibility and safeguards are not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Government argued that the dispersal "had been necessary in the interests of national security, for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others and the prevention of disorder or crime." The Court does not explicitly accept or reject these aims — it proceeds directly to the necessity analysis without formally finding a legitimate aim. The legitimate aim question is thus not explicitly resolved by the Court; it is effectively bypassed in favor of the necessity analysis. The aims are mentioned in the parties' submissions but not adopted by the Court. So legitimate_aim is not_discussed as a formal finding (null conclusion), though the Government's claimed aims (national security, rights of others, prevention of disorder, prevention of crime) are mentioned in submissions.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

This is the central element explicitly discussed. The Court states: "Turning to the question of whether the dispersal of the assembly of 6 May 2014 and the applicants' conviction were necessary in a democratic society..." It concludes: "the authorities in the present cases have not adduced relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the protest" and "The authorities also failed to acknowledge that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful assembly was by itself protected by Article 11 of the Convention." The Court also notes the chilling effect of the sanctions (30 and 20 days of administrative detention). Necessity is explicitly discussed and found not satisfied (no — i.e., the interference was not necessary).

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation of the state in this judgment. The case is analyzed entirely through the lens of negative obligations (unjustified interference). So positive_obligation is not_discussed (null).
