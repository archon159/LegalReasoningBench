**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court explicitly concludes that Article 11 has been violated. This is found in the final paragraph of the Court's assessment section: 'There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention.' The Court reaches this conclusion by reference to its prior judgments in Gafgaz Mammadov and Huseynli and Others, finding 'that in the present cases the applicants' right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined in the said judgments.'

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

**(i) Assembly exists:** The Court explicitly discusses the existence of assemblies. The text refers to 'the dispersal of the demonstrations of 2 April and 17 April 2011' and 'the assemblies of 18 October 2011 and 20 October 2012,' treating these as assemblies without question. This is implied rather than explicitly analyzed as a doctrinal step.

**(ii) Participation:** The applicants' participation is implied throughout — the Court discusses their arrests at or in connection with the demonstrations, and the first and fourth applicants' arrests 'prior to the assemblies' were measures 'to prevent them from participating in opposition protests.' Participation is implied rather than explicitly analyzed as a doctrinal step.

**(iii) Peaceful assembly:** The Court explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assembly: 'The Court notes from the outset that none of the official records or the domestic court decisions submitted by the parties suggests that the demonstration of 2 April 2011 was violent. The Court therefore accepts the second applicant's assertion that that demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up until his arrest.' This is an explicit discussion of the peaceful sub-element, at least for the 2 April 2011 demonstration.

**(b) Interference:** The Court implies interference by discussing the dispersal of demonstrations, arrests, and convictions for administrative offences. The Court states: 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies.' This is explicit as to the chilling effect, and the interference is clearly implied throughout the reasoning, though not analyzed as a separate doctrinal step.

**(c) Prescribed by law:** The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference was prescribed by law in this excerpt. It refers to findings in Gafgaz Mammadov (§§ 50-68) and Huseynli and Others (§§ 84-101) by incorporation, but does not set out the analysis here. The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are not discussed in this text.

**(d) Legitimate aim:** The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference served a legitimate aim in this excerpt. Again, it incorporates findings from prior judgments by reference but does not spell out the analysis here.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society:** The Court does not explicitly analyze necessity in this excerpt. However, it does note the chilling effect of the arrests and administrative proceedings: 'Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.' This language is consistent with a necessity/proportionality analysis but is not framed as such explicitly. The Court relies on its findings in the referenced judgments for the full analysis.

**(f) Positive obligation:** The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this excerpt. The case is framed entirely as one of negative interference by the state.

**Summary of findings by element:**

- **Scope (overall):** Implied as satisfied — the Court proceeds on the basis that Article 11 is engaged.
- **Assembly exists:** Implied — demonstrations are treated as assemblies without question.
- **Participation:** Implied — applicants' participation or attempted participation is assumed throughout.
- **Peaceful:** Explicitly discussed for the 2 April 2011 demonstration; concluded yes.
- **Interference:** Implied — dispersals, arrests, and convictions are treated as interferences.
- **Prescribed by law:** Not discussed in this excerpt (incorporated by reference to prior cases).
- **Prescribed by law — foreseeable:** Not discussed.
- **Prescribed by law — accessible:** Not discussed.
- **Prescribed by law — safeguards:** Not discussed.
- **Legitimate aim:** Not discussed in this excerpt.
- **Necessity:** Not explicitly discussed; chilling effect language implies disproportionality, but full analysis is by reference to prior judgments.
- **Positive obligation:** Not discussed.
