**Question 1: Does the Court find a violation of Article 11 ECHR?**

Yes. The Court finds a violation of Article 11 by the Russian Federation, but no violation by the Republic of Moldova.

On the violation finding: "In so far as the lawfulness of the above interference is concerned, no elements in the present case allow the Court to consider that there was a legal basis for limiting the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly. This being so, the Court concludes that the interference in question was not lawful under domestic law. Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

On Russia's responsibility: "In conclusion, and after having found that the applicant's rights guaranteed by Article 11 have been breached (see paragraph 31 above), the Court holds that there has been a violation of that provision by the Russian Federation."

On Moldova's positive obligation: "In the light of the foregoing, the Court concludes that the Republic of Moldova did not fail to fulfil its positive obligations in respect of the applicant. There has therefore been no violation of Article 11 of the Convention by the Republic of Moldova."

So overall, Article 11 is violated (by Russia), but not by Moldova.

**Question 2: Analysis of canonical elements**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicant participated, or whether it was peaceful. However, the Court's discussion of interference implies that an assembly existed and that the applicant participated in it (as organiser). The Court states: "the decision to sanction the applicant with a fine for his organising of a public meeting on 21 September 2012 amounted to an 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11." The reference to a "public meeting" and the applicant's role as organiser implies the existence of an assembly and participation. The peaceful nature of the assembly is not discussed but is implied by the Court's framing of the right as "freedom of peaceful assembly" without any suggestion of violence.

- scope_assembly_exists: implied (yes) — the Court refers to "a public meeting on 21 September 2012" without questioning whether it constitutes an assembly.
- scope_participation: implied (yes) — the applicant is described as "organising" the meeting, which implies participation.
- scope_peaceful: implied (yes) — the Court frames the right as "freedom of peaceful assembly" and there is no suggestion the assembly was not peaceful.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed and confirmed: "It is undisputed between the parties, and the Court agrees, that the decision to sanction the applicant with a fine for his organising of a public meeting on 21 September 2012 amounted to an 'interference by [a] public authority' with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly under the first paragraph of Article 11."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed. The Court finds the interference was NOT prescribed by law: "In so far as the lawfulness of the above interference is concerned, no elements in the present case allow the Court to consider that there was a legal basis for limiting the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly. This being so, the Court concludes that the interference in question was not lawful under domestic law."

The Court does not separately discuss the sub-elements of foreseeability, accessibility, or safeguards. It simply concludes there was no legal basis at all, making the overall "prescribed by law" test fail. The sub-elements (foreseeable, accessible, safeguards) are not individually discussed — the Court's conclusion that there was no legal basis at all subsumes them, but they are not explicitly addressed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Not discussed. Because the Court found the interference was not prescribed by law, it did not proceed to examine whether it served a legitimate aim.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed. The Court stopped its analysis at the "prescribed by law" stage and did not reach necessity.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Explicitly discussed in relation to Moldova: "The Court must next determine whether the Republic of Moldova fulfilled its positive obligation to take appropriate and sufficient measures to secure the applicant's rights." The Court found Moldova did NOT violate its positive obligation: "the Court concludes that the Republic of Moldova did not fail to fulfil its positive obligations in respect of the applicant. There has therefore been no violation of Article 11 of the Convention by the Republic of Moldova." The conclusion on positive obligation is therefore "no violation" (i.e., the positive obligation was fulfilled / not breached), which I code as "yes" (the positive obligation was discussed and the conclusion is that it was not violated — meaning the state complied). However, since the question asks whether the positive obligation was violated, the answer is "no" (Moldova did not violate it). I will code this as conclusion: "no" (the positive obligation was not violated by Moldova).
