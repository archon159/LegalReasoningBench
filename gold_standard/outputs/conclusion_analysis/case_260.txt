**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of Section B.3 (Necessity in a democratic society): "In short, the sanction complained of, however minimal, does not appear to have been 'necessary in a democratic society'. It accordingly contravened Article 11 (art. 11)."

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

*i) Assembly exists:* The Court explicitly discusses the existence of an assembly (a demonstration/procession). In Section A: "prior notice had been given of the demonstration in question and that it was not prohibited." The procession of 12 February 1983 is treated throughout as an assembly. This is explicitly discussed.

*ii) Participation:* The Court explicitly discusses the applicant's participation: "In joining it, the applicant availed himself of his freedom of peaceful assembly." (Section A). This is explicitly discussed and confirmed.

*iii) Peaceful assembly:* The Court explicitly addresses this. In Section A: "In the Commission's opinion, no intentions that were not peaceful could be imputed to the applicant." The Court then notes: "neither the report made by the Chief Superintendent of the Basse-Terre police nor any other evidence shows that Mr Ezelin himself made threats or daubed graffiti." The Court also states in Section B.3: "the freedom to take part in a peaceful assembly - in this instance a demonstration that had not been prohibited - is of such importance that it cannot be restricted in any way, even for an avocat, so long as the person concerned does not himself commit any reprehensible act on such an occasion." The assembly is treated as peaceful from the applicant's perspective. Explicitly discussed.

**(b) Interference**

Explicitly discussed in Section A. The Court rejects the Government's argument that no interference occurred because the sanction came after the event: "The term 'restrictions' in paragraph 2 of Article 11 (art. 11-2) ... cannot be interpreted as not including measures - such as punitive measures - taken not before or during but after a meeting." The Court concludes: "The Court accordingly finds that there was in this instance an interference with the exercise of the applicant's freedom of peaceful assembly." Conclusion: yes, interference found.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

Explicitly discussed in Section B.1. The Court applies its standard: "a norm cannot be regarded as a 'law' unless it is formulated with sufficient precision to enable the citizen - if need be, with appropriate advice - to foresee, to a degree that is reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences which a given action may entail." The Court finds: "In the instant case the legal basis of the sanction complained of lay solely in the special rules governing the profession of avocat. Article 106 of the relevant Decree of 9 June 1972 provides unequivocally that any avocat, even in his non-professional activities, has special obligations ... That being so, the interference was 'prescribed by law'."

*i) Foreseeable:* The Court's discussion of precision and foreseeability is explicit: "a norm cannot be regarded as a 'law' unless it is formulated with sufficient precision to enable the citizen ... to foresee, to a degree that is reasonable in the circumstances, the consequences which a given action may entail." The Court finds the provision sufficiently precise. Conclusion: yes, explicitly discussed.

*ii) Accessible:* The Court does not explicitly discuss accessibility as a separate sub-element. It is implied by the overall finding that the interference was "prescribed by law," but accessibility is not separately analyzed. Conclusion: null, not_discussed (or implied).

*iii) Safeguards against abuse:* The Court does not discuss safeguards against abuse as a separate sub-element. Conclusion: null, not_discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Explicitly discussed in Section B.2. The Government argued the aim was "prevention of disorder." The Court accepts this: "The interference was therefore in pursuit of a legitimate aim, the 'prevention of disorder'." Aims found: prevention_of_disorder.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Explicitly discussed in Section B.3. The Court examines proportionality: "The Court has examined the disciplinary sanction in question in the light of the case as a whole in order to determine in particular whether it was proportionate to the legitimate aim pursued, having regard to the special importance of freedom of peaceful assembly and freedom of expression." The Court finds the sanction disproportionate: "the freedom to take part in a peaceful assembly ... is of such importance that it cannot be restricted in any way, even for an avocat, so long as the person concerned does not himself commit any reprehensible act on such an occasion." Conclusion: the interference was NOT necessary in a democratic society (i.e., the necessity test was not satisfied). The answer to "did government adequately balance" is no.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation on the part of the government in this judgment. The case is framed entirely as a negative interference case. Conclusion: not discussed.
