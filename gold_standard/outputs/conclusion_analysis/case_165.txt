**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

Yes, the Court concludes that Article 11 ECHR has been violated. This is stated in the final paragraph of the reasoning section: "Accordingly, there has been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention."

**2. Analysis of canonical elements:**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether there was an assembly, whether the applicant participated, or whether the assembly was peaceful. However, the Court implicitly accepts all three sub-elements by proceeding to analyze the interference and the prescribed-by-law question. Additionally, the admissibility section notes that "the applicant was arrested, detained on remand and subsequently convicted of having attended a demonstration," which implies that an assembly existed and that the applicant participated in it. The peaceful nature of the assembly is not discussed but is implied by the Court's willingness to proceed under Article 11 without any caveat about violence.

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not explicitly discuss interference as a separate element, but it is clearly implied. The reasoning states that "the applicant was arrested, detained on remand and subsequently convicted of having attended a demonstration," and the Court refers to "the interference resulting from the application of Article 220 § 6." This phrase explicitly acknowledges that there was an interference, even if the Court does not separately analyze this element in a structured way. The relevant quote is: "Hence, the interference resulting from the application of Article 220 § 6 was not prescribed by law."

**(c) Prescribed by law**

This is the central element explicitly discussed. The Court focuses on the foreseeability sub-element: "the foreseeability of Article 220 § 6 has been examined in a similar case (see, Işıkırık, cited above, §§ 56-70), where the Court concluded that the aforementioned provision was not 'foreseeable' in its application since it did not afford the applicant the legal protection against arbitrary interference with his right under Article 11 of the Convention." The Court then concludes: "Hence, the interference resulting from the application of Article 220 § 6 was not prescribed by law."

- **Foreseeable:** Explicitly discussed and concluded negatively (not foreseeable). Quote: "the Court concluded that the aforementioned provision was not 'foreseeable' in its application since it did not afford the applicant the legal protection against arbitrary interference with his right under Article 11."

- **Accessible:** Not explicitly discussed. The Court's overall conclusion that the measure was not prescribed by law could imply this, but the reasoning focuses solely on foreseeability. Marked as not discussed.

- **Safeguards:** The Court references the lack of "legal protection against arbitrary interference," which touches on the safeguards sub-element. The quote "it did not afford the applicant the legal protection against arbitrary interference" implies that safeguards were insufficient, but this is not separately analyzed as a distinct sub-element. It is implied rather than explicit.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not discuss legitimate aim at all. Since it found the interference was not prescribed by law, it did not need to proceed to this element.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

Not discussed. The Court stopped its analysis at the prescribed-by-law stage.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Not discussed. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (state interference with the right), and no positive obligation is identified or analyzed.
