**Question 1: Violation of Article 11**

The Court explicitly concludes that Article 11 has been violated. This is found in two passages:

1. From the assessment section: 'the Court sees no particular circumstances that could compel it to deviate from its findings in those judgments, and finds that in the present cases the applicants' right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined in the said judgments (see Gafgaz Mammadov, cited above, §§ 50-68, and Bayramov, cited above, §§ 44-47).'

2. The formal conclusion: 'There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention.'

**Question 2: Canonical Elements**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (existence of assembly, participation, peacefulness) in the text provided. However, the Court's reference to 'demonstrations' and 'political rallies' and the fact that it proceeds to analyze Article 11 implies that the assembly existed, that the applicants participated, and that the assembly was peaceful. These are implied by the Court's willingness to proceed with the Article 11 analysis and its reference to the applicants' 'right to freedom of assembly.' The Court states: 'the applicants' right to freedom of assembly was breached' — which implies all scope elements are satisfied. The reference to 'dispersal of the demonstrations' and 'participating in political rallies' implies peaceful assemblies existed and the applicants participated.

- **scope_assembly_exists**: Implied — the Court refers to 'demonstrations' and 'political rallies' throughout, and proceeds with the Article 11 analysis.
- **scope_participation**: Implied — the Court refers to 'the applicants' arrests' at the demonstrations and their 'participating in political rallies.'
- **scope_peaceful**: Implied — the Court applies Article 11 (which protects only peaceful assembly) without any suggestion that the assemblies were violent.

**(b) Interference**

The Court explicitly discusses interference. It states: 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations.' The dispersal of demonstrations, arrests, and convictions for administrative offences are identified as the interfering acts. This is explicit.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The sub-elements of 'prescribed by law' (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not explicitly discussed in the text provided. The Court refers to findings in Gafgaz Mammadov (§§ 50-68) and Bayramov (§§ 44-47) by incorporation, but does not spell out these sub-elements in the reasoning excerpt provided. These are not discussed in the text given — the Court simply incorporates by reference the findings from prior cases. Since the Court finds a violation, it is implied that the 'prescribed by law' requirement was either not satisfied or that the necessity test failed, but the specific sub-elements are not discussed in this text.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

Legitimate aim is not explicitly discussed in the text provided. The Court incorporates findings from Gafgaz Mammadov and Bayramov by reference but does not spell out any legitimate aim analysis in this excerpt.

**(e) Necessity**

Necessity is not explicitly discussed in the text provided as a standalone element. However, the Court's reference to the 'chilling effect' of the arrests and administrative proceedings — 'Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate' — touches on the proportionality/necessity analysis implicitly. The Court incorporates by reference the full analysis from Gafgaz Mammadov §§ 50-68 and Bayramov §§ 44-47, which presumably contain the necessity analysis. In the text provided, necessity is implied rather than explicitly analyzed.

**(f) Positive obligation**

Positive obligations are not discussed in the text provided. The case is framed entirely as a negative obligation case (state interference through dispersal, arrests, and convictions).
