**1. Violation of Article 11 ECHR**

The Court explicitly concludes that Article 11 has been violated. This is found in the final paragraph of the Court's assessment section: 'There has accordingly been a violation of Article 11 of the Convention.' The Court reaches this conclusion by reference to its prior findings in Huseynli and Others and Huseynov and Others, stating: 'the Court sees no particular circumstances that could compel it to deviate from its findings in those judgments, and finds that in the present cases the applicants' right to freedom of assembly was breached for the same reasons as those outlined in the said judgments.' The Court also notes the chilling effect of the arrests and administrative proceedings: 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies. Those measures undoubtedly have a chilling effect, which deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations, and, more generally, from participating in open political debate.'

**2. Canonical elements of Article 11 doctrine**

**(a) Scope**

The Court does not explicitly discuss the three sub-elements of scope (existence of an assembly, participation, and peacefulness) in this decision. However, the Court's entire analysis presupposes that Article 11 is engaged. The reference to 'the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013' implies that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated in it. The word 'authorised' also implies the assembly was peaceful (or at least not treated as non-peaceful by the authorities). These elements are implied rather than explicitly discussed.

- **Assembly exists:** Implied by the reference to 'the authorised demonstration of 12 October 2013' and the framing of the entire complaint under Article 11. The Court does not explicitly address whether an assembly existed, but it is taken for granted.
- **Participation:** Implied. The applicants' 'arrests and convictions immediately after the authorised demonstration' presuppose their participation. Not explicitly discussed as a separate sub-element.
- **Peaceful:** Implied. The demonstration is described as 'authorised,' and there is no suggestion it was violent. The Court applies Article 11 without questioning the peaceful nature of the assembly.

**(b) Interference**

The Court does not explicitly frame a separate 'interference' analysis, but it is clearly implied. The Court states: 'The applicants' arrests and administrative proceedings against them could not but have had the effect of discouraging them from participating in political rallies.' This language presupposes that the arrests and convictions constituted an interference with the right to freedom of assembly. The Court also refers to the 'chilling effect' of these measures. Interference is thus implied rather than explicitly discussed as a distinct doctrinal step.

**(c) Prescribed by law**

The sub-elements of 'prescribed by law' (foreseeability, accessibility, safeguards) are not discussed in this decision. The Court resolves the case by reference to its findings in Huseynli and Others and Huseynov and Others without separately addressing the lawfulness of the interference. These elements are not discussed.

**(d) Legitimate aim**

The Court does not explicitly discuss whether the interference pursued a legitimate aim. Again, the Court relies on its prior judgments without separately addressing this element. Legitimate aim is not discussed in this decision.

**(e) Necessity in a democratic society**

The Court does not explicitly conduct a necessity analysis in this decision. It refers to the 'chilling effect' of the arrests and administrative proceedings, which is a concept associated with the necessity/proportionality analysis, but it does so by way of summary reference to the earlier judgments rather than as a standalone proportionality assessment. The necessity element is implied (negatively — i.e., the interference was not necessary) by the finding of a violation, but it is not explicitly discussed as a separate doctrinal step in this decision.

**(f) Positive obligation**

The Court does not discuss any positive obligation in this decision. This element is entirely absent.

**Summary of findings:**

- Scope (overall): implied yes
- Assembly exists: implied yes
- Participation: implied yes
- Peaceful: implied yes
- Interference: implied yes
- Prescribed by law: not discussed
- Prescribed by law (foreseeable): not discussed
- Prescribed by law (accessible): not discussed
- Prescribed by law (safeguards): not discussed
- Legitimate aim: not discussed
- Necessity: implied no (violation found)
- Positive obligation: not discussed
