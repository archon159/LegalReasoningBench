THE FACTS

1.  The applicants, referred to in the annexed table, have all lodged 
before the domestic courts various proceedings based on the restitution 
legislation, adopted in Romania after the fall of the communist regime. 
Their complaints referring to a general inefficiency of the restitution 
mechanism were communicated to the Government mainly under Articles 1

of Protocol No. 1 and/or Article 6 of the Convention, on the dates tabulated 
below.

The Romanian Government (“the Government”) were represented by 
their Agent, Ms C. Brumar, from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

2.  On 12 October 2010, the Court adopted a pilot-judgment in the case 
of Maria Atanasiu and Others v. Romania, (nos. 30767/05 and 33800/06), 
in which it singled out the deficiencies of the restitution mechanism, 
indicating to the respondent state under Article 46 of the Convention that 
new steps needed to be taken in order to process the restitution claims with 
more efficiency.

3.  On 15 May 2013 Law no. 165/2013 came into force, setting out 
various proceedings available to the petitioners seeking to have their 
restitution claims solved.

4.  Based on the parties’ observations and comments regarding the new 
remedies set out by Law no. 165/2013, on 29 April 2014 the Court found in 
its judgment of Preda and Others v. Romania (cited above, §§ 134-140), 
that the mechanism established by the new law offered a range of effective 
remedies that needed to be exhausted by those claimants whose complaints 
referred to one of the following situations: the existence of concurrent titles 
to property with respect to the same immovable; the annulment of the title 
to property in the lack of any compensation; the issue of a final decision 
confirming the right to compensation without fixing the amount; the failure 
to pay the compensation allowed in a final decision; and the prolonged 
failure to give a decision on a restitution claim.

B.  Relevant domestic law

5.  The applicable domestic legislation is indicated and detailed in the 
pilot case Maria Atanasiu and Others v. Romania, cited above, §§ 44-80; 
and Preda and Others v. Romania, cited above, §§ 69-74.

COMPLAINTS

6.  Relying on various provisions of the Convention (see attached table), 
the applicants complained that their restitution and/or compensation claims, 
based on the various relevant domestic laws, have not been dealt with in a 
satisfactory manner by the national authorities, in so far as the respective 
mechanism put in place by the state was ineffective.

THE LAW

7.  The Court firstly finds that, given their common factual and legal 
background, it is appropriate to join the present applications.

8.  The Government raised various objections in relation to certain 
complaints lodged by the applicants. However, the Court need not examine 
them separately since the applications are inadmissible in any event for the 
reasons stated below.

A.  Complaints under Article 1 of Protocol No. 1

9.  The Court recalls that in Preda and Others v. Romania (cited above, 
§ 129), it has held that Law no. 165/2013 provided a framework for the 
redress of the applicants’ complaints under Article 1 of Protocol No. 1 
generated by the application of the restitution laws, which was in principle 
accessible and effective. Hence, in spite of the fact that such a framework 
became available after the introduction of their applications, it held that the 
applicants were obliged to use them, in so far as using them did not 
constitute an excessive burden for them, or for other claimants in a similar 
position (see §§ 134-140 of the judgment and paragraph 4 above).

Similarly, the Court notes that the same reasoning applies for the 
situation where the applicants have a final and binding judgment confirming 
their entitlement to a plot of land, the domestic authorities being under the 
obligation to restore possession on the respective land and issue the 
respective title deed by 1 January 2016, pursuant to Article 11 § 1 of the 
Law no. 165/2013.

10. Having regard to its findings in Preda and Others v. Romania, and in 
so far as the factual situations described above also form the basis of the 
current applications, the Court concludes that the applicants in the present 
cases are also required by Article 35 § 1 to avail themselves of the new 
domestic remedy by pursuing the domestic proceedings under Law 
no. 165/2013.1

1.  Rectified on 14 October 2014; the text was: “9.  The Court recalls that in Preda and

domestic proceedings under Law no. 165/2013.”

11.  It follows that the present applications, in so far as they concern 
alleged violations of Article 1 of Protocol No. 1, must be rejected pursuant 
to Article 35 §§ 1 and 4 of the Convention for non-exhaustion of domestic 
remedies.

B.  Complaints under Article 13 and 46 concerning the lack of an 
effective domestic remedy and the consequential existence of a 
systemic problem

12.  Given that the applicants’ complaints under Article 1 of Protocol 
No. 1 have been rejected for non-exhaustion of domestic remedies, in so far 
as the new remedies provided by the Law no. 165/2013 cannot a priori be 
presumed ineffective (see paragraph 9 above), the related complaints under 
Articles 13 and 46 raised in some of the applications are manifestly ill-
founded and must be rejected in accordance with Article 35 §§ 3 (a) and 4 
of the Convention.

C.  Other complaints

13.  The Court has examined the remainder of the applicants’ complaints, 
as submitted by them (see attached table). However, in light of all the 
material in its possession and in so far as the matters complained of are 
within its competence, the Court finds that they do not disclose any 
appearance of a violation of the rights and freedoms set out in the 
Convention and its Protocols. It follows that the respective parts of the 
applications must be rejected, pursuant to Article 35 §§ 1, 3 and 4 of the 
Convention.

For these reasons, the Court unanimously

Decides to join the applications;

Declares the applications inadmissible.
