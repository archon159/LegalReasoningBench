LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's implicit finding that the scope of Article 11 is engaged rests on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' roles as organisers or participants. F002 and F003 together establish this. However, since both F002 and F003 establish the same point (participation in public assemblies, just from different roles), both are necessary because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants. The most critical facts are F002 and F003.

Interference: The interference finding rests on the arrest, dispersal, and conviction described in the reasoning. These are grounded in F002 and F003, which describe the disproportionate measures. Both are necessary as the applicant group spans both roles.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts. Empty.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts. Empty.

Necessity: The necessity conclusion is reached by reference to prior case-law and the general description of the measures. The only atomic facts available are F002 and F003. Both are necessary to establish that measures were taken against the applicants in the context of public assemblies.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on: (1) the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (establishing scope and triggering Article 11 protection), and (2) disproportionate measures — arrest, dispersal, conviction — were taken against them (establishing interference and grounding the necessity finding). These two points are captured by F002 and F003. F001 is merely a procedural reference to the appended table and is not substantively necessary. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. The irreducible core is therefore F002 and F003.
