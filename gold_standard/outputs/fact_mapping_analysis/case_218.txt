LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis, specifically: (a) the expressive, non-violent nature of the applicant's conduct in the context of a politically significant peaceful demonstration; (b) the severity of the custodial sanction imposed; and (c) the failure of the domestic courts to provide adequate reasoning justifying that sanction in the context of the exercise of a fundamental freedom. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

→ F012: The demonstration was part of a series of protests about Parliament's failure to adopt electoral reform. [Establishes the public interest context and the weight of the right, triggering the requirement for very strong justification.]
→ F025: The applicant turned towards the police officers, holding beans in his hand and shouting loudly that beans used to be 'gruel for slaves'. [Establishes the expressive, non-violent character of the conduct.]
→ F026: The applicant then threw the beans at the police officers, crying out 'slave gruel for the police'. [Establishes the physical but non-injurious nature of the conduct; the Court treats this as expressive rather than violent.]
→ F024: It does not appear that the demonstration on 29 November 2019 was dispersed. [Establishes absence of escalation, supporting the non-violent characterization.]
→ F086: The Tbilisi City Court sentenced the applicant to eight days' administrative detention. [Establishes the severity of the sanction, which triggers heightened scrutiny.]
→ F081: On at least two previous occasions — in 2008 and 2014 — the applicant had been ordered to pay administrative fines. [Establishes that the prior record relied upon by the domestic court fell outside the legally permissible one-year window, undermining the 'personality' ground for the custodial sentence.]
→ F110: The Tbilisi City Court cited Articles 33 and 35 and reasoned that 'in view of the applicant's personality and the degree of seriousness of his actions', the imposition of a fine or a verbal reprimand would not ensure the attainment of the objectives of punishment. [Establishes the entirety of the domestic courts' proportionality reasoning, which the ECtHR found insufficient.]
→ F128: Article 35 §§ 1 and 2 of the Code of Administrative Offences lists 'the commission of a similar offence during the year following conviction' as an aggravating circumstance. [Establishes the legal constraint that rendered the 'personality' ground legally deficient.]
→ F122: The Tbilisi Court of Appeal upheld the lower court's findings without re-examining the merits. [Establishes that the appellate court did not remedy the deficiency in the proportionality reasoning.]
