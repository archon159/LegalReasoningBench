LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope — assembly exists / participation
The reasoning text quote is: "the disciplinary sanction of reprimand imposed on her for her participation in trade union activities."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F006: the applicant participated in a demonstration organised by the trade union on 28 November 2008 (this is the specific trade union activity in question)
- F009: the disciplinary sanction was imposed for her participation in the demonstration of 28 November 2008 (directly maps to the quote)
- F004: the applicant was a member of the local branch of the trade union (establishes her connection to the trade union activity)
- F005: the applicant was secretary of the local branch (further establishes her role in trade union activities)

(a) Scope — peaceful
No factual grounding quote was identified in the reasoning text. However, the atomic facts that could ground this implied finding are:
- F007: the demonstration was held on the theme 'No to violence against women' (the subject matter implies a peaceful civic demonstration)
- F006: the applicant participated in a demonstration (no suggestion of violence in the description)

(b) Interference
The reasoning text quotes are: (1) "the disciplinary sanction of reprimand imposed on her for her participation in trade union activities" and (2) "the sanction complained of, although very light, had been such as to dissuade trade union members from legitimate participation in strikes or other trade union actions to defend the interests of their members."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F008: the District Directorate of National Education imposed a disciplinary sanction in the form of a reprimand on 29 June 2009
- F009: the sanction was imposed for her participation in the demonstration of 28 November 2008
- F010: the sanction was imposed under Article 125 of Law no. 657 on Civil Servants

(e) Necessity
The reasoning text quotes are: (1) "the sanction complained of, although very light, had been such as to dissuade trade union members from legitimate participation in strikes or other trade union actions to defend the interests of their members" and (2) "the Government have not put forward any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion in the present case."
Atomic facts pointed to for the nature and severity of the sanction:
- F008: the sanction imposed was a reprimand (the lightest form of disciplinary sanction)
- F009: the sanction was imposed for participation in the demonstration
Atomic facts pointed to for the absence of Government justification: no specific atomic fact corresponds to this — it reflects the absence of submissions rather than a positive fact in the record. However, the domestic proceedings facts are relevant context:
- F013: the Disciplinary Board dismissed the applicant's objection
- F014: the Board dismissed on the ground that the decision was in accordance with law (this is the only justification offered at domestic level, which the Court implicitly found insufficient)

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope
The Court needs to establish that there was a trade union assembly, that the applicant participated, and that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F006: the applicant participated in a demonstration organised by the trade union (establishes both the existence of the assembly and the applicant's participation — the single most critical fact)
- F007: the demonstration was on the theme 'No to violence against women' (most critical fact for the peaceful character, as the subject matter is inherently civic and non-violent)

(b) Interference
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's Article 11 rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F008: the District Directorate imposed a disciplinary sanction in the form of a reprimand (establishes the governmental act)
- F009: the sanction was imposed for her participation in the demonstration (establishes the causal link between the assembly and the sanction, making it an interference with Article 11 rights)

(c) Prescribed by law
Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary for this element in the Court's reasoning.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary for this element in the Court's reasoning.

(e) Necessity
The Court's necessity analysis turns on the chilling effect of the sanction and the absence of Government justification. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F008: the sanction was a reprimand (its lightness is the key factual premise for the Court's reasoning that even a light sanction can dissuade — "although very light")
- F009: the sanction was imposed for participation in the demonstration (establishes that the sanction directly targeted the exercise of Article 11 rights, making it incapable of being necessary)

(f) Positive obligation
Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on two essential facts: (1) the applicant participated in a trade union demonstration, and (2) she was sanctioned by a public authority specifically because of that participation. These two facts are what make the case an Article 11 case at all and what drive the necessity finding. The theme of the demonstration (F007) is relevant to scope/peaceful but is not what the case turned on — the Court did not rely on it in its necessity analysis. The domestic appeal (F013, F014) adds procedural context but is not what the violation turned on.

The irreducible core is:
- F006: the applicant participated in a demonstration organised by the trade union (establishes the protected activity)
- F008: the District Directorate imposed a disciplinary sanction in the form of a reprimand (establishes the governmental act)
- F009: the sanction was imposed for her participation in the demonstration (establishes the direct causal link that makes the sanction an unjustified interference with Article 11 rights — this is the single most critical fact)
