LIST 1: Factual grounding quotes mapped to atomic facts

**Scope — assembly exists**
Quote: "The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention."
Atomic facts: F006 (public demonstration held on 6 May 2012), F007 (protest against allegedly rigged elections), F008 (approved by Moscow city authorities as march followed by meeting at Bolotnaya Square).

**Scope — participation**
No discrete factual quote from the reasoning section. Implied by the prosecution itself. Atomic facts: F024 (applicant arrived to take part in the march), F025 (walked to Bolotnaya Square), F026 (passed metal detectors and joined protestors in front of stage).

**Scope — peaceful (as to applicant personally)**
Quote: "it does not transpire from the description of his offences that he was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character."
Quote: "there is nothing in the case file to suggest that the applicant was among those responsible for the onset of clashes between the protesters and the police."
Quote: "The Court concludes that the applicant was not regarded as one of the individuals responsible for the disruption of the assembly or prompting the use of force by the police."
Atomic facts: F010 (march was peaceful), F112 (court found defendants acquired criminal intent at 5 p.m. at the latest — but the applicant's own acts came later), F117 (applicant punched and kicked officer K. — but this is the act itself, not the onset), F119 (applicant saw police beating people at 7.30 p.m.), F120 (applicant found himself in first line and saw harsh arrests), F122 (applicant moved towards officer K. in state of fury after being hit). The key atomic facts establishing that the applicant was not among those who initiated the disorder are: F010 (march peaceful), F119, F120, F122 (his acts came at the height of clashes, not at the onset).

**Interference**
Quote: "the applicant's prosecution and criminal conviction for participation in mass disorder constituted an interference with the exercise of the freedom of assembly."
Quote: "he was found guilty of two criminal offences, namely participation in acts of mass disorder (Article 212 of the Criminal Code) and committing violent acts against a police officer (Article 318 of the Criminal Code). He was sentenced to three years and seven months' imprisonment."
Atomic facts: F109 (found guilty under Article 212 § 2 and Article 318 § 1), F130 (sentenced to three years and seven months' imprisonment), F131 (sentence calculated on basis of three-year term under Article 212 § 2 partly concurrent with one year and eight months under Article 318 § 1).

**Prescribed by law**
The Court makes no independent factual findings; it defers entirely to Yaroslav Belousov. The only factual anchors are the existence of the criminal provisions themselves: F139 (Article 212 § 1), F140 (Article 212 § 2), F141 (Article 212 § 3), F142 (Article 318 § 1). No atomic facts from the circumstances of the case are independently relied upon.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court states the conclusion by reference to Yaroslav Belousov without independent factual elaboration. The factual anchor is simply the nature of the charges: F054 (charges under Article 212 § 2), F055 (charges under Article 318 § 1). No further atomic facts are independently relied upon.

**Necessity**

(i) Nature and severity of acts and sentence:
Quote: "he was found guilty of two criminal offences... He was sentenced to three years and seven months' imprisonment under those provisions for hitting a police officer, once on the head and once on the body, which did not endanger the latter's life or health, while participating in an authorised public assembly."
Atomic facts: F073 (struck officer once on the head), F074 (kicked officer once on the body while lying on ground), F114 (violence did not endanger life or health), F130 (sentenced to three years and seven months), F131 (three-year term under Article 212 § 2 partly concurrent with one year and eight months under Article 318 § 1), F008 (assembly was authorised).

(ii) Disproportionate weight of mass disorder sentence:
Quote: "the sentence of one year and eight months' imprisonment under Article 318 of the Criminal Code for using violence against a public official, namely hitting him, although severe, was more lenient than the partly concurrent three-year prison sentence for participation in acts of mass disorder."
Atomic facts: F131 (sentence breakdown).

(iii) Government's justification and its implications:
Quote: "The main justification advanced by the Government for such a harsh sentence was the serious risk of civil unrest at the time, in particular the threat to political stability and public order. The gravity of the punishment was thus attributable to the general context of the applicant's assault on the policeman, not to the harm he had thus inflicted."
No specific atomic fact from the case file directly corresponds to this — it reflects the Government's submissions on merits, which the Court characterises. The closest atomic facts are F014 (numerous clashes at Bolotnaya Square) and F018/F019 (criminal proceedings opened on 6 May 2012).

(iv) Additional prison time attributable to mass disorder conviction:
Quote: "his staying at the site of the rally accounted for an additional one year and eleven months to his prison term."
Atomic facts: F131 (sentence breakdown — three years under Article 212 § 2 minus one year and eight months under Article 318 § 1 gives the additional time).

(v) Failure of domestic courts to specify acts constituting mass disorder separately from assault:
Quote: "the domestic courts have not specified the applicant's acts attributable to participation in the mass disorder under Article 212 of the Criminal Code, as opposed to the assault on the policeman under Article 318 of the Code. In describing his participation in the mass disorder, they merely referred to his use of violence against a police officer, also classified as a separate criminal offence."
Atomic facts: F109 (convicted under both provisions), F114 (finding of violence against officer K.), F117 (specific acts found by court — punching and kicking officer K.). The domestic court's findings on mass disorder (F110, F111, F112, F113) describe the general disorder but do not specify the applicant's individual acts beyond the assault.

(vi) Absence of pre-existing intention:
Quote: "the criminal file did not suggest any pre-existing intention by the applicant to participate in mass disorder."
Atomic facts: F024 (applicant arrived to take part in the march — no suggestion of intent to disorder), F118 (applicant pleaded not guilty and testified he wanted to see why meeting was not starting), F127 (applicant insisted his actions were provoked by panic and events around him). The domestic court's finding in F112 (defendants acquired criminal intent at 5 p.m. at the latest) is the finding the Court implicitly questions or distinguishes as to this applicant.

(vii) Absence of responsibility for onset of clashes:
Quote: "there is nothing in the case file to suggest that the applicant was among those responsible for the onset of clashes between the protesters and the police."
Atomic facts: F010 (march was peaceful), F119 (applicant saw police beating people at 7.30 p.m. — i.e. he was present as a bystander before acting), F120 (found himself in first line and saw harsh arrests), F122 (moved towards officer K. in state of fury after being hit).

(viii) Timing and context of the applicant's act:
Quote: "As far as can be discerned from the indictment, he hit the police officer at the height of the clashes, when the police were already arresting protesters."
Atomic facts: F115 (between 5.10 p.m. and 8.20 p.m. — height of clashes), F117 (applicant punched and kicked officer K.), F119 (police beating people at 7.30 p.m.), F120 (police harshly arresting people), F122 (applicant moved towards officer K. in state of fury after being hit).

(ix) Failure of domestic courts to examine circumstances or rebut applicant's allegations:
Quote: "The domestic courts did not establish the particular circumstances in which that behaviour occurred and dismissed the applicant's arguments about excessive use of force by the police as unfounded while leaving his allegations unrebutted."
Atomic facts: F128 (domestic court dismissed defendants' arguments about protecting someone or being victims of police force as farfetched), F129 (court noted no medical document proving injuries on applicant's body), F136 (applicant in appeal insisted he was victim of unlawful police action — not rebutted by courts).

(x) Characterisation of applicant's conduct as sporadic:
Quote: "The applicant's conduct therefore, although involving violence, was sporadic in nature and, in the light of the domestic courts' findings, did not attain the degree of aggression which the Court has found in other cases to amount to violence calling for resolute measures."
Atomic facts: F073 (struck once on head), F074 (kicked once on body), F114 (violence did not endanger life or health), F117 (specific acts found by court).

(xi) Chilling effect:
Quote: "the applicant's criminal conviction, and especially the severity of his sentence, must have had the effect of discouraging him and other opposition supporters, and the public at large, from attending demonstrations... The chilling effect of the sanction imposed on him was further amplified by the large-scale proceedings in the case, which attracted widespread media coverage."
Atomic facts: F130 (three years and seven months' imprisonment), F084 (extension order concerned eleven defendants — indicating large-scale proceedings), F098 (criminal case against ten participants in the demonstration), F099 (ten defendants charged).

**Positive obligation**
Quote: "the Court is not required to assess the authorities' alleged failure to discharge their positive obligation in respect of the conduct of the assembly at Bolotnaya Square as a separate issue under Article 11 of the Convention."
Atomic facts: F011 (barriers narrowed entrance to meeting venue), F012 (barriers restricted space allocated for meeting), F013 (police cordon forced protestors to remain within barriers), F014 (numerous clashes between police and protestors), F015 (police ordered meeting to finish early), F016 (police began to disperse participants).

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
Assembly exists: F006, F008 — the demonstration took place and was officially approved, which is the minimum needed to establish an assembly within Article 11 scope.
Participation: F024, F026 — the applicant arrived to take part and joined the protestors at the venue.
Peaceful (as to applicant): F010 (march was peaceful, establishing the assembly's initial character), F119, F120, F122 (applicant's acts came at the height of clashes, not at the onset, establishing he was not among those who initiated disorder).

**Interference**
F109 (conviction under both provisions), F130 (sentence of three years and seven months) — these are the minimum facts establishing that a criminal conviction and sentence were imposed on the applicant, constituting the interference.

**Prescribed by law**
F140 (Article 212 § 2 — the provision under which the applicant was convicted for mass disorder), F142 (Article 318 § 1 — the provision under which the applicant was convicted for assault). These are the legal provisions whose lawfulness the Court affirms by reference to Yaroslav Belousov. No circumstantial atomic facts are strictly necessary here.

**Legitimate aim**
F054 (charges under Article 212 § 2), F055 (charges under Article 318 § 1) — the nature of the charges establishes the aims of preventing disorder and crime and protecting the rights of others. No further atomic facts are strictly necessary.

**Necessity**
The strictly necessary atomic facts are:
F008 (assembly was authorised — relevant to the context of the conviction)
F010 (march was peaceful — establishes the assembly's initial character and that the applicant was not among those who initiated disorder)
F073 (struck officer once on head — establishes the limited nature of the act)
F074 (kicked officer once on body — establishes the limited nature of the act)
F114 (violence did not endanger life or health — establishes the low severity of the harm)
F117 (domestic court's specific finding of punching and kicking — the only acts attributed to the applicant under both charges)
F119 (applicant saw police beating people at 7.30 p.m. — establishes timing and context)
F122 (applicant moved towards officer K. in state of fury after being hit — establishes reactive, not premeditated, nature)
F128 (domestic court dismissed applicant's arguments as farfetched — establishes failure to examine circumstances)
F130 (sentence of three years and seven months — establishes severity)
F131 (sentence breakdown — establishes the disproportionate weight of the mass disorder conviction relative to the assault conviction)
F112 (domestic court found criminal intent acquired at 5 p.m. at the latest — the Court implicitly questions this as applied to the applicant, given the timing of his acts)

**Positive obligation**
F011, F013, F014, F015, F016 — these establish the conduct of the authorities at the assembly that gave rise to the positive obligation complaint, which the Court folds into the main analysis rather than examining separately. Since the Court declines to examine this as a separate issue, no atomic facts are strictly necessary to support a separate positive obligation finding. The element is not resolved.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment. The Court found a violation because: (a) the applicant's acts were limited and reactive, not premeditated or initiating; (b) the domestic courts failed to examine the circumstances or distinguish the mass disorder conviction from the assault; (c) the sentence for mass disorder was disproportionately severe relative to the actual harm caused; and (d) the conviction had a chilling effect. The irreducible facts are:

F008 — assembly was authorised (contextualises the conviction as arising from participation in a lawful assembly)
F010 — march was peaceful (establishes the assembly's initial character and that disorder was not inherent)
F073 — struck officer once on head (establishes the limited, specific nature of the applicant's act)
F074 — kicked officer once on body (establishes the limited, specific nature of the applicant's act)
F114 — violence did not endanger life or health (establishes the low severity of harm caused)
F117 — domestic court's specific finding of punching and kicking officer K. (the only acts attributed to the applicant — and the same acts used to ground both convictions)
F119 — applicant saw police beating people at 7.30 p.m. (establishes timing: his acts came at the height of clashes, not at the onset)
F122 — applicant moved towards officer K. in state of fury after being hit (establishes reactive, not premeditated, nature of the act)
F128 — domestic court dismissed applicant's arguments as farfetched without rebutting them (establishes failure to examine circumstances)
F130 — sentence of three years and seven months (establishes the severity of the sanction)
F131 — sentence breakdown (establishes that the mass disorder conviction added one year and eleven months beyond the assault sentence, and that the mass disorder sentence was harsher than the assault sentence despite the latter being the only specific act identified)
