LIST 1: Factual grounding mapped to atomic facts

**Scope**

(i) Assembly exists
The Court's grounding: 'the Moscow authorities rejected Mr Frolov's notifications of a public religious event' and the description of planned events as public gatherings requiring notification under the Public Events Act.
Atomic facts: F059 (first notification for a meeting with up to fifteen people), F074 (second notification for a meeting), F092 (Religions Act establishes public religious events follow Public Events Act procedure), F094 (Public Events Act defines 'public event' including meetings), F095 (purposes of a public event defined in the Act).

(ii) Participation
The Court's grounding: 'There is no dispute in the instant case that Mr Frolov did send the required notification within the time-limits established by law.'
Atomic facts: F059 (Mr Frolov submitted first notification on 1 April 2013), F074 (Mr Frolov submitted second notification on 8 April 2013).

(iii) Peaceful
The Court's grounding: 'The Court notes the indisputably peaceful character of the planned religious events. Participants intended to assemble in support of their religion, Vaishnavism, and a particular lifestyle associated with it which in their view offered health benefits. There was no reason to presume a risk of any disturbance of public order or breach of peace on their part.' And: 'It has not been shown that unlawful means of conversion, infringing the rights of others, have been or were likely to be employed by Mr Frolov or other participants.'
Atomic facts: F059 (notification described a meeting to promote Vaishnavism and a healthy lifestyle, up to fifteen people), F074 (second notification for a meeting to promote Vaishnavism), F078 (a few participants gathered at the designated location — no disorder recorded), F079 (police told participants to disperse — no violence or disorder noted).

**Interference**
The Court's grounding: 'the Moscow authorities rejected Mr Frolov's notifications of a public religious event'; 'The authorities however held that the planned event could not proceed because missionary activities — which the promotion of Vaishnavism was taken to be — were inconsistent with the purposes of a public event as defined in the Public Events Act and also incompatible with the respect for the religious beliefs of others.'
Atomic facts: F060 (prefecture rejected first notification), F075 (prefecture rejected second notification on purpose grounds), F076 (prefecture rejected second notification on form grounds), F079 (police told participants to disperse on 20 April 2013), F067 (Meshchanskiy District Court upheld refusal), F069 (Moscow City Court dismissed appeal on first notification), F070 (Moscow City Court found missionary activities did not correspond to lawful purposes), F071 (Moscow City Court found activities incompatible with religious beliefs of others), F080 (Ostankinskiy District Court dismissed review of second notification), F082 (Moscow City Court dismissed appeal on second notification), F083 (Moscow City Court held no provision for public event of religious nature in form of meeting).

**Prescribed by law**
First ground (purpose): The Court's grounding: 'The Act does not contain a list of permissible purposes or, for that matter, a requirement that a public event should pursue only permissible purposes. The Act does not specify how the purpose of the event should be assessed or provide for the authorities' discretion in determining which purposes are permissible and which are not. The provision to which the domestic courts referred, section 2(1) of the Act, provides a general definition of a 'public event' which may be held for the purposes of 'free expression and shaping of opinions ... on various issues related to ... social and cultural life'.' And: 'The Russian courts gave no reasons whatsoever for their finding that the promotion of Vaishnavism and a healthy lifestyle fell outside the scope of that broad definition.'
Atomic facts: F094 (section 2(1) defines public event), F095 (section 2(1) defines purposes broadly), F060 (prefecture rejected first notification citing section 2(1)), F068 (Meshchanskiy District Court found promotion of Vaishnavism did not correspond to purposes in section 2(1)), F070 (Moscow City Court found missionary activities did not correspond to lawful purposes — no reasons given for why Vaishnavism falls outside the broad definition).

Second ground (form/type): The Court's grounding: 'they failed to specify what type would have been appropriate in the circumstances. The Moscow City Court's finding that the forms of religious events were limited to 'worship, rites and ceremonies' does not sit well with the provisions of the Religions Act which established that religious events outside places of worship were to be conducted under the rules of the Public Events Act and, accordingly, be cast in one of the forms specified in it.'
Atomic facts: F076 (prefecture rejected second notification because Religions Act did not mention 'meeting' as form of religious event), F083 (Moscow City Court held no provision for public event of religious nature in form of meeting), F084 (Moscow City Court held religious events could only take form of worship, rite or ceremony), F085 (Moscow City Court held promoting Vaishnavism was not a service of worship, rite or ceremony), F092 (Religions Act section 16(5) establishes public religious events follow Public Events Act procedure — which does include meetings), F094 (Public Events Act defines public event to include meetings), F096 (subsections 2-6 of section 2 describe various types of public events including meetings).

**Legitimate aim**
The Court references the Government's argument about prevention of disorder and rights of others but does not make an affirmative factual finding. The Court notes: 'the right to freedom of assembly may be restricted for the prevention of disorder and the protection of the rights of others which are both legitimate aims under the Convention.' The factual grounding deployed here is essentially the same as under necessity (absence of disorder risk, absence of unlawful means). No atomic facts are adopted by the Court as establishing a legitimate aim affirmatively. The closest atomic facts are those establishing the authorities' stated reasons: F060, F071, F075.

**Necessity**
The Court's grounding:
- Content-based restriction: 'The Court notes that the domestic authorities did not have any objections to the planned events being held at a specific location or time... Rather, their objections related to the religious nature of planned events and accordingly amounted to content-based restrictions on freedom of assembly.'
Atomic facts: F060 (rejection based on purpose/content — promotion of Vaishnavism), F070 (Moscow City Court: missionary activities did not correspond to lawful purposes), F071 (Moscow City Court: incompatible with religious beliefs of others), F075 (second rejection based on purpose/content).
- Peaceful character and absence of disorder risk: 'The Court notes the indisputably peaceful character of the planned religious events... There was no reason to presume a risk of any disturbance of public order or breach of peace on their part.'
Atomic facts: F059 (notification described peaceful meeting, up to fifteen people), F078 (participants gathered peacefully), F079 (no disorder — police merely told participants to disperse).
- Absence of unlawful means: 'It has not been shown that unlawful means of conversion, infringing the rights of others, have been or were likely to be employed by Mr Frolov or other participants.'
Atomic facts: F059 (notification described promotion of Vaishnavism and healthy lifestyle — no indication of unlawful means), F078 (participants gathered without incident).
- Comparable events in another region: 'it is also significant that the textually identical notifications filed in another Russian region were not met with any objections. There is no evidence of any disturbances during those events which appear to have been able to proceed peacefully.'
Atomic facts: No atomic fact in the provided list directly corresponds to the Ulyanovsk events proceeding without objection. The applicant's submission about Ulyanovsk (referenced in the merits section) is not formalised as an atomic fact in the facts section. The closest atomic facts relate to the Ulyanovsk anti-cult project (F020-F058) but these concern hostile statements, not the holding of public religious events without objection. There is no atomic fact in the provided list that captures the Ulyanovsk events proceeding without objection. This factual grounding therefore cannot be mapped to any provided atomic fact.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts to map.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was a planned public assembly, that Mr Frolov attempted to participate/organise it, and that it was peaceful.
- F059: establishes the planned assembly (meeting, up to fifteen people, to promote Vaishnavism and healthy lifestyle) and Mr Frolov's participation as organiser.
- F074: establishes the second planned assembly and Mr Frolov's participation.
- F092: establishes the legal framework under which public religious events are treated as assemblies under the Public Events Act.
- F078: establishes that participants gathered peacefully (supports peaceful character).

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that the authorities rejected the notifications and prevented the event.
- F060: prefecture rejected first notification.
- F075: prefecture rejected second notification (purpose ground).
- F076: prefecture rejected second notification (form ground).
- F079: police told participants to disperse — direct physical interference.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court needs to establish: (a) the broad statutory definition that the courts failed to apply consistently; (b) the courts' failure to give reasons for finding Vaishnavism outside that definition; (c) the inconsistency between the courts' finding on form and the statutory framework.
- F095: section 2(1) defines purposes broadly ('free expression and shaping of opinions on various issues related to social and cultural life') — the broad definition the courts departed from without reasons.
- F068: Meshchanskiy District Court found promotion of Vaishnavism did not correspond to purposes — no reasons given.
- F070: Moscow City Court found missionary activities did not correspond to lawful purposes — no reasons given.
- F083: Moscow City Court held no provision for public event of religious nature in form of meeting.
- F084: Moscow City Court held religious events limited to worship, rite or ceremony.
- F092: Religions Act section 16(5) establishes public religious events follow Public Events Act procedure — directly contradicts the courts' finding on form.
- F094: Public Events Act includes 'meeting' as a form of public event — directly contradicts the courts' finding.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court does not make an affirmative finding on legitimate aim. The only atomic facts relevant are those establishing the authorities' stated reasons (which the Court does not adopt as establishing a legitimate aim).
- F071: Moscow City Court found activities incompatible with religious beliefs of others — this is the 'aim' the Court addresses under necessity and finds insufficient.
No atomic facts strictly necessary to support an affirmative legitimate aim finding.

**Necessity**
The Court needs to establish: (a) the restriction was content-based; (b) the events were peaceful with no disorder risk; (c) no unlawful means were used or likely; (d) comparable events elsewhere proceeded without incident.
- F060: rejection based on content (promotion of Vaishnavism) — establishes content-based restriction.
- F071: Moscow City Court's finding of incompatibility with religious beliefs of others — the specific 'aim' the Court finds fails the necessity test.
- F059: notification described peaceful meeting to promote Vaishnavism and healthy lifestyle — establishes peaceful character and absence of indication of unlawful means.
- F078: participants gathered peacefully — corroborates peaceful character.
- F079: no disorder recorded — corroborates absence of disorder risk.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on: (1) Mr Frolov notified the authorities of a planned peaceful public religious meeting; (2) the authorities rejected the notifications on content-based grounds (the religious/missionary nature of the event); (3) the statutory framework did not support the authorities' grounds for rejection (the broad definition of public event covered the planned activity; the Religions Act directed public religious events to the Public Events Act which includes meetings); (4) the courts upheld the rejection without giving reasons for why Vaishnavism fell outside the broad statutory definition, and on a form-ground that contradicted the statutory framework; (5) the events were peaceful with no disorder risk and no unlawful means.

Irreducible atomic facts:
- F059: establishes the planned assembly, Mr Frolov's role as organiser, and the peaceful/lawful nature of the intended event.
- F060: establishes the content-based rejection of the first notification.
- F075: establishes the content-based rejection of the second notification.
- F092: establishes that the Religions Act directed public religious events to the Public Events Act — making the courts' form-based reasoning legally inconsistent.
- F094: establishes that 'meeting' is a recognised form under the Public Events Act — directly contradicting the courts' finding.
- F095: establishes the broad statutory definition of purposes — the definition the courts departed from without reasons.
- F068: establishes that the Meshchanskiy District Court upheld the rejection without explaining why Vaishnavism fell outside the broad definition.
- F070: establishes that the Moscow City Court upheld the rejection on purpose grounds without reasons.
- F071: establishes the 'incompatibility with religious beliefs of others' ground — the specific ground the Court finds fails the necessity test.
- F083: establishes the Moscow City Court's form-based reasoning (no provision for meeting of religious nature) — the reasoning the Court finds inconsistent with the statutory framework.
- F078: establishes that participants gathered peacefully — supporting the finding of no disorder risk.
- F079: establishes police dispersal — confirming the interference was real and that no disorder occurred.
