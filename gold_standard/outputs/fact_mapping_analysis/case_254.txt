LIST 1: Factual grounding for each element as found in the Court's own assessment

As established in the previous analysis, the Court's assessment section is extremely compressed and contains no independent statement of facts. It relies on the framing of the case and on Lashmankin and Others by reference. The factual grounding must therefore be reconstructed from the atomic facts that correspond to what the Court presupposed or implicitly relied upon.

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful):**
The Court's phrase 'the interference with the applicant's freedom of assembly' presupposes an assembly took place and the applicant was connected to it. The atomic facts that correspond to this presupposition are:
- F036: On 26 March 2017, the applicant held a meeting at the specially designated location for public events in Krylya Sovetov Park. [assembly exists, participation]
- F010: On 14 March 2017, the applicant notified the Kazan Town Administration of her intention to hold a meeting. [participation as organiser]
- F037: According to the applicant, about 1,500 people attended the meeting. [assembly exists]
- F039: According to the Government, about 400 people participated. [assembly exists]
For peacefulness, the Court does not adopt any facts, but the absence of any finding of violence is implicit. The most relevant atomic fact is:
- F036 (the meeting was held — no finding of violence attached to it)
No atomic fact explicitly states the assembly was peaceful in terms adopted by the Court, but F036 is the closest.

**Interference:**
The Court states 'the interference with the applicant's freedom of assembly.' The acts constituting interference are the refusal to approve the location, the arrest, and the conviction. Corresponding atomic facts:
- F014: The Kazan Town Administration refused to approve the meeting on 16 March 2017.
- F045: The applicant was arrested on her way home after the meeting.
- F063: The Aviastroitelnyy District Court found the applicant guilty of offences under Articles 19.3 and 20.2 § 2.
- F071: The applicant was sentenced to a fine of 1,000 roubles.
- F072: The applicant was sentenced to twenty hours of community work.

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court states the interference 'was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's "quality of law" requirements.' This finding is made by reference to Lashmankin and Others and the similarity of the issues. The atomic facts that ground the quality-of-law failure are those showing the legal framework was applied in a way that lacked sufficient safeguards — specifically the refusal without well-reasoned proposals and the conviction despite the court having found the refusal unlawful:
- F014: Refusal to approve the meeting.
- F015: The refusal was based on the claim that other (unspecified) public events were scheduled — i.e., no well-reasoned proposal.
- F033: The Vakhitovskiy District Court found the town administration had not made any well-reasoned proposals.
- F034: The town administration's failure to propose an alternative was found unlawful.
- F063: The applicant was nonetheless convicted.
- F070: The Aviastroitelnyy District Court did not reply to the applicant's argument that the refusal had been annulled.
- F085: The Supreme Court held the judicial decision finding the refusal unlawful did not amount to unconditional approval.
- F086: The Supreme Court held the judicial decision did not transform the event into an authorised one.
- F106: The domestic provisions are summarised in Lashmankin and Others (the legal framework at issue).

**Legitimate aim:**
The Court does not discuss legitimate aim in its assessment. No atomic facts are adopted by the Court for this element.

**Necessity:**
The Court states the interference was 'moreover not "necessary in a democratic society".' This finding is also made by reference to Lashmankin. The atomic facts that ground the disproportionality are those showing the severity of the sanctions relative to the circumstances:
- F036: The meeting was held at a specially designated location.
- F063: The applicant was convicted.
- F071: Fine of 1,000 roubles.
- F072: Twenty hours of community work.
- F034: The refusal had been found unlawful by a domestic court.
- F014: The refusal to approve the location.

**Positive obligation:**
Not discussed. No atomic facts correspond to this element.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:**
- F036: The applicant held a meeting at Krylya Sovetov Park on 26 March 2017. This is the single most critical fact establishing that an assembly took place and that the applicant participated in it as organiser. It is strictly necessary.

**Interference:**
- F014: The Kazan Town Administration refused to approve the meeting. This is the primary act of interference by the state.
- F063: The applicant was convicted of administrative offences. This is the culminating act of interference (conviction).
- F045: The applicant was arrested. This is the second major act of interference.

**Prescribed by law:**
- F034: The domestic court found the town administration's failure to propose an alternative location unlawful. This is the critical fact showing the legal framework was applied unlawfully — the very basis on which the Court finds the quality-of-law requirement was not met.
- F063: The applicant was nonetheless convicted despite the refusal having been found unlawful. This shows the legal provisions lacked adequate safeguards.
- F070: The Aviastroitelnyy District Court did not address the applicant's argument that the refusal had been annulled. This shows the absence of safeguards in the application of the law.
- F085/F086: The Supreme Court held the annulment of the refusal did not make the event authorised. These show the structural deficiency in the legal framework — the most critical facts for the quality-of-law finding. F085 is the most representative.

**Legitimate aim:**
No atomic facts — not discussed by the Court.

**Necessity:**
- F034: The refusal had been found unlawful by a domestic court. This is the most critical fact for disproportionality — the applicant was sanctioned for holding an event whose prohibition had been judicially annulled.
- F072: Twenty hours of community work. This is the more severe of the two penalties and is the most representative fact for disproportionality of sanction.

**Positive obligation:**
No atomic facts — not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two interlocking facts: (1) the applicant held a meeting at a location whose prohibition had been judicially annulled before the event took place, and (2) she was nonetheless arrested, convicted and sanctioned. These two facts are what made the interference fail both the quality-of-law and necessity tests. The irreducible core is:

- F036: The applicant held the meeting at Krylya Sovetov Park (the assembly took place — necessary for any interference to exist).
- F034: The domestic court had found the refusal to approve the meeting unlawful (critical for both quality-of-law and necessity — the entire violation turns on this).
- F014: The Kazan Town Administration refused to approve the meeting (the primary act of interference).
- F063: The applicant was convicted of administrative offences (the culminating interference and the fact that makes the necessity finding concrete).
- F045: The applicant was arrested (a significant component of the interference).
- F085: The Supreme Court held the judicial annulment of the refusal did not make the event authorised (the structural legal deficiency that grounds the quality-of-law finding).
