--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on two distinct limbs. The core facts are:

- F007: three-day notification period could not be observed — directly establishes the spontaneous assembly problem
- F009: applicants stood with banners at a distance, peacefully — establishes absence of any public order concern
- F025: conviction based solely on lack of notification, no other ground — establishes that no public safety or disorder consideration was relied upon
- F030: identical reasoning applied to both applicants — establishes that neither court examined special circumstances

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two findings: (1) the applicants were conducting peaceful solo demonstrations that were reclassified as a group event and punished solely for lack of notification, without any public order justification; and (2) compliance with the notification requirement was objectively impossible given the two-day notice of the parliamentary session, and the courts never examined whether this warranted an exception. The irreducible facts are:

- F005: The second reading was announced only two days before 19 December 2012 — this is the pivotal fact making compliance with the three-day notification period impossible and triggering the spontaneous assembly analysis
- F007: The three-day notification period could not be observed — directly establishes why the applicants could not notify
- F009: Applicants stood with banners at a distance from other protesters near the State Duma — establishes the peaceful, non-disruptive nature of the conduct and the solo character of each demonstration
- F025: The domestic courts found the event unlawful solely because no notification had been submitted — establishes that the only ground for conviction was procedural non-compliance, with no public order or safety justification
- F022 and F028: Both applicants were convicted — establishes that the interference took the concrete form of criminal punishment, completing the necessity analysis
