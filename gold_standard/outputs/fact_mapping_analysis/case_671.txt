LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies; F003 establishes that others were participants. Together they establish that (i) public assemblies existed and (ii) the applicants participated in them. No atomic fact supports the peacefulness sub-element.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are the closest available atomic facts, as they refer to "disproportionate measures taken against" the applicants in the context of public assemblies. No atomic fact explicitly states arrests or convictions, but F002 and F003 are the necessary foundation for the interference finding.
- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary, as the necessity assessment is grounded in the nature of the complaints (measures against organisers and participants of public assemblies). Without these facts, there is no basis for the necessity finding.
- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turned on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed to establish at minimum: (i) that there were public assemblies (scope), (ii) that the applicants were organisers or participants (scope/interference), and (iii) that measures were taken against them in that context (interference/necessity). All of this is captured by F002 and F003. F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table and is not substantively necessary for the legal conclusion. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, the irreducible factual core is F002 and F003.
