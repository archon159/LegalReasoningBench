LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: The Court's implied finding that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated rests on the existence of the public events and the restrictions placed on them. The most critical fact is the general existence of restrictions on public events, which presupposes the existence of those events. F002 is the most representative (restrictions on location imply the existence of public events). However, since all three (F002, F003, F004) together establish the full picture of what was restricted, and scope is implied rather than independently grounded, F002 alone is the most critical representative fact.
- F002

(b) Interference: The interference finding is directly grounded in the existence of governmental restrictions on public events. All three restriction facts are relevant, but the core point is that restrictions were imposed by authorities. F002, F003, and F004 together establish this, but F002 is the most representative single fact. However, since the Court's interference finding encompasses all three types of restriction, all three are strictly necessary to capture the full scope of the interference finding.
- F002, F003, F004

(c) Prescribed by law: The quality of law finding is grounded in the same legal provisions that imposed the restrictions. The existence of those restrictions (F002, F003, F004) is what triggers the quality of law analysis. Since the Court found the legal provisions governing these restrictions failed the quality of law test, the most critical facts are those establishing that restrictions were imposed by legal provisions — again F002, F003, F004. However, since the Court's reasoning does not distinguish between the three types of restriction for this element, F002 alone is the most critical representative.
- F002

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.
- (empty)

(e) Necessity: The necessity finding is grounded in the same restrictions. The most critical fact is the existence of the restrictions themselves. F002 is the most representative.
- F002

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.
- (empty)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two findings: (1) that there were governmental restrictions on public events (establishing interference and scope), and (2) that those restrictions were based on legal provisions failing the quality of law standard and were not necessary in a democratic society. The factual core that is absolutely necessary for the Court to reach its conclusion of a violation is the existence of the restrictions on public events imposed by the authorities — specifically F002, F003, and F004, since the Court's complaint description and its interference/prescribed-by-law/necessity findings all rest on these three facts collectively. F005 and F006 concern remedies complaints and are not part of the Article 11 violation finding on the merits. F001 is purely administrative.

The irreducible core is: F002, F003, F004 — because without the establishment of restrictions on location, time, and manner of public events, there is no interference, no quality of law analysis, and no necessity analysis, and therefore no violation finding. Among these, if forced to identify the single most critical, F002 (location restrictions) is the most representative, as location restrictions were the central issue in Lashmankin to which the Court analogized. But all three are necessary to fully ground the Court's conclusion as stated.
