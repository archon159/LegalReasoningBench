LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies." This maps to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies in Moscow.") which is the Court's own framing of the situation. F001 establishes who the applicants are but adds no factual content about the assemblies themselves. No other atomic fact corresponds to this grounding.

Scope (peaceful character):
There is no factual grounding quote for this element — it was implied solely by the Court's silence. No atomic fact addresses the peaceful or non-peaceful character of the assemblies. No mapping is possible.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences." This maps to F002, which is the closest atomic fact. No atomic fact specifically mentions arrest or conviction for administrative offences as a discrete fact — these details appear only in the reasoning section itself, not in the atomic facts provided.

Necessity:
The factual grounding was the same negative formulation ("the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion") and the same three acts (dispersal, arrest, conviction). This again maps only to F002. No atomic fact provides specific factual content about the proportionality of the measures, the authorities' justification, or the severity of the sanctions.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were participants in public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope analysis (assembly exists, participation). Without F002, there is no factual basis for the Court to treat Article 11 as engaged at all.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it identifies the measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) that constitute the interference. No other atomic fact provides this.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary — the Court's necessity finding is grounded in the same measures described in F002. Without knowing that dispersal, arrest, and conviction occurred, the Court cannot assess whether these were necessary in a democratic society.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire factual basis for this conclusion, as visible in the atomic facts, reduces to F002. This single fact establishes: (a) that there were public assemblies (scope); (b) that the applicants participated in them (scope); and (c) that disproportionate measures were taken against them (interference and necessity). F001 is formally necessary to identify who the applicants are, but adds no substantive factual content that the Court's legal reasoning turns on. F003 is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, the irreducible factual core is F002 alone.
