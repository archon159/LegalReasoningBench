LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists / participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
Note: The reference to arrests, dispersals, and convictions in the same quote also maps to F002 and F003 as the broader context of those complaints, since no separate atomic fact covers arrests or convictions independently.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002 and F003 again, as these are the only atomic facts that describe the measures complained of. There is no dedicated atomic fact for the arrests or convictions as standalone facts.

Necessity:
The factual grounding quote was: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."
This does not point to any specific atomic fact from the list — it is a purely procedural/negative statement. The closest atomic facts are again F002 and F003, as the "material submitted" would include the complaints described therein. F001 is also marginally relevant as it references the appended table containing the details of the applications, which would form part of "all the material submitted."

Peaceful character, prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:
As established in the prior analysis, there is no factual grounding in the reasoning text for these elements, and correspondingly no atomic facts map to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element of law

Scope (implied — assembly exists and participation):
The Court's implicit finding that assemblies existed and that the applicants were organisers or participants rests strictly on:
- F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants).
F001 is not strictly necessary — it points to a table of details but does not itself establish the nature of the assemblies or the applicants' roles.

Interference (explicitly implied):
The finding that state acts interfered with the applicants' freedom of assembly rests on the same facts:
- F002 and F003, which describe the disproportionate measures (arrests, dispersals, convictions) as the subject of the complaints.

Necessity (explicitly discussed):
The Court's necessity finding is grounded in the absence of any new fact displacing its leading-case conclusions. The only atomic facts that could be said to supply the factual substrate for this assessment are:
- F002 and F003, as the complaints about disproportionate measures are the very subject of the necessity analysis.
No atomic fact independently establishes the proportionality or disproportionality of the measures — the Court relies on precedent rather than case-specific facts.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:
Not discussed; no atomic facts map to these elements.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on two things: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants, and (2) that the state took measures against them (arrests, dispersals, convictions) that were not necessary in a democratic society. The necessity finding was reached by assimilation to leading cases, with no new facts displacing that conclusion.

The irreducible atomic facts are:
- F002: establishes that the applicants were organisers subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the core factual predicate for both the scope/interference and necessity findings in respect of organiser-applicants.
- F003: establishes the same for participant-applicants.

F001 is not strictly necessary for the conclusion — it is a procedural reference to an appended table. F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
