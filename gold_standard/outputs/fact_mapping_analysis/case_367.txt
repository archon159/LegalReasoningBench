LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists / participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
This maps to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies") which is essentially the same sentence in atomic form. F001 is also relevant insofar as the applicants and their details are referenced, but it adds no substantive factual content beyond identifying who the applicants are.

Scope (peaceful character):
No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text. No atomic fact corresponds to this sub-element.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
This again maps to F002, which captures the complaint about disproportionate measures taken against participants of public assemblies. The specific acts (dispersal, arrest, conviction) are embedded in F002.

Necessity:
The factual grounding was the Court's statement that it had "examined all the material submitted" and found no reason to depart from its prior case-law, and that the interferences were not "necessary in a democratic society." The only atomic fact that could ground this is F002, since the necessity assessment is triggered by and directed at the measures described in that fact. No atomic fact provides specific factual detail about the conduct of police, the behavior of participants, or the proportionality of sentences — the reasoning text itself did not identify any such facts.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. The Court's engagement with Article 11 at all depends on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' participation in them. F002 is the only atomic fact that establishes this. F001 is not strictly necessary — it identifies the applicants but does not establish the factual predicate for scope.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The interference (dispersal, arrest, conviction) is captured within F002. Without this fact, there is no basis for finding an interference with freedom of assembly.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element was not discussed and no atomic facts correspond to it.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element was not discussed and no atomic facts correspond to it.

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary. The necessity assessment is directed at the measures described in F002. The Court's conclusion that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society is grounded in the nature of those measures as described in F002.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire chain of reasoning — scope, interference, and necessity — rests on a single atomic fact: F002. This fact establishes (i) that there were public assemblies, (ii) that the applicants were participants, and (iii) that disproportionate measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) were taken against them. Without F002, the Court cannot reach any of its conclusions. F001 merely identifies the applicants and is not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

The irreducible factual core is therefore: F002.
