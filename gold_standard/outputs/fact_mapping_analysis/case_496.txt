LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists)**
The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and/or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly references public assemblies existing.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — also directly references public assemblies existing.

**Scope (participation)**
The factual grounding quote was: "as organisers and/or participants of public assemblies."
This maps to:
- F002: covers the organisers dimension.
- F003: covers the participants dimension.

**Scope (peaceful)**
No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text. No atomic facts correspond.

**Interference**
The factual grounding quote was: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as the applicants' arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
This maps to:
- F002: references disproportionate measures against organisers, which the Court treats as the interference.
- F003: references disproportionate measures against participants, which the Court treats as the interference.
Note: The specific measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) are named in the reasoning but are not independently spelled out in the atomic facts beyond the general reference to "disproportionate measures" in F002 and F003. F001 references the appended table which presumably contains case-specific details, but those details are not reproduced as atomic facts here.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**: The Court's reasoning on scope (implied) rests on the characterization of the applicants as organisers and participants of public assemblies. The most critical facts are F002 and F003, which establish both the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' roles in them. Both are necessary because the Court refers to both organisers and participants.

**Interference**: The Court's finding of interference rests on the existence of measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) taken against the applicants. F002 and F003 are the atomic facts that establish that measures were taken against organisers and participants respectively. These are the minimum necessary facts.

**Prescribed by law**: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Legitimate aim**: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity**: The Court's necessity reasoning rests on the similarity of the present case to prior leading cases. The facts that make the case comparable — that there were public assemblies and that disproportionate measures were taken against organisers and participants — are F002 and F003. These are the facts the Court implicitly relied upon when concluding that no distinguishing argument had been presented.

**Positive obligation**: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turned on: (1) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants (establishing scope and the applicability of Article 11); and (2) the fact that disproportionate measures were taken against them (establishing interference and, by analogy with prior case-law, the lack of necessity). These two points are captured by F002 and F003. F002 is essential for the organiser dimension and F003 for the participant dimension — together they cover the full range of applicants and establish the factual basis for the violation finding. No other atomic facts are strictly necessary: F001 points to a table with details but those details are not reproduced as atomic facts, and F004 concerns other complaints not relevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
