Scope (participation): The same quote — "organisers or participants of public assemblies" — maps to F002 for the same reasons.

Scope (peaceful): No factual grounding quote was identified in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact addresses the peaceful character of the assemblies. This maps to no atomic fact.

The only atomic fact that could be said to underlie this is F002, since the nature of the measures (disproportionate interference with assembly) is what the necessity analysis concerns.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation: These elements were not discussed and no factual grounding was identified. They map to no atomic facts.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies — without this, there is no assembly and no participation to speak of, and the Article 11 analysis cannot begin. F001 is not strictly necessary on its own; it merely points to a table.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The reference to "disproportionate measures" in F002 is the factual basis for the Court's finding that there was an interference (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction). Without F002, there is no factual anchor for the interference finding.

Prescribed by law: No atomic fact is strictly necessary here, as this element was not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic fact is strictly necessary here, as this element was not discussed.

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary. The necessity analysis concerns whether the measures taken against the applicants as organisers or participants of public assemblies were justified. F002 provides the only factual basis for this in the atomic facts.

Positive obligation: No atomic fact is strictly necessary here, as this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on: (1) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (2) the fact that disproportionate measures — arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction — were taken against them (interference and necessity). Both of these points are captured in F002. F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table and is not itself a substantive fact that the conclusion turns on. F003 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, the irreducible factual core is F002 alone.
