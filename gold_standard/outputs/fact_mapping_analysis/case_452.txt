LIST 1: Factual grounding for each element, mapped to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists / participation / peaceful):**
The Court's implied factual grounding for scope comes from the introductory characterisation: "The applicants in applications nos. 37565/08, 37581/08, 42250/08, and 39189/09 complained about the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of the public events, as well as the allegedly unlawful arrest of Mr Nagibin and Mr Nikolayev during some of those public events."
This maps to:
- F001 (restrictions on location of public events)
- F002 (restrictions on time of public events)
- F003 (restrictions on manner of public events)
- F004 (unlawful arrest complaints)
- F011 (three applicants involved in public events in 2007-2008 in Rostov-na-Donu)
- F057 (notification of picket, application 37565/08)
- F060 (Mr Nagibin proceeded with the static demonstration on 24 November 2007)
- F075 (notification of meeting and march, application 37581/08)
- F076 (notification of Dissenters' March, application 37581/08)
- F088 (Mr Nikolayev notified town administration of public events, application 42250/08)

For the negative scope finding (Mr Nikolayev's arrest complaint in 42250/08 declared inadmissible), the Court explicitly relied on:
- F091 (Mr Nikolayev intended to take part in the event organised by Mr Nagibin)
- F092 (police officers approached Mr Nikolayev while he was standing near a kiosk)
- F093 (Mr Nikolayev was told to follow police officers, with reasons unrelated to the assembly)
- F094 (Mr Nikolayev was brought to the Kirovskiy district police station)
The Court noted he "was not apprehended at the venue of the public event" — this is grounded in F092/F093/F094 (he was stopped near a kiosk, not at the picket venue) and F091 (he had intended to participate but had not yet arrived).
The Court also noted that "in the administrative proceedings Mr Nikolayev did not argue that the measures taken in his respect had infringed his freedom of assembly" — this is grounded in F095/F096 (he was charged with breach of public order, not assembly-related offence) and F110/F111 (he appealed but the proceedings concerned disorderly conduct, not assembly).

**Interference:**
The Court's explicit statement that "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly" occurred is grounded in the same introductory characterisation:
- F001 (restrictions on location)
- F002 (restrictions on time)
- F003 (restrictions on manner)
- F004 (unlawful arrests)
- F057/F058/F059 (town administration's response to picket notification, application 37565/08)
- F060/F061/F062/F063 (Mr Nagibin proceeded with picket, was apprehended and charged)
- F075/F076/F077/F078/F079/F080 (notification of march and administration's response, application 37581/08)
- F088/F089 (Mr Nikolayev's notifications and administration's location restrictions, application 42250/08)

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court's finding that "the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements" is not grounded in any specific atomic facts in the reproduced reasoning — the Court delegates entirely to Lashmankin. However, the factual predicate for there being a legal basis at all is:
- F058/F059 (town administration's reply citing legal compliance requirements, application 37565/08)
- F078/F079/F080 (town administration's response citing insufficiency of notification, application 37581/08)
- F089 (town administration imposed location restrictions on each of Mr Nikolayev's notifications, application 42250/08)

**Legitimate aim:**
Not discussed. No factual grounding.

**Necessity:**
The Court's finding that the interference was "moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'" is again delegated to Lashmankin with no independent factual analysis. The factual predicate is the same as for interference and prescribed by law — the restrictions imposed by the authorities:
- F001/F002/F003 (nature of restrictions)
- F058/F059 (administration's response to picket notification)
- F078/F079/F080 (administration's response to march notification)
- F089 (location restrictions on Mr Nikolayev's notifications)

**Positive obligation:**
Not discussed. No factual grounding.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two findings: (1) the legal provisions governing public assemblies did not meet quality of law requirements, and (2) the interference was not necessary in a democratic society. Both findings were made by reference to Lashmankin, with the factual predicate being that the authorities imposed restrictions on the location (and other conditions) of the applicants' planned public events, and that the applicants had notified the authorities and attempted to hold those events. The irreducible core is:
- F011 (applicants were involved in public events — establishes the assembly context)
- F057 (notification of picket in application 37565/08 — establishes the assembly and the trigger for the restriction)
- F060 (Mr Nagibin proceeded with the picket — establishes participation and the moment of interference)
- F061 (Mr Nagibin was apprehended during the picket — establishes the concrete interference)
- F075 (notification of meeting and march in application 37581/08 — establishes the assembly and trigger for restriction)
- F078 (administration's response citing insufficiency — establishes the interference and its legal basis)
- F088 (Mr Nikolayev notified of public events in application 42250/08 — establishes the assembly and trigger)
- F089 (administration imposed location restrictions on each of Mr Nikolayev's notifications — establishes the interference and its pattern, which is the core of the quality of law and necessity findings)
