**LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)**

The conclusion that Article 11 was violated requires, at minimum, establishing: (i) there was a planned assembly; (ii) the applicant intended to participate; (iii) state action prevented that participation (interference); and (iv) that interference was not justified. The Court resolves (iv) by reference to prior case law rather than independent factual analysis, so the irreducible factual core in this case is:
- F025: Opposition groups were planning to hold a demonstration on 24 May 2012. (the assembly)
- F026: The second applicant was planning to participate. (participation/scope)
- F027: The second applicant was stopped by plainclothes police officers on the morning of 24 May 2012. (the act of interference begins)
- F030: The second applicant was held at the police station for around seven hours. (the interference prevented participation — the demonstration was on 24 May 2012 and he was held throughout the day)
- F033: The Binagadi District Court convicted the second applicant of an administrative offence. (the conviction as a further element of interference/chilling effect)
These five facts are the irreducible core: without each of them, the Court could not have concluded that the second applicant's right to freedom of assembly was breached by arrest and conviction designed to prevent his participation in the demonstration.
