LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three pivotal issues: (1) the peaceful nature of both assemblies combined with the absence of justification for their dispersal; (2) the spontaneous nature of the second assembly and the existence of a legal exemption for spontaneous assemblies, undermining the lawfulness of the dispersal; and (3) the convictions and sanctions imposed without acknowledging that participation in a peaceful assembly is itself protected. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

- F009: demonstration of 20 October 2012 intended to be peaceful — establishes peaceful character of first assembly, central to finding no sufficient justification for dispersal
- F010: demonstration of 20 October 2012 conducted in a peaceful manner — confirms peaceful conduct, essential to necessity finding
- F019: when judgment announced, those outside began to protest — establishes spontaneous trigger of second assembly, essential to lawfulness and necessity analysis
- F020: protest of 6 May 2014 was brief, spontaneous and peaceful — establishes peaceful and spontaneous character of second assembly, essential to both prescribed by law and necessity findings
- F021: police immediately dispersed assembly as soon as protesters started chanting slogans — establishes the immediacy and lack of proportionate response, essential to necessity finding
- F043: convicted and sentenced to ten days' administrative detention — establishes the concrete interference and its severity, essential to chilling effect and necessity finding
- F044: convicted and sentenced to fifteen days' administrative detention — same for second case
- F053: Article 5 § IV of Law on Freedom of Assembly: no prior written notification required for spontaneous assemblies — establishes that the dispersal of the second assembly lacked legal basis, essential to prescribed by law analysis
- F005: organisers gave notice to BCEA on 15 October 2012 — establishes that the first assembly was notified, essential to the finding that authorities failed to take notification into account
- F042: first-instance court found applicant had failed to stop participating in unauthorised protest — establishes that the domestic courts failed to acknowledge the protected status of participation in a peaceful assembly, essential to necessity finding
