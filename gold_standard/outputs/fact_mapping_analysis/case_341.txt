LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three core findings under necessity: (1) the demonstration was small, brief, and peaceful; (2) the content of the slogans was political speech on a matter of public concern, not contempt of court; (3) the domestic decision imposing the maximum penalty lacked relevant and sufficient reasons and rested on a false factual premise. These are the facts that were essential:

- F012: The demonstration took place outside the Tbilisi Court of Appeal — establishes the assembly and its location.
- F033: The demonstration lasted only about three minutes — establishes the extremely short duration, undermining the disruption claim.
- F007: Five persons participated — establishes the minimal scale.
- F027: The only offensive phrase was directed at the Minister of Interior, not the judiciary — establishes that the speech could not constitute contempt of court.
- F034: The force came from the bailiffs, not the applicants — establishes the peaceful nature of the assembly.
- F067: The decision relied on the factually incorrect statement that the applicants were inside the court building — establishes that the key justification for the interference was untrue.
- F065: The President imposed thirty days' detention on the basis of the bailiffs' written submissions alone — establishes both the severity of the penalty and the one-sided basis of the decision.
- F117: Article 208 § 6 provided for detention of up to thirty days as the maximum — establishes that the most severe available penalty was imposed.
- F108: Section 76(3) § 1 (f) and F118: Article 208 § 6(1) applied only to conduct inside the court-house — establishes the foreseeability problem and the mismatch between the law and the facts.
- F019: The applicants were in the yard, not inside the building — establishes the factual mismatch with the legal provisions and the falsity of the decision's premise.
