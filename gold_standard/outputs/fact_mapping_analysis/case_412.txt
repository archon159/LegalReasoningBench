LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope — assembly exists**
Quote: 'The applicant in the present case attempted to organise a meeting of local residents to protest against the Moscow government's town-planning policy.'
Atomic facts: F004 (the NGO decided to hold an assembly of Krylatskoye district residents), F005 (purpose: protest against mayor's failure to respond), F006 (purpose: protest against planned construction), F007 (purpose: vote of no confidence), F009 (applicant and co-organisers informed the Prefecture), F011 (assembly scheduled for 4 September 2003), F012 (expected attendance of about a hundred persons).

**Scope — participation**
Quote: 'the applicant was fined for having taken part in an unauthorised assembly' and 'The applicant in the present case attempted to organise a meeting of local residents...'
Atomic facts: F020 (applicant was among those who gathered), F071 (applicant fined 1,000 roubles for organising an unauthorised assembly), F009 (applicant was one of the co-organisers who notified the Prefecture).

**Scope — peaceful character**
No factual grounding identified in the Court's own reasoning. However, the following atomic facts are relevant background: F019 (a few dozen residents gathered), F021 (no loudspeakers deployed), F022 (no attempts to start a general discussion). These are not explicitly cited by the Court for this purpose.

**Interference**
Quote: 'permission for the assembly was withdrawn on the eve of the day when it was due to take place. The meeting was dispersed by the police and the applicant was fined for having taken part in an unauthorised assembly.'
Atomic facts: F016 (prefect cancelled his decision of 29 August 2003 on 3 September 2003), F017 (reason given: operative information about terrorist activities), F018 (police instructed to prevent the assembly), F023 (police dispersed the crowd by force), F071 (applicant fined for organising an unauthorised assembly).

**Prescribed by law**
The Court does not make its own factual finding on this element. The applicant's submission (F074: Russian law did not permit cancellation of an authorised assembly) is noted but not adopted by the Court as a finding. No atomic facts are adopted by the Court as grounding for a prescribed-by-law conclusion.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court notes the Government's claim but does not independently establish the factual basis. The relevant background facts are: F017 (prefect's stated reason: operative information about terrorist activities), F087 (letter from police command citing information about potential terrorist attacks in places of mass gatherings). These are referenced in the Court's necessity analysis rather than as a separate legitimate aim finding.

**Necessity — Government's failure to produce evidence**
Quote: 'The Government's submissions in the present case were confined to the affirmation that the information on a potential terrorist attack had indeed been channelled by the law-enforcement authorities to the prefect... The Court notes that the Government did not corroborate the affirmation with any material or offer an explanation as to why it was not possible to produce evidence substantiating their allegation.'
Atomic facts: F017 (prefect's decision citing operative information), F087 (letter from police command to prefect citing terrorist threat information).

**Necessity — what happened in domestic proceedings (attempts to obtain evidence)**
Quote: 'It appears that the District Court initially considered such evidence relevant to the proceedings on the applicant's complaint and made attempts to obtain it from the police. The local police replied that it had no such information, whereas the district police command refused to make it available to the judge, citing its confidential character.'
Atomic facts: F078 (District Court asked Krylatskoye police station to produce operative information), F079 (District Court asked police command of Western Administrative District to produce operative information), F080 (Krylatskoye police station replied it had no such information), F081 (police command replied material was classified and could not be made available).

**Necessity — refusal to relinquish jurisdiction**
Quote: 'The applicant then asked the court to make use of the possibility provided for in domestic law of relinquishing jurisdiction to a higher court competent to examine classified information. The District Court refused that request, holding that the availability of such information was no longer relevant for determination of the lawfulness of the prefect's decision.'
Atomic facts: F082 (applicant asked District Court to relinquish jurisdiction to City Court), F083 (District Court refused the request), F084 (District Court held the purpose was to establish lawfulness of prefect's decision, not whether information existed).

**Necessity — improper shifting of burden of proof**
Quote: 'the District Court posited the assumption that the information had actually existed and "presupposed the threat of violence" against Moscow residents. It also held that the applicant had failed to disprove that information by producing evidence to contradict it. The Court observes that, in doing so, the District Court shifted onto the applicant the burden of proof, which was obviously impossible to satisfy without access to the police files.'
Atomic facts: F088 (District Court found no evidence contradicting the information in the police letter had been produced), F085 (District Court rejected the applicant's complaint).

**Necessity — District Court's unsupported finding of real threat**
Quote: 'The District Court also stated – without specifying the reasons for that finding – that the terrorist threat had been not merely potential but a real one which justified the police's forcible actions in dispersing a public assembly which endangered the life and limb of citizens.'
Atomic facts: F091 (District Court found police actions compatible with Moscow regulations and Russian Constitution, aimed at preventing mass action in connection with a real threat to life, health and security of citizens), F085 (District Court rejected the applicant's complaint).

**Necessity — City Court endorsement in summary fashion**
Quote: 'In reviewing the matter on appeal, the City Court endorsed the District Court's reasoning in summary fashion.'
Atomic facts: F095 (Moscow City Court rejected the applicant's appeal in summary fashion).

**Necessity — selective cancellation / Day of the City festivities**
Quote: 'it transpires from the public documents and media reports – submitted by the applicant and not contradicted by the Government – that the public festivities organised by the Moscow mayor and government had proceeded in accordance with the approved programme on the days immediately following the scheduled date of the applicant's meeting. Although the number of participants at those festivities significantly exceeded the number expected for the applicant's planned meeting, that meeting was the only public event to have been cancelled on account of "an expected outbreak of terrorist activities".'
Atomic facts: F024 (Day of the City celebrated on 5, 6 and 7 September 2003), F025 (programme approved by Moscow Government resolution), F026 (programme included sixty-one events), F028 (events took place in major public thoroughfares), F029 (applicant submitted media reports showing no scheduled events had been cancelled), F030 (festivities attended by thousands of people), F012 (applicant's assembly expected to be attended by about a hundred persons).

**Positive obligation**
No factual grounding identified. The Court mentions the positive obligation only in its statement of general principles and does not apply it to specific facts.

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was a planned assembly, that the applicant participated/organised it, and (implied) that it was peaceful.
- F004: the NGO decided to hold an assembly — establishes the assembly existed
- F009: applicant was a co-organiser who notified the Prefecture — establishes participation/organisation
- F011: assembly scheduled for 4 September 2003 — establishes the specific assembly at issue
- F020: applicant was among those who gathered — establishes actual participation

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish the three measures it identifies: withdrawal of permission, dispersal, and fine.
- F016: prefect cancelled his decision on 3 September 2003 — withdrawal of permission
- F023: police dispersed the crowd by force — dispersal during assembly
- F071: applicant fined for organising an unauthorised assembly — punitive measure after assembly

**Prescribed by law**
The Court makes no finding. No atomic facts are strictly necessary for a Court finding on this element.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court does not independently establish the factual basis for a legitimate aim. The only relevant background fact is:
- F017: prefect's stated reason (operative information about terrorist activities) — this is the claimed aim, but the Court does not accept it as established

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity analysis turns on: (a) absence of evidence for the terrorist threat, (b) the domestic courts' flawed reasoning, and (c) the selective cancellation of only the applicant's meeting.
- F017: prefect's stated reason — the claimed justification the Court scrutinises
- F080: Krylatskoye police station had no such information — undermines the factual basis for the threat
- F081: police command refused to produce material, citing confidentiality — explains why evidence was unavailable
- F083: District Court refused to relinquish jurisdiction — prevented proper examination of classified material
- F088: District Court found no contradicting evidence had been produced — shows improper burden-shifting
- F091: District Court found police actions justified by a real threat — the unsupported finding the ECtHR criticises
- F095: City Court endorsed District Court reasoning in summary fashion — shows appellate endorsement without independent scrutiny
- F024: Day of the City celebrated on 5-7 September 2003 — the comparator event
- F028: Day of the City events took place in major public thoroughfares — same type of mass gathering
- F029: no scheduled Day of the City events were cancelled — the critical contrast
- F030: festivities attended by thousands — shows larger gatherings were not cancelled
- F012: applicant's assembly expected about a hundred persons — contrast with scale of Day of the City

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts are strictly necessary; the Court does not apply this element to the facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two interlocking findings: (1) the Government failed to substantiate the terrorist threat that was the sole stated justification for banning the assembly; and (2) the selective cancellation of only the applicant's anti-government meeting, while the Moscow government's own much larger festivities proceeded unimpeded, demonstrated that the ban was arbitrary. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

- F016: the prefect cancelled the assembly authorisation on 3 September 2003 — establishes the interference
- F017: the stated reason was operative information about terrorist activities — establishes the only justification offered
- F080: Krylatskoye police station had no such operative information — directly undermines the stated justification
- F081: police command refused to produce the material, citing confidentiality — explains the evidentiary gap and the Government's failure to substantiate
- F088: District Court found no contradicting evidence had been produced — shows the domestic court improperly shifted the burden
- F091: District Court found the threat was real and justified police action — the unsupported domestic finding the ECtHR rejects
- F095: City Court endorsed District Court reasoning in summary fashion — shows the domestic review was inadequate
- F024: Day of the City celebrated on 5-7 September 2003 — the comparator event establishing selective treatment
- F029: no scheduled Day of the City events were cancelled — the critical contrast showing arbitrariness
- F030: festivities attended by thousands — shows larger gatherings were not cancelled despite the alleged threat
- F023: police dispersed the crowd by force — confirms the interference was real and coercive
- F071: applicant fined for organising an unauthorised assembly — confirms the punitive dimension of the interference
