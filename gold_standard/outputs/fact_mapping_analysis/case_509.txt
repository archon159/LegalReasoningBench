LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — they establish that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants. Without these facts, the Court cannot even engage with Article 11.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are described as flowing from the applicants' roles as organisers or participants. These are the only atomic facts that ground the interference finding.
- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts — this element was not discussed.
- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts — this element was not discussed.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 — the necessity analysis, though thin, is grounded in the same underlying facts about the measures taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies.
- Positive obligation: No atomic facts — this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on the fact that the applicants — as organisers or participants of public assemblies — were subjected to disproportionate state measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction for administrative offences), and that these measures were found not necessary in a democratic society by analogy with the leading cases. The irreducible atomic facts are:
- F002: establishes that organisers of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the core factual predicate for the Article 11 analysis as applied to organiser-applicants.
- F003: establishes the same for participant-applicants.
These two facts are the only atomic facts in the provided list that are strictly necessary for the Court's conclusion. F001 and F004 are procedural or ancillary and do not bear on the substantive Article 11 conclusion.
