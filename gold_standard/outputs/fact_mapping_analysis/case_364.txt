LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the minimum factual basis for the Court to apply Article 11 at all. F001 is supportive (identifying the applicants) but not independently necessary for the legal reasoning.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it is the only atomic fact that references the measures (disproportionate measures in the context of assemblies) that constitute the interference. Without it, there is no factual basis for the interference finding.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that provides any factual backdrop for the necessity assessment (the assemblies and the measures taken). However, as noted, the Court's necessity reasoning is grounded almost entirely in prior case-law rather than case-specific facts. F002 remains the minimal factual anchor.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core for this conclusion is:

F002 — This is the single atomic fact that establishes (a) that the applicants were participants or organisers of public assemblies (scope), and (b) that disproportionate measures were taken against them (interference). Both of these are logically necessary preconditions for the Court to reach a violation finding under Article 11. Without F002, there is no assembly, no participation, no interference, and no basis for the necessity analysis. F001 identifies the applicants but is procedural; F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
