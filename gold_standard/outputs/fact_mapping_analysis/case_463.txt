The reasoning text states the applicants were "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and refers to "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies." The atomic facts that correspond to these quotes are:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies" — directly corresponds to the characterization of applicants as organisers.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies" — directly corresponds to the characterization of applicants as participants.
No atomic fact speaks to the peaceful character of the assemblies.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court must establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies before Article 11 can apply. F002 covers organisers and F003 covers participants. F001 is background and not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself.

(b) Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" referred to in these facts are the very acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction) that constitute the interference. Without these facts, there is no interference to assess.

(c) Prescribed by law: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

(d) Legitimate aim: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

(e) Necessity: F002 and F003 are the only atomic facts available that underlie the necessity assessment, since the measures complained of as disproportionate are the subject of the necessity finding. However, the Court's necessity reasoning is driven by precedent rather than specific facts, so the factual grounding here is thin.

(f) Positive obligation: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For this conclusion, the Court must have established: (1) that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (bringing them within Article 11's scope), and (2) that disproportionate measures were taken against them (establishing interference and grounding the necessity finding). Both F002 and F003 are essential — F002 for the organisers and F003 for the participants. These are the only atomic facts that do real work in the legal reasoning. F001 is purely administrative and F004 concerns other complaints not relevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
