LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists / participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This maps to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies.") which is essentially the same sentence. F001 is also relevant in that it establishes that there are identifiable applicants, but it adds no substantive content about assemblies or participation. F003 is not relevant to scope.

Scope (peaceful):
No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact speaks to the peaceful nature of the assemblies. No mapping is possible.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This maps to F002, which refers to "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies" — the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are the concrete instantiation of what F002 describes as "disproportionate measures." No other atomic fact captures this.

Necessity:
The Court's necessity finding was grounded not in specific case facts but in reference to prior case law and a general statement about having examined "all the material submitted to it." Among the atomic facts, F002 is the closest anchor, as it describes the measures complained of. F001 references the appended table containing case-specific details, which the Court presumably reviewed as part of "all the material submitted to it," but the Court does not explicitly adopt any specific fact from that table in its reasoning. No atomic fact directly corresponds to the necessity grounding.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:
None of these elements were discussed, and no atomic facts correspond to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It is the only atomic fact that establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the basis for the implied conclusions that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. F001 is not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself, only for identifying the parties.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The reference to "disproportionate measures" (arrest, dispersal, conviction) is the factual basis for the interference finding. Without F002, there is no factual anchor for the conclusion that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' freedom of assembly.

Necessity: No atomic fact is strictly necessary in the sense that the Court explicitly adopted a specific fact to ground its necessity finding. The Court relied on precedent and a general reference to the case file. However, F002 is the minimum factual anchor — it describes the nature of the measures, which is the subject of the necessity assessment. Without knowing what measures were taken, no necessity assessment is possible. F001 is referenced implicitly ("all the material submitted to it" presumably includes the appended table), but the Court does not adopt any specific fact from it explicitly.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as these elements were not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the doctrinal structure of Article 11, the Court must at minimum establish: (1) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (2) that governmental measures interfered with that participation (interference). Both of these rest on F002. The necessity finding itself is grounded in precedent rather than in any additional atomic fact beyond F002. F001 is referenced implicitly but no specific content from the appended table is adopted by the Court in its reasoning. Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact that is strictly necessary and sufficient to support the Court's path to its final conclusion, as grounded in the facts it explicitly adopted.
