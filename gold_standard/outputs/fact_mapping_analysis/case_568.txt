LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that there were public assemblies with organisers; F003 establishes that there were participants. Together they establish that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. No atomic fact supports the peacefulness sub-element.

- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The complaints of disproportionate measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) are grounded in these two facts. Without F002 and F003 there is no factual basis for any interference finding.

- Prescribed by law: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

- Legitimate aim: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

- Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court's necessity finding is grounded in the similarity of the present case to leading cases, and the "issues" referred to are the disproportionate measures against organisers (F002) and participants (F003). F001 is referenced only in the most general sense ("all the material submitted") and is not independently necessary beyond what F002 and F003 already establish.

- Positive obligation: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly — namely the dispersal of assemblies and the arrest and conviction of organisers and participants — were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core is:

- F002: establishes that there were public assemblies with organisers who were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the minimum fact needed to ground both the scope and interference elements for the organiser applicants.
- F003: establishes that participants of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the minimum fact needed to ground both the scope and interference elements for the participant applicants.

Without F002 and F003, the Court cannot establish that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated, or that any interference occurred — all of which are logically necessary preconditions for the necessity finding and thus for the final conclusion of a violation. F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary for the conclusion: F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints not relevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
