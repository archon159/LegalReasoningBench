LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied)
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies, and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
The reference to assemblies existing and being dispersed is captured by F002 and F003 together, as both presuppose the existence of public assemblies. F001 is background only. F004 is irrelevant to this element.

(b) Interference (explicit)
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies, and their conviction for administrative offences" — adopted by the Court through its use of the word "interferences."
This maps to:
- F002: covers the organisers dimension of the interference (arrest, dispersal, conviction as organiser).
- F003: covers the participants dimension of the interference (arrest, dispersal, conviction as participant).
No other atomic fact more precisely captures the specific acts of interference described.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)
The factual grounding was noted as absent in terms of case-specific facts — the Court relied on prior case-law and a general reference to "all the material submitted to it." The only case-level facts referenced were the same general description of measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction), which maps back to F002 and F003. No additional atomic fact corresponds to the necessity assessment.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope (implied): The Court's implied finding that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated, and that the assemblies were peaceful rests on the existence of "public assemblies" in which the applicants were "organisers" and "participants." The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003, as both are needed to establish the full scope (organisers and participants both covered). F002 alone would suffice to establish the existence of an assembly and participation by organisers; F003 adds participants. Together they are the minimum needed.
Strictly necessary: F002, F003.

(b) Interference (explicit): The Court's finding of interference rests on the same facts — the arrest, dispersal, and conviction of organisers and participants. Both F002 and F003 are necessary, as the Court refers to both roles.
Strictly necessary: F002, F003.

(c) Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.
Strictly necessary: none.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.
Strictly necessary: none.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit): The Court's necessity finding is grounded in its prior case-law rather than case-specific facts, but the predicate for applying the necessity test at all is that there were assemblies and interferences with them. The minimum factual basis is again F002 and F003.
Strictly necessary: F002, F003.

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.
Strictly necessary: none.

LIST 3: Atomic facts strictly necessary for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity finding — the Court concluded that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed: (1) the existence of public assemblies (implied by F002 and F003); (2) the applicants' role as organisers and/or participants (F002, F003); and (3) the fact that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against them (F002, F003). These are the irreducible factual core. F001 is purely administrative background. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Irreducible core: F002, F003.
