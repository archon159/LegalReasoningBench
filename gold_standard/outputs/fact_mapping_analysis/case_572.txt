LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope — all three sub-elements
The reasoning quotes are: "participants of peaceful public assemblies" and "the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies."
- F006: "On the dates indicated in the appended table, the applicants participated in various political demonstrations." — This is the atomic fact establishing both that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated in them. It is the direct factual referent of "participants in public assemblies."
- F007: "The applicants were apprehended at the venue of the demonstrations." — This corroborates participation (they were physically present at the venue).
- F018 and F019: The domestic courts' findings about shouting slogans and refusing to stop participating in assemblies for which no advance notification had been submitted. These are the only facts in the record that touch on the character of the assemblies and the applicants' conduct within them. The Court's characterisation of the assemblies as "peaceful" is implicitly grounded here — the domestic courts did not find any violent conduct, only participation in unnotified assemblies and refusal to comply with police orders.

(b) Interference
The reasoning quote is: "their arrest followed by their conviction for an administrative offence."
- F007: "The applicants were apprehended at the venue of the demonstrations." — This is the arrest.
- F008: "The applicants were transferred to various police stations in Moscow or St Petersburg after being apprehended." — This elaborates the arrest/detention.
- F013: "On the dates stipulated in the appended table, the applicants were convicted under Article 19.3 § 1 of the Code of Administrative Offences." — This is the conviction, the second limb of the interference.
- F014 and F015: The specific sanctions (fines and administrative imprisonment) imposed as a result of the conviction. These are the concrete measures constituting the interference.

(e) Necessity
The reasoning states: "the measures applied to the applicants as peaceful participants in the public assemblies did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society." The Court grounds this by reference to its leading cases and the absence of any new fact or argument. The atomic facts underlying "the measures applied" are the same as those for interference:
- F007, F008, F013, F014, F015: as above.
Additionally, the Court's necessity assessment implicitly draws on the nature of the assemblies:
- F006: participation in political demonstrations.
- F018, F019: the domestic courts' basis for conviction (unnotified assembly, refusal to comply) — these are the facts the Court implicitly found insufficient to justify the measures.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated, and that the assemblies were peaceful. The most critical atomic facts are:
- F006: establishes both the existence of assemblies ("various political demonstrations") and the applicants' participation in them. This is the irreducible core.
- F018/F019: the only facts in the record bearing on the character of the conduct — the domestic courts found no violence, only participation in unnotified assemblies and refusal to comply with police orders. These ground the "peaceful" characterisation by negative implication.

(b) Interference
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the governmental acts that interfered with the applicants' assembly rights:
- F007: the apprehension/arrest at the venue — this is the primary act of interference.
- F013: the conviction under Article 19.3 § 1 — this is the second and legally significant act of interference (the one that carries lasting consequences).
- F015: administrative imprisonment for some applicants — this is the most severe sanction and the most significant dimension of the interference for the necessity analysis.

(e) Necessity
The necessity finding turns on whether the measures corresponded to a pressing social need. The strictly necessary facts are those describing the measures applied and the context (peaceful assembly) in which they were applied:
- F006: the assemblies were political demonstrations — relevant to the weight of the right at stake.
- F013: conviction under Article 19.3 § 1 — the legal measure whose necessity is assessed.
- F015: administrative imprisonment — the most severe sanction, critical to the disproportionality finding.
- F018/F019: the domestic courts' stated reasons for conviction (unnotified assembly, refusal to comply) — these are the facts the Court implicitly found insufficient to justify the measures as corresponding to a pressing social need.

(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (f) Positive obligation: not discussed; no atomic facts map to these elements.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on: (i) the applicants participated in peaceful political assemblies; (ii) they were arrested and convicted; (iii) the sanctions included administrative imprisonment; (iv) the domestic courts' only stated basis was participation in unnotified assemblies and refusal to comply with police orders — conduct the Court (following its leading cases) found insufficient to justify the measures. The irreducible facts are:
- F006: participation in political demonstrations — establishes scope (assembly, participation) and the context for the necessity assessment.
- F007: apprehension at the venue — establishes the primary act of interference.
- F013: conviction under Article 19.3 § 1 — establishes the legal interference whose necessity is assessed.
- F015: administrative imprisonment — establishes the severity of the sanction, critical to the disproportionality finding.
- F018/F019: domestic courts' stated reasons — establish what the government relied on to justify the measures, which the Court found insufficient.
