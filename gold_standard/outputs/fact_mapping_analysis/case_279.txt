  - F002: The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies. [the measures — arrest, dispersal, conviction — are the interference complained of by organisers]

  - Note: The atomic facts do not separately itemise arrest, dispersal, or administrative conviction; F002 and F003 are the closest available atomic facts, as they refer to "disproportionate measures" which encompass these acts.

(e) Necessity
- Factual grounding: The Court states it examined "all the material submitted" and found no new facts or arguments to depart from prior case-law. No specific facts are cited. The only atomic facts that could be said to underlie the necessity analysis are again F002 and F003, since the "disproportionate measures" language in those facts is precisely what the necessity test evaluates. No atomic fact independently establishes facts about proportionality, conduct of authorities, or weight of legitimate aim.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that there were public assemblies and that some applicants were organisers; F003 establishes that other applicants were participants. Together they ground the implied findings that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. Without these two facts, the Court could not apply Article 11 at all. The peaceful character of the assemblies is not grounded in any atomic fact.

- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" referred to in both facts are the factual basis for the Court's identification of an interference (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction). Both are needed because the applicants include both organisers (F002) and participants (F003).

- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts correspond to this element. It was not discussed.

- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts correspond to this element. It was not discussed.

- Necessity: F002 and F003 are the only atomic facts that could support the necessity analysis, since the Court's finding that the measures were not necessary in a democratic society is grounded in the existence of those measures against organisers and participants. However, the Court does not cite specific facts about proportionality — it relies on prior case-law. F002 and F003 are nonetheless the minimal factual foundation.

- Positive obligation: No atomic facts correspond to this element. It was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two things: (1) that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (bringing them within the scope of Article 11), and (2) that disproportionate measures were taken against them (establishing interference and grounding the necessity finding). Both F002 and F003 are strictly necessary: F002 covers organisers and F003 covers participants. Without both, the Court could not have reached its conclusion as to the full set of applicants. No other atomic fact is essential — F001 is merely a procedural reference and F004 concerns unrelated complaints.
