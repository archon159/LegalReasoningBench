LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
- "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies": This maps to F002 (organisers), F003 (participants). The reference to "public assemblies" and their "dispersal" is contained within the same sentences as F002 and F003, so these are the closest atomic facts. F001 is background only.
- "organisers and participants of public assemblies": F002 and F003 directly.
- Peacefulness: no atomic fact corresponds to this — there is no atomic fact addressing the character or conduct of the assemblies.

(b) Interference
- "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences": This is contained within the complaints described in F002 and F003 ("disproportionate measures"). No separate atomic fact isolates the arrest or conviction as a standalone fact — these details are embedded in the complaint framing of F002 and F003.

(e) Necessity
- "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion": This is a negative finding — no specific atomic fact is pointed to. The Court relies on similarity to prior cases rather than on any specific fact from the present case. The only atomic facts that could be said to underlie this are again F002 and F003, since the "issues similar to those in the present case" concern the treatment of organisers and participants of public assemblies.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of law

- Scope: The Court's implied finding that there was a (peaceful) assembly in which the applicants participated rests on F002 and F003. F002 establishes the applicants as organisers of public assemblies; F003 establishes them as participants. These are the minimum facts needed to satisfy the scope element. No atomic fact addresses peacefulness, so that sub-element has no factual support beyond the implicit framing.
- Interference: The interference finding (arrest and conviction) is embedded in the "disproportionate measures" language of F002 and F003. These are the only atomic facts that could ground this element.
- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.
- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.
- Necessity: The necessity finding is grounded in the same facts as scope and interference — F002 and F003 — since the Court's conclusion that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society is based on the treatment of organisers and participants of public assemblies, by reference to prior cases involving similar issues.
- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on two things: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants, and (2) that disproportionate measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against them. Both of these are captured by F002 and F003. F001 is purely administrative background. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. The irreducible core is therefore F002 and F003.
