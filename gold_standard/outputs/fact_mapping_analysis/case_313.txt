LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's finding that the assembly was peaceful rests critically on: F032 (application to hold a peaceful rally), F034 (purpose was support for freedom of speech). The absence of any public order intent is the key factual point; F032 and F034 are the most critical.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The interference finding rests on the single act of rejection: F035 (Municipality rejected Hyde Park's application on 7 October 2005). This is the irreducible fact establishing interference.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's analysis of lawfulness turns on: (1) what the law required — F069 (Section 12(6): rejection only on grounds of sections 6 and 7); (2) what the Municipality actually did — F035 and F036/F037 (the stated reasons, which do not correspond to sections 6 and 7); (3) the exclusivity of local authority competence — F069; (4) the lateness of the court decisions — F033 (planned date), F044 (Court of Appeal decision date), F054 (Supreme Court decision date). The most critical facts are F069 (the legal standard), F036/F037 (the actual reasons given, which fall outside that standard), and F044/F054 (lateness of judicial decisions).

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts are necessary; the Court declined to decide this point.

--- NECESSITY ---
The necessity analysis turns on: (1) the Municipality's reasons — F036 (other events scheduled); (2) the absence of capacity concerns — F036 (implicitly, no evidence the park was too small); (3) the peaceful nature of the planned rally — F032, F034; (4) the inconsistency of the reasons with the Assemblies Act — F069, F056, F057; (5) the disregarding of court decisions — F033, F044, F054. The most critical facts are F036 (the only reason given by the Municipality), F032/F034 (peaceful nature of the rally), and F069 (the legal standard against which the reason is measured).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. No atomic facts.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: Hyde Park applied to hold a peaceful rally (F032) in support of freedom of speech (F034) on 14 October 2005 (F033). The Municipality rejected that application (F035) solely on the ground that other events were scheduled in the park (F036). That reason did not correspond to the only grounds on which rejection was legally permissible under Section 12(6) of the Assemblies Act (F069) — namely evidence that sections 6 and 7 would be breached with serious consequences (F056, F057). There was no suggestion the rally would endanger public order. The courts gave additional reasons (F044, F054) but these came after the planned date of the rally and were therefore disregarded. These are the facts the conclusion actually turned on.
