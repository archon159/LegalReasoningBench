LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists): The reasoning text states "public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies" — this maps to F002 and F003, which describe the applicants as organisers and participants of public assemblies respectively. F001 is also relevant as it references the appended table containing details of the applications, which would include details of the assemblies.

Scope (participation): The reasoning text states "organisers and/or participants of public assemblies" — this maps directly to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants).

Scope (peaceful): No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and none of the atomic facts F001-F004 address the peaceful character of the assemblies. No mapping possible.

Interference: The reasoning text states "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence" — this maps to F002 and F003, as the measures were taken against the applicants as organisers and participants. F001 may also be relevant as the details of each application (including the specific measures) are in the appended table.

Necessity: The Court states it examined "all the material submitted to it" and found nothing to distinguish the case from its prior case-law. The only factual anchor in the atomic facts is F002 and F003, describing the disproportionate measures against organisers and participants. No atomic fact speaks to the specific circumstances that would ground a necessity analysis.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's finding that Article 11 is engaged requires that there were public assemblies and that the applicants participated in them. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003 — these establish that the applicants were organisers and participants of public assemblies. F001 is a reference fact pointing to the table but does not itself establish the substance.

Interference: The Court explicitly refers to dispersal, arrest, and conviction as the interferences. These measures are described as having been taken against the applicants as organisers (F002) and participants (F003). Both F002 and F003 are strictly necessary.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Necessity: The Court's necessity finding is grounded in the characterisation of the measures as disproportionate, which is reflected in F002 and F003 ("disproportionate measures"). These are the only atomic facts that bear on the necessity assessment, even if the Court's reasoning is primarily driven by case-law rather than case-specific facts.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For this conclusion, the Court needed to establish: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope — F002, F003), and (2) that governmental measures were taken against them (interference — F002, F003). The description of the measures as "disproportionate" in F002 and F003 also directly supports the necessity finding. F004 is not necessary for the Article 11 conclusion as it concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions. F001 is a purely administrative reference fact. The irreducible core is therefore F002 and F003, which together establish the existence of assemblies, the applicants' roles as organisers and participants, and the fact that disproportionate measures were taken against them — the essential factual foundation for the violation finding.
