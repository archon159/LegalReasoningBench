For the existence of assemblies and participation:
- F005: applicant took part in rally on 4 December 2011

The Court presumes legitimate aim from the legal framework:

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three essential findings: (1) the assemblies were peaceful and the applicant was an ordinary participant; (2) the convictions were based solely on non-notification/non-approval, without adequate judicial reasoning establishing that dispersal orders were given and disobeyed; and (3) the penalties were disproportionate. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

Peaceful participation as ordinary demonstrator:
- F001: case concerns participation in peaceful assemblies
- F005: participation in rally on 4 December 2011
- F006: rally was spontaneous protest against alleged election fraud on same day
- F031: participation in protest rally on 6 December 2011 relating to recent election
- F064: participation in rally on 31 December 2012
- F075: participation in public assembly on 5 April 2013

Convictions based solely on non-notification, without adequate reasoning on dispersal orders:
- F018: courts held event not notified (4 Dec 2011) as basis for conviction
- F030: Deputy President stated it was not 'possible' to ascertain whether police had acted lawfully (4 Dec 2011)
- F036: offence report for 6 Dec 2011 based only on breach of notification requirement
- F065: applicant arrested allegedly prior to hearing any specific order (31 Dec 2012)
- F068: court only found police informed participants of non-notification, not that specific dispersal order was given (31 Dec 2012)
- F079: appeal court found police officer only once pronounced phrase that event was unlawful (5 Apr 2013)
- F080: video did not confirm repeated orders to cease participation (5 Apr 2013)
- F082: conviction under Art 19.3 for disobedience to dispersal order was quashed (5 Apr 2013)
- F090: reviewing court reclassified case without assessing reality of orders (5 Apr 2013)

Disproportionate penalties:
- F039: eleven-day detention for 6 December 2011
- F067: maximum fine of 20,000 roubles (EUR 497) for 31 December 2012
- F145: ten-fold increase in fines introduced June 2012 creating chilling effect
