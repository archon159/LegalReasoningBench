LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope:
- F002 is strictly necessary: it establishes that there were LGBT public assemblies that were banned, which implies assemblies were planned and applicants sought to hold them. This is the minimum fact needed to establish that Article 11's scope is engaged.
- F003 adds that the applicants made applications to hold events, confirming participation was attempted. However, F002 alone already implies this since a ban presupposes applications or plans. F003 is the most direct evidence of participation attempts and is therefore included as strictly necessary for the participation sub-element.

Interference:
- F002 is strictly necessary: the ban imposed by domestic authorities is the governmental act constituting interference. This is the irreducible fact for interference.

Prescribed by law:
- No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element is not discussed.

Legitimate aim:
- No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element is not discussed.

Necessity:
- F002 is strictly necessary: the Court's necessity finding is grounded in the existence of the ban on LGBT public assemblies. Without F002, there is no act to assess for necessity.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the existence of a ban on LGBT public assemblies imposed by domestic authorities (F002). This is the single fact that drives the entire chain of reasoning: it establishes scope (assemblies were planned), interference (the ban is the governmental act), and the necessity finding (the ban did not correspond to a pressing social need). F003, while relevant to confirming that applicants sought to hold events, is subsidiary to F002 in the sense that the ban itself presupposes applications or plans. However, F003 is also necessary because it establishes that the applicants actually engaged with the authorities (made applications), which is what makes the interference concrete and personal to them rather than abstract. Without F003, the applicants would lack standing as persons directly affected. Therefore, both F002 and F003 are in the irreducible core.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table and does not bear on the substantive conclusion; F004 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11.
