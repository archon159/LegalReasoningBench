LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope:**
The Court refers to the assembly as 'the planned peaceful assembly' and notes the Government did not dispute the applicant's reliance on Article 11. The relevant atomic facts pointing to the existence of a planned assembly and the applicant's role as organiser are: F003 (notification of planned demonstration), F004 (scheduled time), F005 (location in front of Venyige Street prison), F006 (purpose of the demonstration). The peaceful character is asserted by the Court without explicit factual grounding beyond the label, but F006 (protest against political persecution) and the absence of any suggestion of violence are implicitly relevant. F003 also establishes the applicant's participation as organiser.

**Interference:**
The Court grounds interference solely on the Government's non-denial. No atomic facts from the list are explicitly cited by the Court for this element. However, the existence of a ban presupposes a planned assembly: F003, F004, F005 are the background facts that make the interference intelligible. The Court does not cite them explicitly for this element, but they are the necessary backdrop.

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court states: 'The Court observes that the ban was based on section 8(1) of the Assembly Act and considers that it was thus prescribed by law.' This refers to the legal basis for the ban, not to any of the atomic facts listed (F001-F008). None of the atomic facts directly correspond to this finding — it is grounded in the legal framework, not the factual circumstances.

**Legitimate aim:**
The Court refers to traffic concerns and the rights of other road users. The atomic facts relevant here are F005 (location on Venyige Street, a public road), F007 (Venyige Street is a broad cul-de-sac), F008 (Venyige Street has a service lane). These are the facts that make the traffic-related legitimate aim intelligible, though the Court does not cite them explicitly for this element.

**Necessity:**
The Court explicitly grounds its necessity analysis in the following:
- 'the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues' — this relates to F005, F007, F008 (the location and its road characteristics).
- 'the demonstration would have seriously hampered the free flow of traffic in the area' — again F005, F007, F008.
- 'a similar problem relating to the very same location' (reference to Körtvélyessy) — F005 (Venyige Street location).
- The planned peaceful assembly — F003, F004, F005, F006.
F007 and F008 are particularly relevant because the road characteristics (broad cul-de-sac with service lane) were central to the traffic disruption argument that the Court found insufficient.

**Positive obligation:**
Not discussed. No atomic facts correspond.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:**
F003 (the applicant notified police of a planned demonstration — establishes assembly exists and applicant's participation as organiser) is the most critical. F005 (location) and F006 (purpose) support the existence of a planned assembly. F004 (scheduled time) is secondary. The most critical and representative: F003, F005, F006.

**Interference:**
The interference is the ban on the demonstration. The strictly necessary fact is that there was a planned demonstration: F003. Without a planned assembly, there is no interference to speak of.

**Prescribed by law:**
None of the atomic facts F001-F008 are strictly necessary here. The prescribed-by-law finding rests entirely on the legal framework (section 8(1) of the Assembly Act), not on the factual circumstances. List is empty.

**Legitimate aim:**
The legitimate aim of protecting the rights of others (road users) and preventing disorder is grounded in the location being a public road. F005 (location on Venyige Street) is the critical fact — it is what makes the traffic-related aim relevant. F007 (cul-de-sac) and F008 (service lane) add detail but F005 is the essential one.

**Necessity:**
The necessity analysis turns on whether the traffic disruption justification was sufficient. The critical facts are: F005 (the location — Venyige Street, which is the 'very same location' as in Körtvélyessy), F007 (broad cul-de-sac — relevant to the traffic disruption assessment), F008 (service lane — relevant to whether traffic could be accommodated). F003 is also necessary as it establishes the planned assembly that was banned.

**Positive obligation:**
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on whether the ban on the demonstration at Venyige Street was necessary in a democratic society, specifically whether the traffic disruption justification was sufficient. The Court found it was not, relying on the characteristics of the location (the same location as in Körtvélyessy) and the exclusively traffic-based reasoning of the domestic court.

The irreducible facts are:
- F003: The applicant planned and notified a demonstration — without this, there is no case.
- F005: The location (Venyige Street prison) — this is what connects the case to Körtvélyessy and makes the traffic argument the central issue.
- F007: Venyige Street is a broad cul-de-sac — this is the key physical characteristic that the Court (via Körtvélyessy) found made the traffic disruption argument insufficient to justify the ban.
- F008: Venyige Street has a service lane — this further supports the finding that the road could accommodate the demonstration without disproportionate disruption.

F004 (scheduled time) and F006 (purpose) are contextual but not what the case turned on. F001 and F002 (personal details) are irrelevant to the legal conclusion.
