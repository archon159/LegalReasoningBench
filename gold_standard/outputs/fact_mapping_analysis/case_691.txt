LIST 1: Factual grounding mapped to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---

The Court's factual grounding for scope was:
1. 'The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention' — this refers to the assembly itself existing and having been assessed in prior proceedings. The relevant atomic facts are: F001 (the case concerns a political rally on 6 May 2012 at Bolotnaya Square), F011 (background facts on the assembly are set out in Frumkin v. Russia), F012 (both applicants participated in the rally).

2. 'It was not disputed that both applicants had participated in the assembly.' — F012 (both applicants participated in the political rally on 6 May 2012).

3. 'it is common ground that they were not among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character.' — F002 (applicants were neither convicted of mass disorder nor accused of any other offence), F014 (according to the applicants, they neither violated public order nor resisted the police), F037 (neither applicant was arrested), F038 (neither applicant was charged), F053 (the investigator's decision did not indicate that the first applicant had acted aggressively or shown disobedience), F054 (the first applicant was not listed among persons apprehended on 6 May 2012).

--- INTERFERENCE ---

The Court's factual grounding was: 'The Court notes that it has found a violation of Article 3 of the Convention on account of the applicants' ill-treatment by police during the public assembly.' This finding under Article 3 is grounded in the facts of the physical violence inflicted on the applicants during the assembly: F015 (police allegedly hit the first applicant on the head with a rubber truncheon), F016 (police allegedly hit the second applicant on the head with a rubber truncheon), F022 (photographs show the first applicant with his face bleeding), F027 (medical certificate diagnosing the first applicant with a contused wound of the frontal lobe), F029 (videos show the second applicant surrounded by police when she fell and started screaming), F031 (ambulance called for the second applicant at Bolotnaya Square), F032 (medical certificate attesting head injury for the second applicant), F033 (medical certificate attesting soft tissue bruises on the right side of the second applicant's head), F037 (neither applicant was arrested — relevant to showing the force was not incidental to a lawful arrest of the applicants themselves).

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---

The Court accepted the Government's submission that 'the Police Act allowed police officers to use force and special equipment in order to, inter alia, prevent a crime and suppress resistance shown towards a police officer.' The relevant atomic facts are: F104 (Section 20 of the Police Act — use of physical force to prevent criminal and administrative offences), F105 (Section 20 — use of physical force to arrest individuals who committed offences), F106 (Section 20 — use of physical force to overcome resistance to lawful orders), F107 (Section 21 — exhaustive list of circumstances for use of special means including rubber truncheons), F108 (Section 21 — rubber truncheons permitted to repel attack on civilians or police), F109 (Section 21 — rubber truncheons permitted to overcome resistance to a police officer), F110 (Section 21 — rubber truncheons permitted to repress mass disorder).

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---

The Court found the interference pursued 'the legitimate aim of the prevention of disorder and crime.' This flows directly from the same legal framework as prescribed by law, and from the factual context of the assembly turning disorderly. The relevant atomic facts are: F104, F105, F106, F107, F109, F110. No additional independent factual grounding is offered by the Court at this stage beyond the legal framework already cited.

--- NECESSITY ---

The Court's factual grounding was:
1. 'the Government have not submitted any explanations as to why force had to be applied in respect of the applicants, who were not arrested and did not engage in any acts of violence.' — F037 (neither applicant was arrested), F038 (neither applicant was charged), F002 (applicants were neither convicted of mass disorder nor accused of any offence), F053 (investigator's decision did not indicate the first applicant had acted aggressively or shown disobedience), F054 (first applicant not listed among persons apprehended).

2. 'In the light of its finding that the force used in respect of the applicants was unnecessary and excessive and thus contrary to Article 3 of the Convention' — this imports the Article 3 factual findings: F015 (blow to the first applicant's head with rubber truncheon), F016 (blow to the second applicant's head with rubber truncheon), F027 (medical certificate — contused wound of the frontal lobe for first applicant), F032 (head injury for second applicant), F033 (soft tissue bruises for second applicant), F111 (Section 21 of the Police Act prohibited hitting a person on the head with a rubber truncheon — this is critical because it shows the force used was not even permitted under the law invoked), F112 (Section 22 prohibited use of special means to suppress an unlawful but peaceful manifestation).

3. 'it could have had a chilling effect and discouraged the applicants and others from taking part in similar public gatherings.' — No specific atomic fact is cited here; this is a consequential observation by the Court.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---

Not discussed. No atomic facts to map.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) an assembly existed; (ii) the applicants participated; (iii) the applicants were peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F012: both applicants participated in the rally (establishes participation)
- F002: applicants were neither convicted of mass disorder nor accused of any offence (establishes peaceful character)
- F037: neither applicant was arrested (corroborates peaceful character and non-involvement in disorder)
- F053: investigator's decision did not indicate the first applicant had acted aggressively or shown disobedience (directly supports peaceful character as found by the Court)
- F054: first applicant not listed among persons apprehended (directly supports peaceful character)

F001 and F011 are background context for the assembly existing, but the Court simply imports the prior holding from Yaroslav Belousov; F001 is the most representative for the assembly's existence.

For List 2, the strictly necessary facts for scope are: F001 (assembly exists — representative), F012 (participation), F002 (peaceful — no conviction or accusation), F037 (not arrested), F053 (no aggression noted by investigator), F054 (not apprehended).

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court grounded interference in its Article 3 finding of ill-treatment. The strictly necessary facts are those that establish that police physically harmed the applicants during the assembly:
- F015 (blow to first applicant's head)
- F016 (blow to second applicant's head)
- F027 (medical certificate confirming injury to first applicant)
- F032 (medical certificate confirming head injury to second applicant)

The photographs and videos are corroborating but the medical certificates and the core factual allegations (accepted under Article 3) are the strictly necessary ones.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court accepted the Police Act as the legal basis. The strictly necessary facts are those provisions that the Court (via the Government's submission it accepted) relied upon:
- F104 (Section 20 — use of force to prevent offences)
- F106 (Section 20 — use of force to overcome resistance)
- F109 (Section 21 — rubber truncheons to overcome resistance)

These are the most directly relevant provisions to the situation at hand (the Court summarised the Police Act as allowing force 'to prevent a crime and suppress resistance').

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The legitimate aim finding flows from the same legal framework. The strictly necessary fact is:
- F110 (Section 21 — rubber truncheons to repress mass disorder): this is the most directly relevant provision to the aim of 'prevention of disorder'
- F104 (Section 20 — prevention of criminal offences): relevant to 'prevention of crime'

--- NECESSITY ---
The strictly necessary facts for the necessity finding are those that show the force was applied to applicants who were not violent, not arrested, and not among those causing disorder, and that the force used was prohibited even under the invoked legal framework:
- F037 (neither applicant was arrested — key to showing force was not used in the context of a lawful arrest of the applicants)
- F002 (applicants not convicted or accused of any offence — corroborates non-violent status)
- F053 (investigator's decision did not note aggression by first applicant — directly supports the Court's finding that no justification existed)
- F054 (first applicant not listed among those apprehended — directly supports the Court's finding)
- F111 (Section 21 prohibited hitting on the head with a rubber truncheon — shows the specific force used was not even permitted under the law)
- F015 (blow to first applicant's head — establishes the specific act that was prohibited)
- F016 (blow to second applicant's head — establishes the specific act that was prohibited)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: the applicants were peaceful participants in an assembly who were subjected to unjustified and excessive police force (specifically, blows to the head with rubber truncheons), which was not only disproportionate but also prohibited under the very law invoked to justify it, and for which the Government offered no specific justification in respect of these applicants. The irreducible facts are:

- F012: both applicants participated in the rally (establishes they were exercising Article 11 rights)
- F002: applicants were neither convicted of mass disorder nor accused of any offence (establishes peaceful character — essential for Article 11 to apply and for necessity to fail)
- F037: neither applicant was arrested (establishes that the force was not used in the context of a lawful arrest of the applicants — critical to the necessity finding)
- F015: police hit the first applicant on the head with a rubber truncheon (establishes the interference)
- F016: police hit the second applicant on the head with a rubber truncheon (establishes the interference)
- F111: Section 21 of the Police Act prohibited hitting a person on the head with a rubber truncheon (establishes that the specific force used was not even lawfully permitted — critical to both the Article 3 finding imported into Article 11 and the necessity analysis)
- F053: the investigator's decision did not indicate that the first applicant had acted aggressively or shown disobedience (establishes absence of justification for force against the first applicant specifically)
- F054: the first applicant was not listed among persons apprehended (corroborates absence of justification and peaceful character)
