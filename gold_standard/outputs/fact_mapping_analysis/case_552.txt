LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
- "organisers or participants of public assemblies" maps to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants).
- "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies" maps to F002 and F003, as the arrest presupposes presence at the assemblies in either capacity.
- Peaceful character: no atomic fact corresponds to this; as noted earlier, no factual grounding could be identified in the reasoning text, and none of the four atomic facts addresses the conduct or character of the assemblies.

(b) Interference
- "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" maps to F002 and F003, which are the only atomic facts describing the applicants' roles and the measures taken against them.

(e) Necessity
- "Having examined all the material submitted to it" is a generic reference with no specific atomic fact counterpart beyond the general record of the case. F001 (the appended table of applicants and application details) is the closest candidate as the repository of that material, but the Court does not point to any specific fact. F002 and F003 are implicitly in view insofar as the measures complained of are the subject of the necessity assessment. No atomic fact directly grounds the necessity conclusion, which rests on precedent rather than case-specific facts set out here.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. They establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the minimum factual predicate for the scope of Article 11 to be engaged. Without these facts, there is no assembly and no participation to speak of. The peaceful character has no atomic fact support and is therefore excluded.

(b) Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are described in the reasoning as having been taken against the applicants "as organisers or participants," and F002 and F003 are the atomic facts that establish those roles. No other atomic fact adds to this.

(c) Prescribed by law: No atomic facts correspond; this element was not discussed.

(d) Legitimate aim: No atomic facts correspond; this element was not discussed.

(e) Necessity: F002 and F003 are the only atomic facts in view, as they describe the measures complained of. However, as noted, the necessity conclusion rests on precedent rather than case-specific factual reasoning. These facts are necessary in the minimal sense that without them there would be no interference to assess for necessity, but the Court does not reason from them explicitly.

(f) Positive obligation: No atomic facts correspond; this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on the existence of public assemblies and the fact that the applicants — as organisers or participants — were subjected to measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) in connection with those assemblies. These are the facts that trigger the Article 11 analysis and ground the violation finding.

F002 and F003 are the irreducible core. F002 establishes the organiser dimension and F003 the participant dimension. Together they confirm that public assemblies took place, that the applicants were connected to them, and that disproportionate measures were taken against them — which is what the Court found to constitute a violation. F001 is a procedural reference (the appended table) and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions; neither is necessary for the Article 11 conclusion. The violation finding does not turn on F001 or F004.
