LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope

The factual grounding identified in the reasoning was the phrase \"disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies.\" This maps to:
- F002: \"The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies.\" — directly corresponds to the \"organisers\" part of the quote.
- F003: \"The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies.\" — directly corresponds to the \"participants\" part of the quote.

For peacefulness: no atomic fact corresponds. As noted previously, there is no factual grounding for this sub-element.

(b) Interference

The factual grounding identified was: \"their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences.\" This maps to:
- F002 and F003: These facts establish that the applicants were organisers or participants against whom measures were taken, which is the basis for identifying the interference. No atomic fact specifically describes the arrest or conviction in isolation, but F002 and F003 are the closest available atomic facts, as they describe the \"disproportionate measures\" taken against the applicants in the context of public assemblies.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society

The factual grounding identified was the Court's reference to leading cases and its statement that it found no fact or argument capable of displacing those conclusions. The Court does not ground its necessity assessment in any specific atomic fact from the present case — it relies on precedent. The only atomic facts that are even implicitly relevant are F002 and F003, which establish that the applicants were organisers or participants against whom measures were taken (the same facts that ground the interference element). No atomic fact specifically addresses the necessity assessment.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. They establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies — without this, the Court cannot even frame the complaint as falling within the scope of Article 11. F002 covers organisers; F003 covers participants. Both are needed because the applicant group includes both categories.

- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The \"disproportionate measures\" referred to in these facts are the basis for identifying the governmental acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction) that constitute the interference. Without these facts, there is no interference to assess.

- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts support this element.

- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts support this element.

- Necessity: F002 and F003 are the minimum necessary facts, as they establish the context (measures against organisers/participants of assemblies) that the Court assessed against its precedents. However, the Court's necessity reasoning is almost entirely precedent-driven and does not rely on case-specific atomic facts beyond this framing.

- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts support this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on the existence of governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) taken against people who were organisers or participants of public assemblies. The irreducible factual core is:

- F002: Establishes that organisers of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the essential fact that triggers Article 11 analysis and grounds the interference finding for the organiser applicants.
- F003: Establishes the same for participants — essential for the participant applicants.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary for the Article 11 conclusion: F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions, neither of which bears on the Article 11 violation finding.
