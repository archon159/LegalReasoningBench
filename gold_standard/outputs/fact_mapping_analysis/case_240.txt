LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied)
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of public events."
This maps to:
- F002 (restrictions on location of public events)
- F003 (restrictions on time of conduct of public events)
- F004 (restrictions on manner of conduct of public events)
These three atomic facts together constitute the factual basis from which the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation can be inferred.

(b) Interference (explicit)
The factual grounding quotes were:
1. "The applicants complained principally of the restrictions imposed by the authorities on the location, time or manner of conduct of public events."
2. "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were based on legal provisions..."
The first quote maps to F002, F003, F004. The second is the Court's own legal characterization rather than a factual finding, so it does not map to a separate atomic fact — it is the Court's conclusion drawn from F002, F003, F004.

(c) Prescribed by law (explicit)
The factual grounding quote was: "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were based on legal provisions which did not meet the Convention's 'quality of law' requirements."
This is a legal conclusion, not a factual finding. The underlying factual basis is still the existence of the restrictions themselves: F002, F003, F004. No atomic fact specifically addresses the quality of the legal provisions.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed; no factual grounding identified; no atomic facts map to this element.

(e) Necessity (explicit)
The factual grounding quote was: "moreover not 'necessary in a democratic society'."
Again, this is a legal conclusion with no independent factual grounding in the atomic facts beyond the existence of the restrictions: F002, F003, F004.

(f) Positive obligation
Not discussed; no factual grounding identified; no atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope (implied): The Court's implicit finding that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated rests on the existence of the public events that were restricted. The most critical atomic facts are F002, F003, and F004, since these establish that there were public events (assemblies) and that the applicants were affected by restrictions on them. F001 is background and not strictly necessary for the doctrinal reasoning.

(b) Interference (explicit): The Court's finding of interference rests directly on the fact that restrictions were imposed on the applicants' public events. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002, F003, and F004 — these are what the Court points to when it speaks of "interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly." All three are necessary because the Court's description of the complaints encompasses all three types of restriction.

(c) Prescribed by law (explicit): The Court's finding that the legal provisions did not meet the "quality of law" requirements is grounded in the same factual basis — the existence of restrictions imposed via legal provisions. The strictly necessary atomic facts remain F002, F003, F004. No atomic fact specifically addresses the content or quality of the legal provisions, so the Court's conclusion here is essentially imported from Lashmankin with only the existence of the restrictions as factual anchor.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts strictly necessary.

(e) Necessity (explicit): The Court's finding that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society is again grounded in the existence of the restrictions. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002, F003, F004. As with prescribed by law, the substantive analysis is imported from Lashmankin.

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two findings: (i) the legal provisions imposing restrictions on public events did not meet the quality of law requirements, and (ii) the restrictions were not necessary in a democratic society. Both findings are grounded in the existence of the restrictions themselves. The irreducible factual core is therefore:

- F002: restrictions on location — this is the most paradigmatic type of restriction addressed in Lashmankin and the one most directly tied to the quality-of-law finding (since Lashmankin concerned precisely the Russian authorities' power to propose alternative locations).
- F003: restrictions on time — similarly a direct factual anchor for the interference and quality-of-law findings.
- F004: restrictions on manner — similarly necessary.

All three are needed because the Court's description of the complaints in the reasoning section encompasses all three types, and the violation finding is stated in respect of "each applicant" whose complaints may have involved any or all of these types. F001 (the appended table) is background identification, not a substantive fact driving the legal conclusion. F005 and F006 concern other Convention articles and are irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
