LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) that an assembly existed; (ii) that the applicants participated in it; (iii) that it was peaceful enough to attract Article 11 protection.
- F051: Tree felling commenced on 20 May 2010 attracting protesters — establishes the assembly existed.
- F002: The applicants participated in protest activities — establishes participation.
- F121: The first to fifth applicants were arrested on 28 May 2010 among other protesters — establishes participation in the assembly on the relevant date.
- F133: Video shows a group of protesters at the railroad crossing being approached — establishes the assembly and the applicants' presence.
- F147: Police report states the second applicant was arrested for protesting — establishes participation.
- F122: Charges drawn up for failure to obey police orders, not for the protest itself — supports the peaceful character of the assembly (arrested for disobedience, not violence).
- F123: Offence reports describe passive resistance (dragging feet) not violence — supports peaceful character.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish that a governmental act or omission interfered with the right.
- F121: Arrest of the first to fifth applicants on 28 May 2010.
- F139: Police prising individuals from the group and leading them away — establishes dispersal/removal.
- F157: Conviction and sentence of the first and second applicants to fifteen days' detention — establishes penalty as interference.
- F177: Conviction of the third and fourth applicants — establishes interference.
- F185: Conviction of the fifth applicant — establishes interference.
- F200: Conviction of the sixth applicant to ten days' detention — establishes interference.
- F061: Police remained largely passive in the face of clashes — establishes omission as basis for positive obligation interference.
- F058: Security guards attempted to drive protesters away by pushing them — establishes the acts of quasi-state actors.
- F100: Police observed assault on seventh applicant without reacting — establishes omission.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needed to establish that the legal basis was accessible and foreseeable, and to address the applicants' argument about the need for a judicial order.
- F282: Article 185 of the Code of Administrative Offences — the legal basis for the convictions.
- F053: No formal notification of the protest had been sent — establishes why the judicial procedure for banning could not be launched.
- F273: Article 39 of the Constitution — the provision the applicants relied on for the judicial order argument.
- F274: Article 39 paragraph 2 — restrictions may be established by a court.
- F278: Constitutional Court decision — advance notification must be sufficient for authorities to apply to court if necessary.
- F290: Article 182 of the Code of Administrative Justice — right to apply to court upon receipt of notification.
- F291: Article 182 — action received on the date of the event or thereafter shall be left without examination.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court needed to establish that the interference served a legitimate aim.
- F315: Construction safety rules prohibited access of unauthorised persons to construction sites.
- F316: Rule 1.16 prohibited access of unauthorised persons to construction sites.
- F083: Video evidence from 24 May showing protesters surrounding loggers with chainsaws — establishes danger.
- F085: Video evidence from 25 May showing police blocking protesters' access to tree-felling area — establishes ongoing danger.
- F030: Main Contractor engaged for the road construction project — establishes the rights and interests of others.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court needed to assess proportionality for each group of applicants.

For first and second applicants (violation found):
- F133: Video shows civilian (not police) issuing initial dispersal request — establishes confusion about authority.
- F134: Video shows civilian repeating "Please leave the construction site" — establishes the initial order came from a non-police person.
- F137: By the time police arrived, protesters were already surrounded and contained — establishes that the police order came when movement was already restricted.
- F138: Video shows police officer appearing to say "Do you wish to leave?" then ordering arrest — establishes ambiguity of the order.
- F190: Proceedings against eighth applicant discontinued — establishes inconsistency in treatment of similarly situated persons.
- F192: Court found inconsistencies in police reports and lack of evidence of duly issued instructions in eighth applicant's case — establishes doubt about clarity of order.
- F157: First and second applicants sentenced to fifteen days' detention — establishes severity of sanction.
- F160: District court stated punishment less severe than detention would not be adequate without adequate explanation — establishes failure to reason severity.
- F173: Court of Appeal found insufficient grounds for maximum punishment — establishes that even the appellate court recognised disproportionality.
- F174: Sentence reduced to nine days on appeal — establishes that some disproportionality was acknowledged.
- F167: Lawyer notified of appeal hearing only the day before — establishes procedural unfairness compounding disproportionality.

For third to fifth applicants (no violation):
- F123: Offence reports describe deliberate obstruction (dragging feet, attempting to break free) — establishes deliberate obstructive conduct.
- F083: Video evidence from 24 May showing dangerous protest activity — establishes tolerance shown initially.
- F178: Third applicant fined UAH 136 — establishes proportionate sanction.
- F179: Fourth applicant fined UAH 170 — establishes proportionate sanction.
- F185: Fifth applicant fined UAH 170 then proceedings discontinued with reprimand — establishes proportionate sanction.

For sixth applicant (no violation):
- F196: Sixth applicant acknowledged sitting on ground protesting and refusing to comply with police orders.
- F197: Sixth applicant acknowledged refusing to comply to the point of being dragged away.
- F198: Sixth applicant admitted telling police he would return to the site.
- F200: Sentenced to ten days' detention.
- F211: Court of Appeal could not reduce sentence because defence only sought acquittal, not mitigation.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
The Court needed to establish that the State failed to take adequate measures to protect the seventh and ninth applicants.
- F040: Police action plan approved on 19 May 2010 — establishes advance awareness and preparation time.
- F041: Twenty-seven officers assigned daily — establishes police were deployed.
- F048: Main Contractor asked police to send units on 26 May — establishes advance warning of likely clashes.
- F061: Police remained largely passive in face of clashes — establishes the failure to intervene.
- F100: Police observed assault on seventh applicant without reacting — establishes specific failure to protect.
- F305: Licensing Rules enacted by Ministry of Interior — establishes the regulatory framework for security guards.
- F307: Rule 1.4.1 — security guards' powers limited to access control.
- F308: Rule 1.4.2 — physical security measures limited to preventing/stopping unauthorised intrusions.
- F309: Rule 1.4.3 — coercive measures beyond access control only in urgent/emergency circumstances.
- F311: Rule 2.2.3 — security guards required to notify police without delay and restrict access until police arrive.
- F058: Security guards attempted to drive protesters away by pushing — establishes guards acting beyond their authorised role.
- F059: Clashes took place between security guards and protesters — establishes the dangerous situation.
- F220: Prosecutor questioned MG director on 3 June 2010.
- F222: MG director stated there were many men in black with MG badges who were not company employees — establishes the unidentified persons problem.
- F234: Prosecutor's office decided not to institute criminal proceedings on 24 June 2010 — establishes failure to investigate adequately.
- F044: Main Contractor signed agreement with PS on 21 May 2010 — establishes the presence of a second security provider, relevant to the badge confusion.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on two separate violations: (A) the disproportionate conviction of the first and second applicants, and (B) the failure to protect the seventh and ninth applicants. The no-violation findings for the third to sixth applicants are also part of the conclusion. The following facts are strictly necessary:

For the violation regarding the first and second applicants:
- F133: The initial dispersal request came from a civilian, not police — essential to the confusion finding.
- F137: Protesters were already surrounded and contained when police issued their order — essential to the finding that compliance was practically difficult.
- F138: The police order was ambiguous and issued at ordinary voice volume in a noisy environment — essential to the confusion finding.
- F157: First and second applicants sentenced to fifteen days' detention — essential to the disproportionality finding.
- F160: District court failed to explain why detention rather than a lesser sanction was warranted — essential to the insufficiency of reasoning finding.
- F173: Court of Appeal itself found insufficient grounds for the maximum punishment — essential to confirming disproportionality.
- F174: Sentence reduced to nine days — essential to establishing that some disproportionality was acknowledged but a custodial sentence was still served.
- F167: Lawyer notified of appeal hearing only the day before — essential to the procedural unfairness finding that compounds disproportionality.
- F190: Proceedings against eighth applicant (arrested in same circumstances) discontinued — essential to establishing inconsistency and doubt about the clarity of the order.
- F192: Court found inconsistencies in police reports and lack of evidence of duly issued instructions in eighth applicant's case — essential to establishing that the order's clarity was genuinely in doubt.

For the no-violation findings regarding the third to sixth applicants:
- F123: Offence reports describe deliberate obstruction — essential to establishing that their conduct was more clearly obstructive.
- F178: Third applicant fined a small amount — essential to proportionality finding.
- F179: Fourth applicant fined a small amount — essential to proportionality finding.
- F185: Fifth applicant's proceedings discontinued with reprimand — essential to proportionality finding.
- F196: Sixth applicant acknowledged refusing police orders.
- F197: Sixth applicant acknowledged being dragged away.
- F198: Sixth applicant stated he would return to the site — essential to justifying the custodial sentence.
- F200: Sixth applicant sentenced to ten days — essential to the proportionality assessment.
- F211: Defence only sought acquittal, not mitigation — essential to why the Court of Appeal could not reduce the sentence.

For the violation regarding the seventh and ninth applicants (positive obligation):
- F040: Police action plan approved in advance — essential to establishing the State had sufficient notice and time to prepare.
- F048: Main Contractor warned police of likely clashes before 27 May — essential to establishing advance knowledge of risk.
- F061: Police remained largely passive in face of clashes — essential to the failure to intervene finding.
- F100: Police observed assault on seventh applicant without reacting — essential to the specific failure to protect.
- F307: Security guards' powers limited to access control under Licensing Rules — essential to establishing guards acted beyond their legal authority.
- F309: Coercive measures only permitted in urgent circumstances — essential to establishing the guards' actions were not legally authorised in the circumstances.
- F311: Guards required to notify police and wait — essential to establishing the proper procedure was not followed.
- F058: Security guards attempted to drive protesters away by pushing — essential to establishing guards acted beyond their role.
- F059: Clashes took place — essential to establishing the harm that the positive obligation required the State to prevent.
- F222: MG director stated unidentified persons were wearing MG badges — essential to the finding about unidentified persons and the failure to investigate.
- F234: Prosecutor decided not to institute criminal proceedings — essential to establishing the failure to investigate adequately.
