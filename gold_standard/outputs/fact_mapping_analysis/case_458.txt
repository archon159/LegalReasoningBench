The necessity analysis turns on several distinct factual points:

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on the necessity assessment. The violation was found because: (1) the assembly concerned a matter of public interest; (2) Navalnyy was walking peacefully and showed no violence; (3) the domestic courts justified the arrest and conviction solely by reference to the unlawfulness of the march without any proportionality assessment; and (4) the only disruption identified was traffic/pedestrian disruption, which the Court found insufficient to justify the interference. These are the facts the conclusion actually turned on:

- F009: The purpose of the march was to call for investigation of corruption — establishes the public interest dimension that weighs heavily in the necessity balance.
- F019: Mr Navalnyy was walking peacefully with other participants — establishes the peaceful nature of his conduct, which is central to the Court's finding that tolerance should have been shown.
- F023: The domestic court convicted Mr Navalnyy of organising an unauthorised public event with about 4,000 participants — establishes the conviction and the court's reasoning (by reference to unlawfulness alone).
- F024: The unauthorised public event caused disruption of movement of pedestrians and traffic — establishes the only concrete harm identified, which the Court found insufficient to justify the interference.
- F020: Mr Navalnyy was apprehended by the police — establishes the arrest as the primary act of interference that triggered the Article 11 analysis.
