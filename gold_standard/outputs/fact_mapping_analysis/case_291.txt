LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation):
The reasoning text states: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants and an organiser (application no. 39118/12) of public assemblies."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies."
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies."
These are the atomic facts the Court was pointing to when it described the assemblies and the applicants' roles.

Scope (peaceful character):
As noted previously, there is no factual grounding in the reasoning text for the peaceful character of the assemblies. No atomic fact addresses this either. No mapping possible.

Interference:
The reasoning text states: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies."
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies."
These are the closest atomic facts, as the arrest, dispersal, and conviction are the "disproportionate measures" referred to in F002 and F003. No atomic fact provides more granular detail about the arrest or conviction specifically.

Necessity:
The reasoning text states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."
This is a general reference to the totality of the case material. The only atomic facts available are F002 and F003, which describe the complaints. No atomic fact provides specific factual grounding for the necessity analysis beyond the nature of the measures complained of.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:
None of these elements are discussed in the reasoning, and no atomic facts correspond to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope:
The Court's reasoning on scope (implied) rests on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' participation as participants or organisers. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003 — these establish both that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were participants or organisers. F002 is the most critical for participants; F003 for the organiser in application no. 39118/12. Both are needed to cover the full set of applicants.

Interference:
The interference finding rests on the measures taken against the applicants — arrest, dispersal, and conviction. The strictly necessary atomic facts are again F002 and F003, as these describe the "disproportionate measures" that constitute the interference. No more granular atomic fact is available.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

Necessity:
The necessity conclusion is that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society. The factual predicate for this is the existence of the measures (the interference) against participants and organisers of public assemblies. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the fact that state authorities took coercive measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) against individuals who participated in or organised public assemblies, and that these measures were found not to be necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core is:
- F002: establishes that participants of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the essential fact linking the right (assembly), the persons (participants), and the interference (measures).
- F003: establishes the same for organisers, covering the applicant in application no. 39118/12.

Without F002 and F003, there is no factual basis for any element of the Article 11 analysis — no assembly, no participation, no interference, and no necessity assessment. These two facts are the irreducible core. F001 and F004 are not necessary for the Article 11 conclusion: F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions.
