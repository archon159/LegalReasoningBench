LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (existence of assembly, participation, peaceful character):
The factual grounding quote was: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." This maps to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants). No atomic fact addresses the peaceful character of the assemblies — as noted earlier, there is no factual grounding for that sub-element.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This maps to F002 and F003, as these are the facts describing the applicants as organisers and participants who were subjected to measures. F001 may also be relevant as it references the appended table containing details of the applications, which presumably includes the arrest and conviction details. However, since the Court's reasoning section itself only references the applicants as organisers or participants subjected to disproportionate measures, the most direct mapping is F002 and F003.

Necessity:
The factual grounding was the absence of distinguishing facts relative to prior leading cases. The Court stated it "examined all the material submitted to it" and found no new facts or arguments. This is a negative finding — no specific atomic fact is pointed to as the basis for the necessity conclusion. The closest atomic facts are again F002 and F003, which describe the nature of the complaints (disproportionate measures against organisers and participants), since these are the facts that the Court compared against its prior case-law. F004 (other complaints) is not relevant to the Article 11 necessity analysis.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:
As established, these elements are not discussed and no factual grounding was identified. No atomic facts map to these elements.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies (supporting the existence of an assembly and participation as organiser). F003 establishes that other applicants were participants of public assemblies (supporting participation). Together they are the minimum factual basis for the implied scope finding. F001 is not strictly necessary — it merely points to a table of details rather than establishing the substance.

Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court's interference finding rests on the fact that measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) were taken against the applicants as organisers and participants. These two facts are the direct basis for that finding.

Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The necessity analysis — even conducted by reference to prior case-law — requires knowing that the applicants were organisers or participants subjected to disproportionate measures, since this is what the Court compared to the facts in Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, and Kasparov. Without F002 and F003, there would be no basis for the analogy to those leading cases.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as these elements are not discussed.

LIST 3: Atomic facts strictly necessary for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two things: (1) the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, bringing them within the scope of Article 11; and (2) they were subjected to disproportionate measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction), which constituted an interference that was not necessary in a democratic society. Both of these points rest on F002 and F003. F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table and is not itself a substantive fact driving the conclusion. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Therefore, the irreducible factual core for the final conclusion is: F002 and F003.
