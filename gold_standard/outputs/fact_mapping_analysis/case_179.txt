LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope analysis (assembly exists, participation). Without F002, there is no factual basis for the Court to proceed under Article 11 at all.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it identifies the concrete governmental acts (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) that constitute the interference. No other atomic fact provides this.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary — the necessity finding is grounded in the nature of the measures taken against assembly participants, which is what F002 describes. The Court's reference to "all the material submitted" does not point to any specific additional atomic fact beyond what is captured in F002.

Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned entirely on the fact that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) were taken against persons acting as organisers or participants of public assemblies. This is captured solely and entirely by F002. F001 identifies who the applicants are but adds no substantive content that the Court relied upon in its reasoning. F003 concerns other complaints entirely unrelated to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact that is strictly necessary for the final conclusion.
