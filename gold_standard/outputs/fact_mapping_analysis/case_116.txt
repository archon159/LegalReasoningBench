LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) an assembly existed, (ii) the applicants participated, (iii) it was peaceful.
- Assembly: F010 (seventy to eighty students gathered), F016 (banner displayed), F017 (slogans chanted) — these establish the assembly as a collective expressive gathering
- Participation: F008 (applicants attended the meeting), F036 (applicants accepted they had taken part)
- Peaceful: F069 (domestic court found neither applicants nor other participants used force against gendarmes)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court identified three acts of interference: gendarmes' intervention, arrest, and criminal proceedings.
- F019 (gendarmes used force and dispersed students)
- F020 (applicants arrested)
- F075 (appeal proceedings still pending — establishing the ongoing nature of the criminal proceedings as interference)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court identified section 32 of Law no. 2911 as the legal basis.
- F087 (section 32 §1 — the provision under which the applicants were ultimately convicted at retrial)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — the Court accepted the aims without factual grounding.

--- NECESSITY ---
The necessity analysis turned on several key factual findings:
- F069 (peaceful gathering — no force used by participants: the central factual premise for the proportionality analysis)
- F068 (eleven and a half minutes — the demonstration was dispersed so quickly the applicants could not manifest their views)
- F070 (applicants convicted merely for participating in an unauthorised meeting — the nature of the sanction)
- F011 (gathering took place on university grounds — relevant to the Court's assessment of the danger to public order)
- F009 (chancellor approved the meeting the day before — relevant context showing the meeting was not clandestine and had institutional approval, supporting the absence of danger to public order)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The Court found a violation because: (a) the demonstration was peaceful, (b) it was dispersed so quickly the applicants could not manifest their views, (c) there was no evidence of danger to public order, and (d) the applicants were prosecuted and convicted merely for participating in an unauthorised but peaceful assembly. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

- F069: The domestic court found that neither the applicants nor any other participant used force — this is the foundational fact for the entire proportionality analysis.
- F068: The demonstration lasted only eleven and a half minutes before being dispersed — this grounds the Court's finding that the applicants had insufficient time to manifest their views.
- F070: The applicants were convicted merely for participating in an unauthorised meeting — this grounds the Court's concern about criminalising participation in a peaceful assembly.
- F019: The gendarmes used force to disperse the students — this establishes the act of interference that triggered the Article 11 analysis.
- F009: The chancellor had approved the meeting the day before — this is critical context showing the meeting was not clandestine and had institutional sanction, undermining any claim of danger to public order.
- F011: The gathering moved to the university garden — this establishes the university-grounds location that the Court found relevant to the absence of danger to public order.
