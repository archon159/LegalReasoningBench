LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope (implied):
The Court's implied finding that there was an assembly, that the applicants sought to participate, and that it was peaceful rests on: F077 (the prohibition on participating in public gatherings was imposed — this presupposes an assembly existed and that the applicants were potential participants), F020 (the applicants participated in the rally of 6 September 2015, establishing their character as assembly participants), F099 (the constitutional framework treats assemblies as free when peaceful, contextualising the peaceful character). However, strictly speaking, the only fact the Court itself points to in its merits reasoning is F077. F020 and F099 are background context. I include F077 as the strictly necessary fact.

Interference:
F077 is the critical fact: the court ordered the prohibition on participating in public gatherings on 26 April 2016 as a surety measure. This is the governmental act that constitutes the interference. F076 is also necessary as it establishes the context of release under judicial control within which the prohibition was imposed.

Prescribed by law:
F105 is the most critical fact: Article 191(3) of the Code of Criminal Procedure lists exhaustively the obligations that may be imposed on a person released under judicial control, and a prohibition on participating in public gatherings is not among them. This is the provision the Court specifically references ("Article 191 of the Code of Criminal Procedure does not provide for such a surety measure"). F077 is also necessary as it establishes that the measure was imposed as a surety condition on persons released pending trial, which is the context in which Article 191 applies.

Legitimate aim: no atomic facts — not discussed.

Necessity: no atomic facts — not discussed.

Positive obligation: no atomic facts — not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the "prescribed by law" element. The Court found a violation because the prohibition on participating in public gatherings had no legal basis in domestic law — specifically, Article 191 of the Code of Criminal Procedure did not provide for such a measure. The irreducible facts are:

- F077: The court ordered the prohibition on participating in public gatherings as a surety measure on 26 April 2016 — this is the interference that needed to be prescribed by law.
- F105: Article 191(3) of the Code of Criminal Procedure lists the permissible obligations for persons released under judicial control, and does not include a prohibition on participating in public gatherings — this is the legal provision the Court found insufficient to ground the measure.

These two facts together are the absolute minimum needed: one establishes what was done (the measure imposed), the other establishes that the domestic law did not authorise it. All other facts (the nature of the assembly, the applicants' participation, the prolongation of the measure, etc.) are either background or relate to elements the Court explicitly declined to examine.
