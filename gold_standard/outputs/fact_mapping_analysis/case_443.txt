LIST 1: Factual grounding quotes mapped to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's only factual grounding for scope is the undisputed fact of conviction: 'It has not been disputed between the parties that the applicant's conviction constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly.' This implies an assembly existed, the applicant participated, and it was peaceful.
- Assembly exists: F006 (demonstration held on 14 May 2002), F010 (procession along Baghramyan Avenue)
- Applicant participated: F008 (applicant participated in demonstration), F009 (applicant called on participants to hold a procession), F010 (crowd followed applicant)
- Peaceful: no atomic fact explicitly establishes this; it is implied by the absence of any finding of violence. The closest grounding is F006 and F008, which describe the demonstration without any mention of violence.
- Conviction as undisputed basis: F015, F016, F017, F018 (district court findings and fine)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court grounds interference on: 'It has not been disputed between the parties that the applicant's conviction constituted an interference with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly.' And: 'The Court notes that the fine was imposed on the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO, which prescribed a penalty for the violation of the prescribed rules for organising and holding rallies and street processions.'
- F011 (applicant arrested on 14 May 2002)
- F013 (record of administrative offence prepared)
- F015 (district court found applicant participated in unauthorised procession)
- F016 (district court found offence under Article 180.1 CAO)
- F017 (district court imposed administrative penalty)
- F018 (fine of 500 AMD imposed)
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO prescribes penalty for violation of rules for holding assemblies)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's factual grounding for prescribed by law covers several sub-points:

1. 'The fine was imposed on the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO, which prescribed a penalty for the violation of the prescribed rules for organising and holding rallies and street processions. Therefore, the interference had a basis in domestic law.'
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO)
- F016 (court found offence under Article 180.1)
- F018 (fine imposed)

2. 'It has not been disputed that the CAO was accessible and the Court does not have reason to doubt that.'
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO — its existence and content)

3. 'There is no domestic provision which clearly stated whether the former USSR laws remained or did not remain in force on the territory of Armenia.'
- F042 (Declaration of Independence — only Constitution and laws of Republic of Armenia valid)
- F043 (Declaration of Independence — only Constitution and laws of Republic of Armenia valid on its territory)
- F044 (Article 16 Constitutional Law — valid Constitution and laws effective to extent not contradicting that Law)
- F045 (Article 11 CIS Convention — USSR norms not to be applied)
- F052 (Article 116 Constitution — prior constitution and constitutional laws lose force)
- F053 (Article 116 Constitution — laws effective to extent not contradicting Constitution)

4. 'While the Decision of the Supreme Council of Armenia of 26 February 1992, as pointed out by the Government, explicitly allowed the application of two former USSR laws on the territory of Armenia, no such decision was ever adopted in respect of the Law or the Decree.'
- This fact is referenced from the Government's submissions and accepted by the Court as established. The atomic facts do not include the Decision of the Supreme Council of 26 February 1992 directly, but the Court accepts its existence. The closest atomic facts are F041 (the USSR Law approving the Decree) and F033-F040 (the Decree itself), since the Court is noting that no equivalent explicit adoption decision existed for these instruments. No atomic fact directly corresponds to the Supreme Council Decision of 26 February 1992 — this fact has no direct atomic fact counterpart.

5. 'It is not obvious, however, from the wording of these provisions whether the same applied to the former USSR laws.'
- F042, F043 (Declaration of Independence)
- F044 (Constitutional Law Article 16)

6. 'the Court would draw attention to the absence of any domestic case-law concerning the disputed matter. Notwithstanding the Court's request, the Government failed to submit any examples of domestic practice...'
- No specific atomic fact corresponds to the absence of case-law. This is a negative fact established by the Court's own observation.

7. 'the domestic courts in the present case also failed to refer to any legal act which prescribed the rules for holding rallies and street processions which the applicant was found to have violated.'
- F015 (district court finding — no reference to any legal act prescribing rules)
- F016 (district court finding under Article 180.1 — no reference to underlying rules)
- F025, F026, F027, F028 (Civil Court of Appeal findings — no reference to any legal act prescribing rules)

8. 'The Court notes that following the dissolution of the USSR there was no legal act applicable in Armenia which contained these rules and the relevant law was adopted only on 28 April 2004.'
- F055 (Law adopted on 28 April 2004)
- F056 (Article 16 of 2004 Law — Decree not to be applied from that date)
- F041 (USSR Law approving the Decree — the instrument whose validity was in dispute)
- F033-F040 (the Decree itself)

9. 'The Court accepts that it may take some time for a country to establish its legislative framework in a transition period, but it cannot accept the delay of almost thirteen years to be justifiable...'
- F006 (events of 14 May 2002 — the material time)
- F055 (Law adopted 28 April 2004)
- F042 (Declaration of Independence of 23 August 1990 — starting point of independence)

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court implies scope is satisfied through the undisputed interference finding. The strictly necessary atomic facts are those establishing that an assembly took place and that the applicant participated in it:
- F006 (demonstration held on 14 May 2002 — establishes assembly existed)
- F008 (applicant participated in demonstration — establishes participation)
- F009 (applicant called on participants to hold procession — establishes participation in the procession element)
- F010 (crowd followed applicant in procession — establishes the procession as an assembly)
Peacefulness: no atomic fact strictly necessary as it is never questioned.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that a punitive measure was imposed on the applicant following the assembly:
- F016 (district court found offence under Article 180.1 CAO)
- F018 (fine imposed on applicant)
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO — the legal basis for the fine, establishing it as a punitive measure)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's prescribed-by-law analysis turns on: (a) the CAO providing a basis in domestic law; (b) the absence of any clearly applicable legal act prescribing the rules; (c) the domestic courts' failure to refer to any such legal act; (d) the absence of case-law; (e) the thirteen-year gap before a proper law was adopted. The strictly necessary atomic facts are:
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO — basis in domestic law and accessibility)
- F016 (district court applied Article 180.1 without identifying underlying rules)
- F028 (Civil Court of Appeal applied Article 180.1 without identifying underlying rules)
- F041 (USSR Law approving the Decree — the instrument whose validity was disputed)
- F033 (Decree defined requirement of prior authorisation — content of the disputed instrument)
- F044 (Article 16 Constitutional Law — ambiguous provision on continuing validity of laws)
- F045 (Article 11 CIS Convention — prohibition on applying USSR norms)
- F043 (Declaration of Independence — only laws of Republic of Armenia valid)
- F055 (Law adopted 28 April 2004 — establishes the thirteen-year gap)
- F056 (Article 16 of 2004 Law — confirms Decree was not applied from that date, implying it was previously in disputed status)

Not discussed. Empty.

--- NECESSITY ---
Not discussed. Empty.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the 'prescribed by law' requirement. The Court found a violation because: (1) the applicant was convicted and fined for participating in a procession under Article 180.1 CAO; (2) Article 180.1 CAO referred to 'prescribed rules' whose legal basis was entirely unclear; (3) the domestic courts never identified any legal act prescribing those rules; (4) the relevant law was only adopted in 2004, nearly thirteen years after independence. The irreducible core facts are:
- F009 (applicant called on participants to hold procession — the act for which he was sanctioned)
- F010 (crowd followed applicant — the procession that triggered the sanction)
- F016 (district court found offence under Article 180.1 CAO — the conviction)
- F018 (fine imposed — the sanction constituting the interference)
- F031 (Article 180.1 CAO — refers to 'prescribed rules' without specifying them)
- F028 (Civil Court of Appeal upheld conviction without identifying any legal act prescribing the rules)
- F041 (USSR Law approving the Decree — the only candidate instrument, whose validity was disputed)
- F044 (Article 16 Constitutional Law — ambiguous on continuing validity of USSR laws)
- F045 (Article 11 CIS Convention — prohibition on applying USSR norms, creating the ambiguity)
- F055 (Law adopted 28 April 2004 — establishes the thirteen-year gap and confirms no applicable law existed at material time)
