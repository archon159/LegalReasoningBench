LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The Court found the interference was not necessary in a democratic society because: (1) the applicant was an opposition parliamentary party expressing views on matters of public interest, warranting a narrow margin of appreciation; (2) the slogans and accompanying conduct (burning flags/pictures) were forms of political expression, not calls to violence; (3) the domestic authorities' characterisation of the slogans as calls to violence/hatred was unreasonable; (4) the risk of clashes could not justify a ban since the police could maintain order.

The irreducible factual core:
- F002: CDPP was represented in Parliament — essential for the narrow margin of appreciation
- F003: CDPP was in opposition — essential for the narrow margin of appreciation
- F009: intended to express views on Moldo-Russian conflict in Transdniestria — establishes the public interest subject matter
- F010: the Municipal Council rejected the request — the interference itself (without this, no violation)
- F011: stated ground for refusal (convincing evidence of calls to war of aggression, ethnic hatred, public violence) — the reason the Court found not relevant and sufficient
- F016: slogan 'Down with Voronin's totalitarian regime' — one of the two slogans the Court assessed as political expression
- F017: slogan 'Down with Putin's occupation regime' — the other slogan assessed as political expression
- F018: domestic court's characterisation of first slogan as call to violent overthrow — what the Court rejected as unreasonable
- F019: domestic court's characterisation of second slogan as call to hatred — what the Court rejected as unreasonable
- F020: burning of picture of Russian President — accompanying conduct the Court found expressive not violent
- F021: burning of Russian flag — accompanying conduct the Court found expressive not violent
