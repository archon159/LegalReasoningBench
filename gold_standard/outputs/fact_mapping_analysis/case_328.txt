Scope: The Court's implicit finding that Article 11 applies requires that there were public assemblies and that the applicants participated in them. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants). F001 is a procedural reference and not substantively necessary. The peaceful character (scope sub-element iii) has no atomic fact support.

Interference: The Court explicitly identifies the dispersal, arrest, and conviction as interferences. These measures are described in F002 and F003 (measures taken against organisers and participants). These are the strictly necessary facts.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.

Necessity: The Court's necessity finding is grounded in the similarity of the measures to prior cases. The measures themselves — described in F002 and F003 — are the only atomic facts that could be said to be necessary, as they define what the Court assessed as not necessary in a democratic society.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on the existence of public assemblies, the applicants' role in them, and the measures taken against them (dispersal, arrest, conviction). The irreducible atomic facts are F002 and F003. F002 establishes that measures were taken against organisers of public assemblies; F003 establishes that measures were taken against participants. Together these two facts are the factual core: they establish both that Article 11 was engaged (assemblies existed, applicants participated) and that there was an interference (disproportionate measures). F001 is merely a procedural reference and F004 concerns other complaints not relevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
