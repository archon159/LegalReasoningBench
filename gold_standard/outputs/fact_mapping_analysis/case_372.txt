LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's scope analysis turns on: (i) the existence of an assembly (a picket with four participants displaying placards), (ii) its peaceful character (no disruption, no violence), and (iii) the applicant's participation/organisation. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F029: Four people started to picket — establishes the assembly and its small size.
- F031: Participants displayed placards protesting against corruption — establishes the expressive, peaceful nature of the assembly.
- F032: Participants were arrested — establishes the termination of the assembly and implies the applicant's participation.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court's interference analysis turns on the governmental acts that curtailed the applicant's assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F027: The Prefect refused to approve the public event — the primary act of interference.
- F032: The participants were arrested and brought to a police station — the physical interference.
- F034: The Justice of the Peace found the applicant guilty — the legal/penal interference.
- F036: The applicant was sentenced to a fine — the sanction imposed.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's analysis turns on whether the domestic courts' interpretation of the notification time-limit was arbitrary or flagrantly non-observant of domestic law. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F023: The applicant notified the Prefect on 20 September 2010 of an event scheduled for 23 September 2010 at 10 a.m. — the core timing fact.
- F041: The Tverskoy District Court found the notification was submitted at 11.25 a.m. on 20 September 2010, less than three days before the event at 10 a.m. on 23 September 2010 — the domestic court's factual finding on timing.
- F055: The Public Events Act requires notification no later than three days before the intended picket — the legal standard applied.
- F042: The Tverskoy District Court found the applicant had not complied with the time-limit — the domestic court's legal conclusion.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
Not discussed by the Court. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on: the small and peaceful nature of the assembly, the absence of any disruption, the absence of any domestic assessment of necessity, and the sole ground for arrest being lack of approval. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F029: Four people started to picket — establishes the minimal scale of the assembly.
- F031: Participants displayed placards — establishes the peaceful, expressive nature.
- F030: Participants took shelter in an archway due to rain — supports the finding of no disruption to ordinary life.
- F032: Participants were arrested — establishes the zero-tolerance response.
- F034: The Justice of the Peace found the applicant guilty solely on the basis of lack of approval — establishes that the sole ground for prosecution was the absence of approval.
- F035: The Justice of the Peace found that the applicant had organised a public event which had not received an official approval — establishes the sole legal basis for the conviction.
- F044: The Tverskoy District Court found that by holding a public event which had not received an official approval, the applicant had violated the established procedure — confirms that no other ground was relied upon.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on the following: (1) a small, peaceful picket took place; (2) it caused no disruption; (3) the authorities arrested the participants and prosecuted the applicant solely because the event lacked official approval, without any assessment of necessity; (4) the lack of approval was itself the result of a marginal timing issue (notification submitted hours rather than days late). The irreducible facts are:
- F029: Four people started to picket — establishes the minimal, peaceful assembly.
- F031: Participants displayed placards protesting against corruption — establishes the peaceful, expressive nature.
- F030: Participants took shelter in an archway due to rain — supports no disruption to ordinary life.
- F032: Participants were arrested — establishes the disproportionate response.
- F035: The Justice of the Peace found that the applicant had organised a public event which had not received an official approval — establishes that the sole ground for conviction was lack of approval.
- F044: The Tverskoy District Court confirmed that the violation consisted solely in holding an unapproved event — confirms no other ground.
- F041: The notification was submitted at 11.25 a.m. on 20 September 2010, less than three days before the event at 10 a.m. on 23 September 2010 — establishes the marginal nature of the timing non-compliance that triggered the entire chain of interference.
