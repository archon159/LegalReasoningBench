--- SCOPE ---

Peaceful nature:
- "there is no evidence to suggest that the applicants represented a danger to public order beyond the level of the minor disturbance which is inevitably caused by an assembly in a public place" → F017 (approximately 150 people assembled), F020 (there was a loud noise / minor detonation), F026 (applicants asserted the demonstration was totally peaceful)
- "a decision to disband the ensuing, peaceful assembly solely because of the absence of the requisite prior notice, without any illegal conduct by the participants" → F026 (applicants asserted it was peaceful), F016 (no prior notification given)

--- INTERFERENCE ---

- "the dispersal of the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of the applicants' rights under that provision. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise." → F021 (police decided to disband the assembly), F022 (police forced demonstrators back to a park), F023 (demonstrators dispersed after a while)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---

- "the restriction imposed on the applicants' freedom of peaceful assembly was based on section 14 of the Assembly Act, the wording of which is clear. Therefore, the requirement of foreseeability was satisfied." → F052 (section 14(1) of the Assembly Act requires police to disband assemblies held without prior notification), F039 (District Court stated police dissolved assembly lawfully pursuant to section 14(1))

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---

The Court's conclusion ("the measure complained of pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others") is not grounded in any independently stated factual finding by the Court. It follows the Government's submissions and the general nature of the prior-notification requirement. The closest atomic facts are:
→ F051 (section 6 of the Assembly Act requires police to be informed at least three days before), F052 (section 14(1) requires disbanding of assemblies without prior notification), F035 (District Court found duty to inform police served to protect public interest and rights of others, namely free flow of traffic and freedom of movement)

--- NECESSITY ---

- "the legal basis for the dispersal of the applicants' assembly lay exclusively in the lack of prior notice. The courts based their decision to declare the police measures lawful solely on that argument and did not take into account other aspects of the case, in particular, the peaceful nature of the event." → F033 (District Court held duty to inform applies to every type of demonstration including spontaneous ones), F037 (District Court stated peaceful nature alone is not enough to dispense with duty to inform), F038 (District Court stated it had not dealt with whether assembly was peaceful since lack of notification made it illegal per se), F039 (District Court stated police dissolved assembly lawfully pursuant to section 14(1)), F044 (Regional Court found no exemption from duty of notification and no difference between notified and spontaneous assemblies)
- "the failure to inform the public sufficiently in advance of the Prime Minister's intention to attend the reception left the applicants with the option of either foregoing their right to peaceful assembly altogether, or of exercising it in defiance of the administrative requirements." → F011 (Hungarian Prime Minister decided to attend the reception), F012 (Hungarian Prime Minister made his intention public the day before the event, i.e. on 30 November 2002), F051 (section 6 of the Assembly Act requires police to be informed at least three days before), F028 (applicants pointed out it had obviously been impossible to inform police three days in advance because the Prime Minister had only announced his intention the day before)
- "there is no evidence to suggest that the applicants represented a danger to public order beyond the level of the minor disturbance which is inevitably caused by an assembly in a public place." → F017 (approximately 150 people assembled), F020 (there was a loud noise during the assembly), F021 (police decided to disband the assembly considering it constituted a risk to security)
- "a decision to disband the ensuing, peaceful assembly solely because of the absence of the requisite prior notice, without any illegal conduct by the participants, amounts to a disproportionate restriction" → F016 (applicants did not inform police), F026 (applicants asserted demonstration was totally peaceful), F039 (police dissolved assembly pursuant to section 14(1))
- "in special circumstances when an immediate response, in the form of a demonstration, to a political event might be justified" → F007 (Romanian Prime Minister made official visit to Budapest on 1 December 2002), F008 (Romanian Prime Minister gave a reception on Romania's national day), F011 (Hungarian Prime Minister decided to attend), F012 (Hungarian Prime Minister made his intention public the day before), F013 (applicants were of the opinion the Hungarian Prime Minister should refrain from attending), F015 (applicants decided to organise a demonstration in front of Hotel Kempinski)

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's finding that the assembly was peaceful rests critically on:
- F020: the loud noise / minor detonation (the only potential indicator of non-peacefulness, which the Court dismisses as insufficient)
- F038: the District Court's statement that it had not dealt with whether the assembly was peaceful since lack of notification made it illegal per se (the Court uses this to show the peaceful nature was not properly considered)
- F026: the applicants' assertion that the demonstration was totally peaceful (accepted implicitly by the Court)

The existence of an assembly and participation are implied by:
- F017: approximately 150 people assembled in front of Hotel Kempinski
- F018: the applicants were among those assembled

--- INTERFERENCE ---
- F021: the police decided to disband the assembly
- F022: the police forced the demonstrators back to a park

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
- F052: section 14(1) of the Assembly Act requires police to disband assemblies held without prior notification (the legal basis for the dispersal)
- F051: section 6 of the Assembly Act requires police to be informed at least three days before (the foreseeability of the notification requirement)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
- F051: section 6 of the Assembly Act (three-day notification requirement — the general nature of this provision supports the aim of preventing disorder and protecting rights of others)
- F035: District Court found the duty to inform police served to protect public interest and rights of others (the Court's conclusion on legitimate aim tracks this reasoning)

--- NECESSITY ---
The necessity analysis turned on several critical facts:
- F012: the Hungarian Prime Minister made his intention to attend public only the day before the event (this is the key fact creating the impossibility of prior notification and the special circumstances justifying spontaneous assembly)
- F051: section 6 requires three-day advance notification (this is what made compliance impossible given F012)
- F038: the District Court did not deal with whether the assembly was peaceful since lack of notification made it illegal per se (the Court uses this to show the domestic courts failed to balance properly)
- F044: the Regional Court found no exemption from duty of notification and no difference between notified and spontaneous assemblies (confirms the legal basis for dispersal was exclusively lack of notice)
- F020: the loud noise / minor detonation (the Court dismisses this as insufficient to justify dispersal, finding no real danger to public order)
- F016: the applicants did not inform the police (the sole legal basis for dispersal)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — element not discussed by the Court.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following:
1. F012: The Hungarian Prime Minister made his intention to attend the reception public only the day before the event — this is the pivotal fact creating the special circumstances that made prior notification impossible and justified an immediate response.
2. F051: Section 6 of the Assembly Act required three-day advance notification — this is what made compliance with the law structurally impossible given F012, creating the dilemma the Court identifies.
3. F052: Section 14(1) required the police to disband assemblies without prior notification — this is the legal basis for the interference.
4. F016: The applicants did not inform the police — the sole ground on which the assembly was disbanded.
5. F038: The District Court did not deal with whether the assembly was peaceful since lack of notification made it illegal per se — this is the key domestic court finding the ECtHR criticises as failing to balance the competing interests.
6. F020: The loud noise / minor detonation — the only potential indicator of disorder, which the Court finds insufficient to justify dispersal, confirming the assembly was effectively peaceful and posed no real danger.
7. F044: The Regional Court found no exemption from the duty of notification and no difference between notified and spontaneous assemblies — confirms the legal basis for dispersal was exclusively lack of notice, not any conduct of the participants.
