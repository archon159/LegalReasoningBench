LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies — without this, there is no basis for the Court to engage with Article 11 at all. F001 is not strictly necessary as it merely points to an appended table.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It is the only atomic fact that describes governmental action ("disproportionate measures") against the applicants in the context of assemblies. The specific measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are described in the reasoning text itself but are not separately atomised beyond F002.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts support this element. Not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts support this element. Not discussed.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could anchor the necessity analysis, as it describes the measures complained of. However, as noted, the necessity finding is driven by case-law rather than by specific facts of the instant case. F002 remains the minimum factual anchor.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts support this element. Not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (ii) that governmental measures interfered with that participation (interference). The necessity finding then follows from the application of prior case-law to these basic facts.

The single atomic fact that is strictly necessary and irreducible for the final conclusion is F002. It establishes both that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies and that disproportionate measures were taken against them — the two factual pillars on which the entire Article 11 analysis rests. Without F002, the Court has no factual basis for finding a violation. F001 and F003 are not necessary for the conclusion: F001 is purely administrative, and F003 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11.
