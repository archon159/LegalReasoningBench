LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must at minimum establish: (1) that there were public assemblies (scope); (2) that the applicants participated or organised them (scope/participation); and (3) that governmental measures were taken against them in that context (interference). All of these are captured, at least in summary form, in F002. F001 establishes the existence of the applicants as a defined group but adds no substantive legal content. F003 is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Therefore, the irreducible factual core is F002 alone. It is the single atomic fact that carries the essential factual content — the existence of public assemblies, the applicants' role in them, and the existence of measures taken against them — that the Court needed to ground its Article 11 violation finding. The specific facts about arrests, dispersals, and administrative convictions that the Court also relied upon are not captured in any of the three atomic facts provided, which represents a significant gap in the atomisation of the facts for this case.
