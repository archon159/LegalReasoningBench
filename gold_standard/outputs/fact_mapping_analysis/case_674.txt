LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's scope analysis turns on: (1) whether the assembly was peaceful (no violent intent by organisers or participants); (2) whether the applicant's specific conduct placed him outside Article 11 protection. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F012: assembly was authorised (establishes it was a legitimate assembly)
- F037/F038: organisers gave assurances of peaceful conduct (establishes no violent intent by organisers)
- F040: march proceeded peacefully (establishes peaceful character up to Bolotnaya Square)
- F046/F047: unexpected change of venue layout (explains origin of disorder without violent intent)
- F049/F050: no negotiation by police (establishes authorities' contribution to confusion)
- F051: leaders announced sit-down strike (peaceful response to cordon)
- F035/F036: only tents found in security screening (establishes absence of weapons)
- F103: applicant threw yellow object (the specific act at issue)
- F119/F254: applicant chanted slogans (peaceful expressive conduct)
- F120/F246: yellow object hit police officer causing pain but no injury (establishes limited nature of act)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F005: charged with participation in mass disorder
- F006: charged with violent acts against police officers
- F008/F093: convicted and sentenced to two years and three months
- F087: found guilty by Zamoskvoretskiy District Court

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F275: Article 212 § 2 text (the provision applied, which lacks a definition of mass disorder)
- F060/F061: cordon broken, crowd spilled over (the events that were characterised as mass disorder)
- F262: domestic court's finding that mass disorder took place (establishes that courts applied the provision after thorough assessment)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary here; the Court accepts the legitimate aim without factual elaboration. This element is grounded in legal acceptance rather than specific facts.

--- NECESSITY ---
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F093/F272: sentence of two years and three months, broken down as 21 months under Article 212 and 9 months under Article 318 (the disproportion between the two sentences is central)
- F275: Article 212 § 2 statutory minimum of three years (establishes that even the reduced sentence was below minimum, confirming courts acknowledged minor role)
- F119/F254: applicant chanted slogans (peaceful conduct that contributed to the mass disorder sentence)
- F120/F246: yellow object hit police officer causing pain (the violent act, limited in harm)
- F256/F258: violence not endangering life or health, injuries not entailing health impairment (establishes limited physical harm)
- F263: court acknowledged insignificant participation and sentenced below minimum (establishes domestic courts' own recognition of minor role)
- F273: applicant served sentence in full (establishes full severity of chilling effect)
- F085/F087: large-scale proceedings against twelve individuals, eight convicted (establishes the wide-scale nature of proceedings amplifying chilling effect)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
The Court declines to examine positive obligation as a separate issue. The facts relevant to why it was raised but not separately decided are:
- F046/F047: unexpected change of venue layout not communicated to organisers
- F049/F050: no negotiation by police with protest leaders
- F055/F056: no senior official came to site, no direct communication
These facts are incorporated into the scope analysis rather than generating a separate positive obligation finding.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The violation was found because: (a) the applicant's sentence was grossly disproportionate given his minor role; (b) the bulk of his sentence was attributable to peaceful expressive conduct (chanting slogans, being present); (c) the domestic courts themselves acknowledged his insignificant participation; (d) the sentence was served in full and had a chilling effect. The irreducible facts are:

- F093/F272: the sentence of two years and three months, broken down as 21 months under Article 212 and 9 months under Article 318 — this is the single most critical fact, as it reveals that the peaceful conduct (presence + slogans) accounted for the bulk of the sentence
- F119/F254: the applicant chanted anti-government slogans — the peaceful expressive conduct that drove the mass disorder sentence
- F120/F246: the yellow object hit police officer F. on the shoulder causing pain but no injury — establishes the limited nature of the only violent act
- F256/F258: violence not endangering life or health, injuries not entailing health impairment — establishes the minimal harm caused
- F263: domestic court acknowledged insignificant participation and sentenced below the three-year statutory minimum — the domestic courts' own recognition of the applicant's minor role
- F275: Article 212 § 2 statutory minimum of three to eight years — establishes the sentencing framework and that even the reduced sentence reflected the serious charge
- F273: applicant released after serving his prison term in full — establishes that the full severity of the disproportionate sentence was actually suffered, amplifying the chilling effect
- F047: park at Bolotnaya Square excluded from meeting venue without notice to organisers — establishes that the disorder arose from authorities' own conduct, relevant to whether the applicant's presence at the scene of disorder was blameworthy
- F040: march proceeded peacefully — establishes the peaceful character of the assembly and the applicant's participation in it before the disorder
