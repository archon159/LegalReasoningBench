LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists, participation)**
- "The applicant in the present case held a picket in front of a regional court." → F009
- "the applicant submitted the picket notice eight days before the planned event" → F003, F004, F037 (the notice was sent on 18 March 2003 for a picket planned 25-28 March, i.e. 7-10 days later; the justice of the peace found it was eight days before)
- "the town administration did not consider the alleged delay in notification relevant or important. Not only did it promptly acknowledge receipt of the picket notice, but also gave orders to the police five days in advance to ensure public order at the event." → F006, F008

**Scope (peaceful)**
- "The materials distributed by the applicant and the ideas he advocated during the picket were not shown to contain any defamatory statements, incitement to violence or rejection of democratic principles." → F010, F011, F056
- "it is creditable that the applicant diligently complied with the officials' request and without further argument descended the stairs onto the pavement." → F043, F048

**Interference**
- "The applicant in the present case held a picket in front of a regional court. Some time later he was found administratively liable and fined for having breached the procedure for organising and holding a public assembly." → F009, F026, F058, F059, F060

**Prescribed by law**
- "The administrative liability for failure to comply with the established procedure for organising or holding a public assembly was foreseen by Article 20.2 §§ 1 and 2 of the Russian Code of Administrative Offences, which was the legal basis for the applicant's conviction in the present case." → F069, F070, F058

**Legitimate aim**
- No specific facts cited; the Court simply accepted the aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others. The only implicit factual anchor is the existence of the prosecution itself: F026, F058.

**Necessity — belated notification**
- "it is undisputed that in the instant case the applicant submitted the picket notice eight days before the planned event, whereas the applicable regulations stipulated a ten-day notification." → F003, F004, F037, F038, F065
- "it does not appear that the two-day difference in any way impaired the authorities' ability to make necessary preparations for the picket." → F006, F008
- "the town administration did not consider the alleged delay in notification relevant or important. Not only did it promptly acknowledge receipt of the picket notice, but also gave orders to the police five days in advance to ensure public order at the event." → F006, F008
- "The delay was not held against the applicant in any official documents and did not affect the lawfulness of the picket." → F006, F008 (no official document prior to the administrative report challenged the timing)
- "that transgression surfaced for the first time in the report on the administrative offence, which was compiled six weeks after the assembly." → F026 (report compiled 17 April 2003, picket was 25 March 2003)

**Necessity — obstruction of passage**
- "the picket, in which just a few persons participated, began at about 9 a.m. at the top of the stairs leading to the entrance to the Sverdlovsk Regional Court. Shortly afterwards the court bailiffs and a police officer approached the participants and asked them to go down the stairs. They complied with the request and continued to picket in front of the staircase. The witnesses who arrived at 9.30 a.m. saw the applicant and his colleagues, who were already on the pavement downstairs." → F041, F042, F043, F047, F048, F050
- "it is undisputed that there were no complaints by anyone, whether individual visitors, judges or court employees, about the alleged obstruction of entry to the court-house by the picket participants." → This is a negative fact (absence of complaints) not directly captured in a single atomic fact, but is most closely supported by F048 (after intervention, participants did not subsequently interfere) and the absence of any atomic fact recording complaints by visitors or judges (F044 records only that bailiffs said judges told them, which the Court implicitly discounted)
- "it is creditable that the applicant diligently complied with the officials' request and without further argument descended the stairs onto the pavement." → F043, F048
- "the alleged hindrance was of an extremely short duration." → F041, F043, F047, F048, F050

**Necessity — distributed materials**
- "the domestic courts omitted to indicate any factual basis for their findings on that issue. Their judgments did not contain any analysis as to what the alleged differences were between the declared aims of the picket and the contents of the article which the applicant had distributed during the picket." → F052, F060, F061 (the justice of the peace found variance but gave no analysis; the district court simply endorsed)
- "The materials distributed by the applicant and the ideas he advocated during the picket were not shown to contain any defamatory statements, incitement to violence or rejection of democratic principles." → F010, F056
- "the alleged discrepancy between the aims of the picket and the disseminated materials was raised for the first time in the letter by the deputy President of the Sverdlovsk Regional Court, that is the official reporting directly to the President of the Regional Court who had been the target of criticism in the distributed publications." → F012, F015, F016, F021, F023
- "The terms employed in the deputy president's letter, such as the statement that the picket participants had 'committed thereby an administrative offence', prejudged the assessment of the facts by the competent judicial authority and expressed the opinion that the applicant was guilty even before he had been proved guilty according to law." → F021, F022, F023

**Necessity — nature of speech / political debate**
- "the purpose of the picket was to attract public attention to the alleged dysfunction of the judicial system in the Sverdlovsk Region. This serious matter was undeniably part of a political debate on a matter of general and public concern." → F005, F010, F011

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was a picket (assembly), that the applicant participated/organised it, and that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F009: the picket was held (assembly exists, participation)
- F003: the applicant sent the notice (participation/organisation)
- F010: materials distributed were press clippings about the court president (relevant to peaceful character — no incitement to violence)
- F043/F048: participants complied with officials and descended (peaceful conduct)

Most critical: F009 (assembly and participation), F010 (peaceful — nature of materials), F048 (peaceful — compliance with authorities)

**Interference**
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F009: the picket was held
- F058: the applicant was found guilty and fined
- F059: the fine was imposed
- F060: the conviction was upheld on appeal and became final

Most critical: F058, F059, F060 (the punitive measure constituting interference)

**Prescribed by law**
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F069: Article 20.2 § 1 CAO (legal basis for organising offence)
- F070: Article 20.2 § 2 CAO (legal basis for holding offence)
- F058: the applicant was convicted under these provisions

Most critical: F069, F070 (the statutory provisions)

**Legitimate aim**
The Court accepted this without factual elaboration. The only necessary factual anchor is the existence of the prosecution and the charges:
- F026: the report on administrative offences (establishing what the prosecution was about)

Most critical: F026

**Necessity**
The strictly necessary facts, broken down by the three charges:

*Belated notification:*
- F003: notice sent 18 March 2003 (establishing the actual date of notice)
- F004: picket planned 25-28 March 2003 (establishing the planned date, allowing calculation of the 7-8 day gap)
- F065: 10-day notification requirement under the 1988 Decree
- F006: town administration acknowledged receipt promptly (showing no prejudice)
- F008: police ordered to maintain order 5 days in advance (showing no prejudice to preparations)
- F026: report compiled 17 April 2003 (showing the charge surfaced 6 weeks after the picket)

*Obstruction of passage:*
- F041/F042: bailiffs testified participants were on the porch blocking passage at ~9.10 a.m.
- F043: participants went down after police officer approached (compliance)
- F048: after intervention, participants did not subsequently interfere
- F050: defence witnesses arrived at 9.30 a.m. and saw participants already on pavement (short duration)

*Distributed materials:*
- F005: declared aim of picket (access to court)
- F010: actual materials distributed (Novaya Gazeta article about court president)
- F052: justice of the peace found variance between aims and materials
- F012, F021, F022, F023: deputy president's letter prejudging guilt and initiating proceedings
- F060, F061: district court simply endorsed without analysis

*Political speech:*
- F005: declared aim (judicial protection — matter of public concern)
- F010, F011: materials and activities (corruption allegations, dismissal petition — political speech)

Most critical across necessity: F003, F004, F065, F006, F008, F026, F043, F048, F050, F005, F010, F052, F012, F021, F022, F023, F060

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts apply — this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three charges, none of which the Court found to be a relevant and sufficient justification. The irreducible facts are:

1. The picket was held and the applicant was punished for it: F009, F058, F059, F060
2. The notification was only 2 days short, and the authorities acted as if it were timely: F003, F004, F065, F006, F008, F026
3. The obstruction was minimal, brief, and the applicant complied: F043, F048, F050
4. The distributed materials concerned a matter of public concern (judicial corruption), and the charge of variance was initiated by an interested official who prejudged guilt: F005, F010, F012, F021, F022, F023
5. The domestic courts provided no analysis of the variance charge: F052, F060, F061

These facts are the ones the case actually turned on — without them, the Court could not have found that none of the three charges provided relevant and sufficient justification, and therefore could not have concluded that the interference was not necessary in a democratic society.
