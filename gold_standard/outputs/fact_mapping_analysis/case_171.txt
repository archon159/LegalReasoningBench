LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of legal reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that assemblies were planned and held, and that the applicants were involved. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F003: applicants wished to hold a march in Warsaw (establishes planned assembly)
- F013: applicants informed Mayor of planned stationary assemblies (establishes planned assemblies)
- F028: march took place on 11 June 2005 despite refusal (establishes assembly occurred)
- F032: nine stationary assemblies held on 11 June 2005 (establishes assemblies occurred)

**Interference**
The Court's finding of interference rests on: (1) the official bans being in force when the assemblies were held, removing the presumption of legality; (2) the chilling effect; (3) the unavailability of effective remedies before the assemblies took place. Strictly necessary facts:
- F010: Traffic Officer refused permission for the march on 3 June 2005 (the ban on the march)
- F016: Mayor of Warsaw banned the stationary assemblies on 9 June 2005 (the ban on stationary assemblies)
- F028: march took place despite the ban (assemblies held under ban, without presumption of legality)
- F038: Local Government Appeals Board decision of 22 August 2005 — after the assemblies (remedy came too late)
- F049: Mazowsze Governor quashed refusals on 17 June 2005 — after the assemblies (remedy came too late)

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's finding rests on three pillars: the Local Government Appeals Board finding the march refusal unlawful, the Mazowsze Governor finding the stationary assembly refusals unlawful, and the Constitutional Court finding the Road Traffic Act provisions incompatible with the Constitution. Strictly necessary facts:
- F038: Local Government Appeals Board quashed decision of 3 June 2005 as unlawful
- F041: Local Government Appeals Board found no evidence organisers had been obliged to submit a traffic organisation plan (core reason for unlawfulness of march refusal)
- F049: Mazowsze Governor quashed refusals of 9 June 2005
- F055: Mazowsze Governor found requirement to submit permit under Road Traffic Act lacked legal basis in 1990 Assemblies Act (core reason for unlawfulness of stationary assembly refusals)
- F103: Constitutional Court found Road Traffic Act restrictions in breach of proportionality requirement
- F104: Constitutional Court concluded section 65 of Road Traffic Act incompatible with Constitution in so far as it applied to assemblies
- F093: provisions repealed as result of Constitutional Court judgment (relevant to the Court's caveat about timing)

**Legitimate aim**
- No atomic facts strictly necessary; element not examined.

**Necessity**
- No atomic facts strictly necessary; element not examined.

**Positive obligation**
The Court articulates the positive obligation principle in general terms and connects it to vulnerability of minorities. The strictly necessary fact grounding the case-specific relevance is:
- F004: purpose of march was to alert public opinion to discrimination against minorities (establishes the minority/unpopular views context)
- F017: applicants active in NGOs for persons of homosexual orientation (establishes minority status of applicants)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the finding that the interference was not prescribed by law. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed: (1) that there were official bans (interference); (2) that those bans were found unlawful by domestic authorities themselves; (3) that the underlying legislative framework was found unconstitutional. The assemblies having been held under a ban (without presumption of legality) and remedies arriving too late were necessary to establish interference in the first place. The following facts are strictly necessary:

- F010: refusal of permission for the march (establishes the ban = interference)
- F016: banning of stationary assemblies (establishes the ban = interference)
- F028: march took place despite the ban (establishes assemblies held without presumption of legality)
- F038: Local Government Appeals Board quashed decision of 3 June 2005 as unlawful (establishes interference not prescribed by law — march)
- F041: Local Government Appeals Board found no evidence organisers had been obliged to submit traffic organisation plan (establishes the specific unlawfulness of the march refusal)
- F049: Mazowsze Governor quashed refusals of 9 June 2005 (establishes interference not prescribed by law — stationary assemblies)
- F055: Mazowsze Governor found requirement under Road Traffic Act lacked legal basis in 1990 Assemblies Act (establishes specific unlawfulness of stationary assembly refusals)
- F103: Constitutional Court found Road Traffic Act restrictions in breach of proportionality (reinforces not prescribed by law finding)
- F104: Constitutional Court found section 65 of Road Traffic Act incompatible with Constitution as applied to assemblies (reinforces not prescribed by law finding)
