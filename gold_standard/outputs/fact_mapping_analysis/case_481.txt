--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---

Quote: "States may be required under Article 11 to take positive measures in order to protect a lawful demonstration against counter-demonstrations"
-> No specific atomic fact grounds this — it is a statement of general legal principle drawn from Plattform Arzte fur das Leben.

Quote: "The Constitutional Court dismissed this approach as being too narrow... It went on to say that the prohibition was nevertheless justified or even required by the State's positive obligation under Article 9 to protect persons manifesting their religion against deliberate disturbance by others. In arriving at that conclusion, the Constitutional Court had particular regard to the fact that All Saints' Day was an important religious holiday on which the population traditionally went to cemeteries to commemorate the dead and that disturbances caused by disputes between members of the assembly organised by the applicant and members of Comradeship IV were likely to occur in the light of the experience of previous years."
-> F045 (Constitutional Court found prohibition justified for other reasons), F049 (authorities correctly assumed prohibition necessary to protect public against disturbances), F050, F051 (commemoration of dead protected by Article 9), F052 (Article 9 contains positive obligation to protect persons manifesting religion), F053 (prohibition necessary under Article 11 § 2 for protection of rights of others)

Quote: "it was not alleged that any violent incidents had occurred on previous occasions" and "allowing both meetings while taking preventive measures... was not a viable alternative" — used to reject the positive obligation justification
-> F031, F032, F033, F012

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish that there was a planned assembly, that the applicant sought to participate/organise it, and that it was peaceful.
- F003: applicant notified authority of planned meeting (establishes assembly existed and applicant was organiser)
- F005: meeting on All Saints' Day at Salzburg municipal cemetery (establishes the assembly)
- F008: purpose was to commemorate Salzburg Jews killed by SS (establishes nature and purpose)
- F009: applicant expected about six participants (establishes small, contained assembly)
- F010: participants would carry commemorative messages (establishes peaceful means)
- F011: no chanting or banners that might offend piety or undermine public order (establishes peaceful character explicitly)
- F029: Comradeship IV members mainly former SS members (establishes the counter-demonstration context the Court relies on)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
- F012: Federal Police Authority prohibited the planned meeting
- F026: remedies ordered not to have suspensive effect
- F027: as a result, the planned demonstration could not take place

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
- F013: prohibition relied on section 6 of the Assembly Act
- F060: section 6 requires prohibition of assemblies endangering public order and security

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
- F015: prohibition on ground of endangering public order and security (prevention of disorder)
- F023: risk of offending religious feelings of cemetery visitors (protection of rights of others)
- F050: All Saints' Day is important religious holiday on which population visits cemeteries (contextualises the rights of others)

--- NECESSITY ---
- F004: applicant is member of parliament (weight of his expressive interest as public figure)
- F008: purpose of meeting (weight of expressive interest — public interest topic)
- F009: about six participants expected (limited scale of assembly)
- F010: participants would carry commemorative messages (peaceful, limited means)
- F011: no chanting or banners (peaceful character; assembly could not hurt feelings of cemetery-goers)
- F018: same time and place as Comradeship IV (essential to the message; also source of tension)
- F029: Comradeship IV members mainly former SS members (establishes the public interest nature of the protest)
- F031: protest campaigns in prior years (background to disturbance risk)
- F032: led to vehement discussions (nature of prior disturbances — not violent)
- F033: required police intervention (prior disturbances required police but no violence alleged)
- F043: Constitutional Court stated prohibition not justified if sole purpose were to protect Comradeship IV ceremony (Court adopts this)
- F035: prohibition found necessary for maintenance of public order (domestic authority's reasoning the Court scrutinises)
- F036: prohibition found necessary for protection of Comradeship IV ceremony (domestic authority's reasoning the Court rejects)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
- F050: All Saints' Day is important religious holiday (basis for Constitutional Court's Article 9 reasoning)
- F051: commemoration of dead as religious tradition protected by Article 9 (basis for positive obligation argument)
- F052: Article 9 contains positive obligation to protect persons manifesting religion (the Constitutional Court's legal basis)
- F053: Constitutional Court found prohibition necessary under Article 11 § 2 for protection of rights of others (the domestic conclusion the ECtHR examines)
- F031: prior protest campaigns (basis for likelihood of disturbance)
- F032: led to vehement discussions (nature of prior disturbances)
- F033: required police intervention (prior disturbances — but no violence)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The Court found the prohibition disproportionate because: (a) the assembly was peaceful and small-scale; (b) it served a genuine public interest expressive purpose; (c) prior disturbances were not violent; and (d) less restrictive alternatives (police presence) were available. The following facts are strictly necessary:

- F004: applicant is member of parliament — establishes the heightened weight of his expressive interest on a matter of public concern
- F008: purpose was to commemorate Salzburg Jews killed by SS — establishes the public interest nature of the assembly's message
- F009: about six participants expected — establishes the limited scale, directly informing proportionality
- F010: participants would carry commemorative messages — establishes the peaceful, non-disruptive means
- F011: no chanting or banners — establishes the peaceful character; the Court explicitly says the assembly "could not have hurt the feelings of cemetery-goers"
- F012: unconditional prohibition imposed — establishes the severity of the interference (the Court calls it "a very far-reaching measure")
- F027: demonstration could not take place — establishes the complete denial of the right
- F029: Comradeship IV members mainly former SS members — establishes why the coincidence in time/place was an essential part of the message and why the protest was on a matter of public interest
- F032: prior protests led to vehement discussions (not violent incidents) — the Court's finding that no violence was alleged is critical to rejecting the disturbance justification
- F033: prior protests required police intervention — establishes that police presence was a known and available tool, supporting the viability of a less restrictive alternative
- F043: Constitutional Court stated prohibition not justified if sole purpose were to protect Comradeship IV — the Court fully agrees, eliminating one potential justification and narrowing the analysis to the cemetery-goers' Article 9 rights
