LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies..."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
Note: The dispersal reference in the quote is part of the same sentence but F002 and F003 are the closest atomic facts. There is no separate atomic fact for dispersal.

Scope (peaceful):
No factual grounding was identified. No atomic facts correspond.

Interference:
The factual grounding quote was: "namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence" and the Court's own use of "interferences."
This maps to:
- F002 and F003 again, as these are the atomic facts describing the complained-of measures. There is no separate atomic fact for dispersal, arrest, or conviction.

Necessity:
The factual grounding was the Court's reference to "all the material submitted" and similarity to prior cases. No specific atomic facts were identified as explicitly adopted by the Court. The only atomic facts available that could loosely ground this are F002 and F003, as they describe the subject matter of the complaints that the Court assessed for necessity.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of law discussed

Scope (implied):
The Court's implied finding that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated rests strictly on F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants). Both are needed because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants. However, since both F002 and F003 establish the same point (participation in public assemblies), the most critical and representative facts are both, as they cover the full applicant group. I include both: F002, F003.

Interference (explicit/implied):
The finding of interference rests on the same factual basis — the measures taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. The most critical atomic fact is F002 and F003, as they identify the complained-of measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) as the subject matter. Both are necessary because the applicant group spans both categories.

Prescribed by law:
Not discussed. No atomic facts.

Legitimate aim:
Not discussed. No atomic facts.

Necessity:
The necessity finding is grounded in the same facts that establish the subject matter of the interference — F002 and F003. These are the only atomic facts available that the Court could have relied upon when concluding that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society.

Positive obligation:
Not discussed. No atomic facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on the existence of public assemblies, the applicants' role as organisers or participants, and the measures taken against them (dispersal, arrest, conviction). The only atomic facts that are strictly necessary to reach this conclusion are F002 and F003 — without these, there is no assembly, no participation, and no interference to assess. F001 is procedural and F004 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. F002 and F003 together constitute the irreducible factual core.
