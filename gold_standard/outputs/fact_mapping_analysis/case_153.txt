--- SCOPE ---

Sub-element: Assembly exists
The Court refers to "the protest in question" and "the demonstration" without grounding this in explicit factual findings. The atomic facts that correspond to this background reference are:
F006: The applicants gathered near the metro station Elmlar Akademiyasi in Baku to protest.
F008: The protest on 6 March 2012 was spontaneous and peaceful.

Sub-element: Participation
Grounding quote: "Given that the third applicant participated in the protest in question..."
Atomic facts:
F006: The applicants, together with a number of other persons, gathered at around 3 p.m. on 6 March 2012 near the metro station Elmlar Akademiyasi in Baku to protest.
F010: The police arrested seventeen protesters, including the applicants, on 6 March 2012.

Sub-element: Peaceful assembly
Grounding quote 1: "in the absence of any evidence to suggest that the third applicant or other protesters presented a danger to public order"
Grounding quote 2: "Nothing in the materials before the Court suggests that the third applicant committed any reprehensible offence during the demonstration."
Atomic facts:
F008: The protest on 6 March 2012 near the metro station Elmlar Akademiyasi in Baku was spontaneous and peaceful.
F017: The administrative-offence reports in respect of the third applicant stated that the third applicant had committed an administrative offence under Article 298 (violation of the rules on holding public gatherings) — notably, this is a procedural/regulatory offence, not a public order offence, which supports the absence of danger finding.
F019: The third applicant was ordered to pay a fine of 10 AZN — a minor sanction consistent with no serious wrongdoing.

--- NECESSITY ---

Grounding quote 1: "in the absence of any evidence to suggest that the third applicant or other protesters presented a danger to public order"

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly, that the third applicant participated, and that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
F006: Establishes both the existence of the assembly and the third applicant's participation in it.
F008: Establishes the peaceful character of the assembly — directly necessary for the scope finding.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the third applicant's assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
F006: Establishes participation (predicate for interference finding).
F009: Establishes the dispersal by police — the core act of interference.
F017: Establishes that administrative proceedings were initiated against the third applicant specifically.
F019: Establishes that a fine was imposed on the third applicant — the concrete legal consequence constituting interference.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on four factual pillars: (1) no danger to public order, (2) vital subject matter of the protest, (3) domestic courts' failure to identify specific wrongdoing by the third applicant, (4) no reprehensible conduct. The strictly necessary facts are:
F008: Establishes the peaceful and spontaneous character of the protest — directly supports the "no danger to public order" finding.
F005: Establishes the context of the protest (ill-treatment of detainees) — directly supports the "vital issues" finding.
F006: Establishes the subject matter of the protest — supports the "vital issues" finding.
F017: Establishes that the charge against the third applicant was a regulatory offence (Article 298), not a conduct-based offence — supports the "no reprehensible offence" finding.
F025: Establishes that the domestic court dismissed the complaints without substantive engagement — supports the finding that courts made no specific findings about the third applicant's conduct.
F032: Establishes that the Supreme Court similarly dismissed the cassation appeal — reinforces the absence of any court finding on the third applicant's specific conduct.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis. The Court found the interference unjustified because: (a) the protest was peaceful and posed no danger to public order; (b) it concerned vital issues (ill-treatment of detainees); (c) the domestic courts made no findings about the third applicant's specific conduct; and (d) the charge against him was purely regulatory. The irreducible facts are:

F006: Establishes that the third applicant participated in a protest against the ill-treatment of detainees — necessary for both scope/participation and the "vital issues" finding in necessity.
F008: Establishes that the protest was peaceful — necessary for the peaceful assembly finding and the "no danger to public order" finding in necessity.
F005: Establishes the subject matter (ill-treatment of political detainees) — necessary for the "vital issues" finding.
F017: Establishes that the charge against the third applicant was a regulatory offence under Article 298, not a conduct-based offence — necessary for the "no reprehensible offence" finding.
F009: Establishes the dispersal by police — necessary to establish interference.
F019: Establishes the fine imposed on the third applicant — necessary to establish the concrete interference.
F032: Establishes that the Supreme Court dismissed the cassation appeal without substantive engagement — necessary for the finding that domestic courts made no specific findings about the third applicant's conduct.
