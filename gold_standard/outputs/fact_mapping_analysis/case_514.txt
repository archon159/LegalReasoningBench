LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

--- SCOPE (implied) ---
The Court's only textual basis was: "it was not in dispute between the parties that there had been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly – and it has no reason to hold otherwise."
This implies: there was a planned assembly (F007, F008), the applicant was the organiser/participant (F007, F008), and it was treated as peaceful (no fact disputes this; F007 describes the purpose as drawing attention to borrowers' plight, which is a peaceful expressive purpose).
Atomic facts: F007, F008

--- INTERFERENCE (explicit) ---
The Court adopted the parties' common position: "it was not in dispute between the parties that there had been an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of peaceful assembly – and it has no reason to hold otherwise."
The interference was the ban on the demonstration. This corresponds to:
F011: the Budapest Police Department dismissed the applicant's application.
F028: the Budapest Administrative and Labour Court held that the demonstration had to be prohibited.
Atomic facts: F011, F028

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW (explicit) ---
The Court identified four factual anchors:

1. "section 8(1) of the Assembly Act provides for two reasons for banning a planned demonstration: where the planned event 'seriously endangers the proper functioning of the representative bodies or courts', or where 'the circulation of traffic cannot be secured by another route'."
Atomic fact: F036

2. "the authorities dealing with the applicant's case did not rely on any of these reasons. In particular, the court hearing the applicant's complaint based its decision on the residents' right to respect for their private life and free movement, as well as the right of official dignitaries to statutory protection."
Atomic facts: F026, F027, F028, F032

3. "these considerations do not figure in the exhaustive list contained in section 8(1) of the Assembly Act, for which reason the Constitutional Court reached the conclusion that the lower court had unlawfully expanded the grounds for banning a demonstration."
Atomic fact: F033

4. "this ruling has effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis of the impugned measure."
Atomic fact: F033 (same ruling; the legal consequence the Court draws from it)

No factual grounding relevant; element not assessed.

No factual grounding relevant; element not assessed.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court implied scope was satisfied. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that there was a planned peaceful assembly in which the applicant sought to participate:
F007: the applicant planned a demonstration to draw attention to the plight of borrowers of foreign-currency loans (establishes assembly and its peaceful expressive purpose).
F008: the applicant applied for acquiescence in the demonstration (establishes participation/organiser role).

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary fact is the act that constituted the interference — the prohibition of the demonstration. The police dismissal (F011) initiated the ban, but the final operative prohibition upheld by the court is what the ECtHR treated as the interference:
F011: the Budapest Police Department dismissed the applicant's application.
F028: the Budapest Administrative and Labour Court held that the demonstration had to be prohibited.
Both are necessary: F011 is the original act; F028 is the judicial confirmation that made the ban definitive and is the act the Constitutional Court later reviewed.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
Three facts are strictly necessary:
F036: the content of section 8(1) of the Assembly Act — the exhaustive list of permissible grounds for banning a demonstration. Without this, the Court cannot establish what the law actually permitted.
F033: the Constitutional Court's finding that the lower court unlawfully broadened the grounds — this is the fact that "effectively, if retroactively, removed the legal basis." This is the pivotal fact for the prescribed-by-law conclusion.
F032: the Constitutional Court's observation that the lower court had construed private life, free movement, and dignitary protection as grounds for restriction — this establishes what the lower court actually did, which is the conduct that F033 condemns. (F026/F027/F028 establish the same point from the lower court's own record, but F032 is the Constitutional Court's authoritative characterisation of it, which is what the ECtHR relied on.)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
Not assessed by the Court. Empty.

--- NECESSITY ---
Not assessed by the Court. Empty.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the "prescribed by law" element. The Court found a violation because the domestic authorities banned the demonstration on grounds not listed in the Assembly Act's exhaustive list, and the Constitutional Court confirmed this was unlawful, thereby retroactively removing the legal basis.

The irreducible facts are:
F007: establishes that there was a planned assembly (necessary to bring the case within Article 11's scope at all).
F008: establishes the applicant's role as organiser seeking to exercise the right (necessary for standing and scope).
F011 or F028: one act of prohibition is necessary to establish interference. F028 is the more critical because it is the judicial ban that the Constitutional Court reviewed and that the ECtHR treated as the operative interference. F011 is also necessary as the originating act, but if forced to choose the single most critical, F028 is it. However, both are included as the interference finding rested on the chain of decisions.
F036: the content of section 8(1) — the exhaustive legal grounds — is strictly necessary because without knowing what the law permitted, the Court cannot find that the authorities went beyond it.
F033: the Constitutional Court's finding of unlawful broadening is the single most critical fact. It is the fact that "removed the legal basis" and directly grounded the "not prescribed by law" conclusion and thus the violation finding.
F032: the Constitutional Court's characterisation of what the lower court did (construing private life etc. as grounds) is necessary to understand why F033 was reached and what conduct was condemned.

Final list: F007, F008, F011, F028, F036, F032, F033
