LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that assemblies took place, that the applicants participated, and that the assemblies were peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F002, F003, F004, F005: the four demonstrations took place (existence of assemblies)
- F013: each applicant attended one of the demonstrations (participation)
- F007, F008: the demonstrations were peaceful (peaceful character — the Court accepts this implicitly by characterising them as 'peaceful' in its necessity analysis)

F006 is relevant but secondary — it is the absence of notice, not the existence of the assembly itself.

Strictly necessary: F002, F003, F004, F005, F007, F008, F013

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F014: the police dispersed the demonstrations
- F015: all applicants were arrested
- F054: the applicants were convicted under Article 298.2
- F055: the applicants were sentenced to administrative detention or fines

Strictly necessary: F014, F015, F054, F055

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's reasoning turns on whether the demonstrations were spontaneous (and thus exempt from prior notice under F067), and whether the applicants substantiated this. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F006: no prior notice was given
- F067: the law exempted spontaneous assemblies from prior notice
- F012: the 29 December demonstration was an immediate response to the death of a disabled war veteran (establishing spontaneity for that date)
- F005: the demonstration of 29 December 2013 (the specific date matters for the spontaneity finding)
- F009: the death of soldiers was a widespread problem (used by the Court to undermine the spontaneity claim for 12 January)
- F002: the 12 January demonstration (the specific date for which spontaneity was rejected)

Strictly necessary: F002, F005, F006, F009, F012, F067

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed by the Court with factual grounding. Empty.

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity finding turns on: (a) no relevant and sufficient reasons were given for dispersal; (b) the authorities failed to acknowledge that peaceful participation in an unauthorised assembly is protected; (c) the measures had a chilling effect. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F007, F008: the demonstrations were peaceful (the core of the protection the authorities failed to acknowledge)
- F014: dispersal of the demonstrations
- F054: conviction under Article 298.2
- F055: sentences of administrative detention or fines (the sanctions producing the chilling effect)
- F071: participation in an assembly organised in breach of rules became explicitly punishable (the legal basis for the chilling effect)
- F072: administrative detention of up to 15 days introduced for the first time (severity of sanction, chilling effect)

F085 and F086 (PACE Resolution) and F090 (Commissioner's report) are corroborating international materials the Court drew on for the chilling effect finding, but the Court's own reasoning is grounded primarily in the domestic facts. However, F090 is directly quoted in substance in the Court's chilling effect paragraph and was explicitly referenced in Gafgaz Mammadov. Given the Court's reliance on Gafgaz Mammadov by reference, these international documents are part of the factual substrate but not strictly necessary for the logical conclusion — the domestic facts suffice.

Strictly necessary: F007, F008, F014, F054, F055, F071, F072

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis — specifically that the authorities dispersed peaceful demonstrations and convicted participants without relevant and sufficient justification, producing a chilling effect. The prescribed-by-law analysis was left open for 29 December and accepted for the other dates, so the violation rests entirely on necessity. The irreducible facts are:

- F007, F008: the demonstrations were peaceful — this is the foundation of the entire Article 11 protection and the core of the Court's finding that authorities failed to acknowledge the protection afforded to peaceful participation
- F014: the police dispersed the demonstrations — the primary act of interference
- F054: the applicants were convicted — the legal consequence that compounds the interference
- F055: the applicants were sentenced to administrative detention or fines — the severity of sanction that produces the chilling effect
- F071: participation in an assembly organised in breach of rules became explicitly punishable — the legal basis that enabled the convictions and the chilling effect
- F072: administrative detention of up to 15 days introduced for the first time — the severity of the new sanction regime underpinning the chilling effect finding

F006 (no prior notice) is a necessary background fact but is not what the violation turned on — the Court accepted lawfulness for three of the four dates and left it open for the fourth, proceeding to necessity in all cases. The violation is grounded in the unjustified response to peaceful assembly, not in the absence of notice per se.

Irreducible core: F007, F008, F014, F054, F055, F071, F072
