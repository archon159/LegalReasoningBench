LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) a picket took place; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) it was directed against her employer and concerned work-related issues (making it a peaceful assembly within Art. 11 scope); (iv) prior notice was given to authorities.
-> F019 (written notice given to Mayor of Rivne on 22 February 1999 — establishes the assembly was pre-notified)
-> F020 (the notice stated employees would picket the management of Komunenergiya — establishes the assembly was directed against the employer)
-> F030 (the applicant and some seventy others took part in the picket on 3 March 1999 — establishes the assembly existed and the applicant participated)
-> F031 (participants expressed concerns about management's failure to ensure timely payment of salaries — establishes the work-related character of the assembly)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish: the dismissal was causally linked to the applicant's participation in the picket.
-> F030 (the applicant took part in the picket on 3 March 1999)
-> F033 (the applicant came to work two hours late on 3 March 1999 as a result of the picket)
-> F044 (the applicant was dismissed on 10 March 1999 for systematic breach of duties, one ground being the absence on 3 March 1999)
-> F055 (Komunenergiya confirmed the dismissal was in part because of the unauthorised absence on 3 March 1999)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needed to establish: the dismissal was grounded in domestic law; a legal procedure for strikes existed with safeguards; the applicant did not follow that procedure.
-> F059 (the Rivne Court concluded the applicant was lawfully dismissed pursuant to Article 40 § 3 of the Labour Code)
-> F074 (Article 40 § 3 of the Labour Code provides the legal basis for dismissal for systematic failure to fulfil duties)
-> F082 (Section 19 of the Act provides the procedure for announcing a strike)
-> F084 (Section 19 requires advance notice to the employer)
-> F090 (Section 27 provides immunity from disciplinary action for participants in a lawful strike — the key safeguard)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court needed to establish: the interference served the protection of the employer's rights under domestic labour law.
-> F074 (Article 40 § 3 of the Labour Code provides the legal basis for the employer's right to dismiss for systematic failure to fulfil duties)
-> F044 (the applicant was dismissed pursuant to Article 40 § 3)

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court needed to establish: the applicant failed to take adequate steps to minimise disruption; the absence caused actual disruption; the applicant did not follow the strike procedure.
-> F033 (the applicant came to work two hours late on 3 March 1999)
-> F035 (the applicant's colleague had to work two extra hours after a night shift because of the applicant's absence)
-> F051 (the applicant claimed she had arranged for a colleague to replace her — accepted only as an assumption by the Court)
-> F052 (the applicant acknowledged she had not informed her direct supervisor in advance)
-> F082 (Section 19 provides the procedure for announcing a strike)
-> F084 (Section 19 requires advance notice to the employer)
-> F019 (the written notice to the Mayor did not indicate planned absence from work)
-> F020 (the written notice stated only that a picket would take place at 7.30 a.m.)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts applicable.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment. The Court found no violation because: (a) the applicant's participation in the picket caused a two-hour absence that disrupted workplace processes; (b) she failed to give advance notice to her direct supervisor; (c) she did not follow the statutory strike procedure which would have afforded her protection from disciplinary action; and (d) the dismissal was grounded in domestic law. The following facts are strictly necessary:

-> F030 (the applicant participated in the picket on 3 March 1999 — establishes the assembly and participation, the foundation of the entire analysis)
-> F033 (the applicant came to work two hours late on 3 March 1999 — the direct consequence of the picket that triggered the disciplinary sanction)
-> F035 (the applicant's colleague had to work two extra hours after a night shift — establishes the actual workplace disruption)
-> F044 (the applicant was dismissed pursuant to Article 40 § 3 of the Labour Code — the interference)
-> F052 (the applicant acknowledged she had not informed her direct supervisor in advance — the key failure underpinning the proportionality finding)
-> F059 (the domestic courts found the dismissal lawful under Article 40 § 3 — grounds the prescribed by law finding)
-> F074 (Article 40 § 3 of the Labour Code — the legal basis for the dismissal)
-> F082 (Section 19 of the Act provides the strike procedure — the procedure the applicant failed to follow)
-> F090 (Section 27 provides immunity for participants in a lawful strike — the safeguard the applicant could have invoked but did not)
