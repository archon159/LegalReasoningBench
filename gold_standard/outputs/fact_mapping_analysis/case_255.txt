LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists / applicable)**
The Court's grounding quote: 'the right to freedom of assembly as protected under Article 11 of the Convention may cover both private and public meetings, including an assembly of an essentially social character... and finds that this provision is applicable in the present case.'
The factual basis for applicability is that a gathering took place at a private café. Relevant atomic facts:
- F007: A group called the 'Che Guevara Fan Club' held a gathering at a private café in Baku to celebrate the eightieth anniversary of the birth of Che Guevara.
- F008: The café was located in the basement of a building in the city centre of Baku.
- F009: There were about twenty-five attendees.
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly provided that the right to hold gatherings in places which are in private ownership is not regulated by that Law (confirms private ownership character).

**Scope (participation)**
The Court implies participation from the interference finding. The grounding is:
- F010: The applicant attended the gathering, accompanied by two colleagues from the IRFS.

**Scope (peaceful)**
The Court's grounding quote: 'the dispersal of a peaceful gathering in a private property.' No explicit factual analysis is given, but the peaceful nature is implied by the absence of any allegation of violence by attendees. The most relevant atomic facts are:
- F007: The gathering was to celebrate the eightieth anniversary of the birth of Che Guevara (social/commemorative character).
- F009: About twenty-five attendees — no suggestion of violence.
- F012: Police entered approximately thirty minutes after the gathering began — no suggestion of disorder prompting entry.

**Interference**
The Court's grounding quote: 'there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly on account of the dispersal by the police of the gathering of 14 June 2008.'
Relevant atomic facts:
- F012: About thirty police officers entered the café approximately thirty minutes after the gathering began.
- F013: Some officers were in uniform, others in plain clothes.
- F014: The police officers suspended the gathering and announced they were going to take the attendees to the police station.
- F019: One officer ordered other officers to take the applicant to the police station.
- F087: The explanation from Nasimi District Police Station no. 22 confirmed that police took measures to identify persons gathered at café 'Alaturka' and twenty-two of them were taken to the police station.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's grounding quotes map to the following atomic facts:

1. 'the Government failed to refer to any domestic law or provisions which could constitute the legal basis for the dispersal of a gathering held in places which are in private ownership.'
- F050: The Government stated the police intervened on the basis of a complaint from neighbours (no legal provision cited).
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly explicitly excluded private gatherings from its scope.
- F141: Article 7 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly sets out permissible restrictions — but only for assemblies regulated by the Law.
- F142: Article 14 sets out police powers in connection with assemblies — but again only for assemblies regulated by the Law.

2. 'Article 4.1 of the same Law clearly provided that the right to hold gatherings in places which are in private ownership is not regulated by this Law.'
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly.

3. 'The Government also did not contest that an assembly held in a private café is not subject to any requirement of notification.'
- F140: Article 4.1 (private gatherings not regulated by the Law, hence no notification requirement).
- F008: The café was in private ownership (basement of a building).

4. 'the Government failed to submit any evidence in support of their argument [about neighbour complaints].'
- F050: The Government's assertion about neighbour complaints — asserted but unsubstantiated.

5. 'the Head of Nasimi District Police Station no. 22 stated that the police had intervened in order to identify the persons gathered and to establish the purpose of the gathering, without referring to any complaint from neighbours.'
- F086: The explanation was issued by Nasimi District Police Station no. 22 on 25 June 2008, signed by the Head M.T.
- F087: The explanation stated that police took measures to identify persons gathered at café 'Alaturka' and twenty-two were taken to the police station.
- F089: Statements were taken from nine of the twenty-two individuals in order to establish the purpose of their gathering.

6. 'The spokesman for the Ministry of Internal Affairs also indicated in his statement of 17 June 2008 that the police had intervened in order to interrupt an unauthorised gathering and to identify its participants, without referring to any complaint from neighbours.'
- F061: The spokesman for the Ministry of Internal Affairs gave an interview on 17 June 2008.
- F064: The spokesman stated that a group of people held an unauthorised gathering at a café and the police requested the interruption of the unauthorised gathering.
- F065: The spokesman stated that twenty people were taken to the police station in order to establish their identity.

Not discussed by the Court. No factual grounding to map.

Not discussed by the Court. No factual grounding to map.

Not taken up by the Court in its own assessment. No factual grounding to map.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needed to establish that the gathering at a private café falls within Article 11. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F007: The gathering was held at a private café (establishes the assembly and its private character).
- F010: The applicant attended the gathering (establishes participation).
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly confirmed the private ownership character and the absence of any regulatory framework applying to it (supports the finding that it is a private gathering within Article 11's scope).

**Interference**
The Court needed to establish that the police dispersed the gathering. The strictly necessary fact is:
- F014: The police officers suspended the gathering and announced they were going to take the attendees to the police station.
- F087: The official police explanation confirmed that police took measures to identify persons gathered and twenty-two were taken to the police station (official confirmation of dispersal).

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's reasoning on this element turns on four points: (i) no legal basis existed for dispersing a private gathering; (ii) the Law on Freedom of Assembly explicitly excluded private gatherings; (iii) the Government's stated reason (neighbour complaints) was unsubstantiated and contradicted by official accounts; (iv) the official accounts showed the real reason was identification of participants, not any lawful basis. Strictly necessary facts:
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly — the private gathering was explicitly excluded from the Law's scope, meaning Articles 7 and 14 (the police powers provisions) did not apply.
- F050: The Government's assertion that the intervention was based on neighbour complaints — the only reason offered, but unsupported by evidence.
- F086 + F087 + F089: The official police explanation (Head of Nasimi District Police Station no. 22) stating the purpose was to identify persons and establish the purpose of the gathering — contradicting the neighbour complaint rationale and revealing no lawful basis.
- F061 + F064 + F065: The Ministry of Internal Affairs spokesman's statement that the police intervened to interrupt an 'unauthorised gathering' and identify participants — again contradicting the neighbour complaint rationale and revealing no lawful basis.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed by the Court. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the 'prescribed by law' requirement. The Court found a violation because: (a) the gathering was at a private café, which was explicitly excluded from the Law on Freedom of Assembly; (b) the Government offered no legal basis for the dispersal; (c) the Government's stated reason (neighbour complaints) was contradicted by official accounts showing the real purpose was identification of participants. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary for this conclusion:

- F007: The gathering was held at a private café — this is the foundational fact that triggers the exclusion under Article 4.1 and the absence of any legal basis.
- F014: The police suspended the gathering and announced they were taking attendees to the police station — establishes the interference.
- F140: Article 4.1 of the Law on Freedom of Assembly explicitly excluded private gatherings from the Law's scope — the legal cornerstone of the 'not prescribed by law' finding.
- F050: The Government's only stated reason was neighbour complaints — the sole legal justification offered, which the Court found unsubstantiated.
- F087: The official police explanation stated the purpose was to identify persons gathered — contradicts the neighbour complaint rationale and confirms absence of lawful basis.
- F064: The Ministry of Internal Affairs spokesman stated the police intervened to interrupt an 'unauthorised gathering' — further contradicts the neighbour complaint rationale and reveals the intervention was treated as targeting an 'unauthorised' assembly, when in fact no authorisation requirement applied to private gatherings.
