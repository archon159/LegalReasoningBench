LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two key findings: (1) for 7 April, the applicant was sanctioned for mere participation in a lawful, peaceful demonstration where traffic had been pre-emptively suspended, meaning the "obstruction" was not a genuine public order concern; (2) for 9 April, the domestic court's factual findings were so sparse and procedurally deficient that they could not justify the severe penalty of five days' detention.

The irreducible facts are:
- F006: the assembly of 7 April 2003 existed and was not prohibited
- F012: the applicant participated in the 7 April demonstration
- F052: the judge's entire finding for 7 April (obstruction and loud noise — the only acts found, identical to Galstyan)
- F010 + F011: traffic was suspended beforehand by traffic police and Government did not dispute this — demolishes the obstruction finding
- F051: the sanction imposed on 7 April (fine — even a minor sanction is impermissible for mere participation)
- F081: the assembly of 9 April 2003 existed
- F083: the applicant participated in the 9 April march
- F118: the judge's entire finding for 9 April (the only factual basis for conviction — strikingly sparse)
- F099: record of administrative offence for 9 April (no substantial detail of reprehensible behaviour)
- F090: police report for 9 April (no substantial detail)
- F122 + F126: hearing of 9 April conducted in same manner as 7 April (no proper evidence-taking, no witnesses)
- F117: five days' detention imposed on 9 April — the harsh penalty that the Court found unjustified
- F119: aggravating circumstance of repetition based on 7 April conduct — which the Court had already found was not reprehensible
