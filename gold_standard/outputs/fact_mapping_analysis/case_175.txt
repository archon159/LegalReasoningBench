LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicants participated; (iii) it was peaceful.
- Assembly exists and participation: The critical facts are the domestic court findings that a group public event was held and that the applicants participated: F071, F072, F102.
- Peaceful: The critical fact is the absence of any finding or suggestion of violence. The most representative facts describing the nature of the conduct (banner-holding) are F071 and F102, which describe the event without any mention of violence.
Strictly necessary: F071, F072, F102.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needs to establish that governmental acts interfered with the applicants' assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the termination of the assembly, the arrests, and the convictions/fines:
- Termination and arrest of first applicant: F019 (arrest).
- Termination and arrest of second applicant: F026 (taken to police station).
- Conviction and fine of first applicant: F068, F069.
- Conviction and fine of second applicant: F097, F103.
Strictly necessary: F019, F026, F068, F069, F097, F103.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's reasoning on foreseeability turns on the legislative gap at the time of the events. The strictly necessary fact is:
- F122: The regional law specifying the minimum distance between solo demonstrations was only amended in December 2012, after the events of 15 September 2012 — establishing that no such distance had been specified at the relevant time.
Strictly necessary: F122.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — element not resolved.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on three points: absence of violence, severity of the response (arrest + non-negligible fines), and failure to explain why a less restrictive response was not used.
- Absence of violence: F071, F102 (domestic court findings describe the event as a group picket with banners — no violence mentioned).
- Arrest of applicants: F019, F026.
- Non-negligible fines: F069 (20,000 roubles fine on first applicant), F103 (20,000 roubles fine on second applicant).
- No explanation for preferring arrest over allowing assembly to continue: F015 (police ordered first applicant to stop — termination without explanation), F025 (police ordered second applicant to show banner — intervention without explanation).
Strictly necessary: F015, F019, F025, F026, F069, F071, F102, F103.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — element not discussed.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis. The Court found a violation because: (a) the applicants held a peaceful assembly (no violence); (b) the authorities terminated the assembly, arrested the applicants, and imposed non-negligible fines; (c) there was no pressing social need for this response given the absence of violence or disruption; and (d) the authorities offered no explanation for not allowing the assembly to continue and imposing a lesser sanction.

The irreducible facts are:
- F071: Establishes that a group public event was held (assembly exists, peaceful in nature — no violence mentioned).
- F102: Establishes the second applicant's participation in the group event (same point for second applicant).
- F019: First applicant arrested (interference — termination and arrest).
- F026: Second applicant taken to police station (interference — termination and arrest).
- F069: First applicant fined 20,000 roubles (non-negligible sanction, central to necessity).
- F103: Second applicant fined 20,000 roubles (non-negligible sanction, central to necessity).
- F015: Police ordered first applicant to stop the picket (termination of assembly without explanation).
- F025: Police ordered second applicant to show her banner and she was then removed (termination without explanation).
- F122: Regional law specifying minimum distance between solo demonstrations only enacted in December 2012, after the events — relevant to the foreseeability/prescribed by law discussion that contextualizes the whole case.
