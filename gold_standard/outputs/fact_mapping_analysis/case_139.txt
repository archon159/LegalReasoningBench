LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

For each element where I identified factual grounding in the reasoning text, I now map the quoted passage to the available atomic facts.

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful)**
The quoted grounding was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
- F002 directly corresponds: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." This is almost verbatim the same language.
- F003 adds that the assemblies were "manifestations for fair elections to the Moscow City Parliament" — this is the nature of the assembly, relevant to scope (assembly exists) and arguably to peaceful character (a political demonstration for fair elections).
- F004 adds time and place: summer 2019 in Moscow. This contextualizes the assembly but does not add to the legal element directly.
- F001 is purely procedural and does not ground any legal element.

**Interference**
The same quoted passage applies: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
- F002 again is the primary correspondence.
- F003 and F004 provide context but are not the direct grounding for the interference finding.

**Necessity**
The Court's conclusion was: "the Court considers that in the instant case the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was not 'necessary in a democratic society'." As noted previously, this is grounded in analogy to prior case-law, not in specific facts. However, to the extent any atomic fact supports the necessity analysis, it would be:
- F002: the nature of the measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) is the conduct whose necessity is being assessed.
- F003: the subject matter of the assembly (fair elections) is relevant to the weight of the right being interfered with — political speech and assembly attract the highest protection under Article 11, which bears on the necessity/proportionality assessment.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**: The Court must establish that there was an assembly, that the applicants participated, and (implicitly) that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary fact is F002 (applicants as participants of public assemblies). F003 is also necessary because it identifies the nature of the assembly (a political manifestation), which supports the inference that it falls within Article 11 scope and was peaceful in character. F004 is not strictly necessary for the legal element, only for context.

**Interference**: The strictly necessary fact is F002, which describes the dispersal, arrest, and conviction — the acts constituting interference. No other atomic fact is strictly necessary for this element.

**Prescribed by law**: Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.

**Legitimate aim**: Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.

**Necessity**: F002 is strictly necessary — it identifies the measures whose necessity is assessed (dispersal, arrest, conviction). F003 is also strictly necessary because the political nature of the assembly (fair elections) is what makes the interference particularly difficult to justify as necessary in a democratic society — political assemblies attract the strongest protection, and this is what connects the present case to the leading cases cited (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov, all involving political assemblies in Russia).

**Positive obligation**: Not discussed. No atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that the interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly was not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated (engaging Article 11 at all); (2) that governmental measures interfered with that participation (dispersal, arrest, conviction); and (3) that the nature of the assembly was such that the interference could not be justified — here the political character of the manifestation (fair elections) is what connects the case to the established line of Russian assembly cases and makes the violation finding inevitable. F001 and F005 are purely procedural and irrelevant to the substantive conclusion. F004 (time and place) is contextual but not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion. The irreducible core is therefore F002 and F003.
