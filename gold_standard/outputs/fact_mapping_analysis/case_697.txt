LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (implied)**
The Court refers throughout to "the applicant organisation's public rally of 19 April 2000" as the subject matter. The atomic facts grounding the existence of an assembly, the applicant's participation, and the peaceful character are:
- F005: The applicant organisation informed the Plovdiv Municipality of its intention to hold a public rally on 19 April 2000.
- F006: The aim of the public rally was to protest against the municipality's actions.
- F007: The aim included a demand that the uprooting and eradication of plant life be stopped.
- F013: The applicant organisation did not hold a rally on 19 April 2000 (implies participation was attempted but prevented).
No facts explicitly establish peacefulness, but no facts suggest otherwise either.

**Interference**
The Court states: "The parties agreed that there had been an interference with the exercise of the applicant organisation's right to freedom of peaceful assembly." The factual basis for the interference is:
- F008: The Plovdiv Municipality informed the applicant organisation that it would not permit the planned public rally.
- F010: The full text of the prohibition letter: "We inform you that the Plovdiv Municipality does not permit the conducting of the [planned] public rally."
- F013: The applicant organisation did not hold a rally on 19 April 2000.
- F011: Police officers visited the offices of the applicant organisation on 19 April 2000.
- F012: Police officers obtained signed declarations from the leaders of the applicant organisation that they would not organise the rally.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court states: "The Plovdiv Regional Court established in its judgment of 28 March 2001 that the municipality's prohibition of the applicant organisation's public rally of 19 April 2000 was issued in violation of the Meetings and Marches Act." And in the admissibility section: "the Plovdiv Regional Court declared null and void the municipality's prohibition on a technicality stemming from the fact that it had been decided not by its mayor but by its secretary."
- F026: The Plovdiv Regional Court declared null and void the prohibition.
- F027: The prohibition had been issued in violation of the Meetings and Marches Act, as it had been decided not by the mayor but by the secretary.
- F028: The prohibition lacked reference to any of the statutory grounds for issuing such prohibitions.
- F009: The prohibition letter was signed by the secretary of the municipality.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. No atomic facts map to this element.

**Necessity**
Not discussed. No atomic facts map to this element.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court implies scope is satisfied. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that a planned public assembly existed and that the applicant sought to participate:
- F005: The applicant organisation notified the municipality of its intention to hold a public rally on 19 April 2000 — this establishes both the existence of a planned assembly and the applicant's participation.
- F006: The aim was to protest — this establishes the expressive/political character of the assembly, confirming it falls within Article 11 scope.

**Interference**
The strictly necessary fact is the act of prohibition that prevented the rally from taking place:
- F008: The municipality informed the applicant it would not permit the rally — this is the core governmental act constituting the interference.
- F013: The applicant organisation did not hold the rally — this confirms the interference had its intended effect.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's finding rests entirely on the domestic court's determination that the prohibition violated the Meetings and Marches Act. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F027: The prohibition was issued in violation of the Meetings and Marches Act because it was decided by the secretary rather than the mayor — this is the specific legal defect the Court adopted.
- F009: The prohibition letter was signed by the secretary — this is the underlying factual basis for F027.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. Empty.

**Necessity**
Not discussed. Empty.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on a single chain: a planned public rally was prohibited by a municipal act that was not prescribed by law, and that prohibition was enforced, preventing the rally from taking place. The irreducible facts are:
- F005: The applicant organisation notified the municipality of its intention to hold a public rally on 19 April 2000 — establishes the assembly and the applicant's attempt to exercise Article 11 rights.
- F008: The municipality prohibited the rally — establishes the governmental interference.
- F013: The applicant organisation did not hold the rally — confirms the interference was effective.
- F027: The prohibition was issued in violation of the Meetings and Marches Act because it was decided by the secretary rather than the mayor — establishes that the interference was not prescribed by law, which is the ground on which the violation was found.
- F009: The prohibition letter was signed by the secretary — the underlying factual basis for the legal defect in F027.
