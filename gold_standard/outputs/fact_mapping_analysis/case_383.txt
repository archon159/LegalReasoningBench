LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (assembly exists / participation)
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies."
This maps to F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This is essentially the same sentence. F001 is also relevant in that it establishes who the applicants are, but it does not itself establish the assembly or participation facts. F002 is the direct match.

(a) Scope (peaceful)
No factual grounding was identified. No atomic facts correspond.

(b) Interference
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This is part of the same sentence as F002, which refers to "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The specific details (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are referenced in the reasoning section itself but are not separately atomised in the facts provided beyond F002. F002 is the closest match, as it captures the general complaint about measures taken. No other atomic fact corresponds more precisely.

(e) Necessity
The factual grounding quote was: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" and the reference to "issues similar to those in the present case" from the leading cases. The Court does not ground its necessity finding in any specific atomic fact from the case. The only atomic fact that could be said to underlie this is F002, which describes the nature of the complaint (measures against organisers/participants of assemblies), since this is what the Court compared to the leading cases. F001 is also implicitly relevant as it identifies the applicants and their applications, which is the material the Court examined. However, no atomic fact directly grounds the necessity conclusion in a case-specific way.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual basis for implying that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated. Without F002, the Court could not even begin its Article 11 analysis.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that measures were taken against the applicants in connection with public assemblies. The specific measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are described in the reasoning section as elaborations of the complaint described in F002.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts are necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts are necessary.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that can be said to underlie the necessity assessment, as it describes the nature of the situation the Court compared to its leading cases. However, the Court's necessity finding is grounded almost entirely in case-law rather than case-specific facts, so the factual support is thin.

Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core for this conclusion is:

F002: This is the single atomic fact that is essential. It establishes (i) that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (scope), and (ii) that measures were taken against them in that context (interference). Without F002, there is no factual basis for any element of the Article 11 analysis. F001 identifies the applicants but does not itself establish any substantive fact about the assemblies or the measures taken. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. The Court's necessity finding is grounded in case-law rather than additional case-specific atomic facts, so no further atomic fact is strictly necessary beyond F002.
