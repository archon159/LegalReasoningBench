LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's reasoning on scope (implied) rests on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' roles as organisers or participants. The most critical atomic facts are F002 and F003, which together establish both the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation. Since F002 and F003 establish the same point from two angles (organisers vs. participants), both are necessary because the applicant group includes both categories.

Interference: The Court's explicit finding of interference rests on the same facts — the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. The most critical atomic facts are F002 and F003, as these are the only facts describing the relationship between the applicants and the measures taken.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts.

Necessity: The Court's necessity finding is grounded in its case-law references rather than independently stated facts. However, the necessity analysis presupposes the existence of an interference with freedom of assembly, which is grounded in F002 and F003. No additional atomic facts beyond those already listed under interference are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must establish at minimum: (i) that there were assemblies (scope), (ii) that the applicants participated in them (scope/participation), and (iii) that governmental measures interfered with their freedom of assembly (interference). The necessity finding is then made by reference to prior case law rather than additional facts.

The irreducible factual core is therefore F002 and F003. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies who faced disproportionate measures; F003 establishes the same for participants. Together, these two facts are the only atomic facts that ground the entire chain of reasoning — scope, participation, and interference — that leads to the violation conclusion. F001 is a formal reference to the appended table and does not itself establish any substantive fact necessary for the legal conclusion. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
