LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly, that the applicant participated, and (implicitly) that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F007: applicant notified Town Hall of intention to organise a static demonstration — establishes that an assembly was planned and the applicant was its organiser/participant
- F013: demonstration was held on 18 October 2007 — establishes that the assembly actually took place
- F014: demonstration held on pavement close to building of Cabinet of Ministers opposite Constitutional Court — establishes the location and nature of the assembly
- F012: applicant notified authorities he disagreed with the proposal to change location and proceeded — establishes participation

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court identifies two acts of interference: the refusal to approve the location, and the arrest and conviction. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F008: Town Hall refused to approve the demonstration at the proposed location — first act of interference
- F015: applicant was forcibly arrested — second act of interference (arrest)
- F026: applicant convicted under Article 20.2 § 2 — second act of interference (conviction)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court acknowledges a legal basis in the statutory ban. The strictly necessary fact is:
- F027: conviction on grounds that applicant held demonstration opposite Constitutional Court, which was prohibited by law — establishes the statutory basis for the conviction
- F055: summary of domestic law in Lashmankin — provides the legal framework context

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary; element not discussed by the Court.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on the fact that the conviction relied solely on a general statutory ban without consideration of specific circumstances, and that this ban had already been found incompatible with Article 11 § 2 in Lashmankin. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F027: conviction on grounds of statutory ban on holding events in vicinity of court buildings — the operative fact showing reliance solely on the ban
- F026: conviction under Article 20.2 § 2 — the legal act whose necessity is assessed
- F055: domestic law as summarised in Lashmankin — provides the basis for the Court's finding that the ban is too broadly drawn

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary; element not discussed.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: (1) the applicant organised and held a peaceful static demonstration at a location of his choosing after the authorities refused to approve that location; (2) he was convicted solely on the basis of a statutory ban on demonstrations near court buildings, without any consideration of the specific circumstances; and (3) that statutory ban had already been found by the Court in Lashmankin to be too broadly drawn to be compatible with Article 11 § 2. The irreducible facts are:

- F007: applicant notified Town Hall of intention to organise a static demonstration — establishes the assembly and the applicant's role
- F008: Town Hall refused to approve the demonstration at the proposed location — establishes the initial interference
- F013/F014: demonstration was held at the location opposite the Constitutional Court — establishes that the assembly took place at the contested location (F014 is the more specific and critical fact)
- F026: applicant convicted under Article 20.2 § 2 — establishes the conviction as the central act of interference
- F027: conviction on grounds that applicant held demonstration opposite Constitutional Court, which was prohibited by law — establishes that the conviction relied solely on the statutory ban, which is the pivot of the necessity analysis
- F055: domestic law as summarised in Lashmankin — establishes the legal framework and the prior finding that the ban is incompatible with Article 11 § 2
