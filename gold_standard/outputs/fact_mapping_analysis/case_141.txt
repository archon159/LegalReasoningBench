LIST 1: Factual grounding quotes mapped to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---
Quote: 'the applicant and others clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia.'
Atomic facts: F012 (applicant joined demonstration in Ayios Kassianos area on 19 July 1989). The existence of an assembly and the applicant's participation are both grounded in F012. The broader demonstration involving others is also supported by F013 and F014 (other applicants also took part).

--- INTERFERENCE ---
Quote: 'The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested.'
Atomic facts: F084 (applicant was arrested by Turkish-Cypriot police after crossing the UN buffer zone and entering the area under Turkish-Cypriot control — this is a Government allegation but the Court proceeds on the basis that arrest occurred); F024 (applicant was taken at gunpoint together with others who had also been arrested); F039 (at around 2.00 p.m. on 20 July 1989, the applicant was given back his personal effects and taken to court together with seven other prisoners — confirming detention); F063 (applicant was sentenced to three days' imprisonment — confirming the arrest and prosecution sequence). The dispersal of the demonstration is supported by F098 (Turkish-Cypriot police and security forces apprehended 111 persons).

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
Quote: 'This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 28-29 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law (see paragraph 32 above).'
Atomic facts: F125 (section 70 defines unlawful assembly), F126 (section 70 on originally lawful assembly becoming unlawful), F127 (section 70 on riot), F128 (section 71 — participation in unlawful assembly is a misdemeanour), F131 (section 14(1)(b) — police officer may arrest without warrant any person committing an offence punishable with imprisonment), F132 (section 14(1)(c) — police officer may arrest without warrant any person obstructing a police officer).

Quote (on TRNC legal basis principle): 'when, as in the Foka case, an act of the TRNC authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law for the purposes of the Convention.'
Atomic facts: F133 (section 9 of Law No. 5/72 on unauthorized entry into prohibited military area), F134 (subsection 12(1) of Aliens and Immigration Law on approved ports of entry), F135 (subsection 12(5) on penalties for contravention), F112 (applicant charged with entering TRNC territory without permission contrary to sections 2, 8 and 9 of Law no. 5/72), F113 (applicant charged with entering TRNC territory other than through an approved port contrary to subsections 12(1) and (5) of the Aliens and Immigration Law).

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
Quote: 'interferences with the right guaranteed by Article 11 of the Convention are in principle justified for the prevention of disorder or crime and for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others where, as in the instant case, demonstrators engage in acts of violence.'
The phrase 'as in the instant case' links this to the factual findings on violence. The relevant atomic facts are those establishing the violent character of the demonstration:
F095 (demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone), F096 (demonstrators broke through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post), F097 (demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers and entered a former school complex), F117 (witnesses declared that the accused had illegally entered TRNC territory, shouted abuse and resisted arrest by pulling and pushing), F118 (knives and other cutting objects found in bags of some demonstrators), F122 (TRNC District Court concluded accused had crossed TRNC borders and resisted UN and Turkish forces).

--- NECESSITY ---
Quote 1: 'in the case of Chrysostomos and Papachrysostomou, the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus. The Court sees no reason to depart from these findings.'
Atomic facts: F095, F096, F097 (UN Secretary General's report findings on demonstrators forcing through UN buffer zone, breaking wire barrier, destroying observation post, breaking through UNFICYP line — these are the evidentiary basis the Commission relied on).

Quote 2: 'in his report, the UN Secretary General stated that the demonstrators had forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia, that they had broken through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post before breaking through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers and entering a former school complex.'
Atomic facts: F095, F096, F097.

Quote 3: 'the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the statements given by some witnesses at trial according to which the area where the accused had entered was TRNC territory.'
Atomic facts: F117 (witnesses — mainly police officers — declared that the accused had illegally entered TRNC territory), F122 (TRNC District Court concluded accused had crossed TRNC borders), F099 (UN Secretary General noted the Ayios Kassianos school complex is situated in the UN buffer zone but Turkish forces claim it to be on their side of the cease-fire line).

Quote 4: 'the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the TRNC borders by some of the demonstrators.'
Atomic facts: F095, F096, F097 (violent conduct), F084 (applicant arrested after crossing UN buffer zone and entering area under Turkish-Cypriot control), F098 (Turkish-Cypriot police apprehended 111 persons), F112, F113 (charges of illegal entry).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Quote: 'the authorities have a duty to take appropriate measures with regard to demonstrations in order to ensure their peaceful conduct and the safety of all citizens. However, they cannot guarantee this absolutely and they have a wide discretion in the choice of the means to be used.'
This is stated as a general legal principle only. No specific atomic facts are identified by the Court in relation to this obligation. The list is empty.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly and that the applicant participated. The single most critical fact is:
F012 (applicant joined the demonstration in Ayios Kassianos on 19 July 1989).
F095 also supports the existence of the assembly (approximately 1,000 Greek Cypriot demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone), but F012 is the primary fact for the applicant's participation.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needs to establish that the demonstration was dispersed and the applicant was arrested. The most critical facts are:
F024 (applicant was taken at gunpoint together with others who had been arrested — establishes arrest);
F098 (Turkish-Cypriot police and security forces apprehended 111 persons — establishes dispersal).

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needs to establish that the interference had a legal basis. The most critical facts are:
F125/F128 (sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code — definition of unlawful assembly and criminal liability);
F131 (section 14(1)(b) of the Criminal Procedure Law — power of arrest without warrant);
F112 (applicant charged under TRNC law for illegal entry — establishes the TRNC legal basis);
F133/F134/F135 (the TRNC laws under which the applicant was charged — establishes the domestic legal basis in northern Cyprus).
The most representative and strictly necessary are F128 (criminal liability for unlawful assembly), F131 (arrest power), F112 (charge under TRNC law), F134 (Aliens and Immigration Law provision).

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court implicitly accepts the legitimate aims on the basis of the violent character of the demonstration. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing violence and border violation:
F095 (demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone),
F096 (broke through UNFICYP wire barrier and destroyed observation post),
F097 (broke through UNFICYP line and entered school complex).

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's proportionality assessment turns on: (1) the violent character of the demonstration; (2) the violation of TRNC borders; (3) the wide margin of appreciation. The strictly necessary facts are:
F095 (demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone — core finding on violence),
F096 (broke through UNFICYP barrier and destroyed observation post — core finding on violence),
F097 (broke through UNFICYP line — core finding on violence),
F117 (witnesses declared accused illegally entered TRNC territory and resisted arrest — supports border violation finding),
F099 (Ayios Kassianos school complex claimed by Turkish forces to be on their side — supports TRNC border violation finding).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary here as the Court invokes this only as a general legal principle without applying it to specific facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment, which itself turned on the violent character of the demonstration and the violation of the TRNC borders. The Court found no violation because the interference was justified by the demonstrators' violent conduct and border violation. The irreducible facts are:

F012: The applicant joined the demonstration — establishes his participation and brings him within Article 11 scope (without this, there is no Article 11 claim at all).
F024: The applicant was taken at gunpoint and arrested — establishes the interference with his Article 11 rights.
F095: Demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone — the primary factual basis for the finding of violent character and the core of the proportionality assessment.
F096: Demonstrators broke through UNFICYP wire barrier and destroyed an observation post — reinforces the violent character finding.
F097: Demonstrators broke through the UNFICYP line and entered the school complex — reinforces the violent character and border violation findings.
F117: Witnesses declared the accused had illegally entered TRNC territory and resisted arrest — supports the border violation finding that justified the intervention.
F128: Section 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (participation in unlawful assembly is a criminal offence) — establishes the legal basis (prescribed by law) without which the interference could not be justified.
F131: Section 14(1)(b) of the Criminal Procedure Law (arrest power) — establishes the legal basis for the arrest.

These facts are the minimum necessary: F012 for scope/participation, F024 for interference, F128 and F131 for prescribed by law, and F095, F096, F097, F117 for the necessity finding that determined the outcome.
