LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation):
The reasoning text states "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies" — this maps directly to F002, which reads "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." F001 is also relevant insofar as it identifies who the applicants are. No other atomic fact corresponds to the peaceful character sub-element, as noted previously.

Interference:
The reasoning text identifies "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" as the acts of interference. This maps to F002, which is the only atomic fact describing the applicants' situation in relation to the assemblies. There is no separate atomic fact enumerating the arrest, dispersal, or conviction specifically — these details appear in the reasoning section itself rather than in the atomic facts provided.

Necessity:
The Court states "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is a general reference to the totality of the case material. The only atomic fact that could correspond to this is F002, which describes the core complaint. F001 is also implicitly referenced insofar as the "material submitted" includes the details of each application set out in the appended table.

No atomic facts correspond to prescribed_by_law, legitimate_aim, or positive_obligation, as these elements were found not_discussed.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element of law

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the minimum factual predicate for the scope of Article 11 to be engaged. F001 is background identification only and not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it is the only atomic fact that grounds the existence of governmental measures against the applicants in the context of public assemblies. The specific acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are described in the reasoning text itself but are not separately atomized; F002 is the closest available anchor.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element was not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element was not discussed.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be said to ground the necessity assessment, as it describes the core situation the Court evaluated. However, the Court's necessity finding is almost entirely driven by reference to prior case-law rather than case-specific facts, so even F002's role here is minimal.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual predicate for this conclusion is: (1) that there were public assemblies (scope), and (2) that the government took measures against the applicants as organisers or participants of those assemblies (interference). Both of these are captured in F002. F001 is background only. No other atomic fact is strictly necessary — the necessity conclusion itself is driven by analogy to prior case-law, not by additional case-specific facts in the atomic list. Therefore, the irreducible core is F002 alone.
