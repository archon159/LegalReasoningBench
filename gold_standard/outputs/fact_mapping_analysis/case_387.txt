LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies."
- F002 corresponds to the reference to applicants as organisers of public assemblies.
- F003 corresponds to the reference to applicants as participants of public assemblies.
These two facts together cover the assembly existence, participation, and (implicitly) the peaceful nature sub-elements, as they are the only facts the Court pointed to for scope.

(b) Interference
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
- F002 and F003 again are the relevant atomic facts, as they describe the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) taken against organisers and participants respectively.
Note: F001 references the appended table with details of applications, which would contain the specific facts about arrests and convictions, but since F001 is purely a reference to an appendix and not itself a substantive fact, it is not the primary grounding. The direct grounding remains F002 and F003.

(c) Prescribed by law
Not discussed; no factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed; no factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(e) Necessity
The factual grounding quote was: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion."
This is a purely comparative/procedural statement with no independent factual grounding from the present case. The Court borrowed its necessity finding from prior cases. However, the underlying facts that made the necessity analysis applicable at all are the same measures described in F002 and F003 (the disproportionate measures against organisers and participants). No additional atomic facts beyond F002 and F003 are identifiable.

(f) Positive obligation

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court's implicit finding that assemblies existed, that applicants participated, and that the assemblies were within the scope of Article 11 rests entirely on the characterisation of the applicants as organisers and participants of public assemblies. Both facts are needed because the applicant pool includes both organisers (F002) and participants (F003).

(b) Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court's finding of interference rests on the measures described — arrest, dispersal, and administrative conviction — which are the subject matter of F002 and F003. Without these facts, there is no interference to analyze.

(c) Prescribed by law: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

(d) Legitimate aim: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

(e) Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The necessity finding — that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society — is grounded in the same measures described in F002 and F003. The Court's conclusion that nothing distinguishes this case from Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, and Kasparov presupposes that the same type of measures (arrests, dispersals, convictions of assembly organisers and participants) are present here.

(f) Positive obligation: No atomic facts. Not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the fact that the applicants — as organisers and participants of public assemblies — were subjected to disproportionate state measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction). This is what triggered the Article 11 analysis and what the Court found to constitute an unjustified interference not necessary in a democratic society.

The irreducible core is:
- F002: establishes that organisers of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the essential fact linking state action to assembly organisers.
- F003: establishes that participants of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this is the essential fact linking state action to assembly participants.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 is a purely administrative reference to an appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions, which are irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
