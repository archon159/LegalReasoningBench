LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied)
The factual grounding quote was: "the applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: applicants as organisers of nationwide protests against the Russian military attack on Ukraine
- F003: applicants as participants of nationwide protests against the Russian military attack on Ukraine
These two facts together establish that public assemblies took place and that the applicants were organisers or participants. The phrase "nationwide protests" corresponds to "public assemblies" in the reasoning text. No atomic fact explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assemblies.

(b) Interference (explicit)
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: organisers subjected to disproportionate measures
- F003: participants subjected to disproportionate measures
The atomic facts refer to "disproportionate measures" which in the reasoning text are specified as arrest and conviction for administrative offences. F002 and F003 are the closest available atomic facts, though they do not specify the nature of the measures in the same detail as the reasoning text does.

(c) Prescribed by law
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)
The Court's necessity finding rests on reference to prior case law and the absence of any distinguishing fact or argument, not on independently stated facts. The only factual anchor in the reasoning text is the general characterisation of the situation, which maps to:
- F002: organisers of protests subjected to disproportionate measures
- F003: participants of protests subjected to disproportionate measures
The Court's statement that it "examined all the material submitted" does not point to any specific atomic fact beyond what is already captured in F002 and F003.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope (implied)
To establish that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated in it, the strictly necessary facts are:
- F002 (organisers of protests)
- F003 (participants of protests)
Both are needed because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants, and the Court's reasoning covers both categories.

(b) Interference (explicit)
To establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' freedom of assembly, the strictly necessary fact is:
- F002 and F003: both describe applicants as subjected to "disproportionate measures," which the reasoning text specifies as arrest and conviction. Both are necessary as the applicant group spans both roles.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the fact that the applicants — as organisers and participants of public assemblies (protests against the Russian military attack on Ukraine) — were subjected to disproportionate measures (arrest and administrative conviction). This is what placed the case within the pattern of the leading cases and grounded the necessity finding. The strictly necessary atomic facts are:
- F002: organisers subjected to disproportionate measures in the context of nationwide protests
- F003: participants subjected to disproportionate measures in the context of nationwide protests

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions, neither of which bears on the Article 11 conclusion. F002 and F003 are the irreducible core because they establish both the assembly context and the interference, which are the two factual pillars on which the violation finding rests.
