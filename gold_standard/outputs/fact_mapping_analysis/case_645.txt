LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope — assembly exists / participation:
The reasoning text quotes: "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
The atomic fact that corresponds most directly is F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This is almost verbatim the language used in the reasoning section. F001 is also referenced implicitly (the list of applicants), but adds no substantive factual content beyond identifying who the applicants are.
Mapping: F002 (primary), F001 (secondary, identifying the applicants).

(b) Scope — peaceful character:
No factual grounding was found in the reasoning text. No atomic fact corresponds to this sub-element.
Mapping: none.

(c) Interference:
The reasoning text quotes: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to F002, which describes the measures complained of. No other atomic fact addresses the nature of the governmental acts.
Mapping: F002.

(d) Necessity:
The reasoning text states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" and refers to leading cases involving "issues similar to those in the present case."
The only atomic fact that could ground this is F002, which describes the nature of the complaints (disproportionate measures against assembly organisers/participants). F001 identifies the applicants and applications. No atomic fact provides specific circumstantial detail about the individual cases that the Court weighed in its necessity assessment.
Mapping: F002, F001.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on: (i) the applicants being organisers or participants of public assemblies (bringing them within the scope of Article 11); (ii) the government having taken measures against them — arrest, dispersal, and conviction — constituting an interference; and (iii) those measures being found not necessary in a democratic society by analogy with prior Russian assembly cases.

All three of these steps are grounded in a single atomic fact: F002. This is the only atomic fact that provides substantive content — it identifies the applicants as assembly organisers/participants and describes the measures taken against them (disproportionate measures). Without F002, the Court cannot establish scope, interference, or the factual basis for its necessity comparison. F001 merely identifies the applicants and is not independently necessary for the legal conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Irreducible core: F002.
