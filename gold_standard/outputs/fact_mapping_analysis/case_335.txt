LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (implied)**

The Court's grounding quote: 'the intervention of the police officers in the demonstration' and 'a demonstration in a public place may cause some disruption to ordinary life' and 'the applicant and many other demonstrators did not act violently' and 'the demonstrators neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence.'

- F007: The applicant took part in a demonstration to mark Women's Day celebrations in Beyazıt Square on 6 March 2005. (establishes assembly exists and applicant participated)
- F011: The Women's Day celebrations ended with a press release issued by women's organisations. (establishes assembly exists)
- F041: Video footage shows police officers hitting demonstrators who try to run away. (implies peaceful nature — demonstrators fleeing, not attacking)
- F049: Expert report: police had not issued warnings before attacking. (supports peaceful nature)
- F050: Expert report: demonstrators had not resisted police intervention. (supports peaceful nature)
- F051: Expert report: demonstrators had not attacked the police but simply tried to run away. (directly supports peaceful nature)
- F072: Istanbul prosecutor decided not to bring criminal proceedings against forty-four police officers — implies no criminal conduct by demonstrators.
- F035: The applicant was not arrested. (supports she was not violent)

**Interference**

The Court's grounding quote: 'The Court considers that the intervention of the police officers, as well as the ill-treatment to which the applicant was subjected, constituted an interference with her rights under Article 11.'

- F012: Police officers started hitting people including the applicant with truncheons and weapon ends when crowds began to disperse.
- F013: Police officers sprayed people including the applicant with tear gas.
- F014: The applicant fell to the ground as a result of blows to her head, face and other parts of her body.
- F015: Whilst on the ground, police officers continued to kick and hit the applicant with sticks.
- F017: The applicant was seriously injured.
- F018: The applicant was left semi-conscious.

**Prescribed by law**

The Court's grounding quote: 'the interference in the present case had a legal basis, namely the Law on Meetings and Demonstration Marches (see paragraphs 29-31 above).'

- F122: Section 10 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act (Law No. 2911) — prior notice requirement.
- F123: Section 22 of the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act — prohibition on demonstrations on public streets.
- F124: Section 22 — demonstrations in public squares must comply with security instructions.
- F125: Section 24 — demonstrations not complying with the Act will be dispersed by force after warnings.
- F022: The request to hold the Women's Day celebrations in Beyazıt Square was rejected. (establishes the demonstration was not in a permitted location, grounding the legal basis for intervention)

**Legitimate aim**

The Court's grounding quote: 'the Government submitted that the interference pursued, among others, the legitimate aim of prevention of disorder and crime, and the Court finds no reason to differ.'

- F022: The request to hold the demonstration in Beyazıt Square was rejected. (supports disorder/crime prevention rationale)
- F029: Demonstrators blocked traffic and insisted on marching to Beyazıt Square. (supports disorder rationale as accepted by Government)
- F030: Security forces intervened and reopened the blocked road. (supports public order rationale)

**Necessity**

The Court's grounding quotes:

(1) 'having regard to the absence of any criminal proceedings against the applicant, as well as the lack of any information to show that proceedings were initiated against any other demonstrator for an alleged breach of that Law, the Court observes that the applicant and many other demonstrators did not act violently'
- F035: The applicant was not arrested.
- F072: Istanbul prosecutor decided not to bring criminal proceedings against forty-four police officers — and by implication no proceedings against demonstrators for breach of the law.
- F073: Thirty-nine persons including the applicant named as victims and complainants (not as defendants).

(2) 'the video footage (see paragraph 14 above), the report of the expert on that footage (see paragraph 15 above), statements taken from the six police officers who accepted having used unnecessary and excessive force against the demonstrators (see paragraph 20 above), and the conclusion reached by the trial court (see paragraphs 24-25 above) show that the demonstrators neither presented a danger to public order nor engaged in acts of violence requiring such a heavy-handed intervention'
- F041: Video footage shows police hitting demonstrators trying to run away.
- F042: Video footage shows demonstrators on the ground being kicked and hit.
- F043: Video footage shows a male demonstrator protecting a woman on the ground being kicked in the face.
- F044: Video footage shows police spraying demonstrators hiding in bushes before hitting them.
- F045: Video footage shows two women on the ground trying to protect themselves being sprayed with tear gas.
- F046: Video footage shows women dragged from shops and cafes and beaten.
- F049: Expert report: no warnings issued before attacking.
- F050: Expert report: demonstrators had not resisted.
- F051: Expert report: demonstrators had not attacked police but tried to run away.
- F052: Expert report: police hit demonstrators including those on the ground, kicked, punched and sprayed them.
- F091: Six defendant police officers accepted during preliminary investigation that they had kicked demonstrators from behind.
- F112: Trial court found some defendant police officers exceeded limits of their powers and used excessive force.
- F113: Trial court concluded five officers had used excessive force and committed the offence of ill-treatment.
- F104: Trial court found video footage shows police intervention against people who had already surrendered or were hiding in bushes.
- F106: Trial court found video footage shows police kicking and hitting persons — mostly women — trying to run away and not carrying banners.

(3) 'the brutality of the dispersal had an inevitable dissuasive effect on people's willingness to demonstrate'
- F041 through F046 (video footage facts) collectively establish the brutality.
- F017: The applicant was seriously injured.
- F020: The incident was widely publicised in national and international media.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed by the Court in its own analysis. No atomic facts to map.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
- F007: Establishes that an assembly took place and that the applicant participated in it — the irreducible minimum for scope.
- F051: Expert report finding that demonstrators did not attack police but tried to run away — the most direct factual basis for the implied finding that the assembly was peaceful.

**Interference**
- F012: Police hit the applicant with truncheons — the core act of interference with her person during the assembly.
- F013: Police sprayed the applicant with tear gas — the second core act of interference.
- F017: The applicant was seriously injured — establishes the severity of the interference.

**Prescribed by law**
- F122: Section 10 of Law No. 2911 — the prior notice requirement that the demonstration allegedly breached, forming the legal basis the Court accepted.
- F125: Section 24 of Law No. 2911 — the provision authorising dispersal by force, which is the direct legal basis for the police intervention.

**Legitimate aim**
- F022: The request to hold the demonstration in Beyazıt Square was rejected — establishes that the demonstration was unauthorised, which is the factual predicate for the Government's disorder/crime prevention rationale that the Court accepted.

**Necessity**
- F051: Expert report: demonstrators did not attack police but tried to run away — most direct factual basis for finding demonstrators were not violent.
- F091: Six police officers accepted during preliminary investigation that they had kicked demonstrators from behind — the most direct admission of excessive force.
- F112: Trial court found that some officers exceeded limits of their powers and used excessive force — the authoritative domestic judicial finding underpinning the Court's necessity conclusion.
- F035: The applicant was not arrested — supports the finding that no criminal proceedings were brought against her, evidencing non-violent conduct.
- F041: Video footage shows police hitting demonstrators trying to run away — the most representative video evidence of disproportionate force.
- F104: Trial court found video shows police intervention against people who had already surrendered or were hiding — directly supports the finding of disproportionate response.

**Positive obligation**
- [] (not discussed)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two pivotal questions: (1) was the assembly peaceful (so as to attract Article 11 protection and make the force disproportionate), and (2) was the police response disproportionate/unnecessary? The following facts are strictly necessary:

- F007: The applicant participated in a demonstration — without this, there is no assembly and no Article 11 claim.
- F051: Demonstrators did not attack police but tried to run away — the critical factual basis for finding the assembly was peaceful and the intervention unjustified.
- F091: Six police officers admitted kicking demonstrators from behind — the most direct evidence of excessive force, accepted by domestic authorities.
- F112: Trial court found officers exceeded limits of their powers and used excessive force — the authoritative domestic judicial finding that the Court relied on to conclude the intervention was disproportionate.
- F035: The applicant was not arrested and no criminal proceedings were brought against her — supports the finding that she was not violent, underpinning the necessity analysis.
- F041: Video footage shows police hitting demonstrators trying to run away — the most representative piece of evidence establishing the disproportionate nature of the response.
- F122: Section 10 of Law No. 2911 — the legal basis accepted by the Court for the 'prescribed by law' element, without which the interference could not even be assessed under paragraph 2 of Article 11.
- F022: The demonstration was held in a location where it had been refused — the factual predicate for the legitimate aim finding, which the Court accepted before going on to find the necessity test was not met.
