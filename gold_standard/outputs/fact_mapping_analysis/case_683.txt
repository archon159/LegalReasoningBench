LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
The factual grounding quote was: "organisers or participants of public assemblies." This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — covers the "organisers" part.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — covers the "participants" part.
Both F002 and F003 together correspond exactly to the Court's phrase "organisers or participants of public assemblies." The existence of public assemblies is embedded in both facts. Participation is likewise embedded. Peaceful character has no factual grounding in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact corresponds to it either.

(b) Interference
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." Looking at the atomic facts, none of F001–F004 explicitly mentions arrest, dispersal, or conviction for administrative offences. F002 and F003 refer to "disproportionate measures" in general terms, which is the closest available mapping, but neither specifies arrest, dispersal, or conviction. F001 notes that details are in the appended table, which may contain this information, but the table is not reproduced here. No atomic fact precisely corresponds to the specific factual grounding quote for interference.

(c) Prescribed by law
Not discussed. No factual grounding. No atomic facts map to this element.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed. No factual grounding. No atomic facts map to this element.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society
The factual grounding was the reference to prior case-law and the absence of distinguishing facts. No atomic fact from F001–F004 corresponds to the prior case-law references. F002 and F003 are the closest, as they describe the complaints that the Court compared to its leading cases. F004 ("other complaints") is not relevant to the necessity analysis under Article 11.

(f) Positive obligation
Not discussed. No factual grounding. No atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: The Court's implied finding that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated as organisers or participants rests critically on F002 and F003. Both are necessary because the Court's own language mirrors both roles. F001 is background and not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself.

(b) Interference: The Court's explicit finding of interference (arrest, dispersal, conviction) is not directly supported by any of the four atomic facts with precision. F002 and F003 refer to "disproportionate measures" which is the closest available grounding, but this is the applicants' characterisation of the measures, not a neutral factual description. No atomic fact is strictly sufficient. However, F002 and F003 are the only available candidates and are included as the best available mapping.

(c) Prescribed by law: No atomic facts. Empty.

(d) Legitimate aim: No atomic facts. Empty.

(e) Necessity: The necessity conclusion rests on the comparison with prior cases involving "issues similar to those in the present case." The present case's issues are defined by F002 and F003 — the complaints of disproportionate measures against organisers and participants of public assemblies. These are the facts that anchor the similarity comparison. F002 and F003 are therefore the strictly necessary atomic facts.

(f) Positive obligation: No atomic facts. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on: (1) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants (scope, implied), and (2) the fact that disproportionate measures were taken against them (interference, necessity). Both F002 and F003 are essential: F002 establishes the organiser dimension and F003 the participant dimension, together grounding both the scope finding and the interference/necessity conclusion. Without both, the Court could not have reached its conclusion across the full range of applicants. F001 and F004 are not essential to the Article 11 conclusion itself.
