LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied)
The reasoning text states "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The atomic facts that correspond to these quotes are:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies" — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote and the existence of public assemblies.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies" — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote and the existence of public assemblies.
Note: There is no atomic fact that establishes the peaceful character of the assemblies, confirming the earlier observation that this sub-element has no factual grounding in the provided material.

(b) Interference (explicit)
The reasoning text states "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The atomic facts that correspond are:
- F002: covers organisers subjected to disproportionate measures (which the Court identifies as arrest and conviction).
- F003: covers participants subjected to disproportionate measures.
No atomic fact specifically describes the arrest, dispersal, or conviction in isolation — these details appear only in the reasoning section itself, not in the atomic facts as provided. F002 and F003 are the closest available correspondences.

(c) Prescribed by law
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(d) Legitimate aim
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

(e) Necessity in a democratic society (explicit)
The reasoning text states "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" and refers to "issues similar to those in the present case." The atomic facts that correspond are:

- F003: participants subjected to disproportionate measures — same reasoning.
No atomic fact provides specific factual detail about the proportionality assessment (e.g., conduct of authorities, severity of sentences). The necessity conclusion rests on precedent, and the only atomic facts available that touch on the substance are F002 and F003.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: The Court's implied finding that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated requires:
- F002 (organisers of public assemblies — establishes assembly existence and participation as organiser)
- F003 (participants of public assemblies — establishes participation as participant)
Both are necessary because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants, and the Court addresses both categories.

(b) Interference: The Court's explicit finding that there was an interference with freedom of assembly is grounded in the existence of state measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) against organisers and participants. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F002 (organisers subjected to measures)
- F003 (participants subjected to measures)
These are the same facts as for scope, since the interference finding is inseparable from the identification of who was affected and in what capacity.

(c) Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(e) Necessity: The Court's necessity finding — that the interferences were not necessary in a democratic society — is grounded in the characterisation of the measures as disproportionate. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F002 (organisers subjected to disproportionate measures — the word "disproportionate" is the factual hook for the necessity analysis)
- F003 (participants subjected to disproportionate measures — same)

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on: (i) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants (scope/interference), and (ii) the disproportionate character of the state measures taken against them (necessity). The atomic facts that are absolutely essential are:
- F002: establishes that organisers of public assemblies were subjected to disproportionate measures — this single fact captures both the assembly context and the proportionality failure for one category of applicants.
- F003: establishes the same for participants.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 is a procedural reference to an appended table and does not establish any substantive fact; F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. F002 and F003 together form the irreducible core.
