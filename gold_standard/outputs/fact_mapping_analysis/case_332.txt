LIST 1: Factual grounding mapped to atomic facts

**Scope (peaceful assembly)**
The Court's grounding: "It has not been argued by the Government that the organisers and the participants in the events planned by UMO Ilinden – PIRIN had violent intentions. The Court sees no reason to hold otherwise."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F020: The second applicant assured the mayor of Sofia that no disturbances would occur during the rally planned for 10 August 1998.
- F030: The first applicant assured the mayor of Sofia that the rally planned for 12 September 1998 would be entirely peaceful and that no disturbances would occur.
- F002: The applicants are members of UMO Ilinden – PIRIN (establishing who the organisers were).

**Interference**
First ban — Court's grounding: "The Government have not disputed that the first rally planned by UMO Ilinden – PIRIN was effectively banned by the authorities and that this ban constituted an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F021: On 3 August 1998, the mayor of Sofia issued an order prohibiting the rally planned for 10 August 1998.
- F024: A copy of the mayor of Sofia's order prohibiting the rally planned for 10 August 1998 was sent to the Sofia police authorities for enforcement.

Second ban — Court's grounding: "under the Constitution of 1971 the Executive Committee of the People's Council was the executive authority within the municipality... in 1991 it was superseded by the mayor, not by the Municipal Council"; "The failure of a nonexistent body to rule on the applicants' appeal was therefore only natural"; "it may hardly be concluded that the ban had in reality ceased to have effect by reason of the fact that the Municipal Council had not examined it."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F031: On 2 September 1998, the mayor of Sofia issued an order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998.
- F034: A copy of the mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 was sent to the Sofia police authorities for enforcement.
- F044: Since on 12 September 1998 the mayor of Sofia's ban on the rally had not been overturned, the applicants informed the members and followers of UMO Ilinden – PIRIN that the rally would not take place.
- F079: Under the Constitution of 1971, the executive local state organs were the Executive Committees of the district People's Councils.
- F081: The Constitution of Bulgaria of 1991 abolished the Executive Committees of the People's Councils.
- F082: The Constitution of Bulgaria of 1991 established the post of mayor, elected by direct universal suffrage, as the "organ of the executive power in the municipality" under Article 139(1).
- F040: The Sofia District Court held that the mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 had to be appealed first before the Executive Committee of the People's Council.
- F042: The Sofia District Court discontinued the judicial proceedings and sent the appeal to the Executive Committee of the People's Council.
- F075: Section 12(4) of the Meetings and Marches Act provides that the organiser may appeal to the Executive Committee of the People's Council against the prohibition.
- F076: Section 12(5) of the Meetings and Marches Act provides that where the Executive Committee of the People's Council has not decided within the twenty-four hour time-limit, the march, rally or meeting may proceed.
- F090: Section 21(1) of the Local Self-Government and Local Administration Act of 1991 makes no mention of a power of the Municipal Council to examine appeals against acts of the mayor.

**Prescribed by law**
Court's grounding: "the mayor... referred to an alleged threat to public order, which under domestic law could justify an interference with the right to peaceful assembly"; "the bans were imposed in accordance with the procedure laid down in the Meetings and Marches Act."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F022: The mayor of Sofia's order prohibiting the rally planned for 10 August 1998 was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act.
- F032: The mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act.
- F023: The mayor of Sofia opined, without giving further reasons, that the rally planned for 10 August 1998 would "create conditions for breaches of the public order".
- F033: The mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 stated that the event would "create conditions for breaches of the public order", without giving further reasons.
- F073: Section 12(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act provides that the mayor shall be competent to prohibit the holding of a meeting where it would endanger public order in the local community.
- F074: Section 12(3) of the Meetings and Marches Act provides that the prohibition shall be imposed by a written reasoned act not later than twenty-four hours after the notification.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court proceeds on an assumption only ("even assuming that the legitimate aims pursued were public safety and the prevention of disorder") and makes no positive factual finding. The factual grounding is the same as for prescribed by law — the bare references to public order in the bans:
- F023: The mayor of Sofia opined, without giving further reasons, that the rally planned for 10 August 1998 would "create conditions for breaches of the public order".
- F033: The mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 stated that the event would "create conditions for breaches of the public order", without giving further reasons.

**Necessity**
Court's grounding (insufficiency of reasons): "both mayoral bans only made brief references to an alleged threat to public order, without stating the basis for such a conclusion or going into further detail"; "in their observations the Government did not specify any particular reasons to justify the bans, but merely stated that the authorities had acted in conformity with national law and that their actions had not been arbitrary."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F023: The mayor of Sofia opined, without giving further reasons, that the rally planned for 10 August 1998 would "create conditions for breaches of the public order".
- F033: The mayor of Sofia's order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998 stated that the event would "create conditions for breaches of the public order", without giving further reasons.
- F074: Section 12(3) of the Meetings and Marches Act provides that the prohibition shall be imposed by a written reasoned act — relevant because the bans failed to meet even this standard substantively.

Court's grounding (separatist context): "Even if it was not unreasonable for the authorities to suspect that certain leaders of UMO Ilinden – PIRIN (which was later declared unconstitutional), or small groups which had developed from it, harboured separatist views and had a political agenda that included the notion of autonomy for the region of Pirin Macedonia or even secession from Bulgaria and could hence expect that separatist slogans would be broadcast by some participants during the planned rallies, such a probability could not per se justify their banning."
Atomic facts pointed to:
- F013: On 29 February 2000, UMO Ilinden – PIRIN was declared unconstitutional by the Bulgarian Constitutional Court.
- F003: UMO Ilinden – PIRIN apparently has close links with the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden.
- F004: The United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden is an association based in south-west Bulgaria, in the Pirin region.
- F006: During the period 1994–2003, the rallies organised by the United Macedonian Organisation Ilinden were, with minor exceptions, systematically banned by the Bulgarian authorities.

**Positive obligation**
The Court does not address positive obligations in its own assessment. No atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope (peaceful)**
The critical fact is the absence of any allegation of violent intent. The most representative atomic facts establishing the peaceful character of the planned assemblies are:
- F020: The second applicant assured the mayor that no disturbances would occur (first rally).
- F030: The first applicant assured the mayor that the rally would be entirely peaceful (second rally).

**Interference**
For the first ban, the strictly necessary fact is the issuance of the ban itself:
- F021: The mayor of Sofia issued an order prohibiting the rally planned for 10 August 1998.

For the second ban, the key facts are: the ban was issued; the body to which the appeal was referred no longer existed; and the ban therefore remained in effect:
- F031: The mayor of Sofia issued an order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998.
- F081: The Constitution of 1991 abolished the Executive Committees of the People's Councils.
- F082: The Constitution of 1991 established the mayor as the organ of executive power in the municipality (i.e., the Executive Committee was superseded by the mayor, not the Municipal Council).
- F044: The applicants informed members that the rally would not take place because the ban had not been overturned.
- F090: Section 21(1) of the Local Self-Government and Local Administration Act makes no mention of a power of the Municipal Council to examine appeals against acts of the mayor.

**Prescribed by law**
The strictly necessary facts are that the bans were issued under the relevant statutory provision and referred to the statutory ground:
- F022: The first ban was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act.
- F032: The second ban was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act.
- F073: Section 12(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act empowers the mayor to prohibit a rally that would endanger public order.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court assumes rather than finds a legitimate aim. The only factual basis for even the assumed aim is the bare reference to public order in the bans:
- F023: The first ban referred to "conditions for breaches of the public order" without further reasons.
- F033: The second ban referred to "conditions for breaches of the public order" without further reasons.

**Necessity**
The case turned on the inadequacy of reasons. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F023: The first ban made only a bare reference to public order without stating the basis.
- F033: The second ban made only a bare reference to public order without stating the basis.
- F013: UMO Ilinden – PIRIN was later declared unconstitutional (the Court acknowledged this as context for the authorities' suspicions but held it could not per se justify the bans).

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts — not discussed by the Court.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity test — specifically the failure of the authorities to give relevant and sufficient reasons for the bans. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary for the Court to reach its conclusion:

- F021: The mayor of Sofia issued an order prohibiting the rally planned for 10 August 1998. [establishes interference — first ban]
- F031: The mayor of Sofia issued an order banning the rally planned for 12 September 1998. [establishes interference — second ban]
- F023: The first ban referred only to "conditions for breaches of the public order" without giving further reasons. [establishes insufficiency of reasons for necessity]
- F033: The second ban referred only to "conditions for breaches of the public order" without giving further reasons. [establishes insufficiency of reasons for necessity]
- F022: The first ban was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act. [establishes prescribed by law]
- F032: The second ban was issued pursuant to section 12(2)(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act. [establishes prescribed by law]
- F073: Section 12(2) of the Meetings and Marches Act empowers the mayor to prohibit a rally that would endanger public order. [establishes legal basis and the legitimate aim assumed]
- F081: The Constitution of 1991 abolished the Executive Committees of the People's Councils. [establishes that the second ban remained in effect — necessary for interference finding on second ban]
- F082: The Constitution of 1991 established the mayor as the organ of executive power (Executive Committee superseded by mayor, not Municipal Council). [establishes that the second ban remained in effect]
- F044: The applicants informed members that the rally would not take place because the ban had not been overturned. [establishes actual effect of second ban]
- F013: UMO Ilinden – PIRIN was declared unconstitutional. [acknowledged by Court as context for authorities' suspicions but held insufficient to justify the bans — directly relevant to necessity conclusion]
- F020: The second applicant assured the mayor that no disturbances would occur. [supports peaceful character — scope]
- F030: The first applicant assured the mayor that the rally would be entirely peaceful. [supports peaceful character — scope]
