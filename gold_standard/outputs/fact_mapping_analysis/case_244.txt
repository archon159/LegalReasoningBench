LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's finding that the meetings constitute assemblies within Article 11 turns on their bi-communal character and the aim of dialogue between communities. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F013: The Movement organises bi-communal meetings of a political, cultural, medical or social character. (This establishes the bi-communal nature of the meetings across their varied forms.)
- F015: The applicant is normally unable to obtain a permit to visit the southern part of Cyprus to participate in various bi-communal meetings. (This establishes that the applicant sought to participate.)
- F016: Between 8 March 1992 and 14 April 1998, only 6 out of 46 requests were granted. (This establishes the scale of attempted participation.)

The specific instances (F019-F026) are illustrative of the "varied nature" of meetings but F013 captures the essential point. However, since the Court explicitly relied on the variety of meetings to make its point about their shared bi-communal character, F013 is the most critical representative fact.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary facts for the interference finding are:
- F016: Between 8 March 1992 and 14 April 1998, only 6 out of 46 requests were granted. (This is the core quantitative fact establishing the pattern of refusals.)
- F015: The applicant is normally unable to obtain a permit to visit the southern part of Cyprus to participate in bi-communal meetings. (This establishes the general pattern of which F016 is the specific measure.)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The strictly necessary facts for the "not prescribed by law" finding are:
- F030: The letter from the Health Minister stated the decision was communicated "orally" by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Defence — establishing that the basis for restriction was not a written, accessible law.
- F032: The applicant received no reply from the Prime Minister when he asked for the content of the Council of Ministers' decision — establishing that the legal basis was not disclosed.
- F035: The refusal of 18 May 1994 cited "security reasons, in the public interest, and because the applicant had made propaganda against the State" — establishing that reasons given were vague and shifting, not grounded in a precise legal rule.
- F037: The applicant received no answer from the Deputy Prime Minister when asking whether the Council of Ministers' decision was still in force — further establishing the opacity of the legal basis.

These facts collectively establish that there was no identifiable, accessible, foreseeable legal rule governing the permit refusals.

Not discussed. Empty.

--- NECESSITY ---
Not discussed. Empty.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not applied. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two pivotal findings: (1) there was an interference with the right to peaceful assembly (established by the pattern of permit refusals preventing participation in bi-communal meetings), and (2) that interference was not prescribed by law (established by the absence of any disclosed, accessible, foreseeable legal rule). The irreducible facts are:

- F013: The Movement organises bi-communal meetings — establishing that the meetings are assemblies within Article 11.
- F015: The applicant is normally unable to obtain a permit to visit the southern part of Cyprus to participate in bi-communal meetings — establishing the general pattern of interference.
- F016: Between 8 March 1992 and 14 April 1998, only 6 out of 46 requests were granted — establishing the concrete, quantified interference.
- F030: The Health Minister's letter stated the restriction was communicated orally — establishing the absence of a written, accessible legal basis.
- F032: The applicant received no reply when he asked for the content of the Council of Ministers' decision — establishing that the legal basis was not disclosed or accessible.
- F035: The refusal cited vague grounds (security reasons, public interest, propaganda against the State) — establishing the absence of a precise, foreseeable legal rule with adequate safeguards.
