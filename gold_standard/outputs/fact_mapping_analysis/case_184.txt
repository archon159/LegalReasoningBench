Scope (assembly exists, participation):
The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The phrase is almost verbatim identical. F001 is also relevant insofar as it establishes that there are identifiable applicants, but it adds no factual content about the assemblies themselves. F003 is not relevant to scope.
Atomic facts: F002 (primary), F001 (background only).

Scope (peaceful character):

This maps to F002, which refers to "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The arrest and conviction are the specific measures referred to. No other atomic fact addresses this.
Atomic facts: F002.

Not discussed. No factual grounding. No atomic facts map to this element.

All of this traces back to F002 as the sole atomic fact describing the circumstances. F001 establishes the applicants but adds no substantive factual content for the necessity assessment.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies — without this, there is no basis for finding that Article 11 is engaged at all. F001 is not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning; it is administrative background.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" — arrest and conviction — described in F002 are the factual basis for the finding of interference. No other atomic fact contributes to this.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed.

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary. The Court's necessity finding rests on the similarity of the present case to leading cases, and the similarity is grounded in the facts described in F002 — namely that the applicants faced state measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) as participants or organisers of public assemblies. Without F002, there is no factual basis for the necessity conclusion.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire chain of reasoning — scope (assembly, participation), interference (arrest and conviction), and necessity (by analogy to leading cases) — rests on a single atomic fact: F002. This is the only atomic fact that provides substantive content about what happened to the applicants and why Article 11 is engaged. F001 merely identifies the applicants and cross-references a table; F003 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. F002 is therefore the sole irreducible factual foundation for the violation conclusion.
