LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court implies the assembly exists and that the applicant participated, and explicitly finds it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing what the planned event was and that the only objection was its subject matter (not violence): F002 (planned demonstration), F003 (notification content — public health subject matter), F013 (refusal on subject-matter grounds, no allegation of violence).

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary fact is the act of refusal itself and its basis: F004 (refusal by Deputy Prefect). F013 is also necessary as it confirms the judicial endorsement of the refusal, completing the interference. F011 and F012 establish the legal basis invoked.
Strictly necessary: F004, F013.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court identifies the legal basis (the Public Events Act) and notes its unduly wide discretion, but folds the issue into proportionality. The strictly necessary facts establishing which provision was applied and how it was used are: F004 (refusal under the Act), F013 (court's application of the statutory definition). F011 and F012 provide the content of the statutory definition that was applied.
Strictly necessary: F004, F011, F012, F013.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court finds no legitimate aim was identified by the authorities. The strictly necessary facts are those showing what reason was actually given and that no Article 11 aim was invoked: F004 (refusal citing statistics not matching Ministry data — a subject-matter objection), F005 (the specific stated reason), F013 (court's subject-matter reasoning with no reference to any Article 11 aim).
Strictly necessary: F004, F005, F013.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on: (1) the subject matter of the picket (public health), (2) the purely content-based nature of the refusal, (3) the absence of any justification linked to a pressing social need. Strictly necessary: F003 (subject matter of picket — public health), F013 (content-based refusal by domestic court).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts are necessary — this element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two essential points: (1) the applicant planned a peaceful public event on a public health topic, and (2) the authorities refused it purely on the basis of its subject matter, without identifying any legitimate aim or pressing social need. The irreducible facts are:
- F003: establishes the content of the picket (public health statistics) — the basis for the Court's finding that this was a matter of public interest deserving Article 11 protection.
- F004: the refusal itself — the interference.
- F013: the domestic court's purely content-based reasoning (the picket did not fit the statutory definition of a public event) — the basis for the Court's finding of a formalistic, content-based restriction with no legitimate aim and no necessity justification.

F005 is also relevant as it shows the specific factual objection raised (statistics not matching Ministry data), but F013 is the more critical fact as it is the legal crystallisation of the content-based approach. F007 (cancellation) is a consequence rather than a cause. F014 (appeal court upholding) adds confirmation but is not strictly necessary beyond F013.

Irreducible core: F003, F004, F013.
