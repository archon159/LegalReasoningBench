LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope — existence of an assembly**
The reasoning text states "public assemblies" and "dispersal of these assemblies." The atomic facts that correspond to this are F002 (applicants complained of measures taken as organisers or participants of country-wide protests), F003 (protests organised by Navalnyy), and F004 (protests took place on 9 September 2018). These three facts together establish that assemblies existed.

**Scope — participation**
The reasoning text states "organisers and/or participants of public assemblies" and "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies, and their conviction for administrative offences." The atomic fact that corresponds most directly is F002, which describes the applicants as "organisers or participants of country-wide protests."

**Scope — peaceful character**
As noted previously, there is no factual grounding in the reasoning text for this element. None of the atomic facts address peacefulness either. No mapping is possible.

**Interference**
The reasoning text states "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies, and their conviction for administrative offences." The atomic fact that corresponds is F002, which describes "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants" — this is the closest atomic fact to the arrest and conviction described in the reasoning. No atomic fact explicitly mentions arrest or conviction, but F002 is the most direct correspondence.

**Necessity**
The reasoning text states "the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not 'necessary in a democratic society'" and grounds this entirely in prior case-law rather than specific facts. The Court says it has "examined all the material submitted to it" but cites no specific facts. The only atomic facts that could be said to underlie this conclusion are F002 (the nature of the measures), F003 (the political context — protests organised by Navalnyy), and F004 (the date of the protests). However, as noted, the Court does not explicitly cite any of these in its necessity reasoning.

**Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation**
None of these elements are discussed, and no atomic facts correspond to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**: The Court's implicit finding that scope is satisfied rests on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' participation in them. The strictly necessary atomic fact is F002, which establishes both that assemblies took place ("country-wide protests") and that the applicants were organisers or participants. F003 and F004 add context (who organised them and when) but are not strictly necessary to establish scope — F002 alone suffices.

**Interference**: The strictly necessary atomic fact is F002, which describes "disproportionate measures taken against them" — this is the factual basis for the finding that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' freedom of assembly. No other atomic fact adds to this.

**Prescribed by law**: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

**Legitimate aim**: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

**Necessity**: The Court's necessity finding is grounded in prior case-law rather than specific facts, but the factual predicate for applying that case-law is the similarity of the present case to Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, and Kasparov. The strictly necessary atomic fact is F002, which establishes the nature of the measures (disproportionate measures against protest organisers/participants). F003 may also be relevant given that Navalnyy is explicitly named in one of the leading cases (Navalnyy and Yashin v. Russia), suggesting the Court's reference to prior case-law is directly connected to the same political actor.

**Positive obligation**: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on two things: (1) that the applicants participated in public assemblies, and (2) that the state took measures against them (arrest, dispersal, conviction) in connection with those assemblies, which the Court found not necessary in a democratic society by reference to prior case-law. The irreducible atomic fact is F002, which establishes both the existence of the assemblies, the applicants' role in them, and the nature of the state's response. F003 is also relevant because the connection to Navalnyy links the case directly to the prior leading case-law the Court relies upon for its necessity finding. F004 (the date) is not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion. F001 and F005 are procedural and not necessary for the substantive conclusion.
