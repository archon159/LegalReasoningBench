LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:** F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the Court's implied conclusions that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. Without F002, there is no factual basis for the scope analysis at all.

**Interference:** F002 is strictly necessary. The reference to "disproportionate measures" in F002 is the factual basis from which the Court derives that there was a governmental act (arrest, dispersal, conviction) interfering with the applicants' freedom of assembly. No other atomic fact speaks to this.

**Prescribed by law:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element is not discussed.

**Legitimate aim:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element is not discussed.

**Necessity:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary in the strict sense — the Court's necessity finding is grounded in precedent (Frumkin, Navalnyy and Yashin, Kasparov), not in case-specific facts drawn from the factual section. However, F002 is a precondition in the sense that without the existence of assemblies and measures against them, there would be nothing to assess for necessity.

**Positive obligation:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary — this element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on two things: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated, and (2) that the state took measures against them in that context. Both of these are captured in F002. F001 merely identifies the applicants by reference to a table and adds no substantive factual content relevant to the legal conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact that is strictly necessary for the Court to reach its conclusion under Article 11. It is the factual foundation for scope, interference, and (by analogy with precedent) necessity — the three elements that together drive the violation finding.
