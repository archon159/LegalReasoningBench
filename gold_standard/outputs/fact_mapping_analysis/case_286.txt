LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:** F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were participants or organisers — the two conditions (assembly exists, participation) that must be satisfied for Article 11 to be engaged. F001 is a general reference to the appended table and is not independently sufficient.

**Interference:** F002 is strictly necessary. The reference to "disproportionate measures" against participants/organisers of public assemblies is the factual basis for the finding that there was an interference with the applicants' freedom of assembly.

**Prescribed by law:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary here as this element was not discussed.

**Legitimate aim:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary here as this element was not discussed.

**Necessity:** F002 is the only atomic fact that can be said to underlie the necessity assessment, as it describes the nature of the measures (disproportionate measures against assembly participants/organisers) that the Court found were not necessary in a democratic society. However, the Court's reasoning on necessity is almost entirely case-law-driven rather than fact-driven in this section.

**Positive obligation:** No atomic facts are strictly necessary here as this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core for this conclusion is:

F002: This is the single atomic fact that is strictly necessary. It establishes (a) that there were public assemblies, (b) that the applicants were participants or organisers, and (c) that measures were taken against them — all of which are prerequisites for the Article 11 analysis and the violation finding. Without F002, there is no factual basis for any element of the Court's reasoning.

F001 is a procedural reference to the appended table and does not independently establish any fact necessary for the conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
