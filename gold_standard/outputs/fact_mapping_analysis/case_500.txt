LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each doctrinal element

**Scope:**
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly, that the applicant participated, and that the peaceful character question is engaged. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F011: establishes both that an assembly existed and that the applicant participated in it
- F094: establishes the violent character (demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone) - the single most critical fact on the peaceful character sub-element, adopted from the UN Secretary General's report
- F095: establishes further violent conduct (broke through wire barrier, destroyed UNFICYP observation post) - necessary to support the finding of violence
- F096: establishes further violent conduct (broke through UNFICYP line, entered school complex)

F082 and F097 are relevant to interference rather than scope. F117 and F123 are relevant to the TRNC territory point used under necessity. For scope, the core facts are F011, F094, F095, F096.

**Interference:**
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's assembly right. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F011: applicant was demonstrating (establishes the assembly context)
- F082: applicant was arrested by Turkish-Cypriot police (the core act of interference)

F097 provides context but F082 is the most directly relevant to the applicant specifically.

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court needs to identify a legal basis. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F127: section 70 Cypriot Criminal Code (unlawful assembly definition)
- F130: section 71 Cypriot Criminal Code (criminal liability for participation)
- F133: section 14(1)(b) Criminal Procedure Law (power of arrest without warrant)

The TRNC laws (F112, F113, F135, F136, F137) are also referenced but the Court's primary legal basis identification rests on the Cypriot Criminal Code and Criminal Procedure Law provisions. However, since the Court also invokes the principle that TRNC laws provide a legal basis, and the charges were actually brought under TRNC law (F112, F113), these are also necessary. The most critical are F127, F130, F133 (Cypriot law basis) and F112, F113 (TRNC law basis actually applied).

**Legitimate aim:**
The Court implicitly accepts legitimate aims based on the violent character of the demonstration. The strictly necessary facts are the same core violence facts:
- F094: demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone
- F095: broke through wire barrier, destroyed UNFICYP observation post
- F096: broke through UNFICYP line

These are the facts that establish the disorder/public safety justification.

**Necessity:**
The Court's proportionality finding rests on the violent character of the demonstration and the border violation. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F094: demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone (core violence fact)
- F095: broke through wire barrier, destroyed UNFICYP observation post
- F096: broke through UNFICYP line
- F117: witnesses at trial stated accused illegally entered TRNC territory and resisted arrest (accepted by Court as uncontradicted)
- F123: TRNC court concluded accused crossed TRNC borders at unapproved entry point (accepted finding)

F112 and F113 (charges under TRNC law) are relevant context but the necessity finding turns on the factual conduct, not the legal charges.

**Positive obligation:**
No atomic facts are strictly necessary - the Court's mention is purely abstract and doctrinal.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on whether the interference with the applicant's assembly right was justified. The Court found no violation because: (1) there was an interference (applicant arrested while demonstrating); (2) it was prescribed by law; (3) it pursued legitimate aims; and (4) it was proportionate given the violent character of the demonstration and the border violation. The facts that were absolutely essential to this conclusion are:

- F011: establishes the applicant participated in the demonstration (necessary for Article 11 to be engaged at all)
- F082: establishes the interference (arrest by Turkish-Cypriot police)
- F094: demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone - the single most critical fact establishing the violent/unlawful character that justified the interference
- F095: broke through wire barrier and destroyed UNFICYP observation post - corroborates violent character
- F096: broke through UNFICYP line and entered school complex - corroborates violent character
- F117: witnesses at trial (accepted by Court) stated accused illegally entered TRNC territory - critical for the border violation finding that justified the intervention
- F127 and F130: legal basis in Cypriot Criminal Code (prescribed by law element)
- F133: legal basis for arrest without warrant (prescribed by law element)

However, strictly narrowing to what the case actually turned on (the justification for the interference, i.e., the necessity finding), the irreducible core is: F011 (assembly and participation), F082 (interference), F094, F095, F096 (violent character justifying the response), F117 (border violation accepted by Court), and F127/F130/F133 (legal basis). Since the prescribed by law finding was not contested in substance and the Court resolved it briefly, and since the case truly turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment, the most essential facts are those establishing the violent conduct: F094, F095, F096, F117, supplemented by F011 (engagement of Article 11) and F082 (interference). The legal basis facts (F127, F130, F133) are necessary for the prescribed by law element but not what the case turned on in terms of outcome.
