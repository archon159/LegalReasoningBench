LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicants participated; (iii) it was sufficiently peaceful to fall within Article 11.
- F029: establishes the authorised assembly — the foundation for finding an assembly existed
- F031: establishes the trigger for the move to the Rector's office (the 'election'), contextualising the assembly
- F032: establishes that the applicants as a group moved to the Rector's office as part of their protest — participation
- F062: acting Rector confirmed no foul language used in his office — critical for the peacefulness finding
- F110: police confirmed no physical resistance — critical for the peacefulness finding
- F129: applicants occupied office for about two hours — relevant to the 'not at the core' observation but not excluding them from Article 11

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish: removal by police + administrative sanctions.
- F035/F036: police arrived and went to acting Rector's office — establishes the removal
- F045: it took more than one hour for police to negotiate removal — establishes the nature of the removal
- F120: Tbilisi City Court found applicants guilty of administrative offences — establishes the administrative sanction
- F121: fine of 100 Georgian laris imposed — establishes the concrete interference by way of sanction

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needed to establish: legal basis for removal and for administrative sanctions; foreseeability of Article 166 CAO.
- F160: Law on the Police authorised police to disperse unlawful assemblies — legal basis for removal
- F158: Article 9(1) of Law on Assembly and Demonstrations prohibited assemblies inside governmental institutions — additional legal basis
- F163: text of Article 166 CAO — legal basis for administrative sanction and foreseeability analysis
- F164: text of Article 173 CAO — legal basis for resistance charge
- F131: domestic court's non-arbitrary interpretation of Article 166 as applicable to the Rector's office — supports foreseeability
- F129: disruption to University functioning — factual predicate for foreseeability of Article 166 application

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court needed to establish: disruption to public order and harm to rights of others.
- F039: at least twenty people forced their way into Mr G.Kh.'s office — establishes the nature of the entry
- F040/F149: dozens of protesters in corridor chanting slogans — establishes scale of disruption
- F057: University remained paralysed, meetings disrupted, administration prevented from carrying out duties — establishes disruption to rights of others
- F064: acting Rector testified incident lasted about an hour and a half, paralysing his work and functioning of whole administration — establishes duration and impact
- F147/F148/F149: Imedi video recording confirming forceful entry and scale of protest — independent corroboration of disruption

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court needed to establish: proportionality of removal and of administrative sanctions.
For removal:
- F029: pre-authorised meeting allowed — establishes tolerance shown before removal
- F004: applicants had been allowed to protest for months — establishes broader tolerance
- F045/F109: police negotiated for over an hour before securing removal — establishes proportionate approach
- F110/F112: no physical force used — establishes minimal means used
- F046: applicants allowed to continue protest after removal — establishes limited scope of interference
For sanctions:
- F075/F076: criminal proceedings discontinued — establishes that the most serious sanction was not applied
- F121/F123: fines of GEL 100 — establishes modest level of sanction
- F135/F136: applicants refused to obey police for more than an hour — establishes factual basis for proportionality of sanction
- F039: forceful entry into Rector's office — establishes factual basis justifying some sanction

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts applicable.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on whether the interference (removal + fines) was proportionate to the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others, given that the protest fell within Article 11's scope. The following facts are strictly necessary:

- F029: The applicants had a pre-authorised meeting — establishes they were not denied assembly rights entirely, and that the University showed tolerance
- F032: The applicants as a group moved to the acting Rector's office demanding his resignation — establishes the nature of the conduct that triggered the interference
- F039: At least twenty people forced their way into Mr G.Kh.'s office — establishes the character of the entry justifying intervention
- F057: University remained paralysed, meetings disrupted, administration prevented from carrying out duties — establishes the legitimate aim (rights of others, public order)
- F062: Acting Rector confirmed no foul language used in his office — establishes the assembly was sufficiently peaceful to fall within Article 11
- F110: No physical resistance to police — establishes peacefulness and that minimal means were used
- F045/F109: Police negotiated for over an hour before securing removal — establishes proportionate approach to removal
- F046: Applicants allowed to continue protest after removal — establishes limited scope of interference with assembly rights
- F075/F076: Criminal proceedings discontinued — establishes that the most serious sanction was not applied
- F121: Fines of GEL 100 — establishes the modest and proportionate level of the administrative sanction
- F135/F136: Applicants refused to obey police for more than an hour — establishes factual basis for the administrative sanction under Article 173 CAO
- F004: Applicants had been allowed to protest for months — establishes the broader context of tolerance that supports proportionality
