LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's finding that Article 11 applies requires that there were assemblies and that the applicants participated in them. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003, which establish that the applicants were organisers and participants of public assemblies. F001 is background only and not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself.

Interference: The Court's finding that there was an interference requires that some governmental act affected the applicants' freedom of assembly. The measures described (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are grounded in F002 and F003, which describe the complaints about disproportionate measures. These are the most critical facts.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.

Necessity: The Court's conclusion that the interference was not necessary in a democratic society is grounded in the same facts that establish the nature of the measures taken against the applicants. F002 and F003 are the necessary facts, as they describe the disproportionate measures that the Court found unjustified.

Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts support this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The final conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For the Court to reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, it must be established that: (i) there were public assemblies, (ii) the applicants were organisers or participants, and (iii) disproportionate measures were taken against them. All three points are captured by F002 and F003. F002 covers organisers and F003 covers participants — together they are the irreducible factual core. F001 is merely a reference to an appended table and is not itself a substantive fact driving the conclusion. The conclusion turns on the existence of assemblies, the applicants' involvement, and the measures taken — all of which are encapsulated in F002 and F003.
