LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The assembly existed and was authorised: F006, F008
The applicant was not among those who initiated the violence: F099, F100 (unidentified persons started the disorder, not the applicant)
The applicant's involvement came only at the height of clashes when police were already arresting: F016, F106

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The applicant was prosecuted and convicted: F098
The applicant was sentenced to imprisonment: F117, F118

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The prosecution was based on identified criminal law provisions: F036, F037, F126, F128
The domestic courts failed to provide adequate safeguards (no verification of applicant's account, refusal to hear defence witness, appellate court did not address this): F114, F123, F124

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
There were clashes at the demonstration providing a basis for the aims of preventing disorder and crime and protecting rights of others: F014, F018, F019

--- NECESSITY ---
Nature and severity of the sentence: F117, F118
The acts for which the applicant was convicted included peaceful expression (chanting slogans at an authorised assembly): F103, F008
The violent acts were limited in severity (no danger to life or health, minor injury): F105, F107, F115
No pre-existing intent to participate in disorder: F108, F099, F100
Applicant's involvement was reactive, not initiating: F016, F106
Lack of procedural safeguards (refusal to hear defence witness, appellate court silent): F114, F123, F124
Chilling effect amplified by large-scale proceedings: F088, F117

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — the Court explicitly declined to assess this as a separate issue and reached no conclusion on it.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the disproportionality of the criminal conviction and sentence. The key facts are:

1. The assembly was authorised (F008) — establishing that the applicant was exercising a protected right.
2. The applicant was convicted and sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment (F098, F117, F118) — the interference itself.
3. The sentence included a three-year term for participation in mass disorder under Article 212 § 2, which was harsher than the one year and four months for the actual assault (F118) — showing the disproportionate weight placed on participation rather than the violent act.
4. The acts constituting 'participation in mass disorder' included chanting anti-government slogans at an authorised assembly (F103, F008) — peaceful expression penalised.
5. The violent acts were minor and did not endanger life or health (F105, F107, F115) — limiting the weight of the legitimate aim.
6. The applicant was not among those who initiated the clashes; unidentified persons started the disorder (F099, F100), and the applicant threw tarmac only when police were already arresting protesters (F016, F106) — no pre-existing intent, reactive involvement.
7. The domestic courts refused to hear the defence witness Officer Sh. and the appellate court did not address this (F123, F124) — absence of procedural safeguards.
8. The large-scale proceedings and severity of sentence produced a chilling effect on political participation (F088, F117).
