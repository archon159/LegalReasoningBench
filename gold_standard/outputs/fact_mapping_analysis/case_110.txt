LIST 1: Factual grounding mapped to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful):**
The Court stated: "the applicant was refused permit to attend a meeting held on 20 June 1997 in southern Cyprus" and described it as "a bi-communal meeting" and referred to "engaging in peaceful assembly with people from both communities." The applicant "was refused permit to attend a meeting held on 20 June 1997 in southern Cyprus."
- F100: "On 20 June 1997 the public authorities prevented the applicant and her daughter from attending a meeting organised by a radio station in southern Cyprus, by not giving them permission to cross over to the southern part of Cyprus."
This is the single atomic fact that directly corresponds to the Court's factual grounding for scope (the meeting existed, the applicant tried to attend, and the meeting was a peaceful bi-communal gathering).

**Interference:**
The Court stated: "the refusal of the authorities to grant a permit to the applicant barred her participation in a bi-communal meeting there" and "the Court reiterates at the outset its findings in the cases of Cyprus v. Turkey and Djavit An v. Turkey in relation to the rigorous approach taken by the 'TRNC' authorities to bi-communal contacts after the second half of 1996 by the imposition of restrictions and, indeed, prohibitions."
- F100: The public authorities prevented the applicant and her daughter from attending the meeting by not giving them permission to cross over.
- F178: "The Government submit that crossings of the Green Line to and from the Ledra Palace Gate between the 'TRNC' and southern Cyprus are regulated by the rules and regulations of the 'TRNC' and are subject to restrictions due to security precautions."
- F179: "The Government submit that the 'TRNC' authorities have the right to suspend permission to cross the border at the Ledra Palace Gate."
These facts ground the Court's finding that a governmental act (refusal of permit) constituted an interference.

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court stated: "the respondent Government referred to general restrictions concerning crossing of the 'green line' between the 'TRNC' and southern Cyprus. They did not refer to any law or measures in the 'TRNC' regulating the issuance of permits to Turkish Cypriots living in northern Cyprus to cross the 'green line' into southern Cyprus for the purposes of attending bi-communal meetings. Furthermore, they did not provide any indication as to when refusal of such permits is allowed."
- F178: The Government submitted that crossings are regulated by rules and regulations of the 'TRNC' and subject to restrictions due to security precautions — this is the "general restrictions" the Court refers to as insufficient.
- F179: The Government submitted that the 'TRNC' authorities have the right to suspend permission — again cited as a general claim without specific legal basis.
- F100: The actual refusal of permission to the applicant, which is the measure whose legal basis is being examined.
The Court's finding that there is no specific law regulating permit issuance for bi-communal meetings is grounded in the absence of any such law being cited by the Government, which is reflected in F178 and F179 (the only legal basis offered) and F100 (the measure applied).

**Legitimate aim:** Not discussed. No atomic facts.

**Necessity:** Not discussed. No atomic facts.

**Positive obligation:** Mentioned only as a general principle, not applied to facts. No atomic facts.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:**
The strictly necessary fact is F100, which establishes that there was a meeting (organised by a radio station in southern Cyprus), that the applicant sought to attend it, and that she was prevented. This single fact is sufficient to ground all three sub-elements of scope as discussed by the Court.

**Interference:**
F100 is strictly necessary: it establishes the governmental act (refusal of permission to cross) that constitutes the interference. F178 and F179 are relevant context (they show the regulatory framework invoked by the Government to justify the refusal), but the core interference finding rests on F100 alone. However, F178 and F179 are also necessary insofar as they confirm that the refusal was a deliberate governmental act under a claimed regulatory framework, not an accident or omission — which is relevant to characterising it as an interference. For strict necessity, F100 is the irreducible minimum; F178 and F179 support but are not independently necessary for the interference finding.

**Prescribed by law:**
F178 and F179 are strictly necessary: they represent the only legal basis the Government offered (general restrictions and a general right to suspend permission), which the Court found insufficient to satisfy the "prescribed by law" requirement. F100 is also necessary as the measure whose legal basis is being assessed. Without F178 and F179, there would be no basis for the Court's finding that the Government only pointed to "general restrictions" rather than a specific law.

**Legitimate aim:** No atomic facts (not discussed).

**Necessity:** No atomic facts (not discussed).

**Positive obligation:** No atomic facts (not applied).

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two things: (1) there was a refusal of permission to attend a bi-communal meeting (establishing scope and interference), and (2) the Government could point to no specific law regulating the issuance or refusal of such permits (establishing the "not prescribed by law" finding that led directly to the violation). The irreducible facts are:
- F100: The refusal of permission to cross the green line to attend the meeting — this establishes the assembly, the attempt to participate, and the interference.
- F178: The Government's submission that crossings are regulated by general rules and security precautions — this is the only legal basis offered, which the Court found insufficient.
- F179: The Government's submission that the 'TRNC' authorities have the right to suspend permission — again part of the insufficient legal basis offered.

F100 alone establishes the interference; F178 and F179 together represent the entirety of the legal basis offered by the Government, whose inadequacy is the reason the Court found a violation. Without all three, the Court's conclusion cannot be reached.
