LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation): The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This maps to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies") almost verbatim. F001 is also relevant insofar as it identifies who the applicants are, but it contains no substantive facts about assemblies. F003 is not relevant here.

Interference: The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This again maps to F002, which is the only atomic fact describing the nature of the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction). F001 and F003 do not add to this.

Necessity: As noted in the prior analysis, there is no positive factual grounding for the necessity assessment in the reasoning section. The Court relies on precedent and a negative statement about the absence of distinguishing facts. Among the atomic facts provided, F002 is the closest anchor, as it describes the measures complained of, which are the subject of the necessity assessment. However, no atomic fact provides substantive factual grounding for the necessity conclusion itself.

Peaceful (scope sub-element, implied): No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning, and none of the atomic facts (F001, F002, F003) address the peaceful character of the assemblies.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants. Without this fact, the Court could not even begin its Article 11 analysis. F001 is not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself, only for identifying the parties.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It identifies the concrete governmental acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction for administrative offences) that constitute the interference. No other atomic fact speaks to this.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary for this element.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary for this element.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be considered necessary, as it describes the measures whose proportionality is assessed. However, as noted, the Court does not reason from facts to the necessity conclusion — it reasons from precedent. Still, F002 is the minimal factual anchor.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire legal analysis — scope, interference, and necessity — rests on a single atomic fact: F002. This fact establishes (a) that there were public assemblies, (b) that the applicants participated in them as organisers or participants, and (c) that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) were taken against them. Without F002, there is no Article 11 case at all. F001 is a procedural/identifying fact and F003 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. Therefore, F002 is the sole irreducible factual core for the final conclusion.
