LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's scope analysis turns on: (i) the existence of the Maidan protests as an assembly (implied, no separate factual finding needed beyond the general reference); (ii) the participation of Lutsenko and Verbytskyy in those protests; (iii) their peaceful character. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F011: Lutsenko took an active part in the Maidan protests — establishes his participation.
- F017: Verbytskyy came to Kyiv and took part in the Maidan protests — establishes his participation.
The peaceful character finding rests on absence of evidence of violence by either individual; no atomic fact positively establishes violence by them, so no atomic fact is strictly necessary here beyond the participation facts (which contextualise the absence finding). F018 is relevant context for the broader violence but is not strictly necessary for the peaceful sub-element conclusion about the two individuals.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court's interference finding rests on: (a) the abduction and ill-treatment/killing itself; (b) the link to their protest participation (purpose of intimidation/suppression). Strictly necessary facts:
- F020: abduction from hospital — the triggering act of interference.
- F068: official notification of suspicion stating abduction was part of attempt to suppress Maidan protests — the most direct official finding linking the abduction to protest participation, establishing the purpose element.
- F069: same for M. — corroborates F068.
- F072: N., Ch. and Z. acted on instructions of unidentified representatives of State bodies — establishes state responsibility.
- F093: investigators found evidence that organisers maintained permanent contact with leadership of Ministry of Internal Affairs — corroborates state responsibility.
- F094: unidentified police officers shared information about Lutsenko's telephone conversations and location with organisers — corroborates state involvement.
- F046: Verbytskyy's body found — establishes the ultimate consequence (death) as part of the interference.
- F027: questioned about involvement in Maidan protests — directly establishes the protest-related motive of the abductors.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary; the Court's conclusion rests on an absence of material in the case file. List is empty.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary; same reasoning. List is empty.

--- NECESSITY ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary; the conclusion follows from prior negative findings. List is empty.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
The Court's implied positive obligation reasoning rests on the context of state failure to take appropriate measures. Strictly necessary facts:
- F114: adoption of laws limiting freedom of assembly — establishes the legislative context frustrating peaceful protest.
- F115: internal troops reinforced with titushky allegedly upon coordination with former senior police officials — establishes that rather than taking appropriate measures, authorities escalated the situation.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three pivotal questions under Article 11: (1) did the applicants participate in a protected assembly? (2) was there an interference with their assembly rights? (3) was that interference attributable to the state and aimed at suppressing their protest participation? The prescribed by law, legitimate aim, and necessity tests all failed for the same reason — the interference was an unlawful, purposive act of state-linked violence with no legal basis or legitimate aim. The irreducible factual core is:

- F011: Lutsenko participated in the Maidan protests — necessary to establish scope/participation.
- F017: Verbytskyy participated in the Maidan protests — necessary to establish scope/participation.
- F020: Both were abducted from the hospital — the core act of interference.
- F027: They were questioned about their involvement in the Maidan protests — establishes the protest-related motive of the interference.
- F068: Official notification of suspicion stating the abduction was part of the authorities' attempt to suppress the Maidan protests — the most critical fact linking the interference to protest participation and establishing state responsibility.
- F072: The organisers acted on instructions of unidentified representatives of State bodies — establishes state attribution of the interference.
- F046: Verbytskyy's body was found dead — establishes the ultimate severity of the interference (necessary for the Article 11 violation finding in his case).
- F093: Investigators found evidence that organisers maintained permanent contact with the leadership of the Ministry of Internal Affairs — corroborates state responsibility and is strictly necessary to the Court's finding that the authorities were responsible.
