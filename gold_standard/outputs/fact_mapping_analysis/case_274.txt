LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity assessment. The Court found a violation because: (1) the applicant was convicted and severely punished (three days detention) for conduct at a lawful, peaceful demonstration; (2) the conduct identified — obstructing traffic and making noise — was not reprehensible, since traffic had already been suspended and noise is inherent to a large political demonstration; (3) no violence, obscenity or incitement was involved. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

F012 — the demonstration existed and was held on 7 April 2003 (establishes the assembly)
F017 — traffic was suspended by the traffic police before the demonstration began (makes 'obstruction of traffic' equivalent to mere presence, the linchpin of the necessity finding)
F061 — the applicant was sentenced to three days of administrative detention (establishes the severity of the interference)
F062 — the judge's entire finding identifies only obstruction of traffic and loud noise, with no mention of violence, obscenity or incitement (establishes that the applicant did nothing reprehensible)
F016 — about 30,000 people attended (contextualises noise as inherent and not reprehensible; Government did not dispute this)
