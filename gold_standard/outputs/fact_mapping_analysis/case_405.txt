LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---

Quote: "the case file indicates that the applicants did participate in the demonstrations in question and that those demonstrations were dispersed by the police."
Atomic facts: F004 (applicants attended demonstration of 14 May 2012), F005 (applicants attended demonstration of 21 May 2012), F011 (shortly after the applicants joined the demonstrations, the police started to disperse the protesters).

Quote: "the case file does not contain any information to indicate that the applicants acted in a violent manner, contrary to the allegations made by the Government."
Atomic facts: F009 (applicants submitted demonstrations intended to be peaceful), F010 (applicants submitted demonstrations conducted in a peaceful manner) — but the Court accepted these as established, not merely asserted. No atomic fact positively records violent conduct, which is precisely the Court's point. The closest atomic facts are F009 and F010, which the Court accepted, and F045 (police officers stated they had not applied physical force but had warned participants to disperse — this is the only record of conduct at the demonstrations from the case file, and it does not record violence by demonstrators).

Quote: "there is no evidence to suggest that in the instant case the applicants or other demonstrators presented a danger to public order."
Atomic facts: Again, no atomic fact records danger to public order. The absence is the point. F045 is again relevant as the only record of conduct at the demonstrations from the domestic proceedings.

Quote: "The Court therefore accepts the applicants' assertion that the demonstration had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner up until its dispersal."
Atomic facts: F009, F010 (the applicants' submissions that the Court explicitly accepted).

Quote: "The applicants did not provide the Court with any further information as to the circumstances of the organisation of the assemblies in question."
No atomic fact directly records this absence of information. F078 (relevant domestic law on freedom of assembly summarised in Gafgaz Mammadov) is the closest background fact, but the Court's point is about a gap in the record, not a positive fact.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---

Atomic facts: F004, F005 (demonstrations were unauthorised — this is the factual basis for the assumed aims), F070 (domestic court found applicants removed for violating public order and not obeying police).

--- NECESSITY ---

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) there were assemblies; (ii) the applicants participated; (iii) the assemblies were peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F004 and F005: establish that the applicants attended the demonstrations (both dates).
- F011: establishes that the police dispersed the demonstrations the applicants had joined.
- F009 and F010: the applicants' submissions on peaceful character, which the Court explicitly accepted as established.
- F045: the only case-file record of conduct at the demonstrations, which does not record any violence by demonstrators and which the Court used to confirm absence of violent conduct.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F004, F005: applicants attended the demonstrations.
- F011: police dispersed the demonstrations.
- F070: domestic court confirmed applicants were removed from the venue by police.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court assumed this element arguendo. The only factual basis for the assumption is:
- F004, F005: demonstrations were unauthorised (the factual basis for the legal characterisation).
- F070: domestic court's finding that applicants were removed for violating public order — the closest the case file comes to identifying a legal basis.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
Also assumed arguendo. The factual basis is the same as for prescribed by law:
- F004, F005: demonstrations were unauthorised.
- F070: domestic court's finding on removal for public order violation.

--- NECESSITY ---
This is the element the case turned on. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F004, F005: demonstrations were unauthorised — the sole reason for dispersal.
- F011: police moved to disperse shortly after applicants joined — supports finding of impatience.
- F045: police officers stated no force was used, only warnings to disperse — the domestic authorities' own account, which the Court used to frame the necessity analysis.
- F070: domestic court's finding that applicants were removed for violating public order and not obeying police — the Court's primary source for the "main reason" finding.
- F009, F010: peaceful character of demonstrations, accepted by the Court.
- F006, F007, F008: subject matter of the demonstrations (vital democratic issues).
- F071, F072: domestic court's findings that ill-treatment was not proved and no criminal offence was committed — relevant to the Court's assessment that the domestic authorities found no wrongdoing, yet the Court still found the dispersal disproportionate.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment. The Court found a violation because: (a) the demonstrations were peaceful; (b) the only reason for dispersal was lack of authorisation; (c) the police moved quickly to disperse; (d) the demonstrations concerned vital democratic issues; and (e) the authorities showed no tolerance. The irreducible facts are:

- F004 and F005: the applicants attended the demonstrations — without this, there is no case.
- F011: the police dispersed the demonstrations shortly after the applicants joined — establishes both the interference and the impatience finding.
- F070: the domestic court's finding that the applicants were removed for being in an unauthorised assembly and not obeying police — this is the Court's primary source for the finding that the sole reason for dispersal was lack of authorisation, which is the crux of the proportionality failure.
- F009, F010: peaceful character of the demonstrations, accepted by the Court — essential because the necessity analysis turns on the absence of violence or danger.
- F045: police officers' own statements that no force was used and only warnings were given — the Court used this to confirm the domestic authorities' account while still finding the dispersal disproportionate.
- F006, F007, F008: subject matter of the demonstrations — the Court explicitly relied on these to find that the demonstrators were raising vital democratic issues, which weighs against the proportionality of dispersal.
