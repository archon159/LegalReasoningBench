LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's application of Article 11 requires that there was an assembly and that the applicants participated in it. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003, both of which establish participation in public demonstrations. F002 and F003 are equally critical; both mention "participation in public demonstrations" and together establish the factual basis for scope. However, since they establish the same point, the most representative is F002 (arrest) and F003 (conviction) together, as both are referenced in the reasoning.

Interference: The Court explicitly identifies arrest (F002) and conviction for administrative offence (F003) as the governmental acts constituting interference. Both are strictly necessary because the Court refers to both acts together as the interference.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interference (arrest and conviction) with the applicants' freedom of assembly (participation in public demonstrations) was not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core is:
- F002: establishes the arrest as a governmental act interfering with participation in public demonstrations.
- F003: establishes the conviction for administrative offence as a governmental act interfering with participation in public demonstrations.
These two facts are the essential foundation: without them, there is no assembly, no participation, no interference, and no necessity analysis. F001 and F004 are not necessary for the conclusion. F002 and F003 together are the minimum factual basis on which the entire Article 11 analysis rests.
