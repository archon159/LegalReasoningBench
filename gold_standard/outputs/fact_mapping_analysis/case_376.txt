LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's factual grounding for scope is minimal. It accepts without dispute that the applicant could rely on Article 11 guarantees, and refers to the assembly as a 'planned peaceful assembly.' The atomic facts underlying this are:
- F003: the applicant notified the police that he planned to organise a demonstration (establishes assembly planned and applicant's role as organiser/participant)
- F004: the demonstration was planned to take place from 2 to 7 p.m. on 17 April 2010 (establishes the assembly was a concrete planned event)
- F005: the demonstration was planned to take place in front of the Venyige Street prison (establishes the assembly as a public gathering)
- F006: the purpose was to draw attention to the 'situation of political prisoners' (establishes expressive/assembly character)
The peaceful character is referred to by the Court ('planned peaceful assembly') but no specific fact from the record is cited to establish this. The closest atomic fact is F006 (the purpose was expressive/political, not violent), and the absence of any finding of violence in the record.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court grounds interference on the fact of the ban: 'the Government did not dispute...that the ban on the demonstration had interfered with the exercise of his rights.' The atomic facts are:
- F008: the head of the Budapest Police Department banned the planned demonstration
- F009: the ban was on the grounds that traffic could not be diverted to alternative routes, pursuant to section 8(1) of the Assembly Act

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court states: 'The Court observes that the ban was based on section 8(1) of the Assembly Act and considers that it was thus prescribed by law.' The atomic fact is:
- F009: the ban was issued pursuant to section 8(1) of the Assembly Act

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court accepts the aims of preventing disorder and protecting the rights of others. The factual grounding is the nature of the ban (traffic disruption affecting other road users). The atomic facts are:
- F009: the ban was on the grounds that traffic could not be diverted to alternative routes (points to rights of other road users and prevention of disorder)
- F018: the Budapest Police Department established that lawful parking and traffic circulation in the neighbourhood would become impossible (points to the factual basis for the aim of protecting rights of others)

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's factual grounding for necessity is the most developed. The key quotes and their corresponding atomic facts are:
1. 'the basis for upholding the ban on the assembly related exclusively to traffic issues' — F009 (ban based on traffic/alternative routes), F015 (Regional Court dismissed the case), F018 (police established traffic circulation would become impossible)
2. 'The Government's submissions were, in essence, confined to the affirmation that the demonstration would have seriously hampered the free flow of traffic in the area' — this is a characterisation of the Government's submission, not an adopted fact, so no atomic fact is strictly adopted here by the Court
3. 'the Court bases its findings on those in the Körtvélyessy case...which concerned a similar problem relating to the very same location' — F005 (Venyige Street prison) and F007 (Venyige Street is a broad cul-de-sac with a service lane) are the facts that connect this case to Körtvélyessy
4. The reference to 200 expected participants: F016 (the police reckoned on 200 demonstrators)
5. The traffic expert opinion: F017 (the Budapest Police Department obtained the opinion of a traffic expert)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. No atomic facts correspond.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that there was a planned assembly, that the applicant sought to organise it, and that it was peaceful in character:
- F003: applicant notified police of planned demonstration (establishes assembly and participation)
- F006: purpose was expressive/political (supports peaceful, non-violent character)

F004 and F005 are contextual but not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion on scope.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary fact is the ban itself:
- F008: the head of the Budapest Police Department banned the planned demonstration

F009 adds the legal basis for the ban but is more relevant to 'prescribed by law.'

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The strictly necessary fact is:
- F009: the ban was issued pursuant to section 8(1) of the Assembly Act

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The strictly necessary fact is:
- F009: the ban was on the grounds that traffic could not be diverted to alternative routes (this is what connects the measure to the aims of preventing disorder and protecting rights of others)

F018 supports the factual basis for the aim but the Court does not explicitly adopt it as a finding — it is the domestic authority's finding. However, since the Court does not dispute it and it underlies the traffic-based rationale, it is relevant. Given the Court's explicit reference to the traffic basis of the ban, F009 is the core fact.

--- NECESSITY ---
The strictly necessary facts are those the Court explicitly relies on in its necessity analysis:
- F009: the ban was based exclusively on traffic grounds (the Court notes the ban related 'exclusively to traffic issues')
- F005: the demonstration was planned at Venyige Street prison (the Court cross-references Körtvélyessy as concerning 'the very same location')
- F007: Venyige Street is a broad cul-de-sac with a service lane (this is the geographic fact that connects to Körtvélyessy and was central to the traffic assessment)
- F016: the police reckoned on 200 demonstrators (the Court notes the domestic court's assessment was based on this figure)

F017 (traffic expert opinion) and F018 (police finding on traffic) are part of the domestic record but the Court's necessity finding is that even accepting the traffic rationale, it was insufficient — so these facts are part of the context but not strictly necessary for the Court's own conclusion.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed. Empty.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity assessment. The Court found that the ban — based exclusively on traffic grounds — was not sufficient to meet a pressing social need, relying on its findings in Körtvélyessy concerning the same location. The irreducible facts are:

- F008: the demonstration was banned (establishes the interference that is the subject of the violation finding)
- F009: the ban was based on the ground that traffic could not be diverted to alternative routes under section 8(1) of the Assembly Act (establishes both the legal basis and the exclusive rationale — traffic — that the Court found insufficient)
- F005: the demonstration was planned at Venyige Street prison (establishes the location that connects this case to Körtvélyessy, which is the explicit basis for the Court's factual findings on necessity)
- F007: Venyige Street is a broad cul-de-sac with a service lane (the geographic fact that was central to the traffic assessment and to the Körtvélyessy cross-reference)

F003 and F006 are necessary to establish that there was an assembly within the scope of Article 11, but the case did not turn on scope. F016 (200 demonstrators) is relevant context but not strictly irreducible — the Court's conclusion rests on the insufficiency of the traffic rationale regardless of the precise number. The core of the violation is: there was a ban (F008), based solely on traffic grounds (F009), at a location (F005, F007) where the Court had already found in Körtvélyessy that such a rationale was insufficient.
