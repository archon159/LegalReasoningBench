LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

The factual grounding identified in the previous step rested entirely on one phrase from the reasoning: "concerning disproportionate measures taken by the authorities against organisers and participants of public assemblies." This phrase is the Court's own characterisation of the complaints, not a direct recitation of specific facts. Looking at the atomic facts provided:

- F001 notes that the list of applicants and relevant details are in the appended table — this is the closest available anchor for who the applicants are and what happened to them, but it is purely a reference to an appendix not reproduced here.
- F002 concerns unlawful detention, which relates to a different complaint (deprivation of liberty), not Article 11.
- F003 states that "the applicants raised other complaints under the provisions of the Convention" — this is the atomic fact that corresponds to the Article 11 complaints being before the Court at all.

For scope (assembly exists, participation): The phrase "organisers and participants of public assemblies" maps most closely to F003 (other complaints under the Convention, which include the assembly-related complaints) and arguably F001 (the applicants' details). No atomic fact explicitly describes an assembly or the applicants' participation in one.

For interference: The phrase "measures taken by the authorities" maps to F003 as the source of the complaint, and F001 as the identity of those affected. Again, no atomic fact explicitly describes a governmental measure.

For necessity/proportionality: The word "disproportionate" in the Court's characterisation maps to F003 as the complaint, and F001 as the applicants. No atomic fact explicitly describes the nature or severity of the measures.

In short, the factual grounding quotes point only to F001 and F003 across all three implied elements. F002 is not relevant to Article 11. Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, and positive obligation have no factual grounding and therefore no corresponding atomic facts.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F003 is strictly necessary — it is the fact that the applicants raised complaints under the Convention that brings the Article 11 issue before the Court at all. F001 is also necessary to identify who the applicants are. However, since no atomic fact explicitly describes an assembly or participation, F003 is the most critical representative fact.
- Interference: F003 is strictly necessary — the complaints about measures taken by authorities are captured under "other complaints under the provisions of the Convention."
- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts support this — not discussed.
- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts support this — not discussed.
- Necessity: F003 is strictly necessary — the Court's finding of disproportionality is grounded in the complaints described as "other complaints under the provisions of the Convention," which include the assembly-related ones.
- Positive obligation: No atomic facts support this — not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that disproportionate measures were taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. The only atomic fact that is strictly necessary to reach this conclusion — as the source of the Article 11 complaints and the basis for the Court's reference to Frumkin v. Russia — is F003. F001 is a background fact identifying the applicants but does not itself drive the Article 11 conclusion. F002 is irrelevant to Article 11. Therefore, the irreducible factual core is F003 alone.
