LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied)
The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
The reference to "public assemblies" in both F002 and F003 is what grounds the implied finding that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated. The peaceful character of the assemblies is not grounded in any atomic fact.

(b) Interference (implied)
The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002 and F003: both describe "disproportionate measures" against organisers and participants, which is the factual basis for the interference finding. No atomic fact specifically mentions arrests or convictions, but F002 and F003 are the closest available atomic facts.

(e) Necessity (explicitly discussed)
The factual grounding quote was: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion as to the admissibility and merits of these complaints."
This does not point to any specific atomic fact about the conduct of the assemblies or the nature of the state response. The Court's necessity assessment is grounded in the absence of distinguishing material, not in any positive factual finding. The only atomic facts that can be loosely associated are again F002 and F003, as they describe the measures complained of. F001 (the appended table) may also be relevant as the repository of case-specific details the Court examined and found insufficient to distinguish the case, but F001 is purely procedural and does not itself establish any substantive fact.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. They establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies — the minimum factual predicate for the scope element to be satisfied. Without these facts, there is no basis for the Court to treat Article 11 as engaged at all.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" described in these facts are the governmental acts that constitute the interference. No more specific atomic fact about arrests or convictions is available.
- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts correspond. This element is not discussed and no atomic facts bear on it.
- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts correspond. This element is not discussed and no atomic facts bear on it.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 are the minimum necessary facts. The Court's necessity assessment is grounded in the characterisation of the measures as disproportionate (as complained of), and in the absence of any new distinguishing material. No atomic fact provides positive factual content for the necessity analysis beyond what F002 and F003 supply.
- Positive obligation: No atomic facts correspond. This element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For this conclusion to be reached under the doctrinal structure of Article 11, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), (ii) that the state interfered with that participation (interference), and (iii) that the interference was not justified (necessity). All three of these steps are grounded, at the atomic fact level, exclusively in F002 and F003. These two facts — that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies against whom disproportionate measures were taken — are the irreducible factual core. F001 is procedural and does not add substantive content. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
