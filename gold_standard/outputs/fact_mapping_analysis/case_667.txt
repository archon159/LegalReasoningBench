Sub-element: basis in domestic law
Quote: "the applicant was sentenced to three-day administrative arrest under Articles 185 and 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences. The latter provision prescribed a penalty for breaches of the procedure for organising and holding demonstrations. Therefore, the interference had a basis in domestic law."
- F079: Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences provides that a breach of the procedure for organising and holding meetings, rallies, street marches and demonstrations shall be punishable...
- F080: Article 185-1 provides that the same actions committed within a year or by the organiser shall be punishable by... administrative detention of up to fifteen days.
- F048: The Galytskyy District Court sentenced the applicant to three days of administrative detention.

Sub-element: accessibility
Quote: "The Court has no reason to doubt that the Code was accessible."
- F079: Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences (existence of the Code implies accessibility; no specific atomic fact contradicts this).

Sub-element: foreseeability — inconsistency in domestic court practice
Quote: "The practice of the domestic courts also reveals inconsistencies in this sphere (see paragraph 33 above)."
- F113: The Information note of April 2012 noted that judicial practice contained instances of cases restricting the right to peaceful assembly being decided on the basis of the 1988 Decree, and that this approach was incorrect.
- F118: In a judgment of 29 November 2011, the Kyiv Administrative Court restricted the right to hold a demonstration on account of failure to notify ten days in advance, referring to the 1988 Decree.
- F121: On 16 May 2012 the Kyiv Administrative Court of Appeal quashed that judgment.
- F109: The Supreme Court noted that no legislation had been enacted in Ukraine establishing a mechanism for fulfilling the right to freedom of peaceful assembly.

Sub-element: foreseeability — Constitution requires further elaboration
Quote: "the Constitution of Ukraine provides for some general rules as to the possible restrictions on the freedom of assembly, but those rules require further elaboration in the domestic law"
- F074: Article 39 of the Constitution of Ukraine provides that citizens have the right to assemble peacefully... after notifying the executive authorities beforehand.
- F075: Article 39 provides that restrictions may be established by a court in accordance with the law.
- F076: Article 92 of the Constitution provides that human and citizens' rights and freedoms are determined exclusively by the laws of Ukraine.
- F108: The Constitutional Court held that specifying exact deadlines for timely notification is a matter for legislative regulation.

Sub-element: foreseeability — 1988 Decree not generally accepted
Quote: "The only existing document establishing such a procedure is the 1988 Decree, whose provisions are not generally accepted as the valid procedure for holding demonstrations"
- F113: The Information note noted that applying the 1988 Decree was incorrect.

- F109: The Supreme Court noted that no legislation had been enacted in Ukraine establishing a mechanism for fulfilling the right to freedom of peaceful assembly.

Sub-element: foreseeability — conflict between 1988 Decree and Ukrainian Constitution
Quote: "whilst the Ukrainian Constitution requires advance notification to the authorities of an intention to hold a demonstration and stipulates that any restriction thereon can be imposed only by a court, the 1988 Decree, drafted in accordance with the Constitution of the USSR of 1978, provides that persons wishing to hold a peaceful demonstration have to seek permission from the local administration which is also entitled to ban any such demonstration"
- F074: Article 39 of the Constitution of Ukraine provides for notification-based system.
- F075: Article 39 provides that restrictions may be established only by a court.
- F087: The 1988 Decree lays down the procedure for seeking and granting permission.
- F088: The 1988 Decree provides that an application shall be submitted to the executive committee of the appropriate local Soviet.
- F091: The 1988 Decree provides that the executive committee shall ban a meeting if the goal is contrary to the Constitution of the USSR or poses a threat to public order.
- F114: The Information note noted that the 1988 Decree establishes a permission/authorisation procedure and empowers authorities to ban events, whereas the Constitution provides for notification and empowers only courts to restrict.
- F122: The Kyiv Administrative Court of Appeal noted that the 1988 Decree conflicted with the Constitution as it required permission and authorised executive authorities to ban demonstrations.

Sub-element: foreseeability — original purpose of 1988 Decree
Quote: "From the preamble of the Decree it is clear that it had been intended for a very different purpose, namely for only certain categories of individuals to be provided by the administration with facilities to express their views in favour of a particular ideology, this in itself being incompatible with the very essence of the freedom of assembly guaranteed by the Ukrainian Constitution and the Convention."
- F087: The 1988 Decree lays down the procedure for seeking and granting permission to organise and hold meetings, rallies, street marches and demonstrations in the USSR.
- F091: The 1988 Decree provides that the executive committee shall ban a meeting if the goal is contrary to the Constitution of the USSR.
- F128: The Kyiv Administrative Court of Appeal stated that the 1988 Decree concerned the holding of such events in a non-existent country (the USSR), regulated relations between citizens of the USSR and executive committees of the Soviets of People's Deputies, and considered demonstrations on the basis of their compatibility with the Constitution of the USSR and the constitutions of the union and autonomous republics.

Sub-element: foreseeability — domestic court finding on "non-existent constitutions"
Quote: "As found by a domestic court (see paragraph 36 above), demonstrations under the 1988 Decree were considered on the basis of their compatibility with 'non-existent constitutions of non-existent subjects'."

Sub-element: foreseeability — insufficiency of local authority procedures

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the "prescribed by law" requirement, specifically foreseeability. The violation was found because: (1) the applicant was punished for organising a peaceful demonstration; (2) the legal basis for that punishment was Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences, which referred to a "procedure" that was not formulated with sufficient precision; (3) the only existing procedure — the 1988 Decree — conflicted with the Ukrainian Constitution and was not generally accepted as valid; (4) no parliamentary Act had been enacted for over twenty years despite a constitutional requirement to do so; and (5) domestic courts themselves were inconsistent about the applicable law.

The irreducible core facts are:
- F013: The applicant organised a peaceful demonstration on 12 October 2010. (Establishes the assembly and the applicant's role — necessary for scope and interference.)
- F048: The applicant was sentenced to three days of administrative detention. (Establishes the interference — the punishment.)
- F041: The Galytskyy District Court found the applicant guilty of breaching the procedure for organising and holding a demonstration. (Establishes that the punishment was connected to the assembly procedure.)
- F079: Article 185-1 of the Code on Administrative Offences — the legal basis for the punishment. (Establishes basis in domestic law and the provision whose foreseeability is at issue.)
- F074: Article 39 of the Constitution — notification-based system. (Establishes the constitutional standard and the conflict with the 1988 Decree.)
- F075: Article 39 — restrictions only by a court. (Establishes the conflict with the 1988 Decree's permission/ban system.)
- F076: Article 92 — rights determined exclusively by laws of Ukraine. (Establishes the constitutional requirement for a parliamentary Act.)
- F087: The 1988 Decree lays down a permission-based procedure. (Establishes the conflict with the Constitution.)
- F091: The 1988 Decree empowers the executive committee to ban demonstrations. (Establishes the conflict with the Constitution.)
- F093: The 1991 Resolution on temporary application of Soviet legislation. (Establishes the only legal bridge keeping the 1988 Decree in force — and its explicitly temporary character.)
- F109: The Supreme Court noted no legislation had been enacted establishing a mechanism for the right to freedom of peaceful assembly. (Establishes the legislative gap.)
- F113: The Information note noted that applying the 1988 Decree was incorrect. (Establishes inconsistency in domestic court practice.)
- F128: The Kyiv Administrative Court of Appeal stated the 1988 Decree concerned a non-existent country and considered demonstrations on the basis of compatibility with non-existent constitutions. (Establishes the domestic court finding on "non-existent constitutions" — the key quote used by the Court.)
- F116: The Babushkinsky District Court held that local authority procedures for freedom of assembly had no legal basis. (Establishes that local procedures could not fill the gap.)
