SCOPE (implied): The Court's finding of an Article 11 violation presupposes that the assembly fell within the scope of Article 11. The relevant atomic facts are: F001 (applicants attempted to participate in a public event in support of LGBTI people), F002 (the event was authorised to take place on 12 October 2013 in St Petersburg), F004 (the event was disrupted by violent counter-demonstrators — relevant to the peaceful character of the assembly from the participants' side), F005 (police arrested a group of participants), F006 (applicants were among those arrested).

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two distinct violations: (a) the failure of the State to protect the LGBTI assembly from violent counter-demonstrators (positive obligation), and (b) the arbitrary arrest of the assembly participants by the police (negative obligation). The irreducible facts are:

F001 — establishes the assembly and the applicants' participation (scope, foundation for everything)
F002 — the assembly was authorised (rules out any suggestion it was unlawful)
F004 — violent counter-demonstrators disrupted the event (the threat triggering the positive obligation)
F005 — police arrested participants rather than protecting them (the core failure on both positive and negative limbs)
F006 — applicants were among those arrested (personalises the violation to these applicants)
F009 and F010 — proceedings terminated for lack of any offence (establishes the arrest was unjustified, critical for the negative obligation violation)
F042 — police arrested only LGBTI participants and ignored counter-demonstrators' breaches (establishes the arbitrary and discriminatory character of the interference, critical for both the necessity finding and the Article 14 dimension)
