LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
- Assembly exists: The quote "public assemblies" and "the dispersal of these assemblies" maps to F002 and F003, which refer to the applicants as organisers and participants of public assemblies respectively. No single atomic fact covers both roles, so both F002 and F003 are relevant.
- Participation: The quote "organisers and/or participants of public assemblies" maps directly to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants).
- Peaceful character: No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact addresses this either.

(b) Interference
- The quote "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and/or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence" maps to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants). These are the only atomic facts that describe the applicants' relationship to the assemblies and the measures taken against them.

(e) Necessity
- The Court's necessity finding references "all the material submitted to it" and the prior leading cases, but does not cite specific facts from the case. The only atomic facts that could be said to underlie the necessity conclusion are again F002 and F003, as they establish that measures were taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies — the very conduct the Court found was not necessary in a democratic society.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are both necessary. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies (establishing that assemblies existed and that these applicants participated as organisers). F003 establishes that other applicants were participants. Together they are the minimum factual basis for the scope element. F001 is not strictly necessary as it merely refers to an appended table without adding substantive content. F004 is irrelevant to Article 11 analysis.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The interference (dispersal, arrest, conviction) is described in the reasoning text as measures taken against organisers (F002) and participants (F003). Without these facts, there is no basis for finding an interference with freedom of assembly.
- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary, as the necessity finding is grounded in the existence of measures taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. Without these facts, there is no interference to assess for necessity.
- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum: (1) that there were public assemblies (scope), (2) that the applicants participated in or organised them (scope/interference), and (3) that governmental measures were taken against them in that capacity (interference/necessity). All of this is captured by F002 and F003. F002 is essential because it establishes the organiser role and the measures taken against organisers. F003 is essential because it establishes the participant role and the measures taken against participants. Together, these two facts are the irreducible factual core. F001 and F004 are not necessary for the conclusion.
