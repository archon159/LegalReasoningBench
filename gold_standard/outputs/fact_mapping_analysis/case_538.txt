Scope (participation): The reasoning text states "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies." This maps directly to F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants).

The closest atomic facts are F002 and F003, which describe the "disproportionate measures" taken against the applicants.

The only atomic facts that could be said to underlie this element are those establishing the basic situation — F002, F003, F004 — since the Court's reasoning is that the present case resembles prior cases involving similar facts.

Scope (peaceful): No atomic facts are available. Not discussed.

Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — they establish that measures were taken against the applicants as organisers and participants. F004 is also necessary as it establishes the context (the protests) in relation to which the measures were taken.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

For the Court to conclude that Article 11 was violated, it needed to establish: (1) that there were assemblies (F004); (2) that the applicants participated in or organised them (F002, F003); and (3) that measures were taken against them in that capacity (F002, F003 — which describe the "disproportionate measures"). These are the facts that the entire doctrinal chain rests on. F004 is essential because without the existence of the protests, there is no assembly and no Article 11 issue. F002 and F003 are essential because they establish both the applicants' connection to the assemblies and the fact that state measures were directed at them. The necessity finding, while grounded in precedent, still requires these baseline facts to be established. F005 (the timing before Putin's inauguration) and F006 (other complaints) are not strictly necessary for the Article 11 conclusion and are therefore excluded.
