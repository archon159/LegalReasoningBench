LIST 1: Factual grounding quotes mapped to atomic facts

**Scope — Assembly exists**
The Court refers to "the demonstration of 31 July 2010" and "the demonstration of 10 March 2013" as framing facts. Atomic facts: F003 (demonstration planned 31 July 2010), F004 (demonstration planned 10 March 2013), F019 (police dispersed those gathered on 31 July 2010), F020 (police dispersed those gathered on 10 March 2013).

**Scope — Participation**
The Court states "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 31 July 2010" and "his arrest and conviction following his participation in the demonstration of 10 March 2013." Atomic facts: F017 (applicant attended 31 July 2010), F018 (applicant attended 10 March 2013), F021 (applicant arrested during dispersal on 31 July 2010), F022 (applicant arrested during dispersal on 10 March 2013).

**Scope — Peaceful**
The Court states "the demonstration which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner" (first case) and "unauthorised peaceful demonstration" (both cases). Atomic facts: F013 (applicant says demonstrations intended to be peaceful), F014 (applicant says demonstrations conducted in a peaceful manner). Note: the Court adopts these characterizations as established facts in the necessity analysis, not merely as party submissions.

**Interference**
The Court states: "The dispersal of the demonstrations and the applicant's arrests and convictions could not but have the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies" and "The measures applied in the present cases and the fear of the sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies undoubtedly had a chilling effect on the exercise of freedom of assembly." Atomic facts: F019 (dispersal on 31 July 2010), F020 (dispersal on 10 March 2013), F021 (arrest on 31 July 2010), F022 (arrest on 10 March 2013), F048 (conviction on 31 July 2010), F049 (sentence of 7 days detention, first case), F050 (conviction on 11 March 2013), F051 (sentence of 7 days detention, second case).

**Prescribed by law — Foreseeability/safeguards (first case, concerns)**
The Court states "the existence of serious concerns about the foreseeability and precision of the legislation governing public assemblies, and about the possibility of public assemblies being abusively banned or dispersed" and "the doubts about the credibility of the formal ground, namely Article 310.1 of the CAO, relied on by the authorities to arrest and convict the participant of an unauthorised demonstration." Atomic facts: F065 (text of Article 310.1 CAO — deliberate failure to comply with lawful police order), F027 (administrative offence report citing Article 310.1 CAO), F048 (conviction under Article 310.1 CAO). The Court also notes "a failure by the authorities to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified": F005 (notice given to BCEA on 23 July 2010).

**Prescribed by law — Second case accepted as lawful**
The Court states: "the organisers did not submit proper prior notice to the BCEA of the demonstration of 10 March 2013"; "in accordance with Article 5 § IV of the Law on Freedom of Assembly no prior written notification was required for 'spontaneous assemblies'"; "the applicant failed to sufficiently substantiate his allegation that the demonstration in which he had participated had been spontaneous." Atomic facts: F006 (no prior notice given for 10 March 2013), F063 (Article 5 § IV — no notification required for spontaneous assemblies), F007 (information disseminated via Facebook or press — relevant to spontaneity claim).

**Legitimate aim**
The Court does not itself adopt any facts establishing a legitimate aim. The Government's submissions mention public safety and prevention of disorder/crime, but the Court does not endorse these. No atomic facts are adopted by the Court for this element.

**Necessity**
The Court relies on the following adopted facts:
- "a failure by the authorities to take into consideration the fact that the demonstration had been notified": F005.
- "a lack of relevant and sufficient reasons justifying the dispersal of the demonstration which had been intended to be peaceful and had been conducted in a peaceful manner": F013, F014, F019, F020.
- "a lack of any acknowledgment that the act of participating in an unauthorised peaceful demonstration was by itself protected by Article 11": F017, F018, F048, F050.
- "The dispersal of the demonstrations and the applicant's arrests and convictions could not but have the effect of discouraging him from participating in political rallies": F019, F020, F021, F022, F048, F049, F050, F051.
- "The measures applied in the present cases and the fear of the sanctions that could potentially be applied against participants and organisers of unauthorised peaceful assemblies undoubtedly had a chilling effect": F049, F051, F073, F075, F079, F084, F085, F089.
- "The chilling effect deters other opposition supporters and the public at large from attending demonstrations": F080, F081, F083, F085, F086, F089.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court must establish that there were assemblies, that the applicant participated, and that they were peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are: F003 and F004 (assemblies existed), F017 and F018 (applicant participated), F013 and F014 (peaceful character, adopted by the Court in its necessity analysis).

**Interference**
The Court must establish that state acts interfered with the applicant's freedom of assembly. The strictly necessary facts are: F019 and F020 (dispersal), F021 and F022 (arrest), F048 and F050 (conviction), F049 and F051 (sentence of administrative detention).

**Prescribed by law**
For the first case, the Court notes concerns about foreseeability and the credibility of the legal basis (Article 310.1 CAO), and the failure to consider that the demonstration was notified. Strictly necessary: F005 (notification given — relevant to the credibility of the Article 310.1 charge), F027 (Article 310.1 used as formal ground), F065 (text of Article 310.1). For the second case, the Court accepts lawfulness based on: F006 (no prior notice), F063 (spontaneous assemblies exempt). F007 is relevant but not strictly necessary — the Court's conclusion rests on the applicant's failure to substantiate spontaneity, not on the mode of dissemination per se.

**Legitimate aim**
No atomic facts strictly necessary — the Court does not adopt any facts for this element.

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity analysis turns on: the peaceful nature of the demonstrations (F013, F014); the fact that the first demonstration was notified (F005); the dispersal without relevant and sufficient reasons (F019, F020); the arrests and convictions (F021, F022, F048, F050); the severity of sanctions (F049, F051); and the chilling effect, supported by the legislative context of increased penalties (F073, F075, F079) and international concern (F085, F089). The most strictly necessary facts are: F005, F013, F014, F019, F020, F048, F049, F050, F051, F073, F075, F079, F085, F089.

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: (1) there were assemblies at which the applicant participated; (2) the assemblies were peaceful; (3) the first was notified to the authorities; (4) the authorities dispersed both assemblies and arrested and convicted the applicant without relevant and sufficient reasons; (5) the sanctions imposed had a chilling effect, reinforced by the legislative context of dramatically increased penalties and international concern about Azerbaijan's blanket ban on protests in Baku city centre. The irreducible core is: F003, F004, F005, F006, F013, F014, F017, F018, F019, F020, F021, F022, F048, F049, F050, F051, F063, F073, F075, F079, F085, F089.
