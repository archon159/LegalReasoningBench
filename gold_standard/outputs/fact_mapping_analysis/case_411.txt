LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

**Scope**
The Court needed to establish: (i) that there was some form of assembly or solo demonstration; (ii) that the applicants participated; (iii) that the conduct was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F013: all three applicants decided to hold solo demonstrations — establishes participation and the existence of the event
- F014: they positioned themselves holding banners in the vicinity of the State Duma — establishes the physical act of assembly/demonstration
- F015: they were positioned at some distance from other protesters — establishes the solo/group ambiguity central to the Court's analysis
- F036: domestic court found participation in a picket involving fifty people — establishes the domestic classification of the event as a group assembly
- F018: no orders from police to stop before arrest (first applicant) — supports peaceful character
- F019: second and third applicants left the area and showed posters to journalists — supports peaceful character

**Interference**
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the three acts of interference agreed by the parties:
- F016/F028: arrest of the applicants (F016 for first applicant, F028 for all three)
- F021/F028: escorting to police station
- F034, F039, F040: administrative convictions
- F035, F041, F042: fines imposed
Most critical/representative: F028 (arrest and escorting for all three), F034, F039, F040 (convictions)

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's engagement with this element is limited to noting the reclassification rule and the absence of a distance requirement before January 2013:
- F061: since 6 January 2013 Moscow City Law provides a fifty-metre distance requirement — implies no such requirement existed before, which is the key legal fact the Court relied upon
- F057/F058: domestic law summaries (basis for the reclassification rule discussion)
Most critical: F061 (the absence of a prior distance requirement is the key factual-legal point)

**Legitimate aim**
The Court's finding is essentially negative — no public safety or disorder considerations were relied upon in the convictions. The strictly necessary fact is:
- F033: the administrative-offence record stated only that the applicants had taken part in a non-notified group public event and had refused to end it — this is the sole basis for the charge, with no reference to disorder or safety
- F036: conviction based solely on participation in non-notified picket
Most critical: F033, F036

**Necessity**
This is the central element. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F009: announcement of second reading only two days before — establishes impossibility of complying with three-day notification requirement
- F012: format of solo demonstrations chosen because there was no longer time to observe the minimum statutory three-day notification period — directly establishes the spontaneous assembly context
- F014: applicants positioned themselves holding banners — establishes the nature of the conduct
- F015: positioned at some distance from other protesters — establishes the solo demonstration character
- F033: charges based solely on non-notification, not on disorder — establishes absence of compelling public safety considerations
- F036: domestic court's assessment limited to non-notification — establishes failure to consider special circumstances
- F034, F039, F040: convictions — establish the consequence of the inflexible application of notification rules

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts — element not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two alternative lines of reasoning, both leading to the same conclusion. The irreducible core facts are:

1. The announcement of the parliamentary examination came only two days before the event, making it impossible to comply with the three-day notification requirement (F009, F012) — this is the foundation of the spontaneous assembly argument and the impossibility of prior notification.

2. The applicants positioned themselves peacefully with banners at a distance from other protesters (F014, F015) — this establishes the nature of the conduct and the solo/group ambiguity.

3. The domestic courts convicted the applicants solely on the basis of non-notification, without examining whether special circumstances justified derogation (F033, F036) — this establishes the automatic and inflexible application of the notification rules that the Court found unjustified.

4. The convictions and fines were imposed (F034, F039, F040) — these are the concrete interferences that needed to be justified.
