This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
The phrase "public assemblies" in both F002 and F003 also grounds the implied finding that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element of law

(a) Scope (implied): The Court's implied finding that there was a peaceful assembly in which the applicants participated rests critically on the characterisation of the applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies." Both F002 and F003 are necessary here because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants, and the Court's reasoning covers both. However, the most critical and representative facts are F002 and F003 together, since without establishing that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, Article 11 would not be engaged at all.
- Strictly necessary: F002, F003.

(b) Interference (explicit): The interference finding rests on the same factual basis — the arrest and dispersal of assemblies and conviction for administrative offences. These acts are attributed to applicants in their capacity as organisers (F002) and participants (F003). Both are necessary to cover the full scope of the applicant group.
- Strictly necessary: F002, F003.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on: (i) the applicants being organisers or participants of public assemblies (engaging the scope of Article 11); (ii) governmental measures — arrest, dispersal, and administrative conviction — constituting an interference; and (iii) that interference not being necessary in a democratic society (by analogy with prior cases). The necessity finding is not grounded in any of the four atomic facts. The engagement of Article 11 and the existence of an interference — which are the factual prerequisites for the violation finding — rest on F002 and F003. F001 is background and F004 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. Therefore the irreducible factual core is F002 and F003.
