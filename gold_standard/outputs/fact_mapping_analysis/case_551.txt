LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (implied) — grounded in: "the applicant's conviction for participation in an unauthorised march"
- F008: On 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in a demonstration held in Yerevan. (establishes the assembly and participation)
- F009: The demonstration held in Yerevan on 20 February 2003 was apparently followed by a march. (establishes the march/assembly)
- F021: Judge A. found that on 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in an unauthorised march, thus violating the prescribed rules for organising and holding street marches and demonstrations. (establishes the conviction for participation in the march — the exact phrase the Court uses)
- F020: Judge A. sentenced the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO to ten days of administrative detention. (establishes the conviction)

(b) Interference (explicit) — grounded in: "the applicant's conviction for participation in an unauthorised march undoubtedly interfered with his right to freedom of peaceful assembly"
- F020: Judge A. sentenced the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO to ten days of administrative detention. (the conviction/sentence is the governmental act of interference)
- F021: Judge A. found that on 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in an unauthorised march. (the basis of the conviction)
- F035: The President of the Criminal and Military Court of Appeal changed the penalty to an administrative fine of AMD 1,000. (the conviction was maintained, albeit with reduced penalty — the interference persisted)

(c) Prescribed by law (explicit) — grounded in two sets of facts:

First: "there was no legal act applicable in Armenia which contained the 'prescribed rules' referred to in that provision. The USSR Law of 28 October 1988 was no longer applicable and a new law on assemblies and rallies was adopted only on 28 April 2004."
- F046: A summary of the relevant legislation invoked by the parties in connection with Article 180.1 of the CAO is contained in the judgment in the case of Mkrtchyan v. Armenia (no. 6562/03, §§ 20-28, 11 January 2007). (the Court draws on the Mkrtchyan findings about the legal framework, including the inapplicability of the USSR Law and the absence of a successor law)
- F020: Judge A. sentenced the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO. (establishes which provision was applied)
- F021: Judge A. found that on 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in an unauthorised march, thus violating the prescribed rules for organising and holding street marches and demonstrations. (establishes that the conviction relied on "prescribed rules" that the Court found had no applicable legal basis)

Second: "the interference in the present case similarly took place before the enactment of a new law on assemblies and rallies"
- F008: On 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in a demonstration held in Yerevan. (establishes the date of the relevant conduct — before 28 April 2004)
- F021: Judge A. found that on 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in an unauthorised march. (establishes the date of the act underlying the conviction)

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope (implied):
The minimum facts needed to establish that there was a peaceful assembly and that the applicant participated in it:
- F008: The applicant participated in a demonstration on 20 February 2003 in Yerevan.
- F009: The demonstration was apparently followed by a march.
- F021: The court found the applicant participated in an unauthorised march. (This is the Court's own adopted finding and the most direct basis for the implied scope conclusion.)

(b) Interference (explicit):
The minimum fact needed is the conviction itself, which is the governmental act the Court identifies as the interference:
- F020: Judge A. sentenced the applicant under Article 180.1 of the CAO to ten days of administrative detention.
- F021: Judge A. found that on 20 February 2003 the applicant participated in an unauthorised march. (provides the basis of the conviction)

(c) Prescribed by law (explicit):
The Court's reasoning turns on two points: (i) the provision applied (Article 180.1 CAO) referred to "prescribed rules" that had no applicable legal basis in Armenia at the time, and (ii) the interference occurred before a new law was enacted. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F020: The applicant was convicted under Article 180.1 of the CAO. (identifies the provision at issue)
- F021: The conviction was for violating "the prescribed rules for organising and holding street marches and demonstrations." (identifies the specific gap — the "prescribed rules" had no applicable legal source)
- F046: The relevant legislation is summarised in Mkrtchyan, which established that the USSR Law was no longer applicable and no successor law existed until 28 April 2004. (the Court explicitly relies on this)
- F008: The applicant's participation occurred on 20 February 2003 — before 28 April 2004. (establishes the temporal point that the interference predated the new law)

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts apply.

(e) Necessity: Not discussed. No atomic facts apply.

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts apply.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the "prescribed by law" requirement. The Court found a violation because the applicant was convicted under a provision that referred to rules with no applicable legal basis in Armenia at the time of the conduct. The irreducible facts are:
- F008: The applicant participated in a demonstration on 20 February 2003 — establishing that there was an assembly and participation (scope, implied), and fixing the date as before the new law.
- F020: The applicant was convicted under Article 180.1 of the CAO and sentenced to administrative detention — establishing both the interference and the legal provision applied.
- F021: The conviction was for participating in an unauthorised march in violation of "prescribed rules" — establishing the link to the legally deficient "prescribed rules" framework.
- F046: The relevant legislation is summarised in Mkrtchyan, which established that no applicable legal act containing the "prescribed rules" existed in Armenia at the material time — the core factual-legal basis for the "not prescribed by law" finding.
