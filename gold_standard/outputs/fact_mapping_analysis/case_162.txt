--- NECESSITY ---

On the prior notification ground:

Quote 1: "the Court notes that such notification was required only when a public assembly involved 100 or more participants. Furthermore, if an event involving fewer than a 100 participants spontaneously grew into a bigger event exceeding that number, similarly no notification was required."
Atomic facts: F080 (mass public events defined as 100 or more participants), F081 (non-mass public events defined as fewer than 100 participants), F082 (mass public events require prior written notification, except when non-mass spontaneously becomes mass), F083 (everyone has right to hold non-mass public events without notification)

Quote 2: "The Government alleged that there had been more than a 100 persons gathered in that area and that the assembly had not been spontaneous, but failed to support this allegation with any objective evidence. Moreover, the Government's allegation has no basis in the findings of the domestic court which, in fact, failed to carry out any examination of that question."
Atomic facts: F049 (applicant argued no evidence that more than 100 people had gathered), F050 (applicant argued law did not require prior notification if fewer than 100 people), F063 (police performing duties to prohibit demonstration for which no notification had been made — the domestic court's finding which is silent on the number question)

Quote 3: "the domestic court appears to have simply proceeded on the assumption that the protest, which the applicant had wanted to hold and incited others to join, required a prior notification, without clarifying the number of persons present or the circumstances which gave rise to the gathering in the first place, including the question of whether it could fall into the category of 'spontaneous' gatherings."
Atomic facts: F064 (Administrative Court found no legal basis for demonstration, referring to Section 10), F067 (Administrative Court held mass public events require written notification), F068 (Administrative Court held no notification had been submitted)

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish: (i) there was an assembly or attempted assembly; (ii) the applicant participated or tried to participate; (iii) it was peaceful.
- F029: applicant arrived at Theatre Square — establishes presence
- F030: applicant tried to enter Theatre Square to hold a protest — establishes attempted participation and nature of the event
- F032: applicant called on people to gather and hold a demonstration — establishes the assembly dimension
- F065: Administrative Court's findings (accepted by ECtHR) — the authoritative factual basis the Court relied upon
- F022, F023, F024, F025: events in Yerevan involving violence — used by contrast to establish absence of violence in Gyumri (peaceful character)

Most critical: F030, F032, F065 (assembly and participation); F022/F023 by contrast for peacefulness (but the Court reasons from absence, so these are the backdrop).

Strict necessity: F030 (attempted participation in protest), F032 (calling others to assemble), F065 (domestic court findings accepted by ECtHR as factual basis)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's freedom of assembly.
- F031: police refused entry to Theatre Square for purpose of preventing demonstrations — the core act of interference
- F033: police ordered applicant to leave and stop inciting — continuation of interference
- F035: applicant taken to police station — arrest as interference
- F062: applicant subjected to administrative fine — punitive measure as interference

All four are necessary as the Court identifies the interference as comprising prevention, arrest, and sanction.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
Not assessed by the Court. Empty.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
Not formally assessed by the Court. Empty (the Court records the domestic court's reasoning but makes no finding of its own).

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on two grounds: (1) the prior notification requirement and whether it applied; (2) the state of emergency in Yerevan and whether it justified a blanket ban in Gyumri.

For ground 1 (prior notification):
- F080: mass public events defined as 100 or more participants — the legal threshold
- F082: mass public events require prior notification; non-mass events do not — the legal rule
- F083: right to hold non-mass events without notification — the exemption
- F064/F067/F068: domestic court assumed notification was required without examining number of participants or spontaneity — the failure of reasoning the Court criticises

For ground 2 (state of emergency / public order):
- F026: state of emergency declared in Yerevan — the fact the domestic court relied upon
- F022, F023, F024, F025: violence, deaths, destruction in Yerevan — the events underlying the state of emergency
- F031: police cordoned off Theatre Square for purpose of preventing any demonstrations — the blanket nature of the ban
- F063: police performing duties to prohibit any demonstration for which no notification had been made — confirms blanket approach
- F070: Administrative Court found conduct of mass public event in Gyumri could endanger public order — the domestic court's conclusion the ECtHR finds unsupported

For the absence of violence in Gyumri (critical to the proportionality finding):
- No atomic fact records violence in Gyumri — the Court reasons from absence. F011 (Gyumri is second largest city) is contextual background.

For the applicant's non-violent conduct:
- F030: tried to enter to hold a protest
- F032: called on people to gather
No atomic fact records violent intent — again, reasoning from absence.

Strict necessity for necessity element: F080, F082, F083 (legal framework on notification threshold), F064 or F067/F068 (domestic court's assumption without examination), F026 (state of emergency in Yerevan), F022/F023 (violence in Yerevan as backdrop), F031 (blanket cordon of Theatre Square), F070 (domestic court's unsupported conclusion about Gyumri)

Not discussed. Empty.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis, specifically: (a) the domestic court failed to examine whether the notification requirement actually applied (i.e., whether there were 100+ participants or whether the gathering was spontaneous); and (b) the domestic court imposed a blanket ban on assemblies in Gyumri based solely on the state of emergency in Yerevan, without examining the specific situation in Gyumri or the applicant's conduct.

The irreducible facts are:

1. F030: The applicant tried to enter Theatre Square to hold a protest — establishes that Article 11 was engaged (attempted participation in assembly).
2. F031: Police refused entry to Theatre Square for the purpose of preventing any demonstrations — establishes both the interference and its blanket character.
3. F062: The applicant was subjected to an administrative fine — establishes the punitive dimension of the interference.
4. F080: Mass public events defined as 100 or more participants — the legal threshold that determines whether notification was required.
5. F082: Mass public events require prior notification; non-mass events and spontaneously grown events do not — the legal rule the domestic court failed to properly apply.
6. F083: Right to hold non-mass events without notification — the exemption the domestic court ignored.
7. F026: State of emergency declared in Yerevan — the sole basis on which the domestic court justified the ban in Gyumri.
8. F022: Clashes between protesters and law enforcement in Yerevan — the underlying events justifying the state of emergency, which the Court accepts as serious but finds inapplicable to Gyumri.
9. F063: Police performing duties at Theatre Square for purpose of prohibiting conduct of any demonstration for which no notification had been made — confirms the blanket, indiscriminate nature of the restriction.
10. F070: Administrative Court found conduct of mass public event in Gyumri could endanger public order and public health — the domestic court's conclusion that the ECtHR found unsupported by relevant and sufficient reasons.
