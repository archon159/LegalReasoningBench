LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court must establish at minimum: (1) that there were public assemblies, (2) that the applicants were organisers or participants, and (3) that the state took measures against them in that context. All three points are captured by F002 and F003. F002 (organisers) and F003 (participants) are the irreducible factual core — they establish both the assembly context and the state action against the applicants, which is the foundation for the entire Article 11 analysis. No other atomic facts from the provided list are strictly necessary for the conclusion, since F004 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11, and F001 merely identifies the applicants procedurally.
