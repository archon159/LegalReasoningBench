LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:**
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly, that the applicants participated, and that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F010: establishes the assembly (International Women's Day celebrations) and the applicants' participation
- F138: the acquittal by the domestic court, which the Court uses to establish that the demonstrators did not act unlawfully and did not engage in violence (peaceful character)
- F139: the domestic court's finding of no evidence of resistance, directly supporting the peaceful character of the assembly

**Interference:**
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' Article 11 rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F016: the arrests (dragging into police vehicles) — the primary act of interference
- F018: the hitting of the applicants — the ill-treatment component of the interference
- F019: Ms Ispir was not arrested — necessary to explain why no interference was found for her
- F099: Ms Ispir could not identify the officer — necessary to explain why her pepper spray allegation was unsubstantiated, supporting the no-interference finding for her

**Prescribed by law:**
- F137: the criminal proceedings were brought under the Meetings and Demonstration Marches Act (Law no. 2911), establishing the legal basis the Court relied upon

**Legitimate aim:**
- F040: demonstrations hindered traffic flow — the most concrete fact supporting the disorder concern that grounds the Government's legitimate aim claim, which the Court accepted without elaboration

**Necessity:**
The Court's necessity analysis turns on two axes: (1) the demonstrators were peaceful and not in breach of the law, and (2) they were nonetheless prosecuted. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F138: acquittal — the Court's primary factual anchor for finding the prosecution unjustified
- F139: no evidence of resistance — directly supports the finding that the group was peaceful and not dangerous
- F136: the indictment was filed — necessary to establish that prosecution occurred
- F016: the arrests — necessary to establish the police intervention that is found disproportionate

**Positive obligation:**
- No atomic facts applicable.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: (1) the applicants participated in a peaceful assembly; (2) police arrested and ill-treated them; (3) they were prosecuted for this participation; (4) the domestic court acquitted them and found no evidence of unlawful conduct or violence. These are the facts without which the Court could not have found a violation.

- F010: establishes the assembly and the applicants' participation — without this, there is no Article 11 situation at all
- F016: the arrests — the core act of interference by the state
- F018: the hitting of the applicants — the ill-treatment component of the interference, which together with the arrests constitutes the full interference
- F136: the indictment — establishes that prosecution occurred, which the Court found independently disproportionate
- F138: the acquittal — the Court's central factual anchor: the domestic court itself found the applicants had done nothing wrong, making the prosecution and arrests unjustifiable
- F139: no evidence of resistance — directly establishes the peaceful character of the assembly and undermines any justification for the police intervention
