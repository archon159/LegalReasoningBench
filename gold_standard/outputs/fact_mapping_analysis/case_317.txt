LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's finding that the assembly falls within Article 11 scope is grounded in: the existence of a planned peaceful demonstration (F027), its stated purpose (F028), and the applicants' undertaking to maintain public order (F032). The most critical facts are F027 (establishes there was a planned assembly) and F032 (establishes its peaceful character, corroborated by the absence of any suggestion of disorder).
-> F027, F028, F032

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The interference finding rests on the rejection of the application by the Municipal Council. The critical facts are the application itself and its rejection.
-> F027, F033

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's analysis here turns on the mismatch between what section 12(6) requires (convincing evidence of breach of sections 6 and 7 with serious consequences) and what the Municipality actually did (reject on grounds of the protest being "unfounded and unwelcome"). The critical facts are the legal standard (F071) and the actual ground of rejection (F034).
-> F071, F034

Not decided by the Court. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

--- NECESSITY ---
The necessity analysis turns on: (1) the inadequacy of the Municipality's stated reason (F034), (2) the legal standard that was not met (F071), (3) the absence of any public order concern (F028, F032), (4) the Supreme Court's own finding of no evidence of breach (F050), (5) the lateness of the court decisions relative to the planned demonstration date (F041, F051, F056), and (6) the exclusive authority of local authorities under the Assemblies Act (F069, F071). LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: there was a planned peaceful demonstration (F027) whose purpose was to protest Romanian policy on Moldovan students (F028), the organisers committed to maintaining public order (F032), the Municipal Council rejected the application (F033) on the sole ground that the protest was "unfounded and unwelcome" (F034), which was inconsistent with the legal standard requiring convincing evidence of breach of sections 6 and 7 with serious consequences (F071), there was no evidence of any intent to disrupt public order (confirmed even by the Supreme Court at F050), and the judicial decisions upholding the refusal came long after the planned date of the demonstration (F041, F051, F056), meaning the only relevant decision was the Municipality's, which failed the necessity test.

The irreducible core is: F027, F028, F032, F033, F034, F050, F051, F056, F071.

F041 is subsumed by the broader point about lateness already captured by F051 and F056 (which are even later), but F041 is the first court decision and establishes the pattern; however, since the Court's key point is that all judicial decisions came after the planned date, F051 and F056 are the most probative. F041 (21 February 2005) is also after 5 January 2005 and contributes to the same point, so it is retained as it is the first judicial decision and part of the Court's explicit reasoning about disregarding all court decisions. F069 establishes the exclusive authority of local authorities but this is largely captured by F071 which is the more specific and operative provision. F069 is therefore not strictly irreducible for the final conclusion and is dropped from List 3.

Final List 3: F027, F028, F032, F033, F034, F041, F050, F051, F056, F071.
