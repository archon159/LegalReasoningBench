LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists / participation): The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This maps directly to F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." F001 is also relevant in that it establishes who the applicants are, but it contains no substantive factual content beyond a reference to the appended table. F002 is the direct match.

Scope (peaceful): No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and none of the atomic facts address the peaceful character of the assemblies. No mapping is possible.

Interference: The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This is part of the same sentence as the scope grounding and is contained within F002, which refers to "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The specific details (arrest, dispersal, conviction for administrative offences) are not separately enumerated in the atomic facts — they appear to be subsumed within F002 as the nature of the "disproportionate measures" complained of. No other atomic fact corresponds more precisely.

Necessity: The factual grounding was the Court's reference to leading cases and its statement that it found no fact or argument to reach a different conclusion. This is purely a legal/procedural finding with no specific factual grounding in the atomic facts. F002 is the closest, as it describes the nature of the complaint that the Court assessed. No atomic fact directly grounds the necessity finding beyond the general description of the complaint.

Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation: Not discussed; no factual grounding identified; no mapping possible.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the only factual basis for the Court's implied findings on scope (assembly exists, participation). Without F002, there is no factual basis for the scope element at all. F001 is not strictly necessary as it merely points to a table.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it describes the measures (disproportionate measures against organisers or participants) that constitute the interference. It is the only atomic fact that speaks to what was done to the applicants.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed and no atomic facts bear on it.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be said to support the necessity analysis, as it describes the nature of the complaint (disproportionate measures) that the Court assessed against its prior case-law. However, the necessity finding is grounded almost entirely in the Court's reference to prior case-law rather than in any specific atomic fact. F002 is the minimal factual anchor.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary; this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court found a violation of Article 11 on the basis that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum: (i) that the applicants were participants or organisers of public assemblies (scope and interference), and (ii) that measures were taken against them (interference, which feeds into necessity). Both of these are captured in F002. F001 merely identifies who the applicants are by reference to a table and adds no substantive content. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact that is strictly necessary for the Court's final conclusion under Article 11. It establishes both that there were public assemblies with applicant participation and that measures were taken against them — the two indispensable factual premises for the violation finding.
