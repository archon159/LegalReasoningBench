LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was a planned peaceful assembly and that the applicant was its organiser. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F006: The applicant notified authorities of his intention to hold a peaceful assembly.
- F007: The assembly was planned for an indefinite period.
- F008: The assembly was planned for Shevchenko Park in Kharkiv city centre.

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act prohibited the assembly. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F013: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court prohibited the applicant's planned assembly.
- F010: There is no information as to whether the applicant actually attempted to hold the demonstration on 12 August 2012 — this is necessary because the Court explicitly grounds its interference finding on the irrelevance of whether the applicant attempted to hold the assembly.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's reasoning on this element rests on several distinct pillars:

(i) The absence of a parliamentary law on freedom of assembly in Ukraine, required by the Constitution:
- F030: Article 39 of the Constitution requires advance notification and court-imposed restrictions.
- F032: Article 92 of the Constitution requires that rights be determined exclusively by laws of Ukraine.
- F067: None of the draft laws on freedom of assembly was eventually passed.

(ii) The Temporary Regulations as the actual legal basis used, and their lack of constitutional/legal foundation:
- F048: Decision no. 543 introduced the procedure for organising peaceful gatherings in Kharkiv.
- F049: Decision no. 543 was adopted on the basis of section 38 and section 59 § 6 of the Local Self-Government Act.
- F045: Section 38(1)(b)(3) of the Local Self-Government Act gives delegated powers to city councils for resolving issues concerning the holding of mass events — but only 'in accordance with the law.'
- F014: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court noted that the matters were regulated by the Temporary Regulations.
- F015: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court noted that the Temporary Regulations were binding.

(iii) The domestic courts' failure to look beyond the Temporary Regulations:
- F016: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court noted that the procedure set out in the Temporary Regulations had been observed, limiting itself to procedural compliance.

(iv) The assumptions made by the courts rendering assembly practically impossible (safeguards sub-element):
- F022: The court noted there was 'no evidence to prove that the assembly was not contrary to the interests of the inhabitants of Kharkiv' — this is the key fact grounding the Court's concern about impossible evidentiary burdens and unrestrained discretion.
- F053: The Temporary Regulations prohibited mass gatherings in parks except one — relevant to the unrestrained discretion point.

Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on the 'prescribed by law' element. The Court found a violation because: (1) Ukraine had no parliamentary law on freedom of assembly as required by its own Constitution; (2) the restriction was based on a local municipal act (the Temporary Regulations) that lacked a proper legal basis; and (3) the courts applied that act without questioning its legality, and imposed an impossible evidentiary burden on the applicant. The irreducible facts are:

- F006: The applicant notified authorities of his intention to hold a peaceful assembly — establishes the assembly and the applicant's role as organiser (scope).
- F013: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court prohibited the applicant's planned assembly — establishes the interference.
- F010: No information as to whether the applicant actually attempted to hold the demonstration — establishes that the prohibition alone sufficed for interference.
- F030: Article 39 of the Constitution requires that restrictions be established by a court in accordance with the law — establishes the constitutional requirement for a parliamentary law.
- F032: Article 92 of the Constitution requires that rights be determined exclusively by laws of Ukraine — establishes that local regulations cannot substitute for parliamentary legislation.
- F067: None of the draft laws on freedom of assembly was eventually passed — establishes the continued legislative vacuum.
- F048: Decision no. 543 introduced the procedure for organising peaceful gatherings in Kharkiv — establishes that the Temporary Regulations were the actual legal basis used.
- F045: Section 38(1)(b)(3) of the Local Self-Government Act gives delegated powers only 'in accordance with the law' — establishes that the delegation did not extend to substantive rule-making on fundamental rights.
- F014: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court noted that the matters were regulated by the Temporary Regulations — establishes that the court relied on the Temporary Regulations.
- F015: The Kharkiv District Administrative Court noted that the Temporary Regulations were binding — establishes the court's uncritical acceptance of the Temporary Regulations.
- F022: The court noted there was 'no evidence to prove that the assembly was not contrary to the interests of the inhabitants of Kharkiv' — establishes the impossible evidentiary burden and unrestrained discretion, which is the most concrete illustration of the absence of adequate safeguards.
