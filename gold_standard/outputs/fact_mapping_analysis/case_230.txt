LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful)**

Quote: "The Court notes that the applicant was a member of a group of aliens without valid residence permits who decided to take collective action to draw attention to the difficulties they were having in obtaining a review of their immigration status in France."
-> F001 (applicant was a member of a group of aliens without residence permits), F003 (the group decided to take collective action to draw attention to difficulties in obtaining a review of their immigration status)

Quote: "Their campaign culminated on 28 June 1996 with a decision to occupy St Bernard's Church, in which the group took up residence for approximately two months."
-> F005 (the campaign culminated with the occupation of St Bernard's Church on 28 June 1996)

Quote: "Neither the priest nor the parish council of the church objected to their presence and the religious services and various ceremonies proceeded as planned and without incident."
-> No single atomic fact directly states that the priest and parish council did not object and that services proceeded without incident. F005 establishes the occupation. The closest atomic facts are F005 (occupation began) and implicitly F035 (evacuation occurred the morning after the order, meaning the occupation lasted until then), but no atomic fact directly captures the priest's non-objection or the undisturbed services. This factual grounding has no direct atomic fact counterpart — it appears to be a Court-found fact not separately itemised in the atomic facts provided.

Quote (from necessity section): "even though it was peaceful and did not in itself entail any disturbance of public order or prevent churchgoers from attending services"
-> Again, no atomic fact directly states this. The closest is the absence of any atomic fact asserting that services were disrupted. This is a Court inference rather than a separately itemised atomic fact.

**Interference**

Quote: Same facts as scope above (F001, F003, F005), plus the evacuation itself.

Quote: "the evacuation of the church amounted to an interference"
-> F013 (on 22 August 1996, the Paris Commissioner of Police signed an order for the total evacuation of St Bernard's Church), F035 (the morning following the signing of the evacuation order, the police carried out the evacuation)

**Prescribed by law**

Quote: "under the Law of 9 December 1905 it is the local authorities, not the parish priest, who are responsible for supervising religious ceremonies and they are empowered to act either at the priest's request or on their own initiative."
-> F088 (Section 25 of the Law of 9 December 1905: assemblies for worship remain under the supervision of the authorities in the interests of public order). Also relevant: F076 (Article L. 2512-13 of the General Code of the Territorial Authorities conferring powers on the Paris Commissioner of Police), F079 (Section I, Article 1 of the Ordinance of 12 Messidor, Year VIII). The Court's specific reference to the Law of 9 December 1905 maps most directly to F088 and F089.

**Legitimate aim**

Quote: "The Court notes that the evacuation was ordered to put an end to the continuing occupation of a place of worship by persons, including the applicant, who had broken French law."
-> F013 (evacuation order signed), F014 (evacuation order made on the ground that the occupation was unrelated to religious worship), F033 (activities at St Bernard's Church were totally unrelated to religious worship, which was the exclusive use to which the building could be put under the law of 9 December 1905), F005 (occupation of St Bernard's Church). The applicant's breach of French law maps to F049/F050 (refusal of leave to remain) and F051 (invited to leave French territory).

**Necessity**

Quote: "after two months the continued occupation of the church by illegal immigrants, including the applicant, had developed into a situation – described in a report drawn up by a bailiff on the instructions of the Commissioner of Police – in which the hunger-strikers' health had deteriorated and sanitary conditions become wholly inadequate."
-> F008 (ten men went on hunger strike), F015 (evacuation order made on the ground of marked deterioration in sanitary conditions), F025 (marked deterioration in already unsatisfactory sanitary conditions since 28 June 1996), F034 (situation represented a serious sanitary, health, peace, public-security and public-order risk)

Quote: "the methods used by the police in an intervention that came without warning and was indiscriminate, went beyond what it was reasonable to expect the authorities to do when curtailing the freedom of assembly."
-> F035 (police carried out the evacuation), F036 (police arrived at 6.30 a.m.), F039 (police entered at 7.56 a.m.), F040 (all occupants stopped and questioned), F041 (white occupants immediately released), F042 (dark-skinned occupants sent by coach to detention centre), F044 (more than a hundred released by courts due to police irregularities), F045 (police irregularities extended to making false reports)

Quote: "the police could quite lawfully intervene under domestic law without a request from the parish priest"
-> F088 (Law of 9 December 1905, Section 25: assemblies remain under supervision of authorities in interests of public order)

Quote: "the authorities' fear that the situation might deteriorate rapidly and could not be allowed to continue much longer was not unreasonable."
-> F008 (hunger strike), F021 (World Doctors made public alarming information on very serious consequences of hunger strike at expiration of medically critical period of forty days), F025 (deterioration in sanitary conditions), F026 (number of people grew considerably in last few days, leading to incidents), F034 (serious sanitary, health, peace, public-security and public-order risk)

Quote: "the symbolic and testimonial value of the applicant's and other immigrants' presence had been tolerated sufficiently long enough in the instant case for the interference not to appear, after such a lengthy period, unreasonable."
-> F005 (occupation began 28 June 1996), F013 (evacuation order signed 22 August 1996) — together these establish approximately two months of toleration. Also F035 (evacuation carried out 23 August 1996).

Quote: "The Court does not share the Government's view that the fact that the applicant was an illegal immigrant sufficed to justify a breach of her right to freedom of assembly, as that freedom had already been exercised for two months without intervention on the part of the authorities"
-> F001 (applicant was member of group without residence permits), F005 (occupation began 28 June 1996), F013 (evacuation order 22 August 1996)

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of legal reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needed to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) it was peaceful.
- F001: establishes the applicant's membership in the group (participation)
- F003: establishes the collective action character of the assembly (assembly exists)
- F005: establishes the specific assembly — the occupation of St Bernard's Church (assembly exists, participation)
The peaceful character is a Court inference from the absence of violence and the priest's non-objection; no single atomic fact directly captures this, but F005 is the foundation. No atomic fact directly states the priest's non-objection or undisturbed services, so scope_peaceful rests on a Court-found fact not reducible to a single listed atomic fact. The closest supporting fact is F005.

**Interference**
- F013: the Commissioner of Police signed the evacuation order — this is the governmental act constituting the interference
- F035: the police carried out the evacuation — this is the execution of the interference

**Prescribed by law**
- F088: Section 25 of the Law of 9 December 1905 — this is the specific legal provision the Court cited as the basis for the Commissioner's power to act without the priest's request
- F076: Article L. 2512-13 of the General Code of the Territorial Authorities — cited by the Government and accepted as part of the legal basis for the Commissioner's powers

**Legitimate aim**
- F013: the evacuation order — the act whose aim is being assessed
- F014: the stated ground of the order (occupation unrelated to religious worship) — directly supports the Court's finding that the order aimed to end an occupation in breach of French law
- F033: the activities were totally unrelated to religious worship, the exclusive permitted use under the Law of 9 December 1905 — supports the finding that French law was broken

**Necessity**
The Court's proportionality reasoning turned on several distinct factual points:
- F008: ten men on hunger strike — establishes the health dimension
- F025: marked deterioration in sanitary conditions — the specific condition cited in the bailiff's report that the Court relied upon
- F005 and F013 together: occupation from 28 June to 22 August 1996 — establishes the two-month duration of toleration
- F035: police carried out the evacuation without warning — supports the Court's finding that methods went beyond what was reasonable
- F044: more than a hundred occupants released by courts due to police irregularities — supports the finding that methods were indiscriminate
- F021: World Doctors' alarming information on consequences of hunger strike at forty-day mark — supports the finding that the authorities' fear of rapid deterioration was not unreasonable

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts — element not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality assessment. The Court found no violation because: (a) the assembly was peaceful and had been tolerated for two months; (b) after two months, health and sanitary conditions had deteriorated to a point that made some restriction necessary; (c) the methods used were regrettable but the overall interference was not disproportionate given the margin of appreciation and the duration of toleration. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary for this conclusion:

- F005: the occupation of St Bernard's Church began on 28 June 1996 — establishes the assembly and its start date
- F013: the evacuation order was signed on 22 August 1996 — establishes the governmental interference and the end of the two-month toleration period
- F025: marked deterioration in sanitary conditions since 28 June 1996, with sanitary equipment totally inadequate for long-term use — the specific condition the Court relied upon to find that restriction had become necessary
- F008: ten men went on hunger strike — the health dimension that made the situation urgent
- F021: World Doctors' alarming information on very serious consequences of hunger strike at the forty-day mark — supports the finding that the authorities' fear of rapid deterioration was not unreasonable
- F035: the police carried out the evacuation the morning after the order — establishes the execution of the interference (and its unannounced character)
- F044: more than a hundred occupants released by courts due to police irregularities — supports the finding that methods were indiscriminate and went beyond what was reasonable, which the Court acknowledged but did not treat as decisive
- F088: Section 25 of the Law of 9 December 1905 — the legal basis the Court cited for the Commissioner's power to act without the priest's request, necessary for the prescribed-by-law finding which is a prerequisite for the proportionality analysis
