LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful)**
The reasoning text states: "disproportionate measures taken against them as participants in a peaceful public assembly" and "the measures applied to the applicants as participants in a peaceful public assembly." The atomic facts pointing to these characterisations are:
- F005: The applicants participated in a public event in Moscow on 5 December 2011 — establishes that an assembly took place and that the applicants participated.
- F006: The applicants participated to protest against the elections — establishes the expressive/political character of the assembly.
- F008: The authorities had been duly notified about the assembly — supports the lawful and thus peaceful character of the initial assembly.
- F009: The circumstances of the 5 December 2011 event have already been examined in Navalnyy and Yashin — the Court's reference to that case as establishing scope maps directly onto this fact.
- F025: The subsequent assembly had no advance notification — relevant to the peaceful/lawful characterisation of the assembly the applicants were penalised for.

**Interference**
The reasoning text states: "namely their arrest followed by their conviction for an administrative offence." The atomic facts pointing to this are:
- F010: After the demonstration, the applicants were arrested.
- F019: All the applicants were convicted under Article 19.3 § 1 of the Code of Administrative Offences.
- F020: The applicants were sentenced to either administrative fines or administrative imprisonment.

**Prescribed by law**
Not discussed in the reasoning. No factual grounding was identified. However, F026 (the relevant domestic law is set out in Navalnyy and Yashin) is the only atomic fact that could relate to this element, but since the Court did not discuss it, no mapping is made.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified. No atomic facts map to this element.

**Necessity**
The reasoning text states: "Having examined all the material submitted to it, the Court has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion" and "the measures applied to the applicants as participants in a peaceful public assembly did not correspond to a pressing social need and were thus not necessary in a democratic society." The atomic facts pointing to this are:
- F005: Participation in a public event — the assembly is the context for the measures.
- F008: The authorities had been duly notified — relevant to whether suppression was justified.
- F010: The applicants were arrested — the measure whose necessity is assessed.
- F019: Conviction under Article 19.3 § 1 — the legal measure whose necessity is assessed.
- F020: Sentences of fines or imprisonment — the severity of the measure relevant to proportionality.
- F021: Domestic courts relied on administrative offence records — relevant to the basis for conviction.
- F022: Domestic courts relied on police officers' reports — relevant to the evidentiary basis and thus the justification for the measures.
- F023: Courts considered it unlawful to shout slogans — the specific conduct penalised, relevant to whether the pressing social need existed.
- F024: Courts considered it unlawful to refuse to stop participating — the specific conduct penalised.
- F025: The subsequent assembly had no advance notification — the legal basis for the unlawfulness finding by domestic courts.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No factual grounding identified.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court's finding that the gathering fell within the scope of Article 11 requires establishing: (i) an assembly existed, (ii) the applicants participated, (iii) it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F005: Establishes that an assembly took place and that the applicants participated in it — the irreducible core of scope.
- F008: The authorities were duly notified — critical to the peaceful/lawful character of the initial assembly and thus to scope.
- F009: The circumstances were already examined in Navalnyy and Yashin, where scope under Article 11 was established — the Court explicitly relies on this.

F006 and F025 are contextually relevant but not strictly necessary: F006 adds colour to the assembly's character but the existence and peaceful nature are already established by F005 and F008; F025 is relevant to the subsequent assembly but the Court's scope finding is anchored in the prior case.

**Interference**
The strictly necessary facts are:
- F010: The applicants were arrested — the primary act of interference.
- F019: The applicants were convicted under Article 19.3 § 1 — the legal formalisation of the interference.

F020 (sentences) adds detail about severity but the existence of interference is established by arrest and conviction alone.

**Prescribed by law**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity finding turns on whether the measures corresponded to a pressing social need. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F005: The applicants participated in a public assembly — the context that triggers Article 11 protection.
- F008: The authorities were duly notified — undermines any justification based on disorder or illegality of the initial assembly.
- F010: The applicants were arrested — the measure being assessed for necessity.
- F019: Conviction under Article 19.3 § 1 — the legal measure assessed.
- F025: The subsequent assembly lacked advance notification — the domestic courts' stated reason for finding the conduct unlawful, and thus the factual basis for the necessity assessment.

F021, F022, F023, F024 provide detail about the domestic proceedings but the core necessity assessment rests on the above five facts.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on whether the arrest and conviction of participants in a peaceful, notified public assembly — penalised for conduct during a subsequent spontaneous assembly — was necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible facts are:
- F005: The applicants participated in a public assembly on 5 December 2011 — without this, there is no Article 11 situation.
- F008: The authorities were duly notified about the assembly — this is critical because it undermines any justification for suppression based on lack of authorisation of the initial event.
- F010: The applicants were arrested — the core act of interference.
- F019: The applicants were convicted under Article 19.3 § 1 — the legal measure that constitutes the interference and whose necessity is assessed.
- F025: The subsequent assembly lacked advance notification — this is the domestic courts' stated legal basis for penalising the applicants, and the Court's necessity finding implicitly rejects this as a sufficient justification for the measures taken.
