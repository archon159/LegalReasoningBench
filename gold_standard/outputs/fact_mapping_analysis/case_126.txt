LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists / applicant participated)**
The Court's quote: "The Court notes that the applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Georgios church in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."
Atomic facts: F034 (applicant joined the demonstration on 19 July 1989), F040 (applicant and other women gathered in Ayios Georgios church), F047 (applicant and other women were forcefully dragged into TRNC-controlled area), F048 (applicant was arrested and taken by bus).

**Scope (peaceful — violent character adopted from Commission and UN SG)**
The Court's quote adopting Commission findings: "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."
Atomic facts: F114 (1,000 demonstrators forced their way into UN buffer zone), F115 (demonstrators broke through wire barrier and destroyed UNFICYP observation post), F116 (demonstrators broke through UNFICYP soldiers' line and entered former school complex).

The Court's quote from UN SG report: "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."
Atomic facts: F114, F115, F116.

**Interference**
Same quote as scope: "The Court notes that the applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Georgios church in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators, including the applicant, were arrested."
Atomic facts: F034, F040, F047, F048.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's quote: "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 29-30 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law."
Atomic facts: F132 (section 70 — unlawful assembly), F133 (section 70 — immaterial that original assembly was lawful), F134 (section 70 — riot), F135 (section 71 — participation in unlawful assembly is misdemeanour), F138 (section 14(1)(b) — arrest without warrant for offence punishable with imprisonment), F139 (section 14(1)(c) — arrest without warrant for obstructing police officer).

The Court's additional quote on TRNC legal basis: "the Court recalls its finding that when, as in the Foka case, an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law."
Atomic facts: F140 (section 9 of Law No. 5/72 — illegal entry into prohibited military area), F141 (subsection 12(1) — no person shall enter except through approved port), F142 (subsection 12(5) — offence and penalty for contravention).

**Legitimate aim**
The Court's quote adopting Commission findings on violent character: "the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."
Atomic facts: F114, F115, F116.

The Court's quote on buffer zone and TRNC territory: "the demonstrators had forced their way into the UN buffer zone. According to the 'TRNC' authorities, they also entered into 'TRNC' territory, thus committing offences punishable under the 'TRNC' laws."
Atomic facts: F114, F117 (Turkish-Cypriot police apprehended 111 persons), F118 (Ayios Kassianos school complex — Turkish forces claim it to be on their side of cease-fire line).

The Court's quote on absence of doubt about TRNC territory: "the Court notes that it does not have at its disposal any element capable of casting doubt upon the 'TRNC' authorities' finding that the area where the accused had entered was 'TRNC' territory."
Atomic facts: F118.

**Necessity**
The Court's quote on violent character (Commission findings): F114, F115, F116 (same as above).

The Court's quote from UN SG report (explicitly adopted): F114, F115, F116.

The Court's quote on nature of intervention: "the intervention of the Turkish and/or Turkish-Cypriot forces was not due to the political nature of the demonstration but was provoked by its violent character and by the violation of the 'TRNC' borders by some of the demonstrators."
Atomic facts: F114, F115, F116, F117, F118.

The Court's quote on TRNC territory finding: F118.

**Positive obligation**
No case-specific atomic facts are mapped. The Court invokes only general doctrine (Plattform Arzte, Oya Ataman). No atomic facts from the case correspond to this element.

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

**Scope**
For the assembly to exist and the applicant to have participated: F034 is the core fact (applicant joined the demonstration). F040 adds the specific location (Ayios Georgios church). For the peaceful/violent sub-element, the Court's implicit finding that the assembly remains within scope despite violence rests on the same facts used for necessity — but the key facts establishing the violent character (which the Court adopts but does not use to exclude from scope) are F114, F115, F116. However, since the Court does not exclude from scope, the strictly necessary facts for scope are F034 and F040.

**Interference**
The strictly necessary facts are those showing the demonstration was dispersed and the applicant was arrested: F034 (she was at the demonstration), F047 (she was dragged into TRNC-controlled area), F048 (she was arrested). F040 is supportive but F034, F047, F048 are the core.

**Prescribed by law**
The strictly necessary facts are the legal provisions identified by the Court: F132 and F135 (sections 70 and 71 of the Criminal Code — the primary basis cited), F138 (section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law). The TRNC-specific provisions (F140, F141, F142) are also necessary given the Court's reasoning about TRNC legal basis. However, the Court's primary citation is to sections 70, 71 and 14, so F132, F135, F138 are the most critical. F140 is necessary for the TRNC territorial dimension.

**Legitimate aim**
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the violent character of the demonstration and the territorial violation, which the Court uses to identify the aims of prevention of disorder, prevention of crime, national security and public safety: F114 (demonstrators forced into buffer zone — core fact), F115 (destruction of UNFICYP barrier — establishes violence), F116 (breach of UNFICYP line — establishes disorder), F118 (Turkish forces' claim to Ayios Kassianos area — establishes territorial dimension for national security/public safety).

**Necessity**
The strictly necessary facts are those the Court explicitly relies on to find the interference proportionate: F114 (forced entry into buffer zone), F115 (destruction of UNFICYP barrier), F116 (breach of UNFICYP line — serious threat to peace and public order), F118 (TRNC territorial claim — Court finds no reason to doubt it). These establish both the violent character and the territorial violation that together justify the wide margin of appreciation finding.

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts are strictly necessary — the Court does not apply this element to the specific facts of the case.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on two pivotal factual findings: (1) the violent character of the demonstration (demonstrators forced through UN lines, destroyed UNFICYP infrastructure, constituted a serious threat to public order), and (2) the demonstrators' entry into what the Court accepted as TRNC territory. These facts drove the Court's findings on legitimate aim and necessity, which were the contested elements. The existence of an assembly, the applicant's participation, the interference, and the legal basis were all either uncontested or straightforwardly established.

The irreducible core facts are:
- F034: establishes the applicant was at the demonstration (necessary for scope, interference, and the entire analysis)
- F048: establishes the applicant was arrested (necessary for interference)
- F114: demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone (core fact for both legitimate aim and necessity)
- F115: demonstrators broke through UNFICYP barrier and destroyed observation post (establishes violent character — critical for necessity)
- F116: demonstrators broke through UNFICYP soldiers' line (establishes serious threat to public order — critical for necessity)
- F118: Ayios Kassianos school complex claimed by Turkish forces as their territory (critical for the territorial violation finding, which the Court accepts, supporting national security/public safety aims and the proportionality conclusion)
- F132: section 70 Criminal Code (necessary for prescribed by law)
- F135: section 71 Criminal Code (necessary for prescribed by law)
- F138: section 14 Criminal Procedure Law (necessary for prescribed by law)
