LIST 1: Factual grounding mapped to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists):**
The Court's grounding quote: 'The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Yaroslav Belousov, cited above, §§ 168-71).'
Atomic facts: F006 (public demonstration held on 6 May 2012), F007 (demonstration was to protest against allegedly rigged elections), F008 (demonstration had been approved by Moscow city authorities), F003/F004 (background facts set out in Frumkin and Yaroslav Belousov).

**Scope (participation and peaceful):**
The Court's grounding quote: 'it does not transpire from the description of his offences that he was among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's initial peaceful character.'
Atomic facts: F025 (applicant arrived to take part in the march), F026 (applicant walked with other participants to Bolotnaya Square), F010 (march was peaceful and held without disruptions), F108 (applicant found guilty as charged — i.e. the description of offences is the conviction), F114 (court held applicant tried to liberate arrested persons and pushed police), F116 (court held applicant grabbed Police Officer T.'s hand), F117 (court held applicant built a line of metal barriers).

**Interference:**
The Court's grounding quote: 'the applicant's prosecution and criminal conviction for participation in mass disorder constituted an interference with the exercise of the freedom of assembly.'
Atomic facts: F035 (applicant arrested on suspicion of participation in mass disorder), F043 (charges brought under Article 212 § 2), F069 (charges additionally classified under Article 318 § 1), F108 (applicant found guilty as charged), F128 (applicant sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment), F134 (Moscow City Court upheld first-instance judgment).

**Prescribed by law:**
The Court's grounding quote: 'It finds that the applicant's pre-trial detention and prosecution were lawful' (by reference to Yaroslav Belousov).
Atomic facts: F135 (Article 212 § 1 Criminal Code), F136 (Article 212 § 2 Criminal Code), F138 (Article 318 § 1 Criminal Code), F036 (Basmannyy District Court ordered pre-trial detention), F043 (charges brought under Article 212 § 2), F069 (charges additionally classified under Article 318 § 1).

**Legitimate aim:**
The Court's grounding quote: 'pursued the legitimate aims of preventing disorder and crime and the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.'
Atomic facts: F018 (criminal proceedings opened for mass disorder), F019 (criminal proceedings opened for violence against police), F043 (charges under Article 212 § 2), F069 (charges under Article 318 § 1), F108 (applicant found guilty).

**Necessity:**
The Court's grounding is extensive. Mapping each passage:

(i) 'he was found guilty of two criminal offences... sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment... for building a line of metal barriers and pulling another protestor away from a police officer, which did not endanger the latter's life or health, while participating in an authorised public assembly.'
Atomic facts: F008 (assembly was authorised), F108 (found guilty), F115 (violence did not endanger life or health), F116 (grabbed Police Officer T.'s hand and pulled it off protester), F117 (built line of metal barriers), F128 (sentenced to three years and six months), F129 (sentence calculated on basis of three-year term under Art. 212 partly concurrent with one year three months under Art. 318).

(ii) 'the domestic courts did not establish the particular circumstances of the applicant's behaviour and dismissed his arguments about excessive use of force by the police as unfounded. It appears that no attempts were made to find and question the protester who was the alleged victim of ill-treatment by the police and whom the applicant tried to protect. The courts nevertheless found that the applicant had indeed grabbed the police officer's hand and pulled it off a protester.'
Atomic facts: F029 (applicant's account of police using excessive force), F031 (applicant observed protester being beaten), F032 (applicant grabbed protester and pulled him back), F116 (court held applicant grabbed Police Officer T.'s hand), F127 (court considered defendants' arguments about protecting someone as farfetched).

(iii) 'The main justification put forward by the Government for such a harsh sentence was the serious risk of civil unrest existing at the time... The gravity of the punishment under Article 212 of the Criminal Code was thus attributable to the general context of the applicant's assault on the policeman (already imputed under Article 318 of the Code), not to the harm he thus inflicted. Regard being had to the other acts imputed to the applicant under Article 212, it transpires that his staying at the site of the rally and building a line of metal barriers against the advancing police accounted for an additional two years and three months to his prison term.'
Atomic facts: F117 (built line of metal barriers), F128 (sentenced to three years and six months), F129 (sentence structure: three years under Art. 212 partly concurrent with one year three months under Art. 318), F115 (violence did not endanger life or health), F105 (Police Officer T. testified applicant caused him pain but no injuries).

(iv) 'the criminal file did not suggest any pre-existing intention by the applicant to participate in such acts.'
Atomic facts: F025 (applicant arrived to take part in the march), F026 (applicant walked with other participants), F118 (applicant pleaded not guilty and testified he decided to attend the public gathering), F034 (after events applicant continued to live at usual address and pursue studies — suggesting no pre-existing criminal plan).

(v) 'there is nothing in the case file to suggest that the applicant was among those responsible for the onset of clashes between the protesters and the police. As far as can be discerned from the indictment, he intervened in what he perceived to be police violence against another protester at the height of the clashes, when the police were already arresting protesters. His account of events, in so far as they concerned the arrest of another protester, was corroborated by the statements of Police Officer T.'
Atomic facts: F031 (applicant observed protester being beaten), F032 (applicant grabbed protester and pulled him back), F054 (Police Officer T. identified applicant during identification parade), F055 (Police Officer T. stated applicant grabbed T.'s hand and pulled it off the protestor), F103 (Police Officer T. examined as witness), F104 (Police Officer T. testified applicant impeded him from arresting a protester), F105 (Police Officer T. testified applicant caused him pain but no injuries).

(vi) 'there were no witnesses of the applicant handling the barriers and that he denied his involvement in that act, although he submitted that other protestors had indeed used them to protect themselves from the use of force by the police. The domestic courts did not examine to what extent the barriers could have been used, by the applicant or anyone else, to stay out of the scuffle.'
Atomic facts: F030 (applicant's account: protesters dragged metal barriers to protect themselves), F117 (court held applicant built line of metal barriers), F121 (applicant testified he saw participants bringing forward metal barriers to protect themselves), F122 (applicant testified he was standing close to the barriers).

(vii) 'The applicant's overall conduct therefore, although involving a certain degree of violence, was sporadic in nature and did not attain the degree of aggression...'
Atomic facts: F115 (violence did not endanger life or health), F105 (no injuries caused to Police Officer T.), F116 (single act of grabbing hand), F117 (building barriers).

(viii) 'the applicant's criminal conviction, and especially the severity of his sentence, must have had the effect of discouraging him and other opposition supporters, and the public at large, from attending demonstrations... The chilling effect of the sanction imposed on him was further amplified by the large-scale proceedings in the case, which attracted widespread media coverage.'
Atomic facts: F128 (sentenced to three years and six months), F074 (extension order concerned eleven defendants — indicating large-scale proceedings), F100 (criminal case against ten participants), F101 (ten defendants charged).

**Positive obligation:**
The Court explicitly declines to separately adjudicate this. The factual grounding for why it is folded in: 'the applicant in the present case was convicted of perpetrating acts of mass disorder, which were the acts which interrupted the assembly. Attribution of responsibility for those acts was therefore a central question in the determination of the applicant's criminal charges.'
Atomic facts: F011 (barriers narrowed entrance to meeting venue), F012 (barriers restricted space allocated for meeting), F013 (police cordon forced protestors to remain within barriers), F014 (numerous clashes between police and protestors), F015 (police ordered meeting to finish early), F016 (police began to disperse participants).

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

**Scope:**
For assembly exists: F006, F008 (the assembly took place and was authorised — these are the minimum facts needed to establish it falls within Art. 11 scope).
For participation: F025, F026 (applicant attended and marched — minimum to show he participated).
For peaceful: F010 (march was peaceful), F116, F117 (the specific acts attributed to the applicant — needed to assess whether he personally was among those responsible for initial aggression; the Court found he was not).

**Interference:**
F108 (conviction), F128 (sentence of imprisonment) — these are the core facts establishing that a criminal conviction and prison sentence constituted the interference.

**Prescribed by law:**
F136 (Article 212 § 2 — the provision under which the applicant was convicted), F138 (Article 318 § 1 — the second provision). These are the legal bases; the Court found them lawful by reference to Yaroslav Belousov without independent factual analysis, so no additional atomic facts are strictly necessary beyond the existence of the legal provisions.

**Legitimate aim:**
F018 (criminal proceedings for mass disorder), F019 (criminal proceedings for violence against police) — these establish the aims of preventing disorder/crime and protecting rights of others.

**Necessity:**
F008 (assembly was authorised — relevant to context of participation), F105 (Police Officer T. testified no injuries — establishes minor nature of violence), F115 (violence did not endanger life or health — establishes minor nature), F116 (grabbing Police Officer T.'s hand — the specific violent act), F117 (building line of metal barriers — the other act under Art. 212), F127 (court dismissed arguments about protecting someone as farfetched — establishes domestic courts' failure to examine circumstances), F128 (sentence of three years and six months), F129 (sentence structure showing three years under Art. 212 partly concurrent with one year three months under Art. 318 — critical for disproportionality analysis), F030/F121 (protesters used barriers to protect themselves — context for barrier act), F031, F032 (applicant's account of intervening to protect protester — corroborated by Police Officer T.), F055 (Police Officer T.'s account corroborating applicant's version of events).

**Positive obligation:**
F011, F013, F014, F016 (police actions at Bolotnaya Square — the factual basis for the positive obligation question that the Court folds into the overall analysis).

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Art. 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The Court found a violation because: (1) the assembly was authorised and the applicant participated peacefully until late in the events; (2) his specific acts were minor (no injuries, sporadic violence); (3) the domestic courts failed to examine the circumstances properly; (4) the sentence was grossly disproportionate, particularly the three-year term under Art. 212 on top of the Art. 318 sentence; (5) the chilling effect on future assemblies.

Irreducible facts: F006 (assembly took place), F008 (assembly was authorised), F108 (applicant convicted), F115 (violence did not endanger life or health), F105 (no injuries to Police Officer T.), F116 (specific act: grabbing hand), F117 (specific act: building barriers), F128 (sentence of three years and six months), F129 (sentence structure — three years under Art. 212 partly concurrent with one year three months under Art. 318), F127 (domestic courts dismissed applicant's arguments as farfetched without proper examination), F055 (Police Officer T.'s account corroborating applicant's version — showing minor and reactive nature of conduct).
