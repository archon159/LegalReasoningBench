LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: The Court's implied finding that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated rests strictly on F002 and F003, which establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies. Both are necessary because the Court's reasoning covers both categories.
- Interference: The finding that there was an interference rests on F002 and F003, which describe the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) taken against the applicants as organisers and participants. Both are necessary for the same reason.
- Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.
- Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.
- Necessity: The necessity finding is grounded in the general reference to "all the material submitted" and prior case-law. The only atomic facts that provide any factual anchor are F002 and F003, which establish the nature of the measures complained of. Without these, there would be no "interference" to assess for necessity. However, the necessity finding itself is not grounded in any specific facts beyond those already captured under scope and interference.
- Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on: (1) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants (scope, implied), and (2) the state measures taken against them (interference, explicit), which were found not necessary in a democratic society. The necessity finding itself is not grounded in specific atomic facts beyond the general framing — the Court relies on its prior case-law. The irreducible factual core is therefore:
- F002: establishes that applicants as organisers were subjected to disproportionate measures in the context of public assemblies — essential for both scope and interference.
- F003: establishes that applicants as participants were subjected to disproportionate measures in the context of public assemblies — essential for both scope and interference.
Without F002 and F003, there would be no assembly, no participation, and no interference to assess. These are the two facts the entire Article 11 analysis rests on. F001 and F004 are not necessary for the Article 11 conclusion: F001 merely references the appended table of applicants, and F004 concerns other complaints under different Convention provisions.
