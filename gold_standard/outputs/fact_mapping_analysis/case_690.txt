- F007: applicant distributed banners (establishes organisational role)
- F009: applicant suggested co-workers position themselves at distance (establishes both organisational role and the peaceful/dispersed nature of the event)
- F026: demonstrators positioned at considerable distance (establishes peaceful/non-disruptive character)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the concrete acts of state interference:

- F012: applicant was taken to police station
- F013: applicant was held for three hours
- F014: applicant was found guilty under Article 20.2 § 1 CAO

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The strictly necessary fact is the absence of any statutory distance requirement at the time of the events, which is the core of the foreseeability problem:
- F010: at the time of the events on 12 December 2009, there was no federal or regional statutory requirement concerning the minimum distance between simultaneous solo demonstrations
- F020: the justice of the peace classified the event as a group event based on circumstantial factors rather than any distance-based statutory criterion
- F026: demonstrators were at considerable distance, yet still prosecuted, illustrating the unpredictability of the law

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the absence of any genuine public safety or disorder concern:
- F010: no statutory distance requirement at time (no regulatory framework that could have been evaded)
- F026: demonstrators at considerable distance (no disorder or safety risk materialised)
- F014: prosecution was for failure to notify, not for any actual disorder

--- NECESSITY ---
The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the disproportionality of the prosecution:
- F010: no statutory distance requirement at time of events (regulatory gap undermines the pressing social need for prosecution)
- F014: applicant found guilty and punished
- F020: classified as group event despite dispersed nature

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on two interlocking findings: (1) the law was not sufficiently foreseeable at the time of the events (no distance requirement existed), and (2) the prosecution was not necessary in a democratic society because the demonstrators maintained a considerable distance and no public safety concerns arose, yet the domestic courts still classified the event as a group assembly and punished the applicant as organiser without sufficient reasons. The irreducible facts are:

- F010: no federal or regional statutory requirement concerning minimum distance existed at the time — this is the single most critical fact, underpinning both the prescribed-by-law and necessity findings
- F014: applicant was found guilty and prosecuted — establishes that a concrete, serious interference occurred
- F020: justice of the peace classified the event as a group event — establishes the legal characterisation that triggered the violation
- F026: demonstrators were positioned at a considerable distance from each other — establishes that the conduct did not correspond to the mischief the law was designed to address, undermining both legitimate aim and necessity
- F028: Town Court upheld the judgment — confirms the domestic courts failed to provide sufficient reasons, completing the necessity analysis
