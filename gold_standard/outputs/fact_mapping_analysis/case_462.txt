- F004: "The country-wide protests against corruption organised by Aleksey Navalnyy took place on 26 March 2017." — confirms the assemblies actually took place on a specific date.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: The Court's implied finding that there was an assembly, that the applicants participated, and that Article 11 applies requires at minimum:
- F002: establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (protests).
- F004: establishes that the assemblies actually took place on 26 March 2017, confirming the assembly existed.

F003 is contextual but not strictly necessary beyond what F002 and F004 already establish for the scope finding.

(b) Interference: The Court's explicit finding that there was an interference requires:
- F002: establishes the "disproportionate measures" (arrest, dispersal, conviction) that constitute the interference.

(c) Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(e) Necessity: The Court's finding that the interference was not necessary in a democratic society is grounded in the same factual core as interference and scope:
- F002: the measures taken against organisers or participants are the subject of the necessity assessment.
- F004: the specific occurrence of the protests on 26 March 2017 is part of the factual matrix the Court examined.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on two essential facts: (1) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated as organisers or participants, and (2) that the state took measures against them (arrest, dispersal, administrative conviction) that were found not to be necessary in a democratic society. The case did not turn on the identity of the organiser (F003) or the cross-reference to other proceedings (F005). The irreducible core is:

- F002: establishes both the existence of the assemblies and the nature of the state's interference — the two facts without which no Article 11 violation could be found.
- F004: establishes that the assemblies actually occurred on a specific date, which is strictly necessary to confirm that there was a real assembly to which Article 11 protection attached.

F003 is not strictly necessary for the violation conclusion — the identity of the organiser does not affect the legal analysis as conducted by the Court. F001, F005, and F006 are procedural or ancillary and do not bear on the violation finding.
