LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists, participation): The factual grounding quote was: "The applicants complained principally of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." This maps directly to F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." F001 is also relevant as background (it identifies who the applicants are), but the substantive factual grounding is F002. The peaceful nature of the assemblies had no factual grounding in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact addresses it either.

Interference: The factual grounding quote was: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This is part of the complaint as recited by the Court. It maps to F002, which is the atomic fact capturing the complaint about disproportionate measures against organisers or participants of public assemblies. No atomic fact specifically mentions arrests, dispersals, or administrative convictions — these details appear in the reasoning text but are not captured as separate atomic facts in the provided list.

Necessity: The Court's necessity finding was grounded not in specific case facts but in a reference to prior case-law and a general statement that no new facts or arguments had been presented. No atomic fact in the provided list captures this reasoning. F002 is the closest, as it identifies the subject matter of the complaint, but the necessity reasoning itself is not grounded in any of the three atomic facts beyond the general nature of the complaint.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope analysis (assembly exists, participation). F001 is background but not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that measures were taken against the applicants in connection with public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the interference finding.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be said to support the necessity analysis, as it identifies the nature of the complaint (disproportionate measures in connection with public assemblies). However, the Court's necessity reasoning is almost entirely delegated to prior case-law, so the factual grounding is thin. F002 remains the closest available atomic fact.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core for this conclusion is:

F002: This is the single atomic fact that establishes (a) that there were public assemblies, (b) that the applicants were organisers or participants, and (c) that measures were taken against them — all of which are necessary predicates for the violation finding. Without F002, there is no assembly, no participation, no interference, and no basis for the necessity assessment. F001 merely identifies the applicants and is not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
