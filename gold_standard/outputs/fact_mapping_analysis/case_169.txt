LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts per element

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish: (i) assemblies took place on 12 and 26 January 2013; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) the assemblies were peaceful.
→ F003, F004 (assemblies were planned and took place)
→ F013, F014 (applicant attended/participated)
→ F009, F010 (peaceful character — the only factual basis in the record, and the Court adopts the characterisation without contradiction)

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's assembly rights. The critical facts are the dispersal, arrests, and convictions.
→ F015, F016 (dispersal of the demonstrations)
→ F017, F018 (arrests of the applicant)
→ F063, F064 (convictions of the applicant)

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's reasoning turns on: absence of prior notice; the spontaneous assembly exception; and why that exception did not apply.
→ F005, F006 (no prior notice given)
→ F076 (spontaneous assembly exception under Article 5 § IV)
→ F011 (the widespread nature of soldier deaths, used to undercut the spontaneity argument for 12 January)

**Legitimate aim**
Not explicitly discussed by the Court. No strictly necessary atomic facts.

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity reasoning rests on: the peaceful nature of the demonstrations; the fact that the authorities dispersed them and convicted the applicant without sufficient justification; and the chilling effect of the sanctions regime.
→ F009, F010 (peaceful demonstrations — core to the finding that participation was protected)
→ F015, F016 (dispersal without sufficient justification)
→ F063, F064 (convictions — the concrete measure whose proportionality is assessed)
→ F083 (the severity of the sanctions available under Article 298.2, relevant to chilling effect)
→ F092 (PACE Resolution on chilling effect of amendments in context of blanket ban — supports the Court's own chilling effect finding)

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No strictly necessary atomic facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: the applicant participated in peaceful demonstrations that were dispersed and he was arrested and convicted, and the authorities failed to justify these measures or acknowledge that peaceful participation in an unauthorised assembly is itself protected. The spontaneity exception was not established, so the prescribed-by-law hurdle was cleared by the state. The violation was found at the necessity stage.

The irreducible facts are:
→ F013, F014 (applicant participated in the demonstrations)
→ F009, F010 (demonstrations were peaceful — essential to the finding that participation was protected under Art. 11 and that the authorities failed to acknowledge this)
→ F015, F016 (police dispersed the demonstrations — the primary interference)
→ F017, F018 (applicant was arrested — part of the interference)
→ F063, F064 (applicant was convicted — the concrete sanction whose necessity was assessed and found unjustified)
→ F076 (spontaneous assembly exception — necessary to understand why the Court accepted lawfulness despite absence of notice, i.e. why the case proceeded to necessity rather than failing at prescribed-by-law)
→ F083 (severity of sanctions under Article 298.2 — necessary to ground the chilling effect finding that underpins the violation)
