**LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning**

**SCOPE**
The Court needed to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) it was peaceful.
- Assembly existence: F016, F017, F018 — these establish that there were large-scale protest rallies at Freedom Square. F019 adds the continuous encampment dimension.
- Applicant's participation: F021, F023, F024 — these establish the applicant's role as campaign head, his attendance, and his speeches. F022 is subsumed by F023 and F024.
- Peaceful character: F016, F031 (nine days of protests without violence), and the rejection of the arms build-up narrative: F032, F037, F093, F095, F097 — these are the facts the Court had to address and reject to confirm peacefulness.

**INTERFERENCE**
The Court needed to establish that the conviction constituted a punitive measure interfering with assembly rights.
- F086 (conviction for organising mass disorder), F087 (five-year sentence) — these are the core facts establishing the punitive measure. F088 (amnesty) is relevant context but not strictly necessary for the interference finding.
- F023, F024 — the conviction was for conduct that included participation and speeches at the assembly, making the link between the punitive measure and the exercise of assembly rights.

**PRESCRIBED BY LAW**
Not examined by the Court — no atomic facts strictly necessary.

**LEGITIMATE AIM**
Not examined by the Court — no atomic facts strictly necessary.

**NECESSITY**
The Court's necessity analysis turned on whether the domestic courts provided relevant and sufficient reasons. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F090 — the domestic court's finding that the applicant prepared the crowd for violence and disobedience (the Court found this too vague and unsubstantiated).
- F091, F092 — the only specific acts attributed to the applicant (spreading information, providing household items), which the Court found to be legitimate exercise of assembly rights.
- F093, F095, F097 — the arms build-up findings, which the Court had already rejected as unsubstantiated in prior cases.
- F103 — the finding that the applicant "arranged and managed" mass disorder, which the Court found too abstract.
- F105 — the finding about S.S. carrying out the applicant's orders, also found too vague.
- F052, F053, F056 — the spontaneous relocation and escalation of violence after dispersal from Freedom Square, supporting the Court's finding that violence was not organised by the applicant.
- F032, F034, F035 — the forcible dispersal of the assembly, supporting the finding that violence was a response to heavy-handed police action rather than a planned event.
- F114, F115 — PACE condemnation of the prosecutions as politically motivated, providing the broader context the Court found "extremely relevant."

**POSITIVE OBLIGATION**
Not discussed — no atomic facts strictly necessary.

**LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)**

The case turned on the necessity test — specifically, whether the domestic courts provided relevant and sufficient reasons to justify convicting the applicant for his role in a peaceful assembly. The irreducible facts are:

1. F023, F024 — The applicant participated in and gave speeches at the Freedom Square assembly. This is what his conviction was actually based on, and it is what the Court found to be legitimate exercise of assembly rights.
2. F086, F087 — The applicant was convicted and sentenced to five years' imprisonment. This is the interference that had to be justified.
3. F090 — The domestic court's central finding that the applicant prepared the crowd for violence and disobedience — the Court found this lacked concrete examples of incitement.
4. F091, F092 — The only specific acts attributed to the applicant by the domestic courts (spreading information, providing household items), which the Court found insufficient to constitute reprehensible acts.
5. F103 — The domestic court's finding that the applicant "arranged and managed" mass disorder, which the Court found too abstract and unsubstantiated.
6. F052, F053, F056 — The spontaneous and unorganised nature of the post-dispersal violence, undermining the claim that the applicant organised it.
7. F032, F034, F035 — The forcible dispersal of the peaceful assembly, which the Court found to be the likely trigger for subsequent violence rather than any plan by the applicant.
8. F114, F115 — The PACE findings that the prosecutions were politically motivated, which the Court found "extremely relevant" and warranting particularly close scrutiny.
