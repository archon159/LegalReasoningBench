F002 and F003 are the closest available matches.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are necessary to establish that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants. Both facts are needed because the Court addresses both categories of applicants.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — the "disproportionate measures" referred to in these facts are the factual basis for the interference finding (arrest, dispersal, conviction).
- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — the necessity assessment is grounded in the measures taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies, which is what these facts describe. Without these facts there is no factual basis for the necessity conclusion.
- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The case turned on the fact that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against people acting as organisers or participants of public assemblies. The irreducible factual core is therefore:
- F002: establishes that the applicants were organisers of public assemblies who suffered disproportionate measures — this is the essential factual premise for the entire Article 11 analysis.
- F003: establishes the same for participants.

Both F002 and F003 are essential because together they define who the applicants are, what role they played, and what was done to them — the three facts without which no Article 11 violation could be found. F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary for the violation conclusion itself.
