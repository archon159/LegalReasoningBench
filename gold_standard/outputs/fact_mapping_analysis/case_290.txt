LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court found a violation of Article 11 on the basis that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the doctrinal structure of Article 11, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that there were assemblies (scope), (ii) that the applicants were involved in them (scope/participation), and (iii) that governmental measures interfered with their freedom of assembly (interference). The necessity conclusion then follows from precedent.

The atomic facts that are strictly necessary for this conclusion are F002 and F003. F002 establishes that the applicants were organisers of public assemblies subject to disproportionate measures, and F003 establishes the same for participants. Together they supply the indispensable factual foundation: assemblies existed, the applicants were connected to them, and governmental measures were taken against them. Without these two facts, the Court could not have found any interference with freedom of assembly, and the violation conclusion would have no factual basis.

F001 is a procedural/administrative fact (identifying the applicants and applications) and is not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion. F004 concerns other complaints under the Convention and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
