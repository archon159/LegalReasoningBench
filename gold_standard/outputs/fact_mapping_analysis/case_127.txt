LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope (assembly exists / participation):
The Court's factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and/or participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offences."
This maps to:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "organisers" part of the quote.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the "participants" part of the quote.
Note: The specific acts (dispersal, arrest, conviction) are not separately atomised in the provided facts beyond F002 and F003.

Scope (peaceful character):
No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact addresses this. No mapping possible.

Interference:

This maps to:

Necessity:

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: The Court's reasoning on scope (implied) rests on the existence of public assemblies and the applicants' roles as organisers or participants. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003 — together they establish that there were public assemblies and that the applicants participated in or organised them. Both are needed because the applicant group is mixed (some organisers, some participants).

Interference: The Court's finding of interference rests on the same factual basis — the disproportionate measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) taken against organisers and participants. F002 and F003 are strictly necessary.

Necessity: The Court's necessity finding is grounded almost entirely in precedent, but the factual hook that connects the instant cases to those precedents is the nature of the complaints — disproportionate measures against organisers and participants of public assemblies. F002 and F003 are the necessary atomic facts here as well.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.
Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.
Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the finding that governmental measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) were taken against applicants who organised or participated in public assemblies, and that these measures were not necessary in a democratic society. The Court reached this conclusion by analogy with leading cases involving similar issues.

The irreducible factual core is:
- F002: establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies subjected to disproportionate measures — necessary to ground the Article 11 claim and the interference finding.
- F003: establishes that some applicants were participants of public assemblies subjected to disproportionate measures — equally necessary for the same reasons.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 merely refers to an appended table of applicant details, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions, neither of which bears on the Article 11 conclusion. F002 and F003 are the only atomic facts that are load-bearing for the Court's Article 11 reasoning and conclusion.
