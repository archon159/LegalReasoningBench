--- SCOPE ---
The Court's reasoning on scope turns on: (a) the existence and nature of the gathering; (b) the obstructive but not inherently violent purpose; (c) the specific conduct attributed to the applicants versus other participants; (d) the online calls and slogans not evidencing violent intent; (e) the air mattresses and balaclavas not evidencing violent intent; (f) the applicants not being among those prosecuted for violence.

Strictly necessary facts:
- F001: establishes the protest was against a preannounced eviction (context for obstructive but potentially protected purpose)
- F013: eviction was lawful (relevant to "coercion" analysis)
- F019: gathering of ~150 people (establishes assembly existed)
- F014/F015: online call framed as political/social statement (Court found no violent intent in calls)
- F017: "protest will continue until Mobile Unit come to their senses" (key call cited by Government as evidence of violent intent, rejected by Court)
- F023: banners including "Van der Laan is going down" (Court found these were expressions of dissatisfaction, not calls for violence)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two pivotal issues: (1) whether the applicants' participation in the gathering was sufficiently peaceful to attract Article 11 protection (scope/applicability), and (2) whether the domestic courts — specifically the Supreme Court — conducted the required Convention-compliant analysis (necessity). The violation was found because the Supreme Court failed to apply the autonomous Convention concept of "peaceful assembly" to the individual applicants and instead stopped at the finding that the gathering as a whole was not a "demonstration" under domestic law.

Irreducible facts:
- F039: Six other persons (not the applicants) were arrested and prosecuted for concerted acts of violence. This is the single most critical fact: it establishes that the applicants were distinguished from the violent participants, underpinning the finding that they were peaceful.
- F036: The applicants were arrested for unlawful gathering/disturbing public order — not for violence. This confirms their different legal treatment and the basis of the interference.
- F102/F103: The applicants were convicted by the Court of Appeal for unlawful gathering and failure to comply with dispersal orders. This is the interference that needed justification.
- F104: Fines were imposed (confirming the punitive nature of the interference).
- F068: The Court of Appeal held the aim of the gathering was to seek confrontation and physically prevent the eviction. This is the domestic factual finding that drove the entire domestic legal analysis.
- F129/F130: The Supreme Court dismissed the appeals and upheld the Court of Appeal judgments.
- F134: The Supreme Court held that the applicants' Article 11 argument did not necessitate a different conclusion — i.e., it effectively treated Article 11 as inapplicable without examining individual peaceful conduct.
- F135: The Supreme Court cited only the "violent intentions" exclusion from Article 11 protection, without applying the autonomous Convention concept to the individual applicants' conduct. This is the procedural failure that grounded the necessity violation.
- F017: The online call stating the protest would continue "until the Mobile Unit come to their senses" — this was the key piece of evidence the domestic courts relied on to characterise the gathering as non-peaceful/confrontational, and which the ECtHR assessed differently.
- F019: ~150 people gathered (establishes the assembly and its scale).
