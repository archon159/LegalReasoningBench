LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

**Scope:** The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly and that the applicant participated. The violent character is addressed at the justification stage rather than scope, but the Court implicitly accepts scope by proceeding to interference. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F041: The applicant joined the demonstration in the Ayios Kassianos area on 19 July 1989. (Establishes both assembly and participation.)
- F129: Some 1,000 Greek Cypriot demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone — establishes the assembly existed and its character.
- F130: Demonstrators broke through a wire barrier and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post — establishes violent character (relied on by Court for scope/justification boundary).
- F131: Demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers — same point.

**Interference:** The strictly necessary facts are:
- F041: The applicant joined the demonstration.
- F132: Turkish-Cypriot police and security forces forced their way into the area and apprehended 111 persons (the dispersal and arrest).

**Prescribed by law:** The strictly necessary facts are the legal provisions identified by the Court:
- F160: Section 70 Cypriot Criminal Code (unlawful assembly).
- F163: Section 71 Cypriot Criminal Code (participation in unlawful assembly).
- F166: Section 14(1)(b) Criminal Procedure Law (arrest without warrant).
- F146: Charges under 'TRNC' law — establishes that 'TRNC' laws were applied, triggering the Foka principle.

**Legitimate aim:** The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the violent character of the demonstration and the border violation:
- F129: Demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone.
- F130: Demonstrators broke through a wire barrier and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post.
- F131: Demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers.
- F151: Prosecution witnesses declared the accused had illegally crossed the 'TRNC' border (accepted by Court as not doubted).

**Necessity:** The strictly necessary facts are those establishing proportionality — the violent character and border violation that justified the intervention:
- F129: Demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone.
- F130: Demonstrators broke through a wire barrier and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post.
- F131: Demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers.
- F151: Prosecution witnesses declared the accused had illegally crossed the 'TRNC' border.

**Positive obligation:** No atomic facts correspond.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on two pivotal factual findings: (1) the violent character of the demonstration (breach of UN lines, destruction of UNFICYP property), which justified the interference as necessary for prevention of disorder and protection of public safety; and (2) the violation of 'TRNC' borders by the demonstrators, which the Court accepted as established. These two findings together drove the conclusion that the interference was not disproportionate. The applicant's participation is also strictly necessary to establish that Article 11 was engaged at all.

- F041: The applicant joined the demonstration — necessary to establish his participation and thus that Article 11 was engaged.
- F129: Demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone — the central factual finding on violent character.
- F130: Demonstrators broke through a wire barrier and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post — corroborates violent character.
- F131: Demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers — corroborates violent character and border breach.
- F151: Prosecution witnesses declared the accused had illegally crossed the 'TRNC' border — the factual basis for the border violation finding, accepted by the Court as not doubted.
