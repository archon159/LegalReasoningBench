LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's finding that the gatherings were peaceful (and thus within the scope of Article 11) rests critically on:
- F082: No signs of violence in the video — this is the primary direct evidence the Court accepted.
- F074/F075: The domestic courts, in proceedings that became final on 15 March 2002, never found the gatherings to have been violent — this is the Court's second ground.
- F076: The Government itself submitted the video, making the finding based on the Government's own evidence.

Most critical: F082, F076, F074

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The only strictly necessary fact is the imposition of the ban itself:
- F036: The Ministry of Justice imposed a one-month ban on the CDPP's activities on 18 January 2002.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court's concern about foreseeability rests on the discrepancy between the warning and the ban decision. Strictly necessary:
- F026: The warning of 14 January 2002 was issued under section 27 of the Parties Act.
- F027: The warning stated only that the CDPP had breached section 6 of the Assemblies Act by organising demonstrations on 9, 10, 11 and 13 January 2002.
- F036: The ban decision of 18 January 2002 was issued.
- F048: The ban decision relied on the new ground that the slogan could be interpreted as a call to public violence — a ground not mentioned in the warning.

Most critical: F026, F027, F036, F048

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
No atomic facts are strictly necessary — the Court declined to decide this element.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turned on several distinct factual pillars. Strictly necessary facts:

On the political context and narrowed margin of appreciation:
- F001: CDPP is a parliamentary political party.
- F002: CDPP was in opposition.
- F003: Government's initiative on compulsory Russian study — the subject of the protest.
- F005: The initiative generated a heated public debate.

On the first ground (lack of authorisation):
- F013/F014/F019/F021: The Municipal Council itself considered the legislation unclear and suspended its own decision pending parliamentary interpretation — making non-compliance with the authorisation requirement questionable as a basis for the ban.
- F122: The normal penalty for non-compliance was only an administrative fine of MDL 180-450 (EUR 16-40), making a full ban disproportionate.

On the second ground (children):
- F078: Participants included people of different ages including schoolchildren — establishing children were present.
- F077: Gatherings were held in a public place — supporting the Court's finding that anyone could attend.

On the third ground (calls to violence):
- F083: The Layabout's Anthem was played at the gatherings.
- F084: The lyrics of the chorus — the Court assessed these as a "fairly mild student song."
- F048: The Ministry of Justice's interpretation of the slogan as a call to public violence.

On the chilling effect and timing:
- F036: The ban was imposed on 18 January 2002.
- F054: The ban was lifted having regard to the forthcoming local elections — confirming the ban was imposed on the eve of elections.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
Not discussed; no atomic facts apply.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis — specifically whether the three grounds relied upon by the authorities were relevant and sufficient to justify banning an opposition parliamentary party's activities. The irreducible facts are:

1. F001 + F002: CDPP was a parliamentary opposition party — establishing the heightened scrutiny standard.
2. F003 + F005: The gatherings were about a matter of heated public debate (compulsory Russian study) — establishing the public interest in free expression.
3. F082: The gatherings were peaceful (no violence shown in the Government's own video) — the foundational factual finding underpinning the entire necessity analysis.
4. F036: A one-month ban on the party's activities was imposed — the measure whose proportionality is assessed.
5. F013/F019/F021: The Municipal Council itself found the legislation unclear and suspended its own authorisation decision — undermining the first ground for the ban.
6. F122: The normal sanction for non-compliance with the Assemblies Act was a fine of MDL 180-450 — making the ban disproportionate relative to the available sanction.
7. F078 + F077: Children were present at a public gathering open to all — undermining the second ground.
8. F083 + F084: The song played was a student anthem with the impugned lyric — the Court assessed it as a mild song, not a call to violence, undermining the third ground.
9. F054: The ban was imposed on the eve of local elections — establishing the chilling effect on political participation.
