Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It describes the "disproportionate measures" taken against the applicants, which the Court treats as the interference. No other atomic fact provides this.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element is not discussed.

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary, as this element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The only atomic fact that is strictly necessary to reach this conclusion is F002: it establishes (a) that there were public assemblies, (b) that the applicants were organisers or participants, and (c) that measures were taken against them — all of which are prerequisites for the violation finding. F001 merely identifies the applicants by reference to a table and adds no substantive content. F003 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, F002 is the sole irreducible atomic fact underpinning the Court's conclusion.
