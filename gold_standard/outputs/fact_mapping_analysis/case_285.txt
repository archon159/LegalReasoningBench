LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope — The reasoning text quotes "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence." The atomic facts that correspond to these quotes are F002 (applicants as organisers of public assemblies) and F003 (applicants as participants of public assemblies). F001 is a general reference to the appended table and does not correspond to any specific factual grounding quote. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant here.

(b) Interference — The reasoning text quotes "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence." This maps to F002 and F003, which describe the applicants as organisers and participants subject to disproportionate measures. There is no atomic fact that specifically describes the dispersal, arrest, or conviction as discrete events — these details are embedded in F002 and F003 only insofar as those facts reference "disproportionate measures."

(e) Necessity — The Court states it "has not found any fact or argument capable of persuading it to reach a different conclusion." This is not grounded in any specific atomic fact from the list provided. The necessity conclusion rests on the absence of distinguishing facts and on prior case-law, not on any positively stated fact in the atomic list.

(c) Prescribed by law, (d) Legitimate aim, (f) Positive obligation — Not discussed; no factual grounding; no atomic facts apply.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The Court must establish that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies before Article 11 can be engaged. These two facts together establish the assembly, the participation, and (implicitly) the peaceful character, since no contrary indication is given.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" referenced in both facts — dispersal, arrest, conviction — constitute the interference. Without these facts, there is no interference to assess.
- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts apply. This element is not discussed.
- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts apply. This element is not discussed.
- Necessity: No atomic facts from the provided list strictly support the necessity assessment. The Court's reasoning on necessity is grounded in prior case-law rather than in any of the four atomic facts. However, F002 and F003 are implicitly necessary as background (the measures must exist for necessity to be assessed), but they do not strictly ground the necessity conclusion itself.
- Positive obligation: No atomic facts apply. This element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For this conclusion to be reached, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (engaging Article 11), and (ii) that measures were taken against them (establishing interference). Both F002 and F003 are essential — F002 for the organisers and F003 for the participants. Without either, the Court could not have found a violation in respect of the full group of applicants. F001 and F004 are not essential to the violation conclusion itself.
