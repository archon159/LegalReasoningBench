LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish: (i) an assembly existed; (ii) the applicant tried to participate; (iii) it was peaceful.
- F004: Establishes both that the assembly existed (the March of Dissent) and that the applicant was travelling to participate in it. This is the single most critical fact for scope.
- F005: Establishes the nature and context of the assembly (organised to coincide with EU-Russia summit), supporting its characterisation as a political/opposition rally.
- F008: Establishes that the applicant took concrete steps toward participation (attempted check-in).
The peaceful character is not grounded in any specific atomic fact — the Court asserts it without factual citation.

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish a causal chain: state action → inability to fly → inability to attend rally.
- F026: Establishes that the ticket was officially confiscated (documented in the search record), making the seizure a concrete state act.
- F022: Establishes that the applicant was detained until 1.30 p.m., by which time the flight had departed, making attendance at the rally impossible.
- F039: Establishes the direct causal link — the applicant and others were prevented from taking the flight because their tickets were seized.
- F004: Establishes that the purpose of the flight was to attend the rally, completing the causal chain to the interference with assembly rights.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court's finding rests on its earlier Article 5 analysis. The critical facts are those showing the detention lacked a proper legal basis:
- F026: The ticket was confiscated under Articles 21.1, 27.7 and 27.10 of the Code of Administrative Offences — this is the legal basis invoked by the authorities.
- F106: Article 21.1 is plainly inapplicable (it concerns military draft information), undermining the legal basis claimed.
- F020 and F021: The applicant was physically prevented from leaving, establishing that a de facto detention occurred beyond what the cited legal provisions authorised.
- F095: The domestic court itself found no grounds for applying the administrative detention time-limit, implicitly confirming the absence of a proper legal basis for the detention.
- F096: The domestic court found no grounds for drawing up a detention report, further confirming the detention lacked formal legal grounding.

**Legitimate aim**
Not examined by the Court. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity**
Not examined by the Court. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Positive obligation**
Not discussed. No atomic facts.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three pivotal factual points: (1) the applicant was travelling to a political assembly; (2) state agents physically prevented him from boarding his flight by seizing his ticket and detaining him; (3) this detention had no adequate legal basis. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary:

- F004: Establishes the applicant was travelling to the March of Dissent — essential for scope (assembly, participation) and for the causal chain to interference.
- F026: Establishes the official confiscation of the ticket under specific Code of Administrative Offences provisions — essential for both interference (the concrete state act) and prescribed by law (the legal basis invoked and found deficient).
- F022: Establishes the duration of detention until 1.30 p.m. — essential for interference (the flight had departed, making attendance impossible).
- F039: Establishes the direct causal link between ticket seizure and prevention from boarding — essential for interference.
- F106: Establishes that Article 21.1 (one of the cited legal bases) is plainly inapplicable — essential for the "not prescribed by law" finding.
- F095: The domestic court found no grounds for applying the administrative detention time-limit — essential for confirming the absence of a lawful basis for the detention, supporting the "not prescribed by law" finding.
