**List 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning**

**Scope:** F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were participants of a public assembly, which is the minimum factual predicate for the scope of Article 11. F003 and F004 add descriptive content about the assembly (its subject matter and location/timing) but are not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion that an assembly existed and that the applicants participated. They are therefore excluded from List 2 for scope.

The specific measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) are described in the reasoning text itself rather than in the atomic facts, so F002 is the closest atomic fact.

**List 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)**

The case turned on whether the measures taken against the applicants as assembly participants — dispersal, arrest, and conviction — were necessary in a democratic society. The Court found they were not, by analogy with prior Russian cases involving similar facts. The only atomic fact that is strictly necessary for this conclusion is F002: it establishes both that the applicants were participants in a public assembly (triggering Article 11 protection) and that measures were taken against them (establishing interference), which is the factual predicate for the necessity assessment that led to the violation finding.

F003 and F004 provide context (the assembly was about fair elections, it took place in Moscow in August 2019) but the Court did not rely on these specifics in its reasoning — it resolved the case by reference to the similarity with prior cases, not by analyzing the particular subject matter or timing of this assembly. They are therefore not part of the irreducible core.

F001 and F005 are purely procedural and do not bear on the substantive conclusion.
