LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope — assembly exists and participation:**
Quote: 'both the applicant's presence at the construction site and his apprehension by the police were directly related to his participation in the protest against the construction works in the park'
Atomic facts: F004 (applicant participated in a protest against a construction project in a public park in Kyiv on 20 April 2013); F005 (a police officer approached the applicant about an hour after the protest began); F006 (after refusing to comply, he was handcuffed and brought to the local police station).

**Scope — peaceful:**
Quote: 'it was not alleged that the applicant had manifested any violent behaviour prior to his apprehension by the police'
Atomic facts: F008 is the police report allegation about grabbing clothing and throwing himself to the ground — but the Court explicitly rejects this as an allegation of violent behaviour attributed to the applicant, so it is the absence of such an allegation that matters. F016 (the applicant argued in his appeal that no violent behaviour had ever been attributed to him) is relevant as the factual backdrop the Court accepted. The Court's statement that it 'was not alleged' that the applicant manifested violent behaviour is best grounded in F016 (applicant's own appeal argument, implicitly accepted by the Court) and the absence of any finding of violence in F012/F014 (the domestic court relied on construction site guards' statements about a group, not the applicant personally). F008 is the counter-allegation the Court implicitly dismisses.

**Scope — absence of prior authorisation:**
Quote: 'the staging of a demonstration without prior authorisation where such authorisation is required under domestic law'
No atomic fact explicitly states that no notification was filed. The Government's submission (referenced in the reasoning) mentions 'no official notification had been sent to the authorities' but this is a party submission. The Court treats it as an established circumstance. The closest atomic fact is F004 (the protest took place) combined with the implicit acceptance of the Government's submission. No single atomic fact directly states the absence of notification, but F004 is the closest anchor.

**Interference:**
Quote: 'his forceful removal from the public assembly on 20 April 2013, his ensuing deprivation of liberty for several hours and his conviction for an administrative offence'
Atomic facts: F005 (police officer approached and ordered him into a police car), F006 (he was handcuffed and brought to the police station), F011 (released later on 20 April 2013 — establishing the deprivation of liberty lasted several hours), F012 (found guilty of administrative offence), F013 (fined).

Quote: 'both the applicant's presence at the construction site and his apprehension by the police were directly related to his participation in the protest'
Atomic facts: F004, F005, F006.

**Prescribed by law (lawfulness, implicitly raised):**
Quote: 'The domestic courts found the applicant guilty of non-compliance with a lawful police order without analysing whether the police had in fact given him any order prior to having forced him into their car, and, if so, what that order had been and whether it could be regarded lawful.'
Atomic facts: F005 (police officer ordered him into a police car, allegedly without explanations), F007 (police report stated the applicant maliciously disobeyed a lawful police order), F009 (applicant stated in the police report that he had not received any lawful orders), F012 (domestic court found him guilty as charged), F015 (applicant appealed arguing he had not received any lawful police orders), F018 (court of appeal rejected the appeal with similar reasoning — i.e., also without analysing the lawfulness of the order).

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope (assembly exists, participation, peaceful):**
F004 is strictly necessary — it establishes that there was a protest (assembly) and that the applicant participated in it. F016 is strictly necessary for the peaceful sub-element — it is the factual basis for the Court's acceptance that no violent behaviour was attributed to the applicant personally. F008 is relevant as the counter-allegation the Court implicitly rejects, but since the Court's reasoning turns on the absence of a valid allegation of violence, F016 (the applicant's own unchallenged assertion accepted by the Court) is the more critical fact.

**Interference:**
F006 is strictly necessary — it establishes the forceful removal (handcuffed, brought to police station). F012 is strictly necessary — it establishes the conviction for an administrative offence. F011 establishes the duration of deprivation of liberty (released later the same day, i.e., several hours), which is part of the interference as framed by the Court.

**Prescribed by law (lawfulness):**
F005 is strictly necessary — the police officer gave an order 'allegedly without explanations,' which is the factual basis for the question of whether a lawful order was given. F009 is strictly necessary — the applicant stated he received no lawful orders, which is the factual basis for the dispute about whether any order was given. F012 is strictly necessary — the domestic court convicted him without analysing whether the order was lawful. F015 is strictly necessary — the applicant raised the lawfulness issue on appeal, making it clear the domestic courts had the opportunity to address it and failed to do so.

**Legitimate aim:** No atomic facts — not discussed.

**Necessity:** No atomic facts — not discussed.

**Positive obligation:** No atomic facts — not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on two pivotal points: (1) the applicant was participating in a peaceful protest (bringing him within Article 11 protection), and (2) the domestic courts convicted him of disobeying a lawful police order without ever examining whether such an order was actually given or was lawful — which is the specific ground on which the violation was found.

The irreducible facts are:
- F004: establishes the assembly and the applicant's participation — necessary to bring the case within Article 11 scope.
- F016: establishes that no violent behaviour was attributed to the applicant — necessary to confirm the assembly was peaceful and the applicant retained Article 11 protection.
- F005: establishes that the police officer gave an order 'allegedly without explanations' — the factual seed of the lawfulness dispute.
- F009: establishes that the applicant denied receiving any lawful order — the factual basis for the dispute the domestic courts failed to resolve.
- F012: establishes that the domestic court convicted him without resolving that dispute — the direct basis for the violation finding.
- F015: establishes that the applicant raised the lawfulness issue before the appellate court, confirming the domestic courts had the opportunity to address it and failed — reinforcing the violation finding.
