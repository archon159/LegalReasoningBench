LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needs to establish that there was a planned assembly, that the applicant was its organiser, and that it was peaceful.
-> F004: applicant applied for permission to hold a public service — establishes the existence of a planned assembly and the applicant's role as organiser
-> F005: specific dates and location — confirms the assembly was a concrete planned event
(Peaceful character: no atomic fact directly establishes this; the Court infers it from absence of evidence. F004 and F005 provide background but are not strictly sufficient on their own for the peaceful sub-element.)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needs to establish that the applicant sought to hold an assembly and was refused.
-> F004: application for permission
-> F006: refusal by deputy head of Town Council

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needs to establish that a legal basis existed for the refusal.
-> F023: 1988 Decree was in force at material time
-> F026: Decree empowered authorities to ban assemblies threatening public order

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court needs to establish that the refusal was directed at preventing disorder.
-> F018: domestic court found that a service by one religious organisation may lead to discontent and public disorder — this is the stated justification that the Court accepts as the legitimate aim pursued
-> F026: legal basis for banning assemblies threatening public order — connects the legal framework to the aim

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis turns on several distinct factual points:
(1) The domestic courts' reasoning was based on the minority status of the religion and risk of disorder from opponents:
-> F016: domestic court's finding that Christ's Grace practices a religion different from the majority
-> F018: domestic court's finding that a service may lead to discontent and disorder
(2) The refusal was a blanket ban without consideration of alternatives:
-> F006: outright refusal
-> F007: Town Council had on many occasions refused permission for public services — establishing a blanket policy
(3) The conversion argument was not part of domestic reasoning:
-> F015: domestic court found refusal lawful on grounds of potential disorder (not conversion)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
The positive obligation discussion is not grounded in specific atomic facts beyond those already used for necessity. The most relevant are:
-> F006: outright refusal (from which the Court infers no consideration of protective measures)
-> F007: blanket policy of refusal (reinforcing that no case-by-case evaluation occurred)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: (1) the applicant sought to hold a peaceful public religious assembly and was refused; (2) the refusal was based on the minority status of his religion and speculative risk of disorder from opponents; (3) the authorities applied a blanket ban without considering less restrictive alternatives. The strictly necessary atomic facts are:

-> F004: applicant applied for permission — establishes the exercise of the right
-> F006: refusal — establishes the interference
-> F016: domestic court's finding that the church practices a minority religion — this is the core of the justification the Court rejects
-> F018: domestic court's finding that a service may cause discontent and disorder — this is the stated necessity justification the Court finds unconvincing
-> F007: Town Council had on many occasions refused permission without detailed reasons — establishes the blanket, disproportionate nature of the ban (the "most radical measure" finding)
-> F023: 1988 Decree in force — necessary to establish prescribed by law (without which the analysis would not proceed to necessity)
-> F026: Decree empowered banning of assemblies threatening public order — necessary to establish both prescribed by law and the legitimate aim of preventing disorder
