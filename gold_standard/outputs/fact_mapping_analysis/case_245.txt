LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope (assembly exists / participation)
The Court's factual grounding quotes were: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence" and "participants of public assemblies." These correspond to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies in Moscow"), which is the only atomic fact that speaks to the existence of assemblies and the applicants' participation in them. F001 is also relevant insofar as it identifies who the applicants are, but it contains no substantive factual content about the assemblies themselves.

(b) Scope (peaceful)
No factual grounding was identified in the reasoning text, and no atomic fact speaks to the peaceful character of the assemblies. No mapping possible.

(c) Interference
The Court's factual grounding quote was: "the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence." This maps to F002, which references "disproportionate measures" including dispersal and arrest. No other atomic fact speaks to the specific governmental acts constituting the interference.

(d) Prescribed by law
Not discussed. No mapping.

(e) Legitimate aim
Not discussed. No mapping.

(f) Necessity
The Court grounded its necessity conclusion in prior case-law and a general statement of having reviewed "all the material submitted to it." The only atomic fact that could correspond to the subject matter of that review is F002, which describes the nature of the complaints. F003 notes other complaints but is not relevant to the Article 11 necessity assessment.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element of law

(a) Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that there were public assemblies in Moscow and that the applicants were participants in them. Without this fact, the Court cannot establish that Article 11 is even engaged. F001 is background identification only and not strictly necessary to the legal reasoning.

(b) Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The reference to "disproportionate measures" including dispersal and arrest/conviction is the factual basis for the interference finding. No other atomic fact speaks to the governmental acts in question.

(c) Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(e) Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could ground the necessity assessment, as it describes the nature of the measures complained of. However, it must be noted that the Court's necessity conclusion is driven almost entirely by reference to prior case-law rather than by independent factual analysis of the present case. F002 is nonetheless the minimum factual anchor for the similarity-to-prior-cases reasoning.

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (ii) that governmental measures were taken against them in that context (interference), which then feeds into the necessity analysis. Both of these points rest exclusively on F002. F001 merely identifies the applicants and is not substantively necessary to the legal conclusion. F003 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact that is strictly necessary for the Court's final conclusion.
