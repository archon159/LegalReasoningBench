LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was a peaceful assembly and that the applicants participated in it. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F005: the first applicant planned a peaceful march
- F006: the march was to mark the International Day Against Homophobia (establishing the expressive/assembly character)
- F018: the second to fourteenth applicants gathered and participated in the march
The peaceful character of the assembly is established by the absence of violence from the marchers' side, which is implicit in the Court's framing and supported by F018 (they gathered peacefully) and F022-F025 (violence came from counter-demonstrators, not marchers). The most critical representative fact for peacefulness is F018 combined with F022 (the confrontation came from the other side).

Strictly necessary: F005, F006, F018, F022

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that the applicants' freedom of assembly was interfered with. The disruption came from counter-demonstrators combined with police inaction. Strictly necessary:
- F024: counter-demonstrators blocked the march (making it impossible to proceed — this is the core of the interference)
- F027, F028: police officers present refused to intervene (establishing the State dimension of the interference through omission)
- F032: additional police arrived only after physical violence had already occurred (establishing the delay that constituted the omission)

Strictly necessary: F024, F027, F028, F032

**Prescribed by law**
Not discussed. Empty.

**Legitimate aim**
Not discussed. Empty.

**Necessity**
Not discussed in traditional sense. Empty.

**Positive obligation**
The Court's reasoning on positive obligation turns on three sub-points: (i) advance notice and foreseeability of risk, (ii) failure to take preventive measures, (iii) insufficient police deployment. Strictly necessary facts:
- F007 or F008: prior notice was given nine days in advance (F007 and F008 establish the same point; F007 is sufficient as the most direct)
- F011: the first applicant specifically flagged the risk of violence, putting authorities on notice
- F023: a hundred or more aggressive counter-demonstrators arrived (establishing the scale of the foreseeable threat that materialised)
- F027: only a few police officers were present (establishing insufficiency of deployment)
- F028: those officers refused to intervene (establishing the quality of the response)
- F031: the sixth applicant was knocked down, beaten and kicked (establishing that the failure had concrete harmful consequences)
- F112: 84% of Georgian respondents expressed negative attitudes towards homosexuality (establishing that authorities knew or should have known of the risk from societal attitudes)

Strictly necessary: F007, F011, F023, F027, F028, F031, F112

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the positive obligation of the State to protect a peaceful LGBT assembly from foreseeable violence by counter-demonstrators. The Court found a violation because: (a) the authorities had ample advance notice and knew of the risk; (b) they failed to deploy sufficient forces or take preventive measures; (c) the march was disrupted and participants were physically harmed. The irreducible facts are:

- F007: nine days' advance notice was given to the Ministry of the Interior (establishing the opportunity to prepare)
- F011: the first applicant specifically requested protection from foreseeable violence (establishing that the risk was communicated to authorities)
- F023: a hundred or more aggressive counter-demonstrators confronted the marchers (establishing the scale of the threat that materialised and was foreseeable)
- F024: counter-demonstrators blocked the march entirely (establishing that the assembly was effectively prevented from proceeding)
- F027: only a few police officers were present at the scene (establishing the insufficiency of the State's response)
- F028: those officers refused to intervene when asked (establishing the quality — or lack thereof — of the State's response)
- F031: the sixth applicant was knocked down, beaten and kicked (establishing that the failure caused concrete harm and that the march was disrupted by violence)
- F112: 84% of Georgian respondents expressed negative attitudes towards homosexuality (establishing that the risk was objectively foreseeable from societal context, supporting the 'knew or should have known' finding)
