LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: The Court implies that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated in them. The strictly necessary atomic facts are F002 and F003, which establish that the applicants were organisers and participants of public assemblies. Both are needed because the applicant group includes both organisers and participants, and the Court's reasoning covers both.

(b) Interference: The Court implies interference from the same factual basis — the disproportionate measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction) taken against organisers and participants. F002 and F003 are again the strictly necessary facts, as they describe the measures that constitute the interference.

(c) Prescribed by law: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(d) Legitimate aim: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

(e) Necessity: The necessity finding is grounded in the same factual context as scope and interference — the measures taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. F002 and F003 are the strictly necessary facts. Without them, there would be no factual basis for the necessity assessment at all.

(f) Positive obligation: Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turns on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum: (i) that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies (establishing scope and the applicability of Article 11), and (ii) that disproportionate measures were taken against them (establishing interference and grounding the necessity assessment). Both F002 and F003 are essential — F002 for the organisers and F003 for the participants. These are the only atomic facts from the provided set that are strictly necessary for the violation conclusion. F001 (the appended table) and F004 (other complaints) are not essential to the Article 11 violation finding itself.
