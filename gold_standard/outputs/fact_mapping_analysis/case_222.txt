LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists / applicant participated / peaceful character)**

Quote: "The applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia."
Atomic facts: F022 (applicant joined the demonstration at Ayios Kassianos), F030 (when applicant arrived, grounds were filled with mostly young women who were singing), F033 (applicant noticed Turkish policemen armed with batons), F035 (Turkish policemen rushed towards demonstrators), F036 (some women were grabbed and hit), F037 (applicant was hit and pushed by Turkish policemen).

Quote (on violent character, used in necessity analysis): "The Court notes that in the case of Chrysostomos and Papachrysostomou, the Commission found that the demonstration on 19 July 1989 was violent, that it had broken through the UN defence lines and constituted a serious threat to peace and public order on the demarcation line in Cyprus."
Atomic facts: F081 (demonstrators broke through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed a UNFICYP observation post), F082 (demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers and entered a former school complex).

Quote (UN Secretary General's report, adopted by Court): "the demonstrators had 'forced their way into the UN buffer zone in the Ayios Kassianos area of Nicosia', that they had broken 'through a wire barrier maintained by UNFICYP and destroyed an UNFICYP observation post' before breaking 'through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers' and entering 'a former school complex'."
Atomic facts: F080 (some 1,000 Greek Cypriot demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone), F081 (demonstrators broke through a wire barrier and destroyed a UNFICYP observation post), F082 (demonstrators broke through the line formed by UNFICYP soldiers and entered a former school complex).

**Interference**

Quote: "The applicant and other women clashed with Turkish-Cypriot police while demonstrating in or in the vicinity of the Ayios Kassianos school in Nicosia. The demonstration was dispersed and some of the demonstrators were arrested."
Atomic facts: F022 (applicant joined the demonstration), F035 (Turkish policemen rushed towards demonstrators), F036 (some women were grabbed and hit), F037 (applicant was hit and pushed), F083 (Turkish-Cypriot police and security forces apprehended 111 persons).

**Prescribed by law**

Quote: "This interference had a legal basis, namely sections 70 and 71 of the Cypriot Criminal Code (see paragraphs 22-23 above) and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law."
Atomic facts: F093 (section 70 defines unlawful assembly), F094 (section 70 — immaterial that original assembly was lawful if conduct causes fear of breach of peace), F095 (section 70 — when unlawful assembly executes purpose by breach of peace, it is a riot), F096 (section 71 — participation in unlawful assembly is a misdemeanour), F099 (section 14(1)(b) — police may arrest without warrant any person committing in their presence an offence punishable with imprisonment), F100 (section 14(1)(c) — police may arrest without warrant any person obstructing a police officer).

Quote (on TRNC legal basis, from Foka): "the 'TRNC' was exercising de facto authority over northern Cyprus" and "when an act of the 'TRNC' authorities was in compliance with laws in force within the territory of northern Cyprus, it should in principle be regarded as having a legal basis in domestic law."
Atomic facts: No directly corresponding atomic fact from the facts section — this is a legal conclusion drawn from prior case law (Foka v. Turkey). The closest supporting facts are F093–F100 (the laws themselves) and F101–F103 (TRNC offence provisions), but the de facto authority point is a legal finding, not a factual one from the case file.

The Court does not independently establish facts for legitimate aim but relies on the same facts used in necessity. The relevant atomic facts are those establishing the violent and disruptive character of the demonstration:
Atomic facts: F080 (demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone), F081 (demonstrators broke through UNFICYP barrier and destroyed observation post), F082 (demonstrators broke through UNFICYP line and entered school complex), F083 (111 persons apprehended by Turkish-Cypriot police).

LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needs to establish that there was an assembly and that the applicant participated. The single most critical fact is F022 (the applicant joined the anti-Turkish demonstration at Ayios Kassianos). The violent character of the demonstration — relevant to whether it falls within Article 11's scope — is most critically established by F080 (demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone) and F081 (demonstrators broke through UNFICYP barrier and destroyed observation post). However, since the Court does not exclude the assembly from scope on grounds of violence, the key scope facts are simply F022 and F080.

Strictly necessary: F022, F080, F081, F082.

**Interference**
The Court needs to establish that the demonstration was dispersed and demonstrators were arrested. The most critical facts are F083 (111 persons apprehended) and F035 (Turkish policemen rushed towards demonstrators), with F022 establishing the applicant's presence.

Strictly necessary: F022, F035, F083.

**Prescribed by law**
The Court relies on sections 70 and 71 of the Criminal Code and section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Law. The most critical atomic facts are F093 (definition of unlawful assembly under section 70), F096 (section 71 — participation in unlawful assembly is a misdemeanour), and F099 (section 14(1)(b) — power of arrest without warrant). The TRNC border offence provisions (F101, F102) are also relevant to the legal basis for the intervention.

Strictly necessary: F093, F096, F099.

**Legitimate aim**
The Court implicitly accepts the aims of national security, public safety, prevention of disorder and crime based on the violent and disruptive character of the demonstration. The most critical fact is F080 (demonstrators forced their way into the UN buffer zone, establishing the disorder/security dimension) and F081 (destruction of UNFICYP infrastructure, establishing the public safety/disorder dimension).

Strictly necessary: F080, F081.

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity analysis turns on three key factual pillars: (1) the violent character of the demonstration (F080, F081, F082); (2) the entry into TRNC territory (F084); and (3) the Court's acceptance that it had no basis to doubt the TRNC's territorial claim (F084). The most critical and irreducible facts are F080, F081, F082, and F084.

Strictly necessary: F080, F081, F082, F084.

**Positive obligation**
No atomic facts from the case file are strictly necessary — the Court's reference to positive obligations is purely a statement of general principle with no case-specific factual grounding.

Strictly necessary: none.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (no violation of Article 11)

The case turned on the following: (1) the applicant participated in the demonstration (F022); (2) the demonstration was dispersed and demonstrators arrested, constituting an interference (F035, F083); (3) there was a legal basis for the intervention in the applicable criminal law (F093, F096, F099); and (4) the interference was proportionate because the demonstration was violent and involved forced entry into the UN buffer zone and TRNC territory (F080, F081, F082, F084).

The truly irreducible core — the facts without which the Court could not have reached its conclusion of no violation — are those establishing the violent and disruptive character of the demonstration and the territorial violation, since these are what justified the interference as proportionate. The existence of a legal basis (F093, F096, F099) was also essential. The applicant's participation (F022) is a threshold fact.

Irreducible core: F022, F080, F081, F082, F083, F084, F093, F096, F099.
