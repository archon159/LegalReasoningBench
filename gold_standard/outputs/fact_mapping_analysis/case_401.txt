LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope**
The Court needed to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) the assembly was (initially) peaceful and the applicant was not among the initial aggressors.
→ F006 (demonstration held on 6 May 2012), F008 (approved by Moscow city authorities — confirming it was a lawful assembly), F010 (march was peaceful), F024 (applicant took part in the demonstration), F148 (unidentified persons — not the applicant — called those present to move outside agreed venue), F149 (unidentified persons — not the applicant — called for violence), F150 (defendants acquired criminal intent at 5 p.m. at the latest — no pre-existing intent), F158 (applicant pleaded not guilty), F159 (applicant testified he decided to attend the public gathering)

**Interference**
The Court needed to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's freedom of assembly.
→ F036 (applicant arrested), F037 (applicant arrested on suspicion of violence against police), F147 (applicant found guilty), F169 (applicant sentenced to three years and six months)

**Prescribed by law**
The Court resolved this by reference to prior case law. The only atomic facts strictly necessary are the legal provisions under which the applicant was charged and convicted.
→ F191 (Article 212 § 2 of the Criminal Code), F193 (Article 318 § 1 of the Criminal Code)

**Legitimate aim**
The Court found the aims of preventing disorder and crime and protecting the rights and freedoms of others. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the nature of the conduct charged.
→ F014 (numerous clashes between police and protesters), F018 (criminal proceedings opened for mass disorder), F019 (criminal proceedings opened for violence against police)

**Necessity**
The Court's necessity analysis turned on: the severity of the sentence; the weakness of the evidence; the failure of domestic courts to examine the police's role; the applicant's minor and spontaneous role; and the chilling effect. The strictly necessary facts are:
→ F147 (applicant found guilty under Articles 212 § 2 and 318 § 1), F169 (sentenced to three years and six months), F170 (three-year term under Article 212 § 2, partly concurrent with one year under Article 318 § 1), F152 (court found applicant used metal barriers and shouted slogans), F153 (court found applicant threw pieces of tarmac at least three times), F143 (Police Officer T. testified applicant tried to snatch helmet — only evidence for assault charge), F154 (violence against unidentified police officer — no corroborating evidence identified), F155 (applicant punched unidentified police officer — no corroborating evidence), F173 (applicant denied using metal barriers, said he was standing close to them before arrests), F025 (applicant was beaten up by police during dispersal), F026 (applicant admitted to emergency ward), F029 (multiple bruises on chest), F030 (multiple bruises on spine), F070 (investigator refused to open criminal case into ill-treatment allegations), F168 (domestic court dismissed defendants' arguments about police use of force as farfetched), F175 (applicant argued first-instance court had not assessed his statements about ill-treatment), F150 (no pre-existing intent established), F148 (unidentified persons — not applicant — responsible for initial aggression), F161 (applicant saw police randomly arresting people while hitting them with truncheons), F166 (police ran up to applicant, beat him, arrested him), F137 (criminal case against ten participants — large-scale proceedings)

**Positive obligation**
The Court declined to assess this as a separate issue. The facts that grounded the applicant's complaint (and the Court's decision to subsume it into the necessity analysis) are:
→ F011 (barriers narrowed entrance to meeting venue), F013 (police cordon forced protestors to remain within barriers), F014 (numerous clashes between police and protesters), F016 (police began to disperse participants)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity/proportionality analysis. The Court found a violation because: (1) the applicant had no pre-existing violent intent and was not among the initial aggressors; (2) the evidence for the most serious charge (mass disorder) was weak and the domestic courts failed to examine the police's role in provoking the violence; (3) the sentence of three years for mass disorder was grossly disproportionate given the applicant's minor, spontaneous role; and (4) the chilling effect on political participation was severe. The irreducible factual core is:

→ F008 (assembly was approved — applicant was at a lawful event)
→ F024 (applicant participated in the demonstration)
→ F010 (march was peaceful — assembly initially peaceful)
→ F148 (unidentified persons — not the applicant — responsible for calling people to defy police)
→ F150 (no pre-existing criminal intent established by the trial court)
→ F147 (applicant found guilty under Articles 212 § 2 and 318 § 1)
→ F169 (sentenced to three years and six months' imprisonment)
→ F170 (three-year term under Article 212 § 2, partly concurrent with one year under Article 318 § 1)
→ F143 (Police Officer T.'s testimony — only direct evidence for the assault charge — limited to attempted snatching of helmet)
→ F154 (violence against unidentified police officer — no corroborating evidence identified in judgment)
→ F173 (applicant denied using metal barriers — no witnesses to him handling them)
→ F025 (applicant was beaten up by police during dispersal)
→ F029 (multiple bruises on chest — hospital evidence supporting ill-treatment allegation)
→ F030 (multiple bruises on spine)
→ F070 (investigator refused to open criminal case into ill-treatment — basis for domestic courts' dismissal of applicant's arguments)
→ F168 (domestic court dismissed defendants' arguments about police use of force as farfetched — failure to examine police's role)
→ F161 (applicant saw police randomly arresting people while hitting them with truncheons — context of spontaneous reaction)
→ F137 (criminal case against ten participants — large-scale proceedings amplifying chilling effect)
