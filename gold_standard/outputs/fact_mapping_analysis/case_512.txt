Scope:
The Court's implicit finding that there was a public event involving the applicant rests on F002, F003, and F004 collectively — these establish that a public event existed and that the applicant was subject to restrictions on it. F001 establishes the applicant as a party. However, since scope is only implied (not explicitly reasoned), the strictly necessary atomic fact is whichever most directly establishes the existence of a public event and the applicant's connection to it. F002 is the most representative (restrictions on location of a public event), supported by F003 and F004. F001 is background and not strictly necessary for the legal element itself.

Interference:
The Court's explicit finding of interference rests directly on F002, F003, and F004 — the restrictions on location, time, and manner are precisely what constitute the interference. All three are necessary because the Court's introductory framing references all three types of restriction. However, the most critical is F002 (location), as restrictions on location were the primary focus in Lashmankin and Others, but since the Court references all three, all are included.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences either failed the quality of law test or were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire legal analysis pivots on the existence of governmental restrictions on a public event involving the applicant. Without the fact that restrictions were imposed (F002, F003, F004), there is no interference, no Article 11 issue, and no violation. These are the facts the case actually turned on.

F001 is background identification and not strictly necessary for the legal conclusion itself.
F005 (other complaints) is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

The irreducible core is therefore F002, F003, and F004 — the three types of restriction imposed by authorities on the public event. Of these, F002 (location restrictions) is the most critical given the Lashmankin precedent, which centered on location restrictions, but since the Court's own framing in this case references all three, all three are strictly necessary to fully ground the conclusion as the Court expressed it.
