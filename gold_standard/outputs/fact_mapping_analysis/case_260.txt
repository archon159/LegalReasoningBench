LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) there was an assembly; (ii) the applicant participated; (iii) the assembly was peaceful from the applicant's perspective (he committed no reprehensible act himself).

Strictly necessary facts:
- F004: the demonstration took place on 12 February 1983 (assembly exists)
- F007: the applicant took part in the demonstration (participation)
- F085: it was not alleged that Mr Ezelin participated more actively than by his constant presence and carrying a placard (peaceful — no reprehensible act by applicant)
- F092: the charge against him was failure to dissociate, not commission of any act (peaceful — confirms the omission-only basis of the charge)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicant's freedom of assembly. The key facts are the post-event disciplinary sanction and its basis.

Strictly necessary facts:
- F099: Mr Ezelin was sentenced to the disciplinary penalty of a reprimand (the sanction = the interference)
- F092: the charge was failure to dissociate from the demonstrators' acts (the basis of the sanction, confirming it was tied to assembly participation)
- F105: Court of Cassation confirmed the finding that he never expressed disapproval or left the procession (confirming the sanction's connection to assembly conduct)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court needed to establish that the legal basis was sufficiently precise and foreseeable.

Strictly necessary facts:
- F118: Article 106 of Decree no. 72-468 of 9 June 1972 — the provision imposing obligations on avocats even in non-professional activities (the legal basis)
- F128: Court of Cassation judgment of 9 June 1964 — the avocat's oath bound him to respect due to courts in all circumstances (interpretive gloss establishing foreseeability)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court needed to establish that the sanction pursued prevention of disorder.

Strictly necessary facts:
- F092: the charge was failure to dissociate from unruly incidents (establishes what the sanction was responding to)
- F093: Court of Appeal found this amounted to a breach of discretion under Article 106 (establishes the authorities' characterisation of the conduct as endorsing disorder)

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court needed to assess proportionality: the weight of the penalty against the importance of the right.

Strictly necessary facts:
- F082/F099: the penalty was a reprimand (nature and severity of the sanction)
- F119: Article 107 scale of penalties — reprimand is near the lower end (contextualises the penalty as light)
- F085: Mr Ezelin's participation was limited to presence and carrying a placard (no reprehensible act committed)
- F092: the charge was solely failure to dissociate (the sanction rested on omission, not commission)
- F003: Mr Ezelin is an avocat (the Court explicitly addresses the "even for an avocat" dimension)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts applicable. Element not discussed.


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: (1) the applicant participated in a lawful assembly without himself committing any reprehensible act; (2) he was nonetheless sanctioned — by a post-event disciplinary penalty — solely because he failed to dissociate himself from others' acts; (3) even though the penalty was light, the Court found it disproportionate because the right to peaceful assembly cannot be restricted where the individual himself did nothing wrong.

The irreducible facts are:
- F004: the demonstration took place (assembly existed)
- F007: the applicant participated in the demonstration (participation established)
- F085: it was not alleged that Mr Ezelin participated more actively than by his constant presence and carrying a placard (he committed no reprehensible act)
- F092: the charge and sanction rested solely on his failure to dissociate from others' acts (the omission-only basis of the interference)
- F099: Mr Ezelin was sentenced to the disciplinary penalty of a reprimand (the interference itself)
- F118: Article 106 of Decree no. 72-468 — the legal basis for the sanction (prescribed by law, necessary for the interference to be justiciable)
- F003: Mr Ezelin is an avocat (the Court's reasoning explicitly addresses whether even an avocat can be restricted in this way)
