LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope — The factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
- F002 corresponds to the reference to "organisers of public assemblies"
- F003 corresponds to the reference to "participants of public assemblies"
These two atomic facts are what the Court was pointing to when it described the applicants and the assemblies. F001 is a background fact (the appended table) that underlies all individual applicant details but is not itself the factual grounding for the scope finding. F004 is irrelevant to Article 11.

(b) Interference — The factual grounding quote is the same sentence: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers and participants of public assemblies, namely the dispersal of these assemblies, as well as their arrest followed by their conviction for administrative offence."
- F002 and F003 again correspond to this, as the measures were taken against both organisers and participants.

(e) Necessity — The factual grounding in the reasoning text was the reference to prior leading cases and the absence of distinguishing material. The only atomic facts that correspond to the underlying factual situation are again F002 and F003, since the necessity assessment is grounded in the same characterisation of the complaints (disproportionate measures against organisers and participants). No additional atomic facts in the provided list speak to the proportionality analysis.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each discussed element

- Scope: The Court's implied finding that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated, and that the assemblies were within Article 11's protection rests on F002 (organisers of public assemblies) and F003 (participants of public assemblies). Both are necessary because the Court's reasoning covers both categories of applicant. F002 alone would not cover participants; F003 alone would not cover organisers.

- Interference: The finding that governmental acts interfered with the applicants' freedom of assembly rests on F002 and F003, which describe the disproportionate measures taken against organisers and participants respectively. Both are necessary for the same reason as above.

- Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.

- Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.

- Necessity: The necessity conclusion is grounded in the same characterisation of the complaints. F002 and F003 are the necessary facts. However, since the Court's necessity reasoning is essentially comparative (analogy to leading cases) rather than fact-specific, the most critical atomic facts remain F002 and F003 as the basis for identifying the situation as one involving interference with assembly rights.

- Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts map to this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum to establish: (i) that there were public assemblies in which the applicants were involved (scope), and (ii) that governmental measures were taken against them in that context (interference). These are the two facts the entire reasoning turns on. Both F002 and F003 are strictly necessary — F002 establishes the organiser dimension and F003 the participant dimension, and the judgment covers both categories. Without either, the Court could not have found a violation in respect of all applicants.

F001 is a purely administrative fact (the appended table) and is not part of the legal reasoning. F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore the irreducible core is F002 and F003.
