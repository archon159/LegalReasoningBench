**LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning**

**SCOPE**
The Court needs to establish that there were assemblies, that the applicants participated, and that the assemblies were peaceful. The most critical atomic facts are:
- F003: establishes that the applications concern freedom of peaceful assembly in connection with protests against the landfill
- F140: establishes that the applicants started organising protests (participation and assembly)
- F141: establishes that all applicants except Ms Kotova were charged with administrative offences in connection with their protests — this is the key fact from which the Court infers participation and, by the absence of violent charges, peacefulness
- F143: establishes that Mr Kotov submitted three notices of public events (assembly and participation for the refusal complaints)
- F144: establishes that Mr Kotov submitted a notice of a protest car rally (assembly and participation for the refusal complaints)

**INTERFERENCE**
The most critical atomic facts:
- F141: establishes that the applicants were arrested, charged and convicted — the core interference for most applicants
- F145: establishes that neither of Mr Kotov's applications was approved — the core interference for the refusal complaints

**PRESCRIBED BY LAW**
No atomic facts — the Court accepts this as undisputed without factual grounding.

**LEGITIMATE AIM**
No atomic facts — the Court accepts this as undisputed without factual grounding.

**NECESSITY**
The most critical atomic facts:
- F141: the central fact underpinning the necessity analysis across all three groups of episodes — the applicants were charged with administrative offences in connection with protests; no violent acts were established; the domestic courts' reasoning was cursory; the arrest occurred six days after the meeting; the road protests were treated as unauthorised
- F143: the notices of public events were refused because another event was already planned — necessary for the refusal analysis
- F144: the car rally notice was refused because the road had only one lane in each direction — necessary for the refusal analysis
- F145: the refusals were issued without evidence that simultaneous events could not be managed — necessary for the refusal analysis

**POSITIVE OBLIGATION**
The most critical atomic fact:
- F141: the absence of police assistance to the organiser and the domestic courts' dismissal of this argument are both derived from the administrative proceedings described in F141

**LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)**

The case turned on three groups of episodes. For each, the essential facts are:

1. For the Klin Ice Palace meeting and the road protests: the applicants were arrested and convicted of administrative offences in connection with peaceful protests (F141), with no finding of violence or disorder, no evidence of actual harm to safety, and no police assistance to the organiser — all of which flow from F141.

2. For the refusal of notices: Mr Kotov submitted notices (F143, F144) that were refused (F145) without adequate justification — the authorities did not demonstrate that simultaneous events could not be managed or that alternatives were considered.

3. The peaceful character of the protests (implied by F141 — no violent acts found) is essential to the conclusion that the interference was disproportionate.

The irreducible core facts are therefore: F141, F143, F144, F145.
