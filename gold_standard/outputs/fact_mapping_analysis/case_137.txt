LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---

Scope (assembly exists):
The Court's grounding quote: 'The Court has previously held that the assembly at Bolotnaya Square on 6 May 2012 fell within the scope of Article 11 of the Convention (see Yaroslav Belousov v. Russia, nos. 2653/13 and 60980/14, §§ 168-71, 4 October 2016).'
Atomic facts: F007 (a public demonstration was held in central Moscow on 6 May 2012), F009 (the demonstration had been approved by the Moscow city authorities), F006 (background facts set out in Yaroslav Belousov v. Russia).

Scope (participation):
The Court's grounding quotes:
- 'It does not transpire from any submissions that the applicants were among those responsible for the initial acts of aggression which contributed to the deterioration of the assembly's originally peaceful character.' → F014 (clashes between police and protesters), F017 (applicants took part in the demonstration).
- 'they denied having pushed through the cordon, and even if the applicants found themselves beyond the cordon, there is no evidence that it had been the result of their deliberate effort.' → F022-F025 (first applicant's account), F066-F070 (second applicant's account).
- 'According to the material in the case-file, the police cordon had collapsed because of the pressure of the crowd which had built up owing to the unexpected and unannounced change by the authorities of the venue layout.' → F012 (barriers narrowed entrance), F013 (police cordon forced protestors within barriers).
- 'Apart from that, the applicants' behaviour remained strictly peaceful.' → F024 (first applicant: no breach of public order), F070 (second applicant: did not breach public order).

Scope (peaceful):
Same quotes and same atomic facts as participation above: F012, F013, F014, F017, F022, F023, F024, F025, F066, F067, F068, F069, F070.

--- INTERFERENCE ---

The Court's grounding quote: 'the termination of the demonstration, the applicants' arrest at the venue of the event and conviction for administrative offences constituted interference with their right to freedom of peaceful assembly.'
Atomic facts: F015 (police ordered meeting to finish early and began dispersal), F018 (applicants were arrested at the venue), F020 (applicants were charged with administrative offences), F021 (applicants were convicted of administrative offences).

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---

The Court merged this into necessity and made no independent factual findings. No atomic facts can be mapped to this element as a standalone assessment.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---

The Court's grounding quote: 'The Court is mindful of the authorities' submission that the enhanced security measures, and in particular, the crackdown on those charged with offences committed on 6 May 2012 on Bolotnaya Square, were specifically aimed at preventing illegal campsites from being set up.' This is a reference to the Government's submission, not an independently established fact. The Court does not formally accept it. No atomic facts in the provided list directly correspond to this specific claim about campsites. The closest contextual facts are F014 (clashes at Bolotnaya Square) and F015 (police ordered early termination).
The Court also notes: 'the applicants were not arrested and sanctioned for breaching the rules on public assembly.' → F020 (charged with administrative offences under Art. 19.3 § 1, i.e. disobedience to police, not assembly rules), F037 (first applicant's charge: refusal to obey lawful police orders), F081/F084 (second applicant's charge: disobeying lawful police order).

--- NECESSITY ---

The Court's grounding quotes:
1. 'the domestic courts failed to establish key facts in the administrative proceedings against the applicants, namely whether they had received an order from the police, or that it was lawful, or that the applicants had disobeyed it.' → F039 (first applicant convicted on basis of police officer statements only), F065 (appeal court relied on police testimony), F091 (second applicant convicted on basis of police officer's report), F094 (appeal court upheld conviction).
2. 'it was incumbent on the domestic courts to establish whether the applicants had indeed been involved in breaking through the police cordon.' → F035 (charge: first applicant broke through cordon), F083 (charge: second applicant broke through cordon).
3. 'they rejected the applicants' accounts of events supported by other evidence, including testimony of eyewitnesses, and relied on the police statements and reports.' → F064 (appeal decision did not mention wife's eye-witness statement), F065 (relied on police testimony), F097 (defence witness stated second applicant had not participated in breaking cordon), F098 (defence witness dismissed as unreliable because he was applicant's friend), F095 (second applicant's request to examine arresting officers dismissed).
4. 'The domestic courts' decisions disclose no attempt to establish the relevant facts on the basis of comparative assessment of the conflicting testimonies of the applicants, the defence witnesses, and the police officers.' → F039, F064, F065, F091, F094, F095, F097, F098.
5. 'the measures taken against the applicants must have had the effect of discouraging them and others from attending protest rallies' → F021 (conviction), F040 (fine of RUB 500), F092 (fine of RUB 500).
6. 'The chilling effect of the sanctions imposed on them was further amplified by the large number of arrests made on that day, which attracted wide media coverage.' → F016 (it took police about two hours to clear protestors — implying large-scale operation), F018 (applicants arrested at venue). No atomic fact directly states 'large number of arrests' or 'wide media coverage'; F016 is the closest proxy.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---

The Court's grounding quote: 'It has also found that the domestic authorities failed to discharge their positive obligation to ensure the peaceful conduct of that assembly (see Frumkin v. Russia, cited above, §§ 93-130). Noting that the present case contains no element capable of altering this conclusion, the Court adheres to that finding.'
Atomic facts: F005 (background facts set out in Frumkin), F012 (barriers narrowed entrance to meeting venue), F013 (police cordon forced protestors within barriers), F014 (numerous clashes between police and protesters), F015 (police ordered early termination of meeting).

--- INTERFERENCE ---
F015 (early termination ordered), F018 (applicants arrested at venue), F021 (applicants convicted of administrative offences).

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
No atomic facts strictly necessary — element not separately assessed.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
F020 (charged under Art. 19.3 § 1 — disobedience to police, not assembly rules): strictly necessary to show the mismatch between stated aim and actual charges, which the Court uses to undercut the Government's justification. F037 and F084 (charge descriptions) support the same point but F020 is the most representative.

The core of the Court's necessity finding rests on the failure of domestic courts to properly assess the facts. Strictly necessary facts:
F039 (first applicant convicted solely on police statements), F065 (appeal court relied on police testimony and upheld conviction), F064 (appeal decision did not mention wife's eye-witness statement — showing failure to engage with defence evidence), F091 (second applicant convicted on basis of police officer's report alone), F095 (second applicant's request to examine arresting officers dismissed), F097 (defence witness stated second applicant had not participated in breaking cordon), F098 (defence witness dismissed as unreliable solely because he was applicant's friend — showing inadequate comparative assessment), F021 (conviction — necessary for chilling effect finding).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
F012 (barriers narrowed entrance — key fact showing authorities changed venue layout), F013 (police cordon forced protestors within barriers), F015 (police ordered early termination — showing failure to manage assembly peacefully).

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Art. 11 violated)

The case turned on two pivotal issues: (1) whether the applicants were peaceful participants entitled to Art. 11 protection, and (2) whether the domestic courts adequately assessed the facts before convicting them. The following atomic facts are strictly necessary for the Court's conclusion:

F007: There was a demonstration — the foundational fact establishing Art. 11 is engaged.
F009: The demonstration was approved — confirming it was a lawful assembly within Art. 11 scope.
F017: The applicants participated in the demonstration — necessary to establish they are rights-holders.
F012: Barriers narrowed the entrance — the key fact explaining why the cordon collapsed without the applicants' deliberate action, underpinning the peaceful character finding.
F018: Applicants were arrested at the venue — the concrete act of interference.
F021: Applicants were convicted of administrative offences — the concrete act of interference and the basis for the chilling effect.
F064: The appeal decision did not mention the wife's eye-witness statement — demonstrates failure to engage with defence evidence for the first applicant.
F065: The appeal court relied on police testimony and upheld the first applicant's conviction — demonstrates inadequate comparative assessment.
F095: The second applicant's request to examine arresting officers was dismissed — demonstrates procedural failure in her case.
F097: The defence witness stated the second applicant had not participated in breaking the cordon — the exculpatory evidence that was available.
F098: The defence witness was dismissed as unreliable solely because he was the applicant's friend — demonstrates the domestic court's failure to conduct an adequate comparative assessment, which is the core of the necessity finding.
