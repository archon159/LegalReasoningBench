LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

Scope:
The reasoning text states "organisers or participants of public assemblies" and references "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." The Court's own characterisation in the reasoning mirrors F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies"). F001 is also relevant insofar as it establishes that there are identifiable applicants whose details are in the appended table, confirming participation. No atomic fact explicitly addresses the peaceful nature of the assemblies.

Interference:
The reasoning text states "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This maps directly to F002, which uses nearly identical language.

Necessity:
The Court states it examined "all the material submitted to it" and found no new fact or argument, concluding the interferences were not "necessary in a democratic society." The only factual anchor in the atomic facts is again F002, which establishes the nature of the measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction). No atomic fact provides case-specific factual grounding for the necessity assessment beyond this.

Prescribed by law, Legitimate aim, Positive obligation:
None of the atomic facts correspond to these elements, as they are not discussed in the reasoning and no factual grounding was identified.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope:
F002 is strictly necessary — it establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the minimum factual predicate for the scope analysis (existence of assembly, participation). F001 is supportive but not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself.

Interference:
F002 is strictly necessary — it identifies the concrete governmental acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction) that constitute the interference with freedom of assembly.

Necessity:
F002 is strictly necessary — it is the only atomic fact that describes the nature of the measures whose proportionality the Court assessed. Without it, there is no factual basis for the necessity conclusion.

Prescribed by law, Legitimate aim, Positive obligation:
No atomic facts are strictly necessary for these elements, as they are not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The entire chain of reasoning — from scope (there were assemblies, the applicants participated) through interference (arrest, dispersal, conviction) to necessity (those measures were disproportionate) — rests on a single atomic fact: F002. This fact establishes both the existence of the assemblies and the nature of the governmental measures, which are the two indispensable factual predicates for the violation finding. F001 is background but not part of the legal reasoning chain. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact in the irreducible core.
