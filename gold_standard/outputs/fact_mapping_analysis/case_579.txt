LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

**Scope (assembly exists / participation):**
The reasoning text states: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" and describes applicants as "organisers or participants of public assemblies." The atomic facts that correspond to these quotes are:
- F002: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the reference to organisers of public assemblies.
- F003: "The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as participants of public assemblies." — directly corresponds to the reference to participants of public assemblies.
These two facts together establish that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated (or organised) them.

**Scope (peaceful):**
As noted previously, there is no factual grounding in the reasoning text for the peacefulness of the assemblies. No atomic fact addresses this either. No mapping is possible.

**Interference:**
The reasoning text states: "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This corresponds to:
- F002 and F003: These facts describe the applicants as organisers and participants who suffered disproportionate measures, which is the factual basis for the interference finding. The measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) are the interference.

**Necessity:**
The Court states it has "examined all the material submitted to it" and found no new facts or arguments, relying instead on prior case-law. The only atomic facts that could be said to ground this are again F002 and F003, which describe the measures as "disproportionate" — a characterisation that maps directly onto the necessity assessment. No other atomic facts are available.

**Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:**
None of these elements are discussed in the reasoning, and no atomic facts correspond to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:** F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. They establish that there were public assemblies and that the applicants were organisers or participants — the minimum factual predicate for the scope element to be satisfied. F002 covers organisers; F003 covers participants. Both are needed because the applicant group includes both categories.

**Interference:** F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The description of "disproportionate measures" (arrest, dispersal, conviction) against organisers and participants is the factual basis for the interference finding. Without these facts, there is no identified act of government interfering with assembly rights.

**Necessity:** F002 and F003 are again the only available atomic facts. The Court's necessity conclusion is primarily driven by precedent, but the factual predicate — that measures were taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies — is supplied by these two facts. No other atomic facts are available to ground this element.

**Prescribed by law, legitimate aim, positive obligation:** No atomic facts correspond to these elements, which are not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion, the Court needed at minimum to establish: (1) that there were public assemblies, (2) that the applicants were organisers or participants, and (3) that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against them. All of this is captured in F002 and F003. These are the two atomic facts that are absolutely essential — without them, there is no assembly, no participation, and no interference to assess. The violation conclusion cannot stand without them. F001 and F004 are not essential to the Article 11 reasoning itself.
