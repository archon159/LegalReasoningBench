LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

**Scope:** The Court's implied finding that the events were assemblies within the scope of Article 11, that the applicants sought to participate as organizers, and that the assemblies were planned (with no suggestion of non-peaceful character) rests on:
→ F002 (applicants lodged notices of LGBT public assemblies — establishes that assemblies were planned and applicants were organizers seeking to participate)
→ F003 (refusal of dates — confirms the assemblies were proposed events subject to the notification procedure)

F004 is subsumed by F003 for this purpose; F005 and F007 are relevant to admissibility rather than scope per se. The most critical facts are F002 and F003.

**Interference:** The Court's implied finding that there was an interference with the applicants' Article 11 rights rests on:
→ F003 (local authorities refused to approve the dates proposed by the applicants)
→ F004 (local authorities refused to approve the locations proposed by the applicants)

Both are necessary because the refusal operated on both dimensions (date and venue), and together they constitute the ban the Court refers to.

**Prescribed by law:** Not discussed by the Court in its merits assessment. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Legitimate aim:** Not resolved by the Court in its own assessment. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

**Necessity:** The Court's explicit finding that the ban was not necessary in a democratic society rests on:
→ F003 (blanket refusal of dates — the Court characterizes this as a ban not corresponding to a pressing social need)
→ F004 (blanket refusal of locations — together with F003, constitutes the full scope of the ban)
→ F012 (the Alekseyev judgment, which the Court treats as the leading case establishing the violation on similar facts, is the primary basis for the necessity finding)

F010 and F011 (Lashmankin) are referenced in the admissibility section but not explicitly in the necessity assessment. F012 is the critical precedent anchor.

**Positive obligation:** Not discussed. No atomic facts strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: the applicants organized LGBT public assemblies (F002), the local authorities refused to approve both the dates and locations of those assemblies (F003, F004), and this pattern of blanket refusal was found — by reference to the Alekseyev precedent (F012) — not to correspond to a pressing social need and thus not necessary in a democratic society. These are the facts without which the Court could not have reached its conclusion.

F002: establishes that the applicants were organizers of planned assemblies — necessary to show they were rights-holders under Article 11 and that assemblies were planned.
F003: establishes the interference (refusal of dates) and is the primary factual basis for the necessity finding.
F004: establishes the full scope of the interference (refusal of locations) — together with F003, constitutes the ban the Court found unjustified.
F012: the Alekseyev judgment is the explicit doctrinal and factual anchor for the Court's necessity conclusion — without it, the Court's reasoning has no independent factual or legal basis in this text.

F005, F006, F007 are relevant to the Article 13 finding and the admissibility analysis but are not strictly necessary for the Article 11 violation conclusion itself. F001, F008, F009, F010, F011 are background or procedural and not part of the irreducible core.
