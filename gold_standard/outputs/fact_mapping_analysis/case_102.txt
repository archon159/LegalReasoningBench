LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

(a) Scope
- The quote "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies" maps to:
  - F002 (organisers of public assemblies)
  - F003 (participants of public assemblies)
  These two atomic facts together correspond exactly to the Court's characterisation. F001 is a background fact about the applicant list but does not itself establish the assembly or participation. F004 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant here.

(b) Interference
- The quote "their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences" — this is the Court's description of the measures complained of. The closest atomic facts are again F002 and F003, which describe the applicants as complainants of disproportionate measures as organisers and participants. There is no atomic fact that specifically mentions arrest, dispersal, or conviction — these details appear only in the reasoning section, not in the formalised atomic facts. F002 and F003 are the best available proxies.

(e) Necessity
- The Court's necessity conclusion is grounded in the same characterisation of the complaints: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies." The word "disproportionate" in F002 and F003 is directly relevant here, as it frames the entire complaint in terms of proportionality, which is the core of the necessity test. Again, F002 and F003 are the relevant atomic facts.

No atomic facts correspond to prescribed_by_law, legitimate_aim, or positive_obligation, as these elements were not discussed and no facts were cited in relation to them.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are both necessary. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies (establishing the existence of assemblies and the organisers' participation). F003 establishes that other applicants were participants. Together they are the minimum factual basis for the scope element. However, since F002 and F003 establish the same point from two angles (organisers vs participants), both are needed to cover the full applicant group.

- Interference: F002 and F003 are the most critical facts. The complaint of "disproportionate measures" implies that governmental acts were directed at the applicants in connection with their assemblies. No more specific atomic fact about the nature of the interference (arrest, dispersal, conviction) exists in the atomic facts list.

- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts support this element — it was not discussed.

- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts support this element — it was not discussed.

- Necessity: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The word "disproportionate" in both facts is the factual hook for the necessity analysis. The Court's entire reasoning on necessity is that the measures were disproportionate, which maps directly onto these two facts.

- Positive obligation: No atomic facts support this element — it was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the finding that disproportionate measures were taken against the applicants as organisers or participants of public assemblies — i.e., that there were public assemblies, that the applicants were connected to them, and that the state's response was disproportionate. This maps onto F002 and F003. F001 is merely a procedural reference to the appended table and is not substantively necessary for the legal conclusion. F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.

The irreducible core is therefore F002 and F003: these are the facts that establish both the Article 11 context (public assemblies, organisers and participants) and the nature of the complaint (disproportionate measures), which is what the Court's violation finding rests on.
