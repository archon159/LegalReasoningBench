LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

For the scope element, the factual grounding quote was: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This maps to F002 ("The applicants complained of disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies"), which is the closest atomic fact. F001 is also tangentially relevant as it references the applicants generally, but it adds no substantive factual content beyond identifying that there is a list of applicants.

For the interference element, the same factual grounding quote applies: "disproportionate measures taken against them as organisers or participants of public assemblies, namely their arrest in relation to the dispersal of these assemblies and their conviction for administrative offences." This again maps to F002. No other atomic fact speaks to the specific governmental acts (arrest, dispersal, conviction) identified as the interference.

For the necessity element, the Court stated it had "examined all the material submitted to it" and found no distinguishing facts or arguments, grounding its conclusion entirely in prior case-law. The only atomic fact that could be said to correspond to this is again F002, since the nature of the complaint (disproportionate measures against assembly participants) is what the necessity analysis addresses. No atomic fact specifically addresses the necessity assessment.

F003 ("Some applicants also raised other complaints under the provisions of the Convention") is not referenced in any factual grounding quote and does not correspond to any element discussed.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope of Article 11. Without this fact, the Court could not even frame the complaint as one engaging Article 11.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It identifies the measures complained of (disproportionate measures against assembly participants) which the Court treats as the interference with freedom of assembly. The specific acts — arrest, dispersal, conviction — are described in the reasoning section as elaborations of F002.

Prescribed by law: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Legitimate aim: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be said to be necessary, as it defines the nature of the interference that the Court assessed as not necessary in a democratic society. However, the necessity finding is grounded primarily in prior case-law rather than in any specific atomic fact from this case.

Positive obligation: Not discussed; no atomic facts are strictly necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated rests on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. The only atomic fact that is strictly necessary to reach this conclusion is F002: it establishes both that there were public assemblies in which the applicants participated, and that governmental measures (disproportionate in the applicants' submission, and accepted as such by the Court by reference to prior case-law) were taken against them. Without F002, there is no factual basis for any element of the Article 11 analysis. F001 and F003 are not essential to the conclusion: F001 merely identifies the applicants, and F003 concerns other complaints not relevant to the Article 11 finding.
