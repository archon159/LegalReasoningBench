LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that the applicants were participants in protests (supporting both the assembly-exists and participation sub-elements), and F003 establishes the specific context of those protests (Moscow, 6 March 2022), confirming they were real, identifiable public assemblies. F003 also implicitly supports the peacefulness inference, as the protests are described as protests against an invasion with no suggestion of violence.

Interference: F002 is strictly necessary, as it is the only atomic fact that directly references the measures taken against the applicants ("disproportionate measures taken against them as participants in protests"). F003 provides the context but is secondary.

Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary (element not discussed).

Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary (element not discussed).

Necessity: F002 is strictly necessary, as the Court's necessity finding is grounded in the characterization of the measures as disproportionate against protest participants, which is the core of F002. F003 provides supporting context.

Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary (element not discussed).

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must establish: (1) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope); (2) that the state interfered with that participation (interference); and (3) that the interference was not justified (necessity). The single most essential atomic fact is F002, which simultaneously establishes participation in protests, the existence of those protests as assemblies, and the fact that disproportionate measures were taken against the applicants — which is precisely what the necessity finding turns on. F003 is also necessary because it grounds the assemblies in a specific, identifiable real-world event (protests against the Russian invasion of Ukraine in Moscow on 6 March 2022), without which the Court could not confirm that the assemblies fell within the scope of Article 11 and that the interference was real and concrete.
