LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

SCOPE (implied): The Court's implied conclusions that assemblies existed, that applicants participated, and that the assemblies were peaceful rest on the characterisation of the applicants as organisers or participants of public assemblies. The most critical atomic facts are F002 and F003. Both are necessary because together they cover the full range of applicants (organisers and participants). Neither alone covers all applicants.

INTERFERENCE (explicit): The Court's explicit finding of interference rests on the same characterisation — that measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against organisers and participants of public assemblies. F002 and F003 are again the necessary facts. However, since the interference finding requires only that some governmental act affected the applicants' assembly rights, and since F002 and F003 together establish this for all applicants, both are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated turned on: (1) the existence of public assemblies in which the applicants were organisers or participants, and (2) the fact that governmental measures (arrest, dispersal, conviction) were taken against them in that capacity. These two points are what triggers the Article 11 analysis and what the necessity finding (by reference to prior case-law) is applied to. Without F002 and F003, there would be no assembly, no participation, no interference, and no basis for the violation finding. F001 and F004 are not essential to the violation conclusion — F001 is merely a reference to an appended table, and F004 concerns unrelated complaints.

The irreducible factual core is therefore: F002, F003.
