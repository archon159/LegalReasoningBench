LIST 1: Mapping factual grounding quotes to atomic facts

--- SCOPE ---
The Court's only grounding for scope is the framing of the complaint: 'the refusal of his application to organise a demonstration.' This maps to:
- F002: applicant applied for authorisation for a political demonstration in Kossuth Square
- F007: the demonstration was scheduled for 17 October 2008
- F012: the Budapest Police Department forbade the demonstration
No atomic fact explicitly addresses peacefulness or participation beyond the application itself.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court states: 'it is not in dispute between the parties that there was an interference with the applicant's right to freedom of assembly.' The factual basis for the interference is the prohibition:
- F012: On 15 October 2008, the Budapest Police Department forbade the applicant's planned demonstration.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court states: 'the interference was prescribed by law, namely, by section 8(1) of the Assembly Act.' This maps to:
- F014: The Budapest Police Department considered that 'gravely endangering' the functioning of Parliament was a legitimate ground for prohibiting the demonstration under section 8(1) of the Assembly Act.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court accepts 'the legitimate aims of public safety and the rights and freedoms of others.' The factual basis for the Government's aim as accepted by the Court traces to the police's stated reasons:
- F013: The Budapest Police Department was of the view that the planned demonstration would 'gravely endanger' the functioning of Parliament.
- F015: The Budapest Police Department considered that amplified speeches and music might disturb the Members of Parliament's work.
- F017: The Budapest Police Department considered that participants might physically impede Members of Parliament from driving in and out of Parliament's car park.
- F018: The Budapest Police Department considered that this would amount to restricting the Members of Parliament's freedom of movement.
- F019: The Budapest Police Department considered that this would amount to inhibiting the functioning of the organ of the people's representation.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court's necessity analysis is grounded in several factual findings:

(i) The police's proposed compromise and the applicant's refusal:
'the domestic authorities invited the applicant to exercise his right to demonstrate in cooperation with the authorities, limiting the area in question' and 'the applicant's intransigence in considering the police's conciliatory suggestion'
- F010: On 14 October 2008, the Budapest Police Department proposed that the demonstration be held in a secluded part of Kossuth Square.
- F011: The applicant refused the Budapest Police Department's suggestion.

(ii) The failure to demonstrate necessity of the restriction — no relevant and sufficient reasons:
'the authorities did not provide relevant and sufficient reasons for the proposed restriction'
- F013: The Budapest Police Department was of the view that the planned demonstration would 'gravely endanger' the functioning of Parliament.
- F015: The Budapest Police Department considered that amplified speeches and music might disturb the Members of Parliament's work.
- F016: The Budapest Police Department estimated the number of participants at 150 to 200 persons.
- F017: The Budapest Police Department considered that participants might physically impede Members of Parliament from driving in and out of Parliament's car park.

(iii) The comparator demonstration — the Court's key factual finding:
'The Court notes the applicant's unrefuted assertion that another demonstration planned on exactly the same location for 15 October 2008 had not been forbidden by the authorities.'
- F021: The applicant submitted that another demonstration with an agenda identical to his, scheduled for 15 October 2008, had not been forbidden on exactly the same location.

(iv) The significance of the comparator — parliamentary commissions in session on 15 October:
'on that date – unlike on 17 October 2008 – five parliamentary commissions were in session, evidently entailing the presence and movement of numerous MPs'
- F023: According to Parliament's official and public activity log, on 15 October 2008 five parliamentary commissions were in session.

(v) Absence of parliamentary activity on 17 October:
'especially since on the date of the event planned by the applicant no parliamentary activity was underway'
- F008: The only event on 17 October 2008 in Parliament's building was a conference organised by the Hungarian Alliance for Enhancing Female Careers.
- F009: No parliamentary session was scheduled for 17 October 2008.

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No factual grounding identified. The Court does not discuss positive obligations.

LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court implies scope is satisfied. The strictly necessary fact is that the applicant sought to organise a demonstration (an assembly), which is the foundation for the entire case:
- F002: applicant applied for authorisation for a political demonstration.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The strictly necessary fact is the prohibition itself:
- F012: The Budapest Police Department forbade the applicant's planned demonstration.

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court accepts this on the basis that the prohibition was issued under a specific statutory provision:
- F014: The Budapest Police Department considered that 'gravely endangering' the functioning of Parliament was a legitimate ground for prohibiting the demonstration under section 8(1) of the Assembly Act.

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court accepts public safety and rights of others. The most critical fact grounding the Government's aim as accepted by the Court is the police's stated concern about parliamentary functioning:
- F013: The Budapest Police Department was of the view that the planned demonstration would 'gravely endanger' the functioning of Parliament.

--- NECESSITY ---
The strictly necessary facts are those the Court actually relied upon to find the interference not necessary:
- F010: The police proposed limiting the demonstration to a secluded part of Kossuth Square (establishing the compromise offer).
- F021: The applicant's unrefuted assertion that another demonstration on the same location on 15 October 2008 had not been forbidden (the comparator, explicitly adopted by the Court).
- F023: On 15 October 2008, five parliamentary commissions were in session (making the comparator more damaging to the Government's position).
- F009: No parliamentary session was scheduled for 17 October 2008 (the date of the applicant's planned demonstration, directly undermining the Government's justification).

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
No atomic facts correspond to this element.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis. The Court found the prohibition disproportionate primarily because: (a) no parliamentary activity was underway on the day of the planned demonstration, making the Government's justification hollow; and (b) a comparable demonstration on a day with more parliamentary activity had not been forbidden, exposing the inconsistency of the prohibition. These two points are the factual core of the violation finding. The prohibition itself and the legal basis are necessary preconditions but not what the case turned on.

- F012: The Budapest Police Department forbade the applicant's planned demonstration. (Establishes the interference — necessary precondition for any violation finding.)
- F009: No parliamentary session was scheduled for 17 October 2008. (Directly undermines the Government's justification that the demonstration would disrupt parliamentary work.)
- F021: The applicant submitted that another demonstration on the same location on 15 October 2008 had not been forbidden — adopted by the Court as unrefuted. (The comparator that exposes the inconsistency of the prohibition.)
- F023: On 15 October 2008, five parliamentary commissions were in session. (Makes the comparator decisive: the other demonstration was allowed on a day with more parliamentary activity, making the prohibition of the applicant's demonstration even less justifiable.)
