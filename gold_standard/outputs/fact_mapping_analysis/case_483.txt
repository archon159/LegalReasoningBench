LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

- Scope: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. F002 establishes that some applicants were organisers of public assemblies; F003 establishes that others were participants. Together they establish that assemblies existed and that the applicants participated in them. No atomic fact addresses the peaceful nature of the assemblies.
- Interference: F002 and F003 are strictly necessary. The measures complained of — arrest, dispersal, conviction — are described in the context of the applicants' roles as organisers and participants. These are the only atomic facts that ground the interference finding.
- Prescribed by law: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Legitimate aim: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.
- Necessity: F002 and F003 are the only atomic facts that can be said to underlie the necessity assessment, since the Court's reasoning on necessity is entirely referential (to prior case law) and does not cite specific facts from the present case beyond the nature of the complaints.
- Positive obligation: not discussed; no atomic facts are necessary.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The case turned on the finding that the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly — their arrest and conviction as organisers or participants of public assemblies — were not necessary in a democratic society. The irreducible factual core is therefore:
- F002: establishes that applicants as organisers suffered disproportionate measures, grounding both the scope (assembly, participation) and interference elements.
- F003: establishes that applicants as participants suffered disproportionate measures, grounding the same elements for the participant applicants.

F001 and F004 are not strictly necessary: F001 is a purely administrative reference to the appended table, and F004 concerns other complaints under other Convention provisions, which are not relevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
