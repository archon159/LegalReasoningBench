LIST 2: Strictly necessary atomic facts for each element of law

--- SCOPE ---

The Court's scope analysis turns on: (i) the existence of an assembly (a large group of protestors), (ii) the applicants' participation in it, and (iii) its peaceful character. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F007: applicants attended a gathering (establishes participation in an assembly)
- F009: the gathering was approved by authorities (contextualises the assembly as lawful at its origin)
- F010: applicants were walking to another authorised gathering (establishes participation/attempted participation)
- F012: Government alleged some 1,500 people took part in the procession (establishes the assembly as a group event, grounding the Court's "large group" observation)
- F015: route blocked by riot police (establishes the context in which participation was interrupted)
- F016: applicants arrested (establishes the moment participation was terminated)

For peacefulness specifically, the Court makes a bare assertion without detailed factual elaboration, but the background facts of an authorised political rally support it:
- F004: opposition politicians organised protest rallies
- F005: part of a public campaign for democratic forces

--- INTERFERENCE ---

Strictly necessary facts:
- F015: route blocked by riot police (dispersal = interference)
- F016: applicants arrested (arrest = interference)
- F057: first applicant sentenced to five days' administrative detention (sanction = interference)
- F070: second applicant sentenced to five days' administrative detention (sanction = interference)

No atomic facts strictly necessary — the Court assumes this element without factual grounding.

--- NECESSITY ---

Strictly necessary facts:
- F051: Justice of the Peace established that first applicant had marched in an unauthorised public event — the sole basis for the conviction being the unauthorised nature of the event (establishes that formal unlawfulness was the only justification)
- F022: first applicant charged with breaching established procedure for conducting public events (confirms the charge was based solely on lack of authorisation)

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the following: the applicants participated in what was at most a peaceful, if unauthorised, procession; they were arrested and sentenced to administrative detention solely because the procession lacked authorisation, without any assessment of the disturbance caused; and the sanction was a deprivation of liberty imposed on a prominent public figure, amplifying the chilling effect on political participation. The irreducible facts are:

- F007: applicants attended an assembly (establishes scope — assembly and participation)
- F010: applicants were walking to another authorised gathering (establishes the peaceful, non-threatening character of their conduct)
- F015: route blocked by riot police (establishes the interference by dispersal)
- F016: applicants arrested (establishes the interference by arrest)
- F051: Justice of the Peace found the first applicant had marched in an unauthorised public event — this is the sole basis for conviction, establishing that formal unlawfulness was the only justification for the interference
- F022: first applicant charged with breaching established procedure for conducting public events (confirms the charge was based solely on lack of authorisation)
- F057: first applicant sentenced to five days' administrative detention (establishes the severity of the sanction and the deprivation of liberty)
- F070: second applicant sentenced to five days' administrative detention (same)
- F073: applicants served sentences in detention centres (confirms actual imprisonment)
- F001: first applicant is a former World Chess Champion (grounds the chilling effect reasoning that was central to the Court's finding of disproportionality)
- F002: first applicant is a political activist (same)
- F004: opposition politicians organised protest rallies (grounds the political context essential to the chilling effect on open political debate)
