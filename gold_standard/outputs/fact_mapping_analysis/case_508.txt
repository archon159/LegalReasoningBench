LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court implies scope is satisfied through its description of the events as public assemblies with political purposes. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing that assemblies were planned, that the applicants were the organizers, and that the events had a political/expressive character (implying peacefulness).
Key facts: F007 (notification of picket), F009 (location: State Duma), F011 (purpose: protest against pension reform), F041 (notification of gathering), F044 (purpose: commemorate 1965 protest), F045 (purpose: urge authorities to respect rights), F046 (promise not to disrupt traffic — implies peaceful character)

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court accepts as undisputed that the refusals to approve the locations constituted interference. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the refusals.
Key facts: F012 (deputy prefect refused to approve location for Pleshkov), F047 (Moscow Department informed applicants another event was scheduled — effectively refusing Pushkin Square), F048 (alternative location proposed: Sokolniki Park)

--- PRESCRIBED BY LAW ---
The Court raises concerns about excessively broad discretion but does not make a definitive finding, relying on prior case-law rather than new facts. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing the legal framework and its acknowledged deficiencies.
Key facts: F113 (Supreme Court Ruling adopted), F119 (Ruling requires specific facts for refusals), F152 (Committee of Ministers recalled necessity of further changes to Public Events Act), F156 (Committee of Ministers urged limiting discretion of local authorities)

--- LEGITIMATE AIM ---
The Court accepts the Government's argument on legitimate aim, with a qualification about vagueness in Pleshkov's case. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing what reasons the authorities gave.
Key facts: F013 (safety requirements cited), F014 (disruption to transport/utilities cited), F015 (obstruction of pedestrians/rights of others cited), F023 (court found location unsuitable for safety reasons)

--- NECESSITY ---
This is the central element. The strictly necessary facts are those establishing: (a) the insufficiency of the authorities' reasoning; (b) the domestic courts' failure to properly scrutinize; (c) the importance of the chosen locations to the events' purposes; (d) the unsuitability of the proposed alternatives; (e) the failure to consider alternative arrangements.

For Pleshkov: F010 (only 20 participants), F012 (refusal), F013 (vague safety grounds), F016 (alternative: Lermontov Park, 3km away), F019 (applicant stressed importance of State Duma location), F022 (court relied only on traffic), F034 (court merely said alternative was accessible to public)

For Astrakhantseva: F044 (purpose tied to Pushkin Square historically), F045 (message targeted nearby authorities), F048 (alternative: Sokolniki Park), F051 (applicants stated Sokolniki Park incompatible — too far from target authorities), F053 (applicants requested non-overlapping time slot), F054 (no reply received to written proposal), F066 (other event ran 7:30am to 8pm), F078 (appellate court dismissed argument about simultaneous events), F080 (celebrations occupied 3,000 sq m — only factual basis given by courts)

--- POSITIVE OBLIGATION ---
The Court implies a positive obligation to facilitate assembly through good-faith efforts to find alternatives. The strictly necessary facts are those showing the authorities failed to make such efforts.
Key facts: F053 (applicants requested non-overlapping time slot), F054 (no reply received), F034 (court merely noted alternative was accessible), F016 (alternative proposed for Pleshkov was 3km away without assessment of suitability)


LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity analysis — specifically, whether the authorities gave relevant and sufficient reasons for refusing the applicants' chosen locations and whether they made genuine efforts to propose suitable alternatives. The following facts are strictly necessary:

F009: The picket was planned in front of the State Duma — establishing why location mattered to Pleshkov's message.
F011: The purpose was to protest pension reform voted by the State Duma — establishing the direct link between message and location.
F013: The refusal cited only vague safety grounds — establishing insufficiency of the original justification.
F016: The alternative proposed was Lermontov Park, 3km away — establishing that the alternative was not within sight and sound of the target.
F022: The domestic court relied exclusively on traffic issues — establishing the narrow and insufficient basis of judicial review.
F034: The appellate court merely stated the applicant was offered an alternative accessible to the public — establishing the failure to assess suitability of the alternative.
F044: The gathering's purpose was tied to Pushkin Square as the site of the 1965 protest — establishing why location was essential to the message.
F045: The message targeted authorities near Pushkin Square — establishing the link between location and target audience.
F048: The alternative proposed was Sokolniki Park — establishing the proposed alternative.
F051: The applicants stated Sokolniki Park was incompatible as it was too far from the target authorities — establishing the unsuitability of the alternative.
F053: The applicants requested a non-overlapping time slot — establishing that a less restrictive alternative was available and requested.
F054: The authorities did not reply to the written proposal — establishing the failure to engage in good-faith facilitation.
F080: The courts found the celebrations occupied 3,000 sq m — establishing the only concrete factual basis given, which the Court found insufficient without attendance figures or capacity assessment.
