LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of law

- Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope analysis (assembly exists, participation). Without F002, there is no factual basis for the Court to proceed under Article 11 at all. F001 is not strictly necessary for the legal reasoning itself — it merely identifies who the applicants are.
- Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. The "disproportionate measures" referred to in F002 — dispersal, arrest, conviction — are the governmental acts constituting the interference. No other atomic fact adds to this.
- Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are relevant; this element was not discussed and no facts bear on it.
- Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are relevant; this element was not discussed.
- Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that could be said to underpin the necessity analysis, insofar as the nature of the measures (dispersal, arrest, conviction of assembly participants) is what the Court assessed as not necessary in a democratic society. However, the Court's necessity conclusion is driven by case law rather than factual analysis, so the factual support is thin.
- Positive obligation: No atomic facts are relevant; this element was not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must establish at minimum: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (ii) that the state interfered with that participation through concrete measures (interference), and (iii) that this interference was not justified (necessity). All three of these steps are grounded — to the extent they are grounded at all — in F002 alone. F002 is therefore the single irreducible atomic fact: it establishes both the existence of public assemblies, the applicants' participation, and the nature of the state's measures against them. F001 identifies the applicants but is not itself a substantive fact that the legal conclusion turns on. F003 concerns other complaints and is irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion. Therefore, List 3 contains only F002.
