LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 was violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. For this conclusion to be reached under the Article 11 doctrinal structure, the Court must establish at minimum: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), and (ii) that governmental measures interfered with that participation (interference), and (iii) that those measures were disproportionate (necessity). All three of these points are grounded in F002 and F003 — the complaints of disproportionate measures against organisers and participants of public assemblies. These two facts are the irreducible core. F002 covers organisers and F003 covers participants; since the Court addresses both categories, both are necessary. No other atomic facts from the provided list are strictly necessary for the conclusion, as F004 concerns other complaints and F001 is merely a reference to the appended table.
