LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

--- SCOPE ---
The Court needed to establish: (i) that there was an assembly, (ii) that the applicants participated, and (iii) that it was peaceful. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F010 and/or F011: That a public gathering actually took place (assembly exists). F010 and F011 together cover both locations; both are necessary since applicants were at both locations.
- F014: That the applicants took part (participation).
- F015: That there is no information the meetings turned violent (peaceful character). This is the most critical fact for the peaceful sub-element, as it is the only factual basis for ruling out violence.
- F006: That the initial purpose was to attend a court hearing — this is specifically what the Court addresses when it says 'even though the participants' initial purpose was to attend a court hearing,' making it necessary to the scope analysis as conducted.

--- INTERFERENCE ---
The Court needed to establish that a governmental act interfered with the applicants' assembly rights. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F019: Apprehension and transfer to police stations (the arrest/transfer limb of interference).
- F020: Conviction under Article 20.2 § 5 (the sanction limb of interference).
- F013: Lack of notification was the sole stated reason — necessary to establish that the interference was triggered by the assembly itself, not by any other conduct.

--- NECESSITY ---
The Court needed to establish that the measures did not correspond to a pressing social need. The strictly necessary facts are:
- F015: No violence — the assembly was peaceful, which is the core factual basis for finding the measures disproportionate.
- F013: The sole reason for the measures was the lack of notification — this is what makes the interference fail the necessity test (punishing people merely for an unauthorised but peaceful assembly).
- F019: Arrest and transfer — the nature and severity of the measure applied.
- F020: Criminal conviction — the nature and severity of the sanction.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on three essential factual points: (1) the applicants participated in a peaceful public assembly; (2) the sole reason for the State's intervention was the absence of prior notification; and (3) the State's response included arrest and criminal conviction. These are the facts without which the violation finding cannot stand.

- F014: The applicants participated in the public meetings — without this, there is no standing under Article 11.
- F015: The meetings did not turn violent — without this, the 'peaceful assembly' finding (and thus the scope of Article 11) collapses, and the necessity analysis changes fundamentally.
- F013: The authorities had not been notified — this is the sole legal basis on which the State acted, making it the pivot of the necessity analysis.
- F019: The applicants were arrested and transferred to police stations — this is the primary interference with the right.
- F020: The applicants were convicted under Article 20.2 § 5 — this is the sanction that completes the interference and whose proportionality is assessed under necessity.
