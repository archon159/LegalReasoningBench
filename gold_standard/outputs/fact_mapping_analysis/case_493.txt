LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned on the necessity assessment — specifically whether the bans were proportionate and based on relevant and sufficient reasons. The irreducible facts are:

-> F007: The pedestrian area was over five metres wide — this directly refutes the police's core justification that the pavement was too narrow, and is the single most important physical fact.
-> F025: The demonstrations were planned for only twenty participants — the small scale makes the traffic disruption argument implausible.
-> F047: The demonstrations were planned as peaceful and silent, lasting only twenty minutes — the minimal nature of the event.
-> F048: The only planned action was to stand in line — reinforcing the minimal footprint and peaceful character.
-> F051: No buses were running after 4.19 p.m. on 24 December 2004 — this is the clearest example of the authorities ignoring a concrete factual clarification and mechanically repeating their justification, which is central to the Court's finding of a violation.
-> F039: The Budapest Police Department reiterated the same pavement-width reasoning for the later demonstrations — establishing the mechanical, non-individualised character of the bans.
-> F026: The prohibition of the 1 November 2004 demonstration — establishing that there was an interference (necessary for any violation finding).
-> F038: The prohibition of the subsequent demonstrations — establishing the repeated, mechanical nature of the interference.
