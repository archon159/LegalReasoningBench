LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion

The Court's conclusion is that Article 11 has been violated because the interferences with the applicants' freedom of assembly were not necessary in a democratic society. To reach this conclusion under the doctrinal structure of Article 11, the Court must at minimum establish: (i) that there were assemblies in which the applicants participated (scope), (ii) that there was a governmental interference (interference), and (iii) that this interference failed the necessity test. All three of these steps, as grounded in the facts available in this judgment, rest on a single atomic fact: F002. This fact establishes that the applicants were participants in public assemblies and that disproportionate measures were taken against them — which is the factual core that drives the entire analysis. No other atomic fact is strictly necessary for the conclusion.

F002 is therefore the sole irreducible atomic fact for the final conclusion. F001 identifies the applicants but adds no substantive content that the Court's legal reasoning depends on. F003 concerns other complaints and is entirely irrelevant to the Article 11 conclusion.
