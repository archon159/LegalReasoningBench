LIST 2: Atomic facts strictly necessary to support each element of the Court's reasoning

(a) Scope: F002 is strictly necessary. It establishes that the applicants were organisers or participants of public assemblies, which is the factual predicate for the scope analysis (assembly exists, participation). Without F002, there is no factual basis for the Court to proceed with an Article 11 analysis at all. No atomic fact supports the peaceful sub-element.

(b) Interference: F002 is strictly necessary. It describes the "disproportionate measures" taken against the applicants, which is the factual basis for the interference finding (arrest, dispersal, conviction for administrative offences).

(c) Prescribed by law: No atomic facts are strictly necessary here, as this element is not discussed.

(d) Legitimate aim: No atomic facts are strictly necessary here, as this element is not discussed.

(e) Necessity: F002 is the only atomic fact that can anchor the necessity analysis. The Court's conclusion that the interferences were not "necessary in a democratic society" is grounded in the nature of the measures described in F002, read against the prior case-law. No other atomic fact adds to this.

(f) Positive obligation: No atomic facts are strictly necessary here, as this element is not discussed.

LIST 3: Irreducible factual core for the final conclusion (Article 11 violated)

The case turned entirely on F002. This is the single atomic fact that: (i) establishes the applicants were participants or organisers of public assemblies (scope); (ii) identifies the state measures taken against them (interference); and (iii) provides the factual basis against which the Court assessed necessity by analogy with prior case-law. F001 is merely an administrative reference to the appended table and adds no substantive factual content. F003 concerns other complaints unrelated to Article 11. Therefore, F002 is the sole atomic fact strictly necessary for the Court's conclusion that Article 11 was violated.
